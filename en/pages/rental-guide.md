Renting a motorbike in Hanoi is simple once you know the sequence: choose the kind of bike, agree the period and the price, bring the right documents, check the bike at handover, and return it on time with fuel in the tank. This guide follows that sequence at Nguyen Ha in Long Bien, with the rules that apply at each step, so that there are no surprises at the counter or at the return.

## 1. Choose the kind of bike

Start from your riding experience and your plans, not from the price list. If you have never ridden a geared bike, choose an automatic scooter such as the Honda Vision or Air Blade: no clutch, no gears, storage under the seat. If you ride manual bikes at home, a Honda Wave, Yamaha Sirius or Honda Blade is light, economical and easy to repair anywhere. 50cc bikes are a separate class with their own licence conditions, and electric models differ in range, charging and paperwork from one model to the next. The [motorbikes page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbikes/) compares the groups; tell us which you prefer and we confirm what is actually available for your dates.

## 2. Check that you may ride it

Vietnam's road traffic law in force since 1 January 2025 (Law 36/2024/QH15) splits two-wheeler licences at 125 cc: class A1 covers motorcycles up to 125 cc, which includes the Vision, Wave and most scooters we rent, and class A covers larger engines. A foreign licence is used together with an International Driving Permit issued under the 1968 Vienna Convention; a permit issued only under the 1949 convention is not recognised in Vietnam. Bikes under 50 cc can be ridden from the age of 16 without a motorcycle licence, which is why 50cc models exist as a class, but every rider must still follow the road rules and wear a helmet. Riding without a licence that covers the bike also voids most travel insurance, so settle this before you choose.

## 3. Agree the period and the price

A rental day is 24 hours from the handover time written in the contract; a shorter rental counts as a day, and there is no hourly rate. Weekly and monthly packages are priced on their own, so compare the package rather than multiplying days. Reference prices are on the [price guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/prices/): manual bikes and automatic scooters from VND 150,000 per day, 50cc bikes VND 200,000 per day, electric models quoted individually. The exact figure for the actual bike is confirmed before you sign.

If your plans might change, think about it now. Returning early does not refund unused rent, and returning late costs VND 30,000 per hour for the first 1 to 5 hours and a full extra day from 6 hours. A renter who is unsure whether a stay will last three weeks or four is usually better off with a weekly package that can be extended than with a month paid up front.

## 4. Bring the right documents and the deposit

Bring your passport or identity card and your driving licence with its International Driving Permit. We check them, photograph them for the contract and hand them straight back; no original is kept once the deposit and the rent are paid. The deposit is cash, VND 2–5 million depending on the bike and the contract, paid together with the rent at signing and returned at the end of the rental under the contract's terms. Because the deposit secures the bike, we do not offer a no-deposit option.

## 5. Inspect the bike at handover

Check the bike with us before you sign: both brakes, the horn, the headlight, the indicators, the mirrors, the tyres and the bodywork. Photograph any scratch or dent so that the handover condition is clear to both sides. Note the fuel level: petrol bikes leave with roughly 0.5 to 2 litres and should come back with about 0.5 to 1 litre, and an empty tank at return costs VND 20,000. For an electric model, confirm the battery level and how and where it is charged.

One basic helmet per rider is included. Raincoats are not part of a short rental, and we do not provide a separate anti-theft lock, so plan on locking the steering and using guarded parking.

## 6. Sign, pay and note the time

The contract records the bike, the rental period, the rent, the deposit and the return time. Pay the rent and the deposit, keep your copy of the contract and any vehicle papers the shop gives you to carry, and write down the handover time: it is the time your rental day, week or month is counted from.

## 7. Ride and park in Hanoi

Ride at the pace of the traffic around you, keep right, signal with a short tap of the horn before overtaking, and expect the flow to be dense but slow. Fuel stations are staffed; you stay on the bike while the attendant fills it, and cash is always accepted. Park only in guarded lots or with a parking attendant, who gives you a numbered ticket that you need to collect the bike again. Helmets are compulsory for the rider and the passenger on every road.

If you plan a day trip outside the city, say so when you book so that we can suggest a suitable bike and confirm the return time. Motorbikes are not allowed on Vietnam's expressways, so day trips use the national roads.

## 8. Return the bike

Bring the bike back to the shop at the agreed time with the fuel it should have. We check it against the handover record, settle any late or fuel charge, and return the deposit under the contract. For weekly and monthly rentals with collection, agree the collection time and place in advance; collection follows the same distance and fee rules as delivery, and there is no collection at Noi Bai airport.
