In heavy rain, a semi-automatic motorbike is easier to ride well than to ride quickly: stay in a lower gear than you would in the dry, keep the throttle and brakes smooth, leave a much larger gap and stop under cover if the road is flooding or you cannot see. Honda's owner's manual for the Wave RSX FI states the essentials in two lines: wet roads are very slippery, and wet brakes lose effectiveness, so brake with extra care in wet weather and, if the brakes are wet, apply them lightly while riding at low speed to dry them.

This guide builds on that to explain how the gearbox, the brakes and your own habits should change in a downpour, what the law expects of your lights, how to read the road when the water is rising and when the right decision is to stop. For wider rain planning, including the season and the streets that flood, see our guide to [riding in Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/). This article is general advice, not a substitute for training.

## What heavy rain does to a small bike

Rain makes four things worse at once. Grip falls, especially on paint, metal covers and the first few minutes of a shower when oil lifts from the surface. Braking distances grow, because water gets between the pads or shoes and the drum or disc. Visibility drops, for you and for others, as spray, glare and a wet visor reduce what everyone can see. And the road itself can hide dangers, from potholes under puddles to manhole covers that sit low.

A light, air-cooled 110 cc bike copes with all of this if you give it room. It does not have the power to punish you for a clumsy throttle, but it has little margin if you lock a wheel or ride into water deeper than you thought.

## Choose a lower gear and stay calm in it

On a wet road, you want the bike to be settled and predictable. That usually means riding a gear lower than you would in the dry and keeping the engine in a comfortable, middle range, so that small throttle movements make small changes in speed. Honda's Wave manual says to reduce the throttle before every gear change and to press the shift lever gently with the toe. In rain, apply that to the letter. A hurried or heavy change can upset the rear wheel at the moment it has least grip.

Change gear in a straight line, not in a bend, and do it early. If you have to slow for a junction, ease off the throttle, brake lightly and change down while the bike is still upright and moving smoothly, as in our guide to [downshifting safely before corners on a small motorbike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/downshifting-safely-before-corners-on-a-small-motorbike/). Avoid changing down by more than one gear at a time, because a sudden jolt of engine braking on a wet surface can make the rear wheel skid. Gentle engine braking is useful in rain if you let it work gradually, as described in our guide to [engine braking on a semi-automatic motorcycle](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/engine-braking-on-a-semi-automatic-motorcycle/).

## The throttle: smooth in, smooth out

The most common cause of a slide in the wet is an abrupt throttle. Roll it on and off gently. Pulling away from a junction, open the throttle just enough to move; a snatch can spin the rear tyre on a painted line. Do not close the throttle sharply in a bend either, because the sudden change in drive can unsettle the bike.

If you feel the rear end move, stay calm. Keep the throttle steady or ease it a little, keep your eyes up and avoid grabbing the brakes. Most slides on a light bike correct themselves if you do not make them worse.

## Braking in heavy rain

Honda's safe-riding training document sets out the braking sequence: release the throttle, use both brakes together with the front stronger than the rear, keep the steering straight and, once stopped, hold the brake with the right foot and put the left foot down. In rain, the sequence is the same, but everything is done earlier and more gently.

- **Start slowing sooner.** Wet stopping distances are longer, so begin well before you would in the dry.
- **Squeeze, do not grab.** Build brake pressure progressively and release a little if a wheel starts to slide.
- **Use both brakes together,** and avoid using only the rear, which locks the wheel, or only the front, which can lose grip on a wet surface.
- **Do not brake in the middle of a bend.** Slow before it, as Honda's cornering advice says.
- **Give a larger gap.** Double the distance you would leave in the dry as a starting point and adjust to what you see.

Brakes that have been through water or heavy spray lose bite for a while. Honda's manual says: if the brakes are wet, apply them while riding at low speed to help dry them. Do this early in a wet ride and after crossing standing water, on a clear stretch with nobody close behind. A few gentle applications are enough. ## Where grip is worst

In a downpour, treat the following as ice-like and cross them upright and in a straight line, with a steady throttle and no braking or gear change:

- painted road markings, pedestrian crossings and arrows;
- metal manhole covers and drain gratings;
- railway tracks and steel plates at roadworks;
- the first minutes of rain after a dry spell;
- leaves, mud and oil near fuel stations and junctions;
- wet speed bumps with painted stripes.

If you must change direction, do it before the marking or after it, not on it. Our guide to [riding a manual motorbike over speed bumps](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-a-manual-motorbike-over-speed-bumps/) covers humps in detail.

## Lights and visibility

Under Article 7 of Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, which we read on the Government portal, a rider who does not use the lights in rain, fog, smoke or dust that limits visibility, or between 18:00 and 06:00, is fined VND 200,000–400,000 as of October 2026. Decree 238/2026/ND-CP, which amends the decree from 15 August 2026, does not change that article. Switch the headlight to dipped beam in heavy rain, keep the lights clean and check that the brake light works. Honda's night-riding advice is to wear bright or reflective clothing and keep a larger gap from other vehicles, and the same applies in a downpour.

A wet visor or helmet shield can blur everything. A clear, scratch-free visor, an anti-fog wipe and a helmet that fits well matter more than any gear. If you wear glasses, expect them to mist and plan to stop and wipe them. Our guide to [helmet rules in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/helmet-rules-in-vietnam/) explains the helmet requirements.

Honda's training document also warns about mirror blind spots and advises turning your head to look over the shoulder, not relying on mirrors alone. In rain, mirrors are covered in drops, so a quick look over the shoulder is even more important. Do not turn your whole body, which can turn the handlebars.

## Water on the road

Not all standing water can be ridden. Treat it by depth and certainty:

- **Shallow, clear puddles on a road you know:** ride through slowly in a low gear with a steady throttle, and dry the brakes afterwards.
- **Water of unknown depth, or flowing, or covering the whole road:** stop and wait, or turn back.
- **Water that reaches the engine, the exhaust or the footboards:** do not ride into it. Water in the exhaust, air intake or electrics can stop the engine and damage it.

Honda's training document lists flooded roads among the places where you need a careful balance technique at low speed: ride in a low gear, keep your head up, look straight ahead and keep the bike upright by using your hips and abdomen. We could not find official guidance on a safe wading depth for these bikes, so the conservative rule is to avoid anything deeper than the bottom of the wheel hub. Our guide to [riding in Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) lists the areas that flood.

If the engine stalls in water, do not keep pressing the starter. Honda's manual says that if the engine does not start within five seconds you should switch off and wait ten seconds before trying again, and repeated cranking drains a small battery. Push the bike to dry ground and call the shop.

## Starting and stopping in the wet

Wet roads make the start and stop cycle harder. When you stop at a junction, put your feet down on a surface that is not slippery, and stay away from painted lines. When you start, use a little more throttle than seems necessary to avoid a stall and then roll it on smoothly to avoid wheelspin. Our guide to [handling stop-and-go traffic on a semi-automatic bike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-handle-stop-and-go-traffic-on-a-semi-automatic-bike/) explains the technique in dense traffic.

With a passenger or luggage, the bike's braking distance and handling suffer more. Honda's manual warns that overloading affects handling, braking and stability, so slow down further and consider waiting out the worst.

## When to stop and wait

The best riding decision in heavy rain is often to stop. Pull over under cover, away from the traffic, when:

- visibility falls so low that you cannot see vehicles ahead;
- the road is flooding or you cannot see its surface;
- you feel the bike slide more than once;
- you are cold, tired or shaking;
- lightning is close.

A shower in Hanoi often passes in half an hour. Waiting in a cafe costs little. Do not stop under a bridge or in a place where you block a lane or a pavement.

## FAQ: riding a semi-automatic bike in heavy rain

### Which gear should I use in heavy rain?

A gear lower than you would in the dry, so the engine is steady and small throttle movements give small changes in speed.

### How do I dry wet brakes?

Honda's manual says to apply the brakes lightly while riding at low speed, in a safe place with nobody close behind.

### Do I have to use my lights in the rain?

Yes. As of October 2026, riding without lights in rain or poor visibility is fined VND 200,000–400,000 under Decree 168/2024/ND-CP.

### Is it safe to ride through floodwater?

Only shallow, known puddles. Do not ride into water of unknown depth, flowing water or water that reaches the engine or exhaust.

### What if the engine stalls in water?

Switch off, wait ten seconds before trying again, avoid repeated cranking and push the bike to dry ground. Call the shop.

### Is it better to wait out the rain?

Often yes. A shorter ride later is better than a slide now.
