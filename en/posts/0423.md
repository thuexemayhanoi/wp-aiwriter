Keep every receipt for anything you pay for on a rented motorbike, and make sure each one shows the date, who did the work, the plate number, what was done and the amount. If a stall gives you no paper, take a photograph of the stall, the repairer and the price, and write a short note on your phone straight away. Then send copies to the shop, ideally the same day. A paper trail matters because repairs on a rental are rarely only your business: the shop owns the bike, the contract decides who pays and any reimbursement depends on proof of what was done and what it cost.

Vietnam's Civil Code, which treats a motorbike rental as a lease of property, allows a lessee in some cases to repair at reasonable cost and ask the lessor to pay, but only after telling the lessor. This guide explains what to keep, what a good receipt contains, how to handle small stalls that do not issue receipts, how to log expenses, how to share them with the shop and how long to keep them.

## Why a paper trail matters

Imagine a puncture on the road to Ninh Binh, repaired at a roadside stall for a small amount. A week later, at return, the shop asks what happened to the tyre, or you ask the shop to cover the repair. Without a record, the conversation is memory against memory. With a note of the date, the place, the work and the price, and a message to the shop at the time, it is a simple comparison.

There are three reasons to keep records.

1. **To show what was done,** so the shop knows the bike's history.

2. **To support a claim,** if the contract or the circumstances mean the shop should pay or share the cost.

3. **To protect yourself,** if someone later suggests that you caused damage, ignored a fault or paid for unauthorised work.

Our guide to [how to report a mechanical problem to the rental shop](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-report-a-mechanical-problem-to-the-rental-shop/) explains the first step, which is telling the shop about the problem. This guide covers what comes after.

## What the Civil Code says about repairs and costs

Vietnam's Civil Code (Law No. 91/2015/QH13), in force since 1 January 2017, sets general rules for a lease of property. As of October 2026, which we read in the text published by the Tuyen Quang Department of Justice, two articles matter here.

- **Article 477** says the lessor must keep the leased property in the agreed condition and fit for use and must repair damage and defects, except minor damage that custom requires the lessee to repair. If the lessor has been notified and does not repair, or repairs late, the lessee may repair the property at reasonable cost, must tell the lessor and may ask the lessor to pay the repair cost.

- **Article 479** says the lessee must preserve and maintain the property and carry out minor repairs, and is not responsible for natural wear.

Two ideas follow. First, the word "reasonable" means the cost should be in line with what such a repair normally costs, so a receipt that shows what was done helps. Second, the lessee must tell the lessor, so a record of when you told the shop matters as much as the receipt. The contract fills in the details, and the shop's own terms may set who pays for what. This is general information, not legal advice.

## What to keep

Keep a record of any of these:

- **Repairs:** tyre or tube repair, brake work, chain work, a bulb, a mirror.

- **Parts:** a new tube, a new tyre, a lever, a bulb, a cable.

- **Recovery:** a tow, a taxi to carry the bike or a pick-up truck.

- **Related costs:** fuel bought to complete a journey, a replacement helmet if the original was lost, parking fees tied to a breakdown.

- **Anything the shop asked you to pay,** with the reason.

You may not claim all of these, but keeping them costs nothing and removes doubt. Do not throw away a receipt because you think the shop will not ask for it.

## What a useful receipt shows

A receipt is more useful the more it tells a stranger. Aim for these details.

| Detail | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Date and time | Shows when the work was done |
| Repairer's name, address or phone | Shows who did it, and lets the shop check |
| Plate number of the bike | Shows which bike it was |
| What was done | A patch, a new tube, a brake adjustment |
| Parts used, with brand if any | Shows what was fitted |
| Amount in VND | Shows the cost |
| Payment method | Cash, card or transfer |
| Signature or stamp, if offered | Shows it came from the repairer |

You do not need every detail. A handwritten note on a scrap of paper that says "tyre repair, plate number, date, amount" is much better than nothing.

## When the stall gives no receipt

Many small roadside stalls in Hanoi do not issue formal receipts. That is normal, so plan for it.

1. **Ask for a written note.** Hand over your phone's notes app or a pen and ask the repairer to write the date, the work and the amount. A phone translation app can help if you do not share a language.

2. **Photograph the price.** If there is a price board, photograph it with the repairer's stall in the frame.

3. **Photograph the stall and the repairer,** with the street sign or shop name if visible.

4. **Photograph the work,** before and after if you can: the damaged tyre, the object removed, the new part.

5. **Photograph the plate number** on the bike beside the stall.

6. **Write your own note immediately,** with the time, place, work and amount, and send it to the shop.

The photographs carry time and location data on most phones, so do not edit or crop them. A set of photographs and a message sent at the time is a good record even without a printed receipt.

## Ask to keep the old part

If a part was replaced, ask to keep the old one, or photograph it clearly. A bent lever, a cracked mirror, a worn tube or a cut tyre shows what was wrong. If the repairer wants to keep it, photograph it first. If the shop later asks to see the part, you will have either the part or its photograph.

## Agree the work with the shop before you pay

Receipts are strongest when the shop has agreed to the work in advance. Before a repair, tell the shop what is wrong, what the repairer proposes and roughly what it will cost, and ask for agreement. Our guides to [punctured tyre during a rental: who to contact](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/punctured-tyre-during-a-rental-who-to-contact/) and [requesting a replacement bike after finding a safety defect](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/requesting-a-replacement-bike-after-finding-a-safety-defect/) explain how. Keep the reply, even if it is one word.

Nguyen Ha's published terms do not set a standard repair procedure or price list for repairs during a rental, so ask the shop what it expects, and do not assume that an expense you pay will be reimbursed.

## A simple expense log

Keep a running list in your phone, a notes app or a paper notebook. One line per item is enough.

| Date | Place | What was done | Amount | Paid how | Shop told | Receipt or photo |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Date | Stall or workshop name, area | Short description | VND amount | Cash or card | Time and how | Photo reference |

Fill it in as you go, not at the end of the trip. A log is easy to share with the shop and makes the final conversation short.

## How to send it to the shop

- **Send photographs and a short note the same day,** by message. Nguyen Ha can be reached on +84 334 699 969, and staff can be called on 0334 699 969, open 09:00 to 21:00 daily.

- **Say what you did and why:** "Rear tyre punctured near X. Repaired at a stall at Y on [date]. Cost [amount]. Receipt attached."

- **Ask the shop to confirm receipt of the message,** and what happens next.

- **Keep the originals,** not only the copies.

- **Bring the paper receipts to the return,** and go through them together with staff. Our guide to [returning your rental motorbike in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/returning-your-rental-motorbike-hanoi/) explains the return routine.

If the shop agrees to reimburse part or all of a cost, ask for the amount and the method to be written down, such as a deduction from the final bill or a cash payment at return.

## If the shop disagrees

Sometimes the shop will say that a cost is yours, or that a repair was unnecessary or too expensive. Stay calm.

- **Show the record,** including the date and time you told the shop.

- **Ask what the shop would have done,** and what that would have cost.

- **Compare the amount with what such a repair normally costs.** A receipt that shows a fair price for the work helps your case.

- **Ask for the shop's position in writing,** with the reason.

- **Agree a reasonable figure** rather than a standoff, and get it written on the contract.

## Storing and backing up

- **Make one folder** on your phone or cloud account for the rental.

- **Name items clearly,** for example "2026-10-21 tyre repair receipt".

- **Back up the same day.**

- **Do not edit photographs.**

- **Keep paper receipts flat and dry,** in an envelope.

- **Keep everything until the deposit has been returned and for a few weeks afterwards,** because a traffic notice or a query can arrive late.

## What not to do

- **Do not pay for a repair you do not understand.** Ask what is being done.

- **Do not accept a receipt with an obviously wrong plate number or date.**

- **Do not throw away the old part** before the shop has seen the record.

- **Do not authorise major work** such as an engine repair or a replacement tyre without telling the shop.

- **Do not let a repairer modify the bike.** Our guide to [what counts as normal wear on a rented Honda Wave](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-counts-as-normal-wear-on-a-rented-honda-wave/) explains why ordinary wear and unauthorised changes are treated differently.

## A quick checklist

1. **Tell the shop before work starts, and keep the reply.**

2. **Ask for a receipt or a written note.**

3. **If none, photograph the price, the stall, the repairer and the plate.**

4. **Photograph or keep the old part.**

5. **Log the date, place, work, amount and when you told the shop.**

6. **Send copies to the shop the same day.**

7. **Back up, and keep it all until the deposit is back.**

## FAQ: keeping repair receipts

### What if the repairer gives no receipt?

Ask for a handwritten note, and photograph the price board, the stall, the repairer, the work and the plate number. Write your own note at once.

### Do I need to tell the shop before a repair?

Yes, where you can. The Civil Code says a lessee who repairs at reasonable cost must tell the lessor, and a shop's agreement makes a claim easier.

### Will the shop reimburse me?

It depends on the contract and the cause. Ask before you pay, and ask for any agreement in writing.

### How long should I keep the records?

Until the deposit has been returned, and for a few weeks afterwards.

### Should I keep the old part?

Yes, or photograph it clearly. It shows what was wrong.

### What if the amount seems high?

Ask for the work to be explained, compare it with what such a repair normally costs and tell the shop before you pay if you can.
