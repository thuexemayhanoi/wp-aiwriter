A scooter may use the two side carriageways of Long Bien Bridge when they are open, but not the railway in the middle, not the narrow pedestrian walkway and not any lane that a temporary notice has closed. Cars are not allowed on the bridge at all, and no vehicle may stop on it or turn round on it. The bridge has also been closed to motorbikes twice in the past year for repairs, once in one direction and once in both, so the legal answer today depends on the notices in force on the day. Chuong Duong Bridge, a short distance away, is the usual alternative. This guide sets out the places around the bridge where a scooter can legally ride, the places where it cannot, and the rules that explain the difference, using Vietnam's 2024 road law and recent Hanoi announcements.

## The short map: where a scooter belongs

| Place | Scooter allowed? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| The two side carriageways of Long Bien Bridge | Yes, when open | Shared with bicycles; cars are banned |
| The central railway track | No | Trains use it, and there is no barrier |
| The narrow pedestrian walkway | No | Not a bike route; park before the ramp |
| Chuong Duong Bridge | Yes | General traffic and a mixed lane for slower vehicles |
| Approach roads such as Long Bien-Xuan Quan and Tran Nhat Duat | Yes, following signs | These carried the diverted traffic in 2025 and 2026 |

## What the bridge is, and what it was built for

Long Bien Bridge was built between 1899 and 1902 by the French and carries a single-track railway down its centre. The national tourism authority describes two side paths for motorbikes and bicycles, about 2.6 metres wide, and a very narrow pedestrian way, and states that cars are banned. It also says that traffic on the bridge keeps to the left, unlike the right-hand traffic of other Hanoi bridges. That statement comes from a tourism page rather than from a traffic regulation, so the safest habit is simple: follow the flow of vehicles in front of you and read any arrows or barriers.

Because the bridge is a working railway, it is not a place for stopping to look. A tourism guide updated in October 2026 asks visitors not to stop in the middle of the motorbike lane for a photograph and not to walk on the rail tracks. The law makes the first of those a rule, not a courtesy.

## Closures in 2025 and 2026

The legal status of the side lanes changed several times in a few months.

- **1 November to 31 December 2025.** Authorities banned vehicles travelling from Bo De Ward toward Hoan Kiem Ward for about two months for maintenance, diverting traffic to Chuong Duong Bridge. A news report from 3 November described vehicles using the only open lane in both directions, which created a chaotic two-way flow. Motorbikes, bicycles and pedestrians were allowed again in that direction from 00:00 on 1 January 2026.

- **February 2026.** Serious damage to a steel joint in span 18 led the railway side to stop trains and close the section, and trains from Hanoi to Hai Phong moved their boarding point to Gia Lam station. This affected the railway line; check separately what applied to the road lanes at the time.

- **28 March to 27 May 2026.** Hanoi banned motorbikes, non-motorised vehicles and pedestrians from the bridge in both directions for 60 days to repair the pedestrian, motorbike and bicycle lanes. Riders were directed to Chuong Duong Bridge by routes such as Long Bien-Xuan Quan, Tran Nhat Duat and Tran Quang Khai, and bicycles were allowed in both directions in Chuong Duong's mixed lane.

- **From 00:00 on 28 May 2026.** The bridge reopened to motorbikes, bicycles and pedestrians in both directions after the deck was resurfaced and the walkway repaired. The Department of Construction told the contractor to remove the temporary signs and restore the previous traffic organisation.

For a renter this history has a practical point. A map app or an older blog may still show a closure, or may not show a new one. The legal position is whatever the barriers and signs say today.

## The rules that apply on and around the bridge

The Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety No. 36/2024/QH15 took effect on 1 January 2025. Several of its articles matter here:

- **No stopping or parking on a bridge** unless traffic organisation allows it (Article 18). That covers photo stops, waiting for a friend and breaking down for convenience.

- **No U-turn on a bridge or at a bridge head** (Article 15), nor at a level crossing, on a narrow road or on a one-way street unless a sign or officer allows it. This is why riders were seen turning round in confusion at the barriers on the first day of the March 2026 closure: an unplanned turn is often not allowed.

- **Reserved lanes stay reserved** (Article 13). A lane marked for one type of vehicle is closed to others, and slower vehicles keep to the right.

- **Railway rules apply near tracks** (Article 24). At a level crossing you stop before the stop line when the barrier or signal tells you to, and you must not park within the railway safety zone (Article 18).

- **Expressways are closed to motorcycles** (Article 25). That matters for the next bridge along the river; see our [guide to ring roads and highways open to motorbikes](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-ring-roads-highways-motorbikes-allowed/).

Penalties for breaking these rules are set in a separate government decree. Our [traffic fines guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/traffic-fines-vietnam-foreign-riders/) explains how they work for visitors.

## When you reach a barrier

If the approach is blocked, do not try to filter through or around it. The 2026 closure notices said that the road should be fully fenced and signed, and that police would regulate traffic at both bridge ends.

1. **Do not stop in the lane.** Pull into a lawful space, or keep moving to the next junction.
2. **Do not turn in front of the barrier.** If the barrier is at the bridge head, a turn there is not allowed; use the signed diversion or a junction a little way back.
3. **Check what is closed.** One direction, both directions, a vehicle type or the whole bridge each need a different plan.
4. **Take the diversion that suits your skill.** Chuong Duong is wider and busier, and it carried heavy congestion during the closures.
5. **Be ready to change the plan.** A walk or a different destination is a legitimate choice.

## Riding the side lanes when they are open

The legal question is only part of a safe crossing. A few practical points:

- **Narrow space, mixed users.** Motorbikes share the side path with bicycles, and pedestrians sometimes drift into it. Keep your speed low and keep your distance.
- **Surface changes.** The deck was resurfaced in 2026, but joints and wet surfaces still call for care after rain.
- **Wind and trains.** Wind over the river can push a light scooter, and the railway shares the structure, so expect trains.
- **Start rolling at the entrance.** Do not stop at the foot of the ramp to decide; the approaches are short.
- **Passengers and luggage.** Wide bags and extra riders make the narrow lane harder.

## Planning a legal route between the east bank and the Old Quarter

A sensible plan has a primary crossing and a fallback. When the bridge is open, the side lanes are the shortest link between the Bo De side and the Hoan Kiem and Old Quarter side. When it is closed, the notices of 2025 and 2026 sent riders to Chuong Duong Bridge, through the Long Bien-Xuan Quan road on the east bank and Tran Nhat Duat or Tran Quang Khai on the west bank. Both of those approaches filled up during the closures, so leave earlier than usual and keep a longer gap than usual.

Two habits keep the plan legal. First, decide the crossing before you reach the ramp, so that you never need to stop or turn on the structure. Second, if you are carrying a passenger or bags, think about whether the narrow side lane suits that load; our [guide to scooters for two riders and luggage](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/scooter-for-two-riders-and-luggage-hanoi/) explains the limits. For a visit to the Old Quarter itself, read the [Old Quarter riding guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-old-quarter/) before you go.

## FAQ about riding near Long Bien Bridge

### Can a motorbike ride across Long Bien Bridge today?

It could after 28 May 2026, when the bridge reopened to motorbikes, bicycles and pedestrians in both directions. Closures can recur, so check the barriers and notices on the day.

### Can a car cross the bridge?

No. The national tourism authority states that cars are banned on the bridge.

### Is the pedestrian walkway a shortcut for a scooter?

No. The walkway is for people on foot, and the railway track in the middle is for trains.

### May I stop on the bridge for a photograph?

No. The 2024 law says that stopping and parking on a bridge are not allowed unless traffic organisation permits them. Park before the ramp and walk.

### What is the alternative when the bridge is closed?

Authorities have used Chuong Duong Bridge, reached by routes such as Long Bien-Xuan Quan on the east side and Tran Nhat Duat on the west. Expect more traffic than usual.
