A three-day motorbike loop that ends back in Hanoi works when you treat it as three short riding days, not one long journey cut into pieces. Pick a shape first (a chain of stops, or a single base with day rides), cap each day at roughly five hours in the saddle including breaks, keep every leg on roads that motorbikes may use, and fix a return-day schedule that gets you to the shop before it closes at 21:00. The distances in this guide come from tourism sources and are quoted as ranges, because published figures vary with the exact road and the exact starting point. Check each leg in a map app before you leave. This is general planning information as of October 2026, not a promise of any road, weather or availability.

## What a loop is, and what it is not

A loop in this article means a trip that leaves Hanoi, sleeps in two different places or in one base for two nights, and returns to the same city on day three without riding any road twice. It is different from a day trip with an overnight bolted on, and it is different from a long-distance tour that ends elsewhere.

That definition matters for a rental. A rental bike has to come back to a particular place, so every route you draw is really a route that finishes in Hanoi, with its last leg shaped by the clock rather than by the scenery. Many first plans fail because the last day is the longest one, and the rider arrives after dark, tired, on the busiest roads of the trip.

Three habits separate loops that work from loops that do not:

- **Plan backwards from the return.** Decide when you must be back, then work out the latest sensible departure on day three.
- **Treat the middle day as the flexible one.** Day two is where you absorb a late start, rain or a long lunch.
- **Keep a bail-out.** Know in advance which town on each leg has a bus, a van or a workshop, in case the bike or the weather defeats you.

## Three loop shapes worth considering

There is no single best loop. The three shapes below use destinations that are already covered by our single-destination guides, so you can read the detail on each leg separately.

| Shape | Idea | Best for | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. The chain | Hanoi to Mai Chau, then Pu Luong, then Cuc Phuong or Ninh Binh, then back | Riders who enjoy changing scenery every day | Long, mixed-road legs and an unpredictable day two |
| B. Ninh Binh base | Ride to Ninh Binh on day one, spend day two on local roads, return on day three | First multi-day riders | Heavy truck traffic on the national highway at both ends |
| C. Mai Chau base | Ride to Mai Chau, do a valley and mountain loop on day two, return on day three | Riders who want hills without nightly packing | A demanding road on day one and day three |

### Shape A: the chain

The chain links the western hills with the karst country to the south. Published figures place Mai Chau at about 135 to 150 km from Hanoi, Pu Luong at about 165 km and Cuc Phuong National Park at roughly 120 to 140 km, which tells you that the three places sit in a rough arc rather than on one line. The stretch between Mai Chau and Pu Luong is quoted anywhere from about 40 km to about 70 km depending on the road chosen and the village you are aiming for, which is a reminder that guidebook numbers are indications, not measurements.

The stretch from Pu Luong to Cuc Phuong is not given consistently in the sources we found, so measure it yourself.

A sensible rhythm is Hanoi to Mai Chau on day one (the longest day on a main road), Mai Chau to Pu Luong or onward on day two (slow and scenic, on smaller roads), and a return from the southern end on day three. Our guide to [riding from Hanoi to Mai Chau](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-to-mai-chau-by-motorbike/) covers the first leg in detail.

### Shape B: the Ninh Binh base

Ninh Binh city is roughly 95 km from central Hanoi, and our [Hanoi to Ninh Binh guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-to-ninh-binh-by-motorbike/) budgets two and a half to three and a half hours of riding. Cuc Phuong National Park lies about 45 km west of Ninh Binh according to one rider's guide, and the Van Long reserve is about 25 km to the north. That gives you a day of local riding with a small number of kilometres and a large number of things to see.

This is the lowest-effort loop on the list. Its weakness is the approach: the old national highway south of Hanoi carries a great deal of freight, and some riders prefer quieter roads beside the Red River on the way out, which adds time.

### Shape C: the Mai Chau base

One tourism guide describes a motorbike loop of about 80 km through the Thung Khe pass and the higher villages of Hang Kia and Pa Co, using Mai Chau as the base. If you stay two nights in the valley, day two becomes that loop plus time on foot or by bicycle, and you avoid packing and unpacking. The price is that you ride the Highway 6 approach twice, and that road has sharp curves and a good deal of truck traffic.

## How far is realistic in a day

Published guidance for riders gives a useful starting point. One rental company suggests averaging about 150 km a day and about 40 km/h overall, while a tourism guide describes the Hanoi to Mai Chau ride as four to five hours including a break for roughly 140 km, which works out nearer 30 km/h. The difference is the terrain: the slow part is leaving the city, and the other slow part is the pass.

Use these as planning numbers rather than targets:

- **Out of the city:** assume the first 20 to 30 km are slow, because of traffic, junctions and lights.
- **National highways:** assume trucks, which means frequent slowing and cautious overtaking.
- **Mountain or hill roads:** halve your expectations. Hairpins, fog and fuel stops take time.
- **Breaks:** plan one every hour or two. Our guide to [planning breaks on a long ride](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-plan-breaks-on-a-long-semi-automatic-bike-ride/) explains why.

A simple test of any day: add up the kilometres, divide by 30 and add an hour for stops. If the answer is more than about six hours, shorten the day. A loop of three comfortable days beats a loop of two heroic days and one miserable one.

## Draft timetable for the chain loop

The following is an illustrative template, not a schedule from any source. Adjust the clock times to the season and to your own pace.

| Day | Leave | Ride | Stop for the night | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Early morning, after the shop opens at 09:00 | Hanoi to Mai Chau via Highway 6 | Mai Chau valley | Fill up before the pass, as one guide notes there is nothing reliable between Tan Lac and the summit |
| 2 | Mid-morning | Mai Chau towards Pu Luong, or the 80 km valley loop in Shape C | Pu Luong or a base to the south | Keep this day short; it absorbs delays |
| 3 | Early | Southern end back to Hanoi | The shop, before 21:00 | Aim to be on the final approach before dusk |

Note that the shop's opening hours are 09:00 to 21:00 daily. If you collect the bike at opening time, you cannot start a long first day at dawn. Either collect the bike the evening before (ask whether that fits your rental day) or choose a destination close enough that a mid-morning start is fine.

## Roads to design around

A loop is only as good as its worst road. Three considerations shape most routes out of Hanoi.

### Expressways are closed to motorbikes

Vietnam's Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety, Law 36/2024/QH15, effective 1 January 2025, says that motorcycles and mopeds may not travel on expressways, apart from road maintenance vehicles. The rule applies to every size of motorbike. A loop therefore has to be drawn on national highways and local roads. Our guide to [Hanoi's ring roads and highways](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-ring-roads-highways-motorbikes-allowed/) lists the main fast roads leaving the city and the ground-level alternatives.

For the south, Hanoi's own traffic plan for holiday periods in April 2026 lists the old National Highway 1 (reached by the Giai Phong and Ngoc Hoi corridor) as a general-traffic route alongside the expressway. For the west, the Vietnam Tourism guide cited above describes Highway 6 through Ha Dong, Xuan Mai and Hoa Binh as the main road to Mai Chau. Treat these as the corridors a motorbike loop can start from, but confirm each junction in a map app set for two wheels and verify it with the road signs on the day.

### Passes, fog and trucks

The Thung Khe pass on Highway 6 between Tan Lac and Mai Chau tops out at roughly 1,000 m according to one guide, and fog there can cut visibility sharply between November and February. The same guide describes Highway 6 as having sharp curves, loose gravel and overtaking trucks, and advises less confident riders to take a bus. That advice is worth taking seriously. Our guide on [manual bikes and steep mountain roads](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/manual-bikes-and-steep-mountain-roads-when-to-choose-another-vehicle/) explains when a small bike is the wrong tool.

### Daylight and weather

Short days in the cooler months mean you should be at your overnight stop before dusk. Rain is a different matter: wet roads lengthen braking distances and slow every leg. If the forecast is poor for the day you planned to cross a pass, swap days or switch to Shape B.

## Agree the trip with the shop before you leave

A multi-day loop is a different arrangement from a city rental. Before you commit:

1. **Tell the shop your route and nights.** Our guide to [taking a Hanoi rental motorbike out of the city](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/take-rental-motorbike-out-of-hanoi/) lists the questions to ask. Do not assume permission; ask.

2. **Ask which bike is available.** The shop confirms the actual bike when you book, and a small bike suits flat roads better than hills.

3. **Check the rental period.** One rental day is 24 hours, and shorter use is charged as a full day. Weekly and monthly packages are priced on their own. Ask how a three-day loop is counted and confirm the total on the [prices page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/prices/) or by phone.

4. **Agree the return time.** Late returns are charged at VND 30,000 per hour for hours one to five and as an extra rental day from six hours, so a plan that depends on arriving at the last minute is a poor one.

5. **Ask about fuel.** Petrol bikes are handed over with about 0.5 to 2 litres and come back with about 0.5 to 1 litre, so you will refuel on the way. An empty tank at return costs VND 20,000.

6. **Ask what to do in a breakdown.** Our guide to [what to do with a rental bike breakdown](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-bike-breakdown-hanoi-what-to-do/) covers the first steps. Keep the shop's number, 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969), in your phone and on paper.

Electric scooters are rarely a good fit for a loop of this kind, because range and charging in rural areas are uncertain. The shop's own guidance is that battery level and charging are confirmed at handover, so ask before assuming any electric bike can make a leg.

## Return-day planning

Return day decides whether the loop feels successful. Work backwards:

- **Latest arrival:** the shop closes at 21:00. Set your target arrival at least two hours earlier.
- **Latest departure:** divide the day's kilometres by 30, add an hour for stops and add an hour for the final approach to the city, which is the slowest part.
- **Fuel:** top up on the last leg so that you are not hunting for a station at dusk.
- **Condition check:** photograph the bike on arrival. Our guide to [returning your rental motorbike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/returning-your-rental-motorbike-hanoi/) explains what staff will look at.

If you are running late, call the shop rather than racing the clock. A short call is cheaper than a rushed final hour.

## A packing and morning checklist

- Helmet that fits and is fastened (the shop provides one basic helmet per rider).
- Rain layer, because a raincoat is not included for short rentals.
- Phone mount only if it does not block the instruments, a charging cable and a power bank.
- Offline map of the whole route, with the shop's address saved. Our guide to [checking whether a navigation app suggests an expressway](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-check-if-a-navigation-app-suggests-an-expressway/) explains how to avoid the wrong road.
- Water, a small first-aid kit and cash for fuel in small towns.
- Riding documents. The [documents guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/documents-to-carry-riding-in-vietnam/) lists what to carry.
- A morning check of tyres, brakes, lights and fuel before every leg.

## What to cut when the plan slips

Every loop meets a delay. Decide your cuts now, not when you are tired:

1. **Drop a stop, not a night.** Skip the optional viewpoint rather than riding after dark.

2. **Shorten day two first.** It is built to absorb change.

3. **Switch shape.** If the pass is fogged in or the rain is heavy, turn a chain into a base-and-day-ride trip.

4. **Use a bus or van for one leg** if the bike or the weather is a problem, and keep the shop informed.

## FAQ: three-day loops from Hanoi

### How many kilometres should I ride each day?

Think in hours rather than kilometres. About five hours of riding including breaks is a comfortable cap, which is roughly 100 to 150 km on mixed roads according to published rider guidance.

### Which loop is easiest for a first multi-day trip?

The Ninh Binh base is the least demanding, because most of day two is local riding on flatter roads.

### Can I use an expressway to save time?

No. Motorbikes are not allowed on expressways under Law 36/2024/QH15. Use national highways and local roads.

### Can I take the bike outside Hanoi at all?

Ask the shop. Permission depends on the rental agreement and the bike, and we do not promise it for any route in advance.

### What if I am late back to the shop?

Call as soon as you know. Late returns are charged by the hour for the first five hours and as an extra day after that, so an early call helps.

### Is a small manual bike right for hills?

Often not. Check the steep-road guide linked above and choose a bike with more engine and good brakes if the route includes passes.
