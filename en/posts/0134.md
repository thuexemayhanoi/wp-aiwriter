For light, easy riding in Hanoi, such as short city hops, one rider and a small bag, both scooters do the job, and the choice comes down to size, features and condition. On Honda Vietnam's figures the Vision is the longer, taller scooter with a 785 mm seat, a 4.8 litre tank, a 16-inch front wheel and modern extras on some versions, including a smart key and idling stop. The Mio, from Yamaha's published figures for various markets, is the smaller one with a seat around 745 to 750 mm and a tank of about 4.1 to 4.2 litres, and it tends to suit shorter riders who want the lowest, simplest machine. This guide compares them point by point, explains where the numbers are not directly comparable and lists what to check on the bike you are actually given.

## What "lightweight riding" means here

The phrase is not a technical term, so it helps to define it. In this guide it describes riding with these features:

- A single rider, or a rider with a light backpack.
- Short to medium city trips on flat, busy streets.
- Frequent stopping, turning and parking in tight spots.
- A rider who values a small, easy-to-handle scooter over speed or luggage space.

It does not mean ignoring safety. A light scooter still needs a good helmet, careful braking and attention. ## The two scooters in brief

The **Honda Vision** is a long-running Honda automatic with an air-cooled 110 cc four-stroke engine. Honda Vietnam describes its engine as a single-cylinder, air-cooled unit with electronic fuel injection, and the manual describes an idling stop system and a Smart Key on some versions.

The **Yamaha Mio** is a long-running Yamaha automatic scooter sold across South-East Asia. Wikipedia describes it as a CVT scooter introduced for the region in 2003, with several variants since, including the Mio Classico that Yamaha Vietnam presented in 2006 and 125 cc versions in other markets. The shop's Vietnamese price page names the Mio and the Mio Ultimo among automatics it may offer, but the bike actually available is confirmed at booking.

That last point matters. Because Mio models span many years and markets, the specifications below are for reference, not a description of the exact scooter you will receive.

A few kilograms either way will not change how either scooter feels. Second, the Mio column mixes models, so treat it as a range.

## Size and handling

The Vision is the bigger scooter by every measure in the table: longer, wider, taller in the seat and with more ground clearance. The Mio is shorter and lower. For lightweight riding the difference shows in three places.

**Manoeuvring.** A shorter wheelbase and length make a scooter feel quicker to turn and easier to push out of a tight parking row. The Mio has the edge here, by a modest margin. The Vision is still compact by any measure, but you will notice its longer body in the narrowest alleys.

**Stability.** A longer wheelbase and larger front wheel generally add calm at moderate speed, on rough patches and when crossing tram tracks or uneven road edges. The Vision's tyre data lists an 80/90-16 front and a 90/90-14 rear. The Mio Classico's data lists 14-inch wheels at both ends. As general guidance, a larger front wheel rolls over potholes and joints more smoothly, while smaller wheels turn slightly quicker. That is an observation about wheel size, not a test of these two bikes.

**Ground clearance.** The Vision's higher clearance is helpful on speed bumps, kerbs and rough sections. The Mio sits lower, so take bumps slowly.

## Seat height and reaching the ground

For a shorter rider the seat height may decide the matter. Honda lists the Vision's seat at 785 mm, and Yamaha figures put the Mio at roughly 745 to 750 mm. Forty millimetres is a small difference on paper, yet when you sit at a red light with a bag between your feet, it can decide whether you stand flat-footed or on your toes.

Seat height is only part of the story. The width of the seat and the shape of the floorboard also decide how easily you reach the ground. If you are short or new to riding, sit on both and ask the shop to hold the scooter upright while you do. ## Features: Vision's extras versus Mio's simplicity

Here the two diverge most.

The Vision, in the versions covered by Honda Vietnam's manual, can have:

- A **smart key**, so you carry a fob instead of inserting a key.
- An **idling stop system** that stops the engine at red lights and restarts it with the throttle.
- A side stand cut-off, which prevents starting with the stand down.
- A rear brake lock for holding the bike while starting.

The Mio generally keeps things simple. The Classico described by Yamaha Vietnam has both electric start and a kick lever, and a hydraulic front disc brake. A kick lever can be a useful backup when the battery is weak, but check that it actually works on the bike you rent.

Neither list is better. Smart key and idling stop save fuel and effort but add things that can go wrong. A smart key adds a fob and a fob battery to look after, and an idling stop system adds a function that relies on the battery being healthy. If you want the simplest machine, tell the shop.

## Fuel tank and stops

The Vision's tank is 4.8 litres, and the Mio's is about 4.1 to 4.2 litres. In practice you will stop for fuel more often on the Mio on longer days. Honda states 1.82 litres per 100 km for the Vision in its tests, which is a sound figure for planning but optimistic for real city traffic.

The shop hands petrol bikes over with about 0.5 to 2 litres, so for either scooter, expect to refuel soon after pick-up. Our guide to [fuel in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/fuel-in-hanoi-petrol-stations-rental-scooter/) covers stations and filling up.

## Age and condition matter more than the badge

A lightweight scooter that is well maintained will feel better than a newer, heavier one that is neglected. Mio models include older units, and rental fleets often use older bikes. Whatever you are given, check:

1. Brakes: lever travel, noise and how the bike stops from walking pace.
2. Tyres: tread, cracks and pressure.
3. Throttle: smooth return, with no sticking.
4. Starting: both electric and, if present, kick.
5. Lights, horn and indicators.
6. Looseness in the steering or a wobble at low speed.

Use our [10-minute inspection checklist](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-scooter-inspection-checklist/) at handover, and photograph any existing marks.

## Which one for which rider?

Lean towards the Vision if you:

- Are of average height or taller.
- Want the larger tank and higher ground clearance.
- Like modern conveniences such as smart key and idling stop, if the bike has them.
- Expect some longer rides or rougher roads.

Lean towards the Mio if you:

- Are shorter or want the lowest seat.
- Value a compact scooter that is easy to turn and park.
- Prefer a simpler machine, with a kick lever as a backup where one is fitted.
- Will keep to short, flat city runs.

## Asking the shop

When you book, ask which scooter will be available, because the shop confirms the bike at booking and does not promise a particular model. Ask:

1. Which exact model and year is the bike?
2. Does it have a smart key or a traditional key?
3. Does it have idling stop, and can I turn it off?
4. How much petrol is in the tank?
5. Is there a kick lever, and does it work?
6. Can I sit on it before I sign?

## FAQ: Vision versus Mio

### Which is lighter?

The figures are close. Honda gives a 97 kg kerb weight for the Vision, and Yamaha's 2006 figures for the Mio Classico were 94 kg wet. The definitions differ, so the real gap is small.

### Which is easier for a short rider?

The Mio has a lower seat on published figures, about 745 to 750 mm against the Vision's 785 mm. Sit on both if you can.

### Which has the bigger tank?

The Vision, at 4.8 litres, against roughly 4.1 to 4.2 litres for the Mio.

### Do both have electric start?

The Vision does. The Mio Classico has electric start and a kick lever. Ask about your bike.

### Which should a first-time automatic rider choose?

Either can suit a careful beginner. Pick the one you can sit on comfortably and control at a walking pace.
