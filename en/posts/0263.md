You cannot measure a scooter battery's health by looking at it, so ask the shop. The useful questions are about age and history (how old is this battery, has it been replaced, has it been through water or a fault), about performance (what range does this bike give from a full charge, how does it behave at the end of a charge) and about care (how is it charged, how is it stored, what happens if it fades during your rental). A good shop will answer plainly and may say "I do not know" about a detail, which is more honest than a confident guess. This guide gives the questions in a sensible order, explains why each one matters using VinFast's published battery guidance, and suggests simple checks you can do yourself at pickup.

## What "battery health" means

A lithium-ion battery loses a little usable capacity with age and use. Engineers call the remaining share its state of health. The decline comes from two kinds of ageing: wear from charge and discharge cycles, and the passing of time, which is faster when the battery is hot or kept full. A general battery reference explains that cycle life is the number of cycles a battery completes before its capacity falls to a defined level, commonly 70 to 80 per cent of the original, and that the result depends on depth of discharge, charge rate, temperature, charge window and chemistry.

VinFast says much the same about its scooters.

Its FAQ states that battery life depends strongly on how often the scooter is used and how it is charged, that the battery lasts longer when used between 20 and 80 per cent of capacity, and that lithium batteries are sensitive to high temperatures, so you should avoid charging them when hot. Its warranty page adds that natural ageing and the gradual reduction in maximum capacity are not covered, because they depend on charging method, charging current, cycles and ambient temperature. In other words, some fade is normal and expected, which is exactly why the questions below matter.

The numbers are worth reading with care. The same FAQ says that, for an older VinFast battery model, testing over 1,000 charge and discharge cycles left about 70 per cent of capacity, which it equates to six to eight years of use. The current warranty page says VinFast scooters with LFP batteries have an eight-year battery warranty. Those are manufacturer statements about specific products, so they tell you what to ask about, not what your rental bike has.

## The twelve questions

### About the battery's age and history

1. **How old is this battery, and is it the original?** Ask for the year or month if the shop knows it. A battery that has been replaced is not a problem in itself, and a fresh one can be a good sign.
2. **Has it ever been in water, dropped or crashed?** Heavy rain is common in Hanoi, so a "yes" is not shocking. What matters is that it was checked afterwards. Our guide to [riding in Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) explains the weather side.
3. **Has this bike shown battery fault symbols?** Faults are not always a battery defect, but a repeat history is worth knowing. The shop's [electric scooters page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/electric-scooters/) lists the kinds of vehicles it may offer.
4. **Which model is it, and what battery does it use?** The model decides everything else. VinFast's own pages show that capacity, chemistry and warranty differ between its older and newer scooters, so a general number from the internet may not describe your bike.

### About performance

5. **What range does this bike give from a full charge in real use?** Ask what the shop has seen recently with ordinary riders in the city, not what the brochure says. Our guide to [renting an electric scooter in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/renting-an-electric-scooter-in-hanoi/) explains why real range is lower than claims.
6. **How does it behave near empty?** Does it slow, cut out or warn early? Ask what the first and second warnings mean.
7. **What does it read when fully charged?** Agree a reading at pickup and photograph it. The shop's own rule is that battery level and charging are confirmed at handover.

### About care

8. **How does the shop charge it?** Which charger, where and for how long? The VinFast Evo 200 manual, for example, says to use only the charger supplied with the bike or an equivalent from VinFast and warns that the wrong charger can damage the electrical system.
9. **Is the battery kept in a sensible state between rentals?** The same manual advises that if the bike will stand for more than 14 days the battery should be charged above 50 per cent and switched off, that a battery below 20 per cent should be recharged within three days, and that it should be charged to 100 per cent at least once a month. A shop that rents a fleet will have its own routine, so ask what it is.
10. **Do you swap or replace batteries in the fleet?** Ask whether the battery in your bike may be swapped for another during the rental and how that is recorded.

### About responsibility

11. **If the battery fades or fails during my rental, what happens?** Ask whether you would get another bike, and whether the time you lose is counted.
12. **What does the contract say about battery damage?** Read the clause before you sign. Our guide to [what the clauses in a Hanoi rental contract mean](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-motorbike-rental-contract-explained/) explains how to read it.

## What the battery's size tells you

Some shops can give you the battery's rated capacity. The Evo 200 manual, for example, lists a nominal capacity of 48 amp-hours at a nominal 70.4 volts. Multiplying the two gives roughly 3.4 kilowatt-hours, which is my own arithmetic, not a figure from the manual. The manual also lists a standard charging time of about 10 hours, and notes that the actual time depends on the state of the battery, such as its starting voltage and how aged it is.

A larger battery gives more range for the same riding style, but it does not tell you the condition. A big old battery can give less than a small new one. If you compare bikes, ask for the rated capacity and the age together.

## Checks you can do at pickup

Without any tools, you can still gather evidence.

- **Read the indicator.** A bike that the shop says is fully charged should show a full or near-full battery. If not, ask why.
- **Look at the first kilometre.** Does the reading drop faster than it should on a short ride? Take a photo before and after, if the shop allows a short test.
- **Check for heat.** After charging, the battery area should not feel hot. The manual says the battery stops working above 60 degrees Celsius internally and lists 55 degrees as the maximum internal temperature during charging. If the bike or the charger feels uncomfortably hot, say so.
- **Look at the display.** A symbol relating to battery temperature or a fault code before you leave is a reason to ask for another bike. The shop's [rental scooter inspection checklist](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-scooter-inspection-checklist/) shows the wider handover.
- **Check the charging port and charger.** Cracks, scorch marks or a loose fit are reasons to say something.

These checks cannot prove the battery is healthy. They can show when something looks wrong.

## Reading the answers

Good signs include a clear answer about age, a shop that can tell you how it charges and stores the bikes, a stated real-world range that is lower than the brochure and a willingness to swap the bike if there is a problem. Warning signs include vague answers about water or faults, a battery that is hot to the touch, a reading that does not match what you were told and a reluctance to put anything about batteries in the agreement. None of these proves a problem, but together they help you decide whether to take the bike, ask for a different one or choose a petrol scooter instead. If you are unsure, the shop's [electric scooters page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/electric-scooters/) and [prices page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/prices/) are a better starting point than a guess.

## How you can look after the battery

Your habits affect battery wear during the rental.

- **Charge when needed rather than letting it run to nothing.** VinFast says the 20 to 80 per cent window is better for lithium-ion life, and the manual adds that you should not hesitate to charge when you can.
- **Do not charge a hot battery.** After a long ride in the sun, let it cool first.
- **Park in the shade.** The manual notes that leaving the bike in temperatures above 45 degrees Celsius or in direct sun can reduce performance.
- **Use only the charger the shop gives you.** Our guide to [charging an electric scooter in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/charging-an-electric-scooter-in-hanoi/) covers hotels and swap points.
- **Report changes early.** A sudden fall in range, a new warning symbol or a bike that does not reach the charge level you started with is information the shop needs.

## FAQ: electric scooter battery health

### Can I tell the battery's health from the display?

Not reliably. The display shows charge level, not health. Ask for the age, history and real-world range.

### Is some capacity loss normal?

Yes. VinFast describes natural ageing and gradual loss of maximum capacity as normal wear.

### What is a good range to ask about?

Ask what the shop sees in ordinary city riding on a full charge, not the manufacturer's best figure.

### Is a battery that has been replaced a bad sign?

Not by itself. A replacement can mean the shop looks after the fleet. Ask when and why.

### Should I fully charge it every day?

Follow the shop's advice. VinFast says 20 to 80 per cent is better for long-term life, while the Evo 200 manual also recommends a full charge at least once a month.

### What if the range is much shorter than promised?

Tell the shop early, while you can still reach a charger or swap the bike.
