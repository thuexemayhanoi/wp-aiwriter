Ride defensively around pedestrians by assuming they have not seen you, keeping your speed low enough to stop within the distance you can see, holding a steady, predictable line so they can judge where you will be, and giving way whenever the law or common sense says so. Law 36/2024/QH15 requires operators to observe the surroundings and slow down or stop where pedestrians are crossing and when they meet groups of pedestrians (Article 12), bans overtaking on pedestrian crossings and when pedestrians are crossing (Article 14), and requires those changing direction to give way to pedestrians (Article 15). The World Health Organization notes that more than half of all road traffic deaths are among pedestrians, cyclists and motorcyclists, so every conflict between a scooter and a person on foot is one where the person is more likely to be seriously hurt.

This guide explains the legal duties, how pedestrians behave in Hanoi, a set of habits that keep you out of trouble, how to handle crossings, turns, markets and crowded lakeside streets, how to signal and communicate, how to ride at night and in rain and what to do if you hit someone. It is general information based on the law and published safety guidance as of October 2026, not legal advice.

## What the law asks of you

Several provisions of Law 36/2024/QH15 bear on a rider near pedestrians.

- **Slow down or stop (Article 12).** Operators must observe the surroundings and slow down or stop for safety at places with pedestrian crossing markings or signals or where pedestrians or wheelchair users are crossing, in crowded areas such as marketplaces and populous areas, and when they encounter pedestrian groups.

- **No overtaking at crossings (Article 14).** Overtaking is prohibited on pedestrian crossings and when pedestrians or wheelchair users are crossing the road.

- **Give way when turning (Article 15).** On changing direction, operators must give way to pedestrians, rudimentary vehicles and oncoming traffic, and may turn only when it will not cause obstruction or danger.

- **Green light (Article 11).** At a green light, vehicles may proceed but must slow down or stop for pedestrians and wheelchair users on the roadway.

- **Stopping and parking (Article 18).** You may not stop or park on pedestrian crossings or within 5 metres of intersections.

- **Horns (Article 21).** Use the horn only to warn of circumstances that may affect safety or to signal overtaking, and not continuously.

- **Pedestrians' own duties (Article 30).** Pedestrians should walk on sidewalks and roadsides, cross at crossings where there are any, and, where there are none, observe oncoming vehicles and cross with hand signals when it is safe. Children under 7 must be escorted by adults when crossing, and all people are asked to help children, pregnant women, old and weak people and people with disabilities cross.

The penalty decree reflects this. A Vietnamese penalty-lookup site that extracts figures from Decree 168/2024/ND-CP lists a fine of VND 1,000,000 to 2,000,000 and 2 licence points for a rider who changes direction without giving way to pedestrians, and legal publications report a fine of VND 10,000,000 to 14,000,000 and 10 points for failing to observe, slow down or stop as required and causing an accident. Check the current text of the decree before relying on a figure, and see our guide to [how to verify the current fine for a traffic offence](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-verify-the-current-fine-for-a-traffic-offence/).

## How pedestrians behave in Hanoi

Local custom does not always match the rulebook, and visitors on a scooter need to understand the difference.

- **Many people cross where they are, not at a marked crossing.** Article 30 expects pedestrians to cross with signals and markings where they exist, but in practice people often cross mid-block, in a steady stream, and expect vehicles to flow around them.

- **They watch the traffic, not always you.** A person who looks left may not look right.

- **They move in groups,** with children, strollers and shopping.

- **They walk on the road** where the pavement is full of parked bikes, stalls and tables.

- **They stop suddenly** to look at a phone, a shop or a friend.

- **They use phones and earphones,** and may not hear a horn.

- **They are older, younger and slower than you expect,** including people with limited sight and hearing.

The aim is not to blame them. A scooter weighs more, goes faster and can stop less quickly than a walker, so the responsibility to slow down falls on you.

## Habits that keep you out of trouble

**1. Assume you are invisible.** Rider-safety guidance from the Motorcycle Safety Foundation tells riders to ride as if they are invisible and to give other road users time and space. Do the same with pedestrians: do not rely on them having seen you.

**2. Use SEE: search, evaluate, execute.** Scan ahead, look for people at kerbs, between parked vehicles and at shop fronts, judge what each might do and adjust your speed and position before you need to react. Our guide to [how to follow a safe distance in Hanoi traffic](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-follow-a-safe-distance-in-hanoi-traffic/) explains the same habit for vehicles.

**3. Keep a speed at which you can stop.** The WHO notes that a pedestrian hit at 65 km/h is 4.5 times more likely to die than one hit at 50 km/h, and recommends 30 km/h or less where people walk and play in numbers. A slow scooter is far safer for the person in front of it.

**4. Be predictable.** A rider who keeps a steady line and a steady speed lets pedestrians judge the gap and cross behind or in front of you. A rider who weaves, accelerates and brakes unpredictably makes it impossible.

**5. Do not use gaps you cannot see through.** A gap between two parked cars may hide a person about to step out.

**6. Cover the brakes** when approaching crossings, markets, bus stops and crowded frontages.

**7. Look at faces and feet,** not only at the body. Feet show where a person is about to move.

**8. Make eye contact** when you can, and wait for a nod or a pause.

**9. Give extra room to children, the elderly and people with disabilities.** Article 30 asks everyone to help them cross.

**10. Never use a pedestrian as a speed bump.** If someone is in your path, stop. Do not squeeze through with a horn blast.

## At a pedestrian crossing

Slow down early, with fingers on the brakes. If a person is on the crossing or about to step on, stop behind the line and let them clear the whole road, not just your lane. Do not edge forward, and do not overtake a vehicle that has stopped at the crossing, because Article 14 bans overtaking there and the vehicle may be stopped for someone you cannot see. At a green light, still look for people crossing late. At a red light, stop behind the line, not on the crossing. ## When you turn

Turns are where scooters most often meet pedestrians, because people cross the side street while you look for a gap in the main flow.

1. **Slow down before the turn,** and signal early.

2. **Look for pedestrians on the road you are entering,** not just for vehicles.

3. **Give way to people crossing,** as Article 15 requires.

4. **Do not turn at speed.** A wide, fast turn leaves no room to stop.

5. **Look again before you commit.** Check the near side and the far side.

## Signals and communication

- **Use your indicator early,** so people can see your intention.

- **Use a short horn tap** as a warning when someone is about to step out. Article 21 allows the horn to warn of circumstances that may affect safety, and bans continuous use. Do not use it between 22:00 and 05:00 in populous areas.

- **Use your lights** in poor visibility, and keep them clean. Our guide to [how to check a scooter's horn and turn signals](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-check-a-scooters-horn-and-turn-signals/) explains the check.

- **Use a hand signal or a slight nod** to show that you are giving way.

- **Do not shout or gesture angrily.** It can startle a person into a bad move.

## At night and in rain

Pedestrians are harder to see at night, in dark clothes and in rain, and umbrellas hide their view of you. Reduce your speed further, use your low beam and watch the edges of the road. ## If you hit a pedestrian

1. **Stop at once,** switch off the engine and, if you can, switch on hazard lights.

2. **Check the person,** and do not move them if they are badly hurt unless there is danger.

3. **Call 115 or 112.** Article 80 of the Law requires people involved in an accident to stop, warn of danger, keep the scene, help the victims and report to the nearest police. Fleeing is an offence. 4. **Do not argue or admit fault.** Describe facts only, and take photographs.

## A quick checklist

1. **Assume pedestrians have not seen you.**

2. **Ride at a speed that lets you stop within what you can see.**

3. **Be predictable: steady line, steady speed, early signals.**

4. **Stop at crossings, and never overtake there.**

5. **Give way to pedestrians when turning.**

6. **Treat children, older people and people with disabilities with extra room.**

7. **Slow to walking pace in markets and crowds, or walk.**

8. **If someone is hurt, stop, call 115 or 112 and the shop.**

## FAQ: riding around pedestrians

### Do pedestrians have right of way in Vietnam?

Law 36/2024/QH15 requires operators to slow down or stop where pedestrians are crossing and to give way to pedestrians when changing direction (Articles 12 and 15). Pedestrians have duties too, but the rider carries the greater responsibility because of the speed and weight of the bike.

### Can I overtake near a crossing?

No. Article 14 bans overtaking on pedestrian crossings and when pedestrians are crossing the road.

### Should I use my horn at pedestrians?

A short tap to warn is allowed. Do not use it continuously, and do not use it as a substitute for slowing down.

### What if people cross in front of me away from a crossing?

Slow down and let them cross. Article 30 allows pedestrians to cross where there are no markings, with hand signals, when it is safe, and Article 12 requires you to slow down or stop.

### Is it safer to ride slowly in a crowd?

Yes. Walking pace gives you time to stop, and the WHO links lower speeds to far lower risk of death for pedestrians.

### What if a pedestrian steps out and I cannot stop?

Brake smoothly with both brakes, steer away only if the space is clear, and stop. Then follow the steps above.
