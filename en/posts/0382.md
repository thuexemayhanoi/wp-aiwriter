A good half-day from Long Bien into Gia Lam takes in two or three stops, not five, and leaves space to wander. The shop is on the east bank of the Red River in Bo De Ward, Long Bien, near Gia Lam bus station, and Gia Lam district borders Long Bien on the east, so the craft villages are a short ride away without crossing the city. The anchor stop is Bat Trang, the pottery village, which a Vietnam Airlines guide places about 13 kilometres from central Hanoi. Around it you can add the old steel Long Bien Bridge for the river views or Kieu Ky, a village known for gold and silver leaf. This itinerary gives two timing versions, a morning loop and an afternoon loop that ends on the bridge in the late light, and tells you which stop to drop first when time runs short.

## The shape of the half-day

The shop opens at 09:00, so a morning rental really starts around 09:30 once you have checked the bike. A half-day then means about four to five hours out, which fits a rhythm of ride, walk, eat, ride and return with a margin. Think of it as three blocks.

- **A river or bridge stop,** which is optional and short.
- **A craft village,** which is the main event and takes the most time on foot.
- **A second village or a quiet loop,** which you add only if you are still comfortable.

The two villages lie on the east bank side of the Red River. Bat Trang is described in guides as being on the left bank of the river, and Gia Lam district is the easternmost urban district of Hanoi, so from the shop you do not need to cross the river to reach either village. Cross only if you want the bridge as an experience, and do not plan the day around the bridge alone.

## Before you set off

Take ten minutes at the shop. Run through the [rental scooter inspection checklist](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-scooter-inspection-checklist/), confirm the fuel level, and ask which model you have been given, since availability is confirmed when you book. Save the shop as a pin on your map, charge the phone and take water. If your route touches rural stretches, download an offline map of the area on Wi-Fi beforehand, because signal can be patchy on dyke roads and village lanes.

Our general guide to the area, [Gia Lam and the East Bank: quiet roads, craft villages and Bat Trang by scooter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/gia-lam-east-bank-bat-trang-by-scooter/), explains the destination in more depth. This itinerary is about how to fit the stops into one session.

## Stop one, optional: Long Bien Bridge

Long Bien Bridge was built by the French between 1899 and 1902 and is described by Vietnam's tourism administration as the first steel bridge across the Red River. A single railway track runs down the middle, with the lane for motorbikes and bicycles on the side, and the tourism guide says traffic keeps left on the bridge and that cars are banned. It is open 24 hours with no entrance fee according to a Vietnam Tourism guide updated on 9 October 2026.

Status matters for 2026. The road section was closed to motorbikes and pedestrians from 28 March for urgent repairs and reopened at 00:00 on 28 May 2026 after nearly two months, with the lane resurfaced. If you cross, follow the signs and the road markings, ride at a steady pace in the motorbike lane, and never stop in the lane for a photograph, as the tourism guide itself warns. Our [first-ride guide to Long Bien and the Red River](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/long-bien-red-river-first-ride/) explains how to approach the crossing.

The bridge is also the best end to a half-day, not the start. That guide gives the best light as early morning from 05:30 to 07:00 and late afternoon from 16:30 to 18:00, and you cannot collect a rental before 09:00. This is why the afternoon version below finishes there.

If you are drawn to the market at the foot of the bridge, read [how to visit Long Bien Market without blocking deliveries](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-visit-long-bien-market-without-blocking-deliveries/) first, as it is a working wholesale site.

## Stop two: Bat Trang pottery village

Bat Trang is the obvious centrepiece. It is a long-established ceramics village in Gia Lam district on the Red River, and a Hanoi culture department article describes reaching it from the city by crossing Chuong Duong Bridge and following the dyke to a quiet village by the river bank with old stone paths. Starting from Long Bien, you are already on the right side of the river. Travel guides describe a ceramic market, workshops and a modern ceramic museum, and some visitors make a bowl themselves at a workshop.

Opening information comes from a Vietnam Airlines guide, so treat it as a guide and check on the day: the village is described as open 08:00 to 17:00 daily with free entry, and the museum as open 08:00 to 17:30 on weekdays and to 18:00 at weekends, with charges for the museum and workshops. I have not confirmed any prices from an official source, so ask before you join anything.

On arrival, park at the first guarded space you find, take a ticket and walk. The lanes are for pedestrians and the odd delivery truck, and a scooter in the old part of the village is slow and unpopular. Plan 90 minutes to two hours on foot, and longer if you add a workshop or a meal.

Buy ceramics last, at the end of the visit, not at the beginning. A bowl in a rucksack is fine, but a set of plates on a small bike is a hazard. If you want more, ask the shop what you can carry safely and ask the seller about packing.

## Stop three, optional: Kieu Ky gold-leaf village

Kieu Ky, also in Gia Lam district, is described by the Hanoi Department of Culture and Sports as the only craft village in Vietnam that inlays silver and gold leaf, and the craft is said to be almost 400 years old. A national-level recognition of the gold-laminating craft as intangible heritage has been reported, and artisans have told reporters that the craft is done by dozens of households today, down from many more in the past.

It is a working craft village, not a ticketed attraction. I found no official visitor hours or arrangements, so treat it as an optional stop where you walk quietly, ask before photographing a workshop and expect that some doors will be closed. If a workshop does welcome you, thank the artisans and do not touch the work. If you find nothing open, you have lost little, and the road itself is a quiet way to see village life on the east bank.

A 2017 culture department article on the area also names the temple of Nguyen Phi Y Lan, the Giong temple relics at Phu Dong and Kim Lan pottery village among the heritage sites within a few kilometres of Bat Trang. These are options for another day, not additions for this half-day.

## Plan A: the morning loop

| Time | Plan |
|---|---|
| 09:00 to 09:30 | Collect the bike, inspect it, save the shop on the map |
| 09:30 to 10:30 | Ride to Bat Trang by the main road, with fuel stop if needed |
| 10:30 to 12:30 | Walk the village, market and, if you want, a workshop |
| 12:30 to 13:30 | Lunch in the village or on the way back |
| 13:30 to 14:30 | Optional Kieu Ky or a quiet loop |
| 14:30 to 15:30 | Return, with a margin for traffic and a fuel top-up |

These times are planning allowances, not measured journey times. Check your map app and adjust. If anything runs late, cut the final optional stop.

## Plan B: the afternoon loop that ends at the bridge

| Time | Plan |
|---|---|
| 12:30 | Collect the bike, inspect it |
| 13:00 to 14:00 | Lunch near the shop, then ride to Bat Trang |
| 14:00 to 15:45 | Village and market, shopping at the end |
| 15:45 | Start back, whatever you have not seen |
| 16:30 to 17:00 | Optional pause at Long Bien Bridge in the late light |
| By 17:30 | Back at the shop, before dusk |

Sunset in Hanoi from late October to January is at about 17:15 to 17:30, so this plan ends near sundown and is not suited to a late start. Work out your own turnaround time by counting back from sunset, as our guide to [riding at night in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-at-night-in-hanoi/) suggests, and leave the bridge pause out if you are behind.

## Where to cut when time slips

Drop things in this order and keep the margin:

1. The extra village or loop.
2. The bridge pause.
3. The workshop or museum.
4. Lunch, replaced by a quick snack, only if you must.

Never cut the fuel stop or the return margin. Our guide to [when to refuel during a day trip](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/when-to-refuel-a-manual-motorbike-during-a-day-trip/) explains how to do it without stress.

## Parking, food and manners

- **Parking.** Leave the scooter at an attended space, lock the steering, and note that the shop does not provide an extra lock. [Parking a motorbike in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-motorbike-in-hanoi/) explains tickets and guarded lots.
- **Food.** Eat where the locals eat, and avoid anywhere that will not state a price. Our [street food loop guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-street-food-loop-by-scooter/) offers ideas for a different day.
- **Behaviour.** These are living villages. Ride slowly, give way to pedestrians and carts, and ask before photographing people at work.
- **Weather.** If rain is forecast, shorten the plan. See [riding in Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/).

## FAQ: a Long Bien to Gia Lam half-day

### Is Bat Trang in Gia Lam?

Yes. Travel and tourism sources place Bat Trang in Gia Lam district on the Red River, about 13 kilometres from central Hanoi by one airline guide. Distances vary by start point.

### Do I need to cross Long Bien Bridge to reach Bat Trang?

Not from the shop. The shop and Bat Trang are both on the east bank. Cross only for the bridge itself.

### How long should I allow?

Four to five hours covers the shop check, the ride, about two hours on foot in Bat Trang, a meal and the return, with a margin.

### Is Kieu Ky open to visitors?

It is a working village, and I could not confirm visitor hours. Treat it as an optional stop and ask before entering workshops.

### Is Long Bien Bridge open to motorbikes now?

Yes. It reopened on 28 May 2026 after repairs. Check the news if you ride on a different date.
