A ferry crossing on a motorbike route needs five checks: that the crossing is running and carries motorbikes, that your rental terms allow it, that you can wear a life jacket and follow the crew's loading instructions, that the weather and river or sea conditions are acceptable, and that you know the last sailing back. None of this is hard, but ferries are the one part of a ride where timetables, terminals and rules sit outside your control. This guide explains what to confirm before leaving Hanoi, what to look at once you reach the pier and how to keep a spare plan if the boat does not sail. It is general information as of October 2026, not an operator timetable.

## Why a ferry changes a motorbike plan

A road trip is flexible. If a road is slow you ride on, stop for a drink or turn round. A ferry is the opposite: it leaves at a set time or when it is full, it carries a limited number of vehicles and the weather decides whether it goes at all. A missed boat can cost an afternoon or, at the end of the day, a night.

That matters more on a rental, because the bike has to be back at the shop at an agreed time. One rental day is 24 hours from handover, and the shop's late-return charge is VND 30,000 per hour for the first five hours, then an extra day from six hours. A ferry delay is not an excuse that changes those terms, so build the margin into your plan rather than hoping for it. The [rental contract guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-motorbike-rental-contract-explained/) explains how handover and return times are counted.

## Ask the shop before you commit to a crossing

Tell the shop where you are going and that a ferry is involved. The question to ask is simple: "Does my rental allow this route, and who do I call if the bike has a problem on the far side?" Our guide to [taking a Hanoi rental motorbike out of the city](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/take-rental-motorbike-out-of-hanoi/) lists the wider points worth settling at booking. Nguyen Ha Motorbike Rental is at Alley 5 Nguyen Van Cu Street, Bo De Ward, Long Bien District, and can be reached on 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969) from 09:00 to 21:00 daily.

Do not assume that a road that exists on a map is covered by your agreement, and do not assume an island is covered because the ferry sells a motorbike ticket. A Hai Phong travel site makes the same point for Cat Ba: if you rent on the mainland, get permission to take the bike onto a ferry and keep the rental company's breakdown contact. Ask for those answers in writing or in the chat history.

## Confirm which crossing is actually running

Crossings change faster than guidebooks. Piers are rebuilt, services move and some run only in season. On the Hai Phong side, for example, one local guide notes that the Dong Bai terminal replaced the old Got departure terminal for the Cat Ba vehicle ferry, while the Tuan Chau to Gia Luan route from the Ha Long side depends on the season, the weather and the access road. A route you read about two years ago may not exist in the same form.

The practical steps are:

- Search for the operator or the terminal by its current name, and look at the date on the page.
- Confirm that the service carries motorbikes, not only foot passengers or cable-car riders.
- Check the timetable in both directions, not only the outbound sailing.
- Save the terminal's location offline so that you can navigate without a signal.

Closer to Hanoi, small river crossings carry people and bikes across the Red River. Hanoi news reports from 2026 mention checks at river crossings such as Tho An in Lien Minh commune. They are local services with local rules and timetables, so ask a local rather than assuming.

## Life jackets: the one rule you will meet at the pier

Expect to be asked to put on a life jacket. Vietnamese press reports from May 2026 say Hanoi's river police are tightening inspections at passenger wharves on the Red River and will not let a boat leave if passengers are not wearing life jackets properly. A Ho Chi Minh City inland waterway port office representative said in 2025 that passengers on river-crossing ferries are required to wear life jackets, while larger enclosed vessels need lifebuoys instead.

For a rider, the consequence is practical. Put the jacket on before the boat moves, fasten it, and do not argue that the crossing is short: the same Hanoi report notes that many people think short distances need no life jacket, while weather and river flow always carry risk. If your helmet is on the bike, you will need a free hand for the jacket, so park or stand the bike first and ask the crew where to put things. If you travel with a child, ask for a jacket that fits them.

## At the terminal: tickets, queues and loading

At a vehicle ferry the order of events is usually the same: buy a ticket for the motorbike, queue in the marked lane, wait for the crew's signal and ride or push the bike on as instructed. A guide for Cat Ba riders gives the steps in the same order: buy the ticket for your motorbike, confirm how the rider and any passenger are charged and follow staff instructions for queuing, boarding and securing the bike.

Things worth doing at the counter:

1. **Ask which ticket you need.** Rider and passenger may be priced separately from the bike. The amounts change, so this guide does not quote them.
2. **Keep the ticket** in a pocket, not in the bike, until you leave the ferry.
3. **Ask which lane to join** if there is more than one.
4. **Check the next departure** and the last one of the day.
5. **Arrive early at busy times.** Weekends and holidays make queues longer, and a full boat does not wait for a latecomer.

## On the ramp and the deck

Ramps and decks are steel or concrete, often wet and sometimes steep. Slow down, keep your feet ready and avoid braking hard on the metal. Staff will usually tell you where to stop and how to leave the bike. Follow them even if it differs from what you did on another ferry.

Some points are common sense rather than rules:

- Use the stand or the position the crew indicates, and leave the bike in gear if you ride a semi-automatic, unless you are told otherwise.
- Lock the steering and do not leave valuables on the seat.
- Keep your bag, phone and helmet with you if you walk to the passenger deck.
- Do not start the engine until the crew gives the signal to unload.
- On leaving, ride off slowly. Others are queueing behind you, and the surface is as slippery as on the way on.

If you ride a bike you do not know well, a ferry ramp is not the place to learn its brakes. Our [10-minute inspection checklist](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-scooter-inspection-checklist/) covers what to test before you leave the shop.

## Weather, water and cancellations

Wind, rain, fog, a storm and rough water can stop a ferry or slow it. A travel guide on vietnamtourism.com notes that the typhoon season runs from June to November and that ferries do not operate during typhoons; it also warns that sea conditions can worsen before a formal warning is issued. If the sea or the river looks rough, reconsider the trip. The decision to cancel is normally made by the authorities and the operator, not by you, and it can come at short notice.

On the road itself, wet weather has its own effects. Our guides to [riding in Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) and [riding a manual motorbike through standing water](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-a-manual-motorbike-through-standing-water/) cover the surfaces and the habits that help. A ferry queue in drizzle is slippery underfoot, so wear shoes with grip rather than sandals.

## The return leg: last sailing and daylight

Plan backwards from the last sailing home. Sunset in Hanoi is around 17:30 in late October and about 17:15 at the start of December, according to published sunrise tables, so a late ferry means the ride from the pier to your bed happens in the dark. Our guide to [riding at night in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-at-night-in-hanoi/) describes the lighting issues; the simpler answer is to leave a margin of at least an hour or two before the final departure.

A simple rule for island or river-side trips: do not plan to take the last boat. Plan for the one before it. If the last boat is cancelled, you stay overnight with your bike, your helmet and a shop that closes at 21:00 and may not be reachable until the next morning.

## When the ferry does not sail

Keep a second plan ready:

- **A later sailing.** If the next boat is within an hour or two, wait with the bike in the queue.
- **A different crossing or road.** Check the map for a bridge, but remember that some fast roads are closed to motorbikes. Our guide to [checking whether a navigation app suggests an expressway](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-check-if-a-navigation-app-suggests-an-expressway/) explains how to avoid a closed ramp.
- **An overnight stay.** Ask the terminal's nearest guesthouse about secure parking before you decide.
- **A message to the shop.** If you will be late back, say so early. Our guide to [what to do about a breakdown with a rental bike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-bike-breakdown-hanoi-what-to-do/) covers what to send.

## FAQ: ferry crossings on a motorbike route

### Do I have to wear a life jacket on a ferry in Vietnam?

On river-crossing ferries, Vietnamese authorities say yes: Hanoi river police have said boats will not leave if passengers are not wearing life jackets properly. Larger enclosed vessels may use lifebuoys instead, so follow the crew's instructions.

### Can I take a rented motorbike on a ferry?

Ask the shop first. The ferry may sell a motorbike ticket, but the rental agreement decides whether the route is allowed. Get the answer before you leave and keep the shop's number to hand.

### Should I stay on the bike while the ferry crosses?

Follow the crew. Most operators tell riders where to leave the bike and where to stand, and you will usually be asked to move to the passenger area during the crossing.

### What if the last ferry is cancelled?

Stay calm and tell the shop early. Look for a guesthouse with secure parking, or an alternative crossing, and do not ride unfamiliar roads in the dark just to save the night.

### Are ferry fares fixed?

Fares depend on the operator, the route and the category of vehicle, and they change. This guide does not quote them. Ask at the ticket counter and carry small notes; our guide to [paying for fuel, parking and tolls](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/paying-fuel-parking-tolls-hanoi-visitor/) covers payment habits.
