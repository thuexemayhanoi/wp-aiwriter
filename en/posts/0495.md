At an unfamiliar intersection, panic comes from deciding too late. The calm method is to slow down early, read what the junction is telling you (lights, arrows, markings, signs and the flow of other riders), choose your lane before you reach it, apply the giving-way rule, look left, right and left again, and then commit smoothly. If you find yourself in the wrong lane, go straight and re-route rather than swerving. Law 36/2024/QH15 requires operators approaching an intersection to observe the surroundings, slow down and give way (Article 22), bans overtaking at intersections (Article 14) and bans stopping within 5 metres of their edges (Article 18). A missed turn costs a minute. A panicked turn can cost much more.

This guide explains how to prepare before you reach the junction, what to read when you arrive, the giving-way rules, how to choose a lane and a path, how to cope with a wrong lane or a missed turn, what to do at different kinds of junction, how to cope with Hanoi's crowds and what to do if something goes wrong. It is general information based on the law and published safety guidance as of October 2026, not legal advice.

## Why unfamiliar junctions feel hard

Several things arrive at once: lights and arrows, lanes that split or merge, riders entering from every side, pedestrians crossing, a map that tells you to turn now and a horn behind you. In Hanoi the flow may look disorderly, though experienced riders read it constantly. Visitors tend to freeze, brake hard or change their mind at the last moment, which are the actions that other riders cannot predict. Our guide to [crossing Hanoi intersections: how traffic actually flows and how to merge](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/crossing-hanoi-intersections/) describes the flow, and this guide gives you a method.

## Before you reach the junction

The best way to avoid panic is to do the thinking earlier.

1. **Look at the route before you ride.** Check the map for the junction's shape and which exit you need. If a junction is complex, look at it at the previous stop. Our guide to [when to stop and check a map instead of looking down](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/when-to-stop-and-check-a-map-instead-of-looking-down/) explains how to stop safely.

2. **Know your exit in words.** For example, "second right after the lights", not just a blue line.

3. **Slow down early.** Article 12 requires operators to slow down at intersections, and a lower speed gives you time to think.

4. **Choose your lane early.** Move into the right lane well before the junction, using your mirrors and your signal. Our guide to [checking mirrors and blind spots before changing lanes](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/checking-mirrors-and-blind-spots-before-changing-lanes/) shows how.

5. **Keep your distance from the bike in front.** If it brakes or swerves, you need room. Our guide to [how to follow a safe distance in Hanoi traffic](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-follow-a-safe-distance-in-hanoi-traffic/) explains how.

6. **Take a breath and relax your arms and shoulders.** Tense riders overreact.

7. **Check that you are in a calm state to ride.** If you are tired, hot or hurried, stop first. Our guide to [what to do if you feel fatigued on a long ride](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-to-do-if-you-feel-fatigued-on-a-long-ride/) explains.

## What to read when you arrive

Read in this order, from the most authoritative to the least.

| Source | What it tells you | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **A traffic officer or guard** | Their command overrides everything else (Article 11) | Follow it |
| **Traffic lights and arrows** | Whether to go, stop or turn | Our guide to [what different traffic light arrows mean for riders](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-different-traffic-light-arrows-mean-for-riders/) explains them |
| **Signs** | Prohibitions, orders, warnings, direction | Our guide to [understanding common motorbike road signs in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/understanding-common-motorbike-road-signs-in-vietnam/) lists the groups |
| **Markings** | Stop lines, lanes, arrows, crossings | Our guide to [how to read motorbike lane markings in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-read-motorbike-lane-markings-in-hanoi/) explains them |
| **Other road users** | How traffic is actually flowing | A guide, not an authority |

Article 11 of the Law gives an order of priority for commands: a traffic guard's instructions, then lights, then signs, then markings. Where a fixed sign and a temporary sign conflict, follow the temporary one. At a green light, vehicles may proceed but must slow down or stop for pedestrians on the roadway. At a red light, stop before the stop line. ## The giving-way rules

Article 22 of the Law sets out who must give way at an intersection. The operator must observe the surroundings, slow down and give way according to these rules.

1. **Priority road.** At an intersection between a non-priority road and a priority road, or between a feeder road and a main road, the vehicle on the non-priority or feeder road must give way to vehicles on the priority or main road from any direction.

2. **No roundabout sign.** At an intersection without a "roundabout ahead" sign, give way to the vehicle approaching from the right.

3. **Roundabout sign.** At an intersection with a "roundabout ahead" sign, give way to the vehicle approaching from the left.

Article 15 adds that when changing direction you give way to pedestrians, rudimentary vehicles and oncoming traffic. Article 22 applies where lights do not control the junction, and where lights fail or flash. If you are unsure, slow down more and let the other vehicle go first.

## Choose a lane and a path

- **Lane markings and arrows** show which lanes turn and which go straight. If you cannot see them, follow the lane the slower traffic uses.

- **Stay in your lane through the junction.** Article 13 requires operators to use their lane. Do not weave.

- **Keep a steady line.** A predictable path lets others adjust.

- **Do not overtake in the junction.** Article 14 bans it at intersections.

- **Do not stop in the middle.** Stopping inside a junction blocks others and puts you in the path of cross traffic. If traffic ahead is stopped and there is no space beyond the junction, wait before it, not in it.

- **Leave room for larger vehicles.** Buses and lorries swing wide. Our guide to [sharing the road with buses while riding a scooter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/sharing-the-road-with-buses-while-riding-a-scooter/) and our guide to [how to ride beside heavy trucks in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-ride-beside-heavy-trucks-in-hanoi/) explain how.

## The scan: left, right, left

Rider-safety guidance, including the Motorcycle Safety Foundation's SEE strategy, tells riders to search constantly, evaluate hazards and execute a plan before they have to react in an emergency, and to look for vehicles that may turn across their path, especially at intersections. In practice:

1. **Look ahead first** for the signal and the flow.

2. **Look to the side the traffic will come from,** then the other side, then the first side again.

3. **Look for pedestrians** on the crossing and at the kerbs.

4. **Look for vehicles turning** across your path, which the Motorcycle Safety Foundation highlights as a leading danger. Cover your brakes.

5. **Look at the drivers' eyes and wheels,** not only the body. A front wheel that turns shows a vehicle about to move.

6. **Do not assume that a green light means safety.** Check cross traffic for vehicles that jump the light.

7. **Choose your moment, then commit.** Hesitation in the middle is the most dangerous thing. If the gap is clear, go smoothly. If not, stay behind the line.

## If you are in the wrong lane or miss the turn

This is the most common panic. The answer is almost always to go on.

- **Do not swerve across lanes.** A sudden move is exactly what the riders behind and beside you cannot predict.

- **Go straight through** at the same speed and in the same lane.

- **Re-route.** Ride to a safe place, stop and let the map recalculate. A loop of a few minutes is cheaper than a collision.

- **Do not make a U-turn** where it is banned. Article 15 bans U-turns at pedestrian crossings, on bridges, at bridge ends, under overpasses, at road-railway intersections, on narrow roads, on slopes, on bends with obscured visibility, on expressways, in tunnels and on one-way roads. Our guide to [why sudden U-turns are risky in city traffic](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/why-sudden-u-turns-are-risky-in-city-traffic/) explains why.

- **Do not stop in the junction** to think. Move on, and stop beyond it.

- **Do not reverse** on the road.

- **Do not follow another rider blindly** through a red light or against the flow.

## Kinds of junction

| Junction | What to do |
|---|---|
| **Signalled crossroads** | Obey lights and arrows; stop behind the line; watch for late turners and pedestrians |
| **Crossing with no lights** | Slow right down; apply Article 22; look both ways; give way to the priority road |
| **Roundabout** | Check for the "roundabout ahead" sign; give way as it directs; keep a steady line |
| **T-junction from a side street** | Stop at the edge, look both ways, and join gaps in the main flow |
| **Alley entrance** | Slow down and expect riders and pedestrians to emerge suddenly |
| **Junction with a railway or a tram** | Slow down, and apply the railway rules. Our guide to [motorbike safety at railway crossings in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbike-safety-at-railway-crossings-in-hanoi/) explains them |
| **Junction with a camera or a police officer** | Obey signals and instructions |
| **Junction with a bus lane** | Check the sign and the markings before you enter. Our guide to [how to check which roads restrict motorbikes](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-check-which-roads-restrict-motorbikes/) explains how |

## Coping with Hanoi's crowds

- **Ride at the speed of the flow,** not faster, and not much slower.

- **Hold your line,** and let others go around you. They do it all the time.

- **Be ready for the squeeze.** Bikes will fill gaps on both sides. Do not react by swerving.

- **Leave a space cushion** in front and, where you can, to the sides.

- **Use your lights and signals** so that others can read you.

- **If it is too much,** pull over somewhere lawful, rest, and walk the last stretch if that is easier. Our guide to [riding in Hanoi's Old Quarter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-old-quarter/) explains when walking is the better choice.

Under Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, as read on the Government portal, a rider who fails to obey the signals commits an offence that Vietnamese legal publications report as carrying a fine of VND 4,000,000 to 6,000,000, and a rider who turns without observing, slowing or signalling can face a fine of VND 600,000 to 800,000. Where a failure to observe, slow down or stop causes an accident, the reported fine is VND 10,000,000 to 14,000,000 with 10 licence points. ## A quick checklist

1. **Read the junction on the map before you arrive.**

2. **Slow down early, and choose your lane early.**

3. **Read the officer, lights, signs, markings, then the flow.**

4. **Apply the giving-way rule: priority road first, then the vehicle on your right unless there is a roundabout sign.**

5. **Scan left, right, left, and look for turning vehicles and pedestrians.**

6. **Commit smoothly or stay behind the line. Never stop in the middle.**

7. **If you are in the wrong lane, go straight and re-route.**

8. **If you feel overwhelmed, pull over lawfully and breathe.**

## FAQ: unfamiliar intersections

### Who has priority at an intersection with no lights?

Article 22 of Law 36/2024/QH15 says the vehicle on a non-priority or feeder road gives way to one on a priority or main road, and otherwise you give way to the vehicle approaching from the right, unless there is a "roundabout ahead" sign, in which case you give way to the vehicle approaching from the left.

### What should I do if I am in the wrong lane?

Go straight, stay in your lane, and re-route afterwards. Do not swerve or turn from the wrong lane.

### Can I stop in the middle of an intersection?

No. Stopping inside blocks others and puts you in the path of cross traffic. Article 18 also bans stopping within 5 metres of the edge of an intersection.

### Can I overtake at an intersection?

No. Article 14 bans overtaking at intersections.

### What if the lights are not working?

Treat it as an unsignalled junction and apply Article 22, or follow an officer's command. Slow down more.

### How do I stay calm?

Prepare early, slow down, breathe, keep your arms loose and accept that a missed turn is cheap.
