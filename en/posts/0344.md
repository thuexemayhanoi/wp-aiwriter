Son Tay is a small town about 40 to 45 km west of central Hanoi, and its main attraction is a laterite citadel built in 1822 that sits among old trees in the middle of the town. That makes it one of the calmer day trips on a rental motorbike: a straight road out, a compact site to walk, a town big enough for lunch and a choice of one extra stop rather than a rushed list. This guide builds a leisurely day around the 09:00 opening time of our shop, describes what the citadel actually contains, compares the three add-ons people usually consider and explains what to check before you leave Hanoi so that the pace stays slow.

## Why Son Tay suits a slow day

Son Tay was one of the four protectorates that guarded the ancient capital, Thang Long, which is why a fortress was placed here. State media describe its citadel as one of twenty built under the Nguyen dynasty and the only one built entirely of laterite, the rust-brown local stone. Today the site is surrounded by a town rather than by countryside, which is part of its charm: ancient banyan roots hold moss-covered gates a few steps from ordinary streets.

For a rider, this has practical advantages. You are not climbing a mountain, there is no single deadline such as a last cable car and the town has cafes, fuel stations and parking. The main challenge is the road out of Hanoi, not the destination.

## Getting there without rushing

A Vietnam News Agency article on the citadel says that if you follow Highway 32 from Hanoi to the west, you reach Son Tay town after about 45 km. The national tourism authority gives about 40 km from the city centre. The difference is mainly where the measurement starts, and from the east bank of the Red River it will be longer still.

Highway 32 is the direct way. The alternative is Thang Long Avenue, whose fast central carriageway is closed to motorbikes while its parallel service roads are open. Our guide to [which ring roads and highways allow motorbikes](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-ring-roads-highways-motorbikes-allowed/) explains how to tell the two layers apart. Motorbikes are not allowed on expressways under Law 36/2024/QH15, in force since 1 January 2025, so check the route type in your map app before you set off. If you rely on voice navigation, our guide to [checking whether a navigation app suggests an expressway](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-check-if-a-navigation-app-suggests-an-expressway/) is worth five minutes.

On a day without a deadline, the better habit is to stop in the first town after the city edge, drink something and check how the bike feels, not to push through to a single stop.

## What the citadel contains

Son Tay Citadel was built in 1822 under Emperor Minh Mang in the style of the French military engineer Vauban, with a square plan, four gates and a surrounding moat. Reports give slightly different sizes. Vietnam News Agency says it covers 16 hectares, while a Hanoi culture department page says 12 hectares, so treat the figure as approximate. The walls are about 5 metres high and each side is about 400 metres long, according to the national news agency, and the moat was around 20 metres wide.

What remains is a fraction of the original, but a worthwhile one:

- **The gates.** Reports differ on how many survive in their original form. The two usually named face Quang Trung Street, the front gate, and Le Loi Street, the back gate.
- **The flag tower.** An octagonal tower about 18 metres high stands inside the walls and was used as a lookout.
- **The Kinh Thien Palace foundation.** The platform of the former palace is among the features the news agency lists as surviving.
- **Two cannons.** A Hanoi culture department page places them at the north gate, as relics of the resistance against French forces.
- **Wells and old trees.** Old wells survive, and the banyans give shade all year.
- **An aircraft display.** In 2014 the town added two MiG-21 jets and a Mi-8 helicopter in an area of more than 4,000 square metres, a modern layer on a nineteenth-century site.

The citadel became a national architectural relic in 1994. A pedestrian zone around the fortress was launched on 30 April 2022 as part of the Son Tay and Xu Doai tourism programme, according to state media, so the streets nearby are meant for walking. Park first, then explore on foot.

I could not confirm an official entry arrangement or opening hours, because the sources I found are travel pages and news reports, not the management board. Check on the day and follow any signs at the gate.

## A leisurely timetable from a 09:00 pickup

A rental shop that opens at 09:00 cannot offer a dawn departure, so this timetable is built to suit a late-morning arrival and an early-afternoon turn for home.

| Time | Plan |
|---|---|
| 09:00 | Collect the bike, inspect it, confirm fuel |
| 09:30 | Leave Hanoi towards Highway 32 |
| 10:45 | Break in the first town beyond the city edge |
| About 11:45 | Arrive in Son Tay, park near the old town |
| 12:00 to 13:00 | Lunch somewhere local, then walk to the citadel |
| 13:00 to 14:30 | Walk the walls, the flag tower and the aircraft display |
| 14:30 to 15:00 | Rest, check the bike and decide whether to add a stop |
| 15:00 | Start back before the light fades |
| By 21:00 | Return the bike to the shop |

These times are a plan, not a quote. Traffic out of Hanoi, a long lunch or a wrong turn can move everything by an hour, and the rule is to protect the return, not the sightseeing.

## One add-on, not three

Son Tay sits near several other places, and the temptation is to stack them. A leisurely day takes one.

### Duong Lam ancient village

The national tourism authority says Duong Lam is close to Son Tay town, and the village is the best known of the three. It is a group of laterite houses in the commune of the same name, with about 956 old houses according to the authority. It is also a living community, so it deserves its own visit and a slower, walking pace. If this is your priority, plan it as the main stop with Son Tay as the add-on, not the other way round.

### Vietnam National Village for Ethnic Culture and Tourism at Dong Mo

The village covers 1,544 hectares in the Dong Mo tourism area of Son Tay, about 40 km west of central Hanoi, according to the tourism authority's Vietnamese page, and it presents the cultures of Vietnam's 54 ethnic groups. It is large, so a visit takes hours, not minutes. Opening hours and ticketing change, and the website I found with that name also hosts unrelated content, so I did not use it as a source. Check the operator's current information before you commit.

### Stay in town

If the morning's ride was tiring, the best add-on is no add-on. A second coffee, a walk along the moat and an early start home is a perfectly good day.

## Fuel, breaks and parking

The shop hands over petrol bikes with only about 0.5 to 2 litres, so fill up before you leave the built-up area. Our guide to [fuel in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/fuel-in-hanoi-petrol-stations-rental-scooter/) explains the petrol stations. On the return, the bike should come back with about 0.5 to 1 litre, so there is no need to arrive with a full tank.

A leisurely day should have more breaks, not fewer. The article on [planning breaks on a long semi-automatic bike ride](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-plan-breaks-on-a-long-semi-automatic-bike-ride/) suggests how often to stop. ## Weather, light and the way home

Autumn and spring are the comfortable seasons for a walk among old walls and trees. In summer the heat can sit heavily on the open road, and a sudden storm can turn the return into a slow, wet ride. Our guide to [riding in Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) explains how to decide whether to leave, and the one on [riding at night](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-at-night-in-hanoi/) explains why the plan above ends before dusk.

If the day goes better than planned and you feel you could ride further, resist the urge to add a stop. A leisurely day is one that ends with energy left over, and the bike returned before closing time.

## FAQ: Hanoi to Son Tay by motorbike

### How far is Son Tay from central Hanoi?

About 40 km according to the national tourism authority, or about 45 km along Highway 32 according to a state news agency article. The starting point you choose changes the figure.

### Which road should I use?

Highway 32 is the direct road. Thang Long Avenue's service roads are an alternative, but its central carriageway is not open to motorbikes.

### Is the citadel worth a stop on its own?

Yes, if you like history and old trees. It is a compact site, so allow an hour or two, not a full day.

### Can I combine it with Duong Lam?

Yes, because the two are close, but a leisurely day is better with one extra stop than three. Duong Lam is best given its own time.

### Is entry free?

I could not confirm an official entry policy, so check at the gate on the day.
