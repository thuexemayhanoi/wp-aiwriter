During festival season Hang Ma Street is best treated as a place you walk into, not ride into: park the scooter outside the busy block, arrive earlier in the day than the photo-hunting crowds and expect the street to be closed to vehicles around key dates. In 2026 the Mid-Autumn market on Hang Ma ran from 11 to 25 September, with the section between Hang Duong and Hang Cot closed to traffic around the clock, according to a Vietnam News Agency report. Since July 2026 the surrounding Old Quarter has also had a Friday to Sunday evening vehicle restriction. This guide explains the festival calendar, how each season changes the street and a step-by-step way to visit by scooter without blocking anyone.

## What Hang Ma Street is

Hang Ma is a short street in the Old Quarter known for paper goods. One heritage-style guide explains that its east end meets Hang Duong, Hang Ngang and Hang Chieu, and that its west end meets Phung Hung beside the railway line. The Vietnam News Agency article quotes the 2026 festival venue as running from the Hang Duong junction to the Hang Cot junction. Traditionally the street sold votive paper offerings used in spiritual ceremonies, and today shops also sell lanterns, toys, masks, costumes and decorations that change with the season.

The street is narrow and lined with display stands, so the road surface is shared by shoppers, photographers and the occasional bike even on an ordinary day. Our overview of [riding in Hanoi's Old Quarter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-old-quarter/) describes the surrounding grid, and the [Hoan Kiem area page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hoan-kiem/) places it on the map.

## A festival calendar for planning

Hang Ma changes its look through the year, and the crowd levels change with it. The seasons below come from published sources, but exact dates move with the lunar calendar, so check each year.

| Season | What the street shows | What to expect |
|---|---|---|
| Mid-Autumn (eighth lunar month) | Star-shaped and carp lanterns, paper masks, traditional toys, lion heads | Family crowds, photo queues, official market with closed street in 2026 |
| Hungry-ghost month (seventh lunar month) | Votive paper goods | Described by one guide as a busy trading period about a month before the main day |
| Halloween and Christmas | Western-style decorations, lights, Santa figures | Evening crowds of young visitors, per Dan Tri's English edition |
| Kitchen Gods day, 23rd of the 12th lunar month | Paper clothing, hats, boots and paper carp | A shopping rush a few days before Tet, noted by Vietnam's tourism photo service |
| Tet run-up | Red and gold ornaments, paper replicas of festive foods | Heavy pre-holiday trade |

The point of the table is not that one season is better than another. It is that the day of the week, the hour and the proximity to a festival date matter more than the month when planning a scooter trip.

## What the 2026 Mid-Autumn market tells you

The Vietnam News Agency report is a useful template for how the authorities treat busy festival periods. It says the traditional Mid-Autumn market took place from 11 to 25 September 2026, centred on Hang Ma between its Hang Duong and Hang Cot junctions, with linked activities at 22 Hang Buom, 87 Ma May and 2 Le Thai To. During the market, the designated section of Hang Ma was closed to traffic around the clock, and authorities carried out inspections on public security, traffic safety, fire prevention, food safety and sanitation.

You cannot assume the same dates, the same section or the same closure next year, but you can expect something similar whenever a festival draws large crowds. If a barrier or a police-style marshal stands at a junction, that decides the matter regardless of what a navigation app says.

## The weekend evening restriction in the Old Quarter

Separate from festivals, Hanoi started a low-emission zone pilot on 1 July 2026. VOV and VnExpress report that the Hoan Kiem Lake pedestrian area, the Old Quarter and the weekend night market form Zone 1, where motorbikes, motorcycles and cars are barred from 19:00 to midnight every Friday, Saturday and Sunday. Hang Ma is not on the list of eleven core streets named in the earlier plan, but it lies within the wider Old Quarter, and the coverage does not publish a street-by-street list for every lane.

The safe approach is simple. On Friday, Saturday or Sunday evenings, assume vehicles cannot use Hang Ma and the lanes around it, and arrive on foot. On other days, still look for temporary barriers.

## Choosing the hour

Hang Ma is busiest in the evening, when couples, families and photographers arrive after work and school, and Dan Tri's reports on Christmas describe the street as more bustling in the evening. A weekday morning or early afternoon is calmer and gives better light for photographs without the crush.

Before a major festival date, shop owners are filling displays and customers are buying in volume, so allow more time for everything. One travel guide recommends avoiding the evening peak just before big festivals and visiting on a weekday earlier in the day.

## Where to leave the scooter

The street itself is not a parking place. Under Article 18 of Law 36/2024/QH15 on road traffic order and safety, in force since 1 January 2025, people parking on a city street must keep close to the right-hand kerb in the direction of travel, within 0.25 metres, and must not obstruct pedestrians or vehicles. The law also lists locations where stopping and parking are not allowed, such as pedestrian crossings and the area within 5 metres of a junction.

A festival street full of stalls has more of these constraints than usual. So:

- **Choose a guarded lot or ticketed point away from the packed block.** Our guide to [parking a motorbike in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-motorbike-in-hanoi/) explains tickets, guarded lots and towing.
- **Walk the last few blocks.** Five minutes on foot costs less time than trying to ride against pedestrian flow.
- **Never park on a stall front, a doorway or a bus-stop zone,** however empty it looks. Shop owners need that space, and a bike in the way is the likeliest cause of a quarrel.
- **Avoid parking in a pedestrian-heavy lane even for a quick photo.** Park first, then walk.

If the street is closed, the Old Quarter's nearby streets such as the ones around Hoan Kiem Lake follow similar rules at weekends. Our guide to [where to park near Hoan Kiem Lake](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/where-to-park-a-scooter-near-hoan-kiem-lake/) explains the walking-street logic.

## Carrying lanterns, masks and decorations

This is where a scooter visit often goes wrong. Lanterns, masks and paper decorations are light but bulky, and they catch the wind. To carry them safely:

1. **Do not hang bags or lanterns from the handlebars,** where they affect steering and can snag a lever.
2. **Keep loads inside the rider's footprint.** Anything sticking out sideways is dangerous in a narrow lane.
3. **Pack fragile items in a bag you can hold between your feet or on the seat in front of you,** only if the bike's design and your balance allow it.
4. **Consider a taxi or courier for large items** and ride home with the small ones.
5. **Buy last.** Do your browsing and photos first, then collect the bike and go.

## Behaving well in a crowded photo street

Hang Ma attracts people who want pictures of the colours, and photography blocks pavements. A few habits keep everyone comfortable:

- Do not stand in the carriageway for a shot, especially with a helmet in your hand and a bike parked behind you.
- Ask before photographing shop staff or customers at close range.
- Step into a doorway or a gap rather than stopping in the middle of the flow.
- If you buy, buy at a stall and move aside to pack your bag.
- Keep your scooter's key and your phone out of back pockets in a crowd.

## FAQ: Hang Ma Street and festivals

### Can I ride a scooter along Hang Ma Street?

On an ordinary day the street carries traffic, but during festival markets it can be closed. In 2026 the Mid-Autumn market section was closed around the clock. Look for barriers and obey them.

### Is Hang Ma open late?

Many shops stay open into the evening, and Dan Tri's English edition describes the street as bustling then. Opening hours vary by shop.

### When is Hang Ma at its most colourful?

Mid-Autumn, Christmas and the run-up to Tet are the best-known periods. Exact dates follow the lunar calendar for the first and last.

### Can I park right outside the shop I want?

Avoid it. Park in a guarded lot or an allowed spot and walk, because the street is narrow and law on parking forbids obstruction.

### Is it better to come without a scooter?

For an evening in the Old Quarter on a Friday, Saturday or Sunday, yes. On a quiet weekday daytime visit, a scooter and a nearby lot work fine.
