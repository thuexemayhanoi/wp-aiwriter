To start a classic Cub 50cc motorbike, put it on level ground, check that the engine stop switch is in the Run position, switch the ignition on, make sure the gear indicator or your foot test says neutral, keep the throttle closed and press the start button or swing the kick starter through in one firm, continuous stroke. Honda's own Super Cub manual follows exactly this order, and adds that if the engine does not catch within about five seconds you should switch off and wait ten seconds before trying again. This guide walks through the whole routine for a rented Cub-style 50cc bike in Hanoi: what to check first, both ways of starting it, what to try when it refuses, and how to pull away without lurching into traffic. It is general information, because the exact bike you are handed may differ from the Honda models described here.

## What makes a Cub different from a scooter

A classic Cub is a step-through bike with a foot gear pedal but no clutch lever. Honda's history pages describe the original Super Cub as having an automatic centrifugal clutch that needed no clutch operation, and the current Super Cub 50 specification lists a four-speed gearbox with a multi-plate clutch that works with the foot pedal. In plain terms: the engine is connected to the wheel by a clutch that lets go by itself when the engine idles and takes up the drive as revs rise, and the clutch also releases for a moment while your foot moves the gear pedal.

That has two consequences for starting. First, you should always begin from neutral, because a bike left in gear can move as soon as the engine catches. Second, because there is no lever to squeeze, the only protection against a surprise lurch is your habit of checking neutral and keeping the throttle closed. ## Before you start the engine: a one-minute checklist

Do these with the bike standing on its stand, before the key goes in. A rental shop will usually walk you through them, but it is worth knowing why each one matters.

| Check | What you are looking for |
|---|---|
| Stand | Bike stable on its centre stand or side stand, on firm, level ground |
| Engine stop switch | In the Run position, not Stop |
| Fuel | Enough in the tank; ask how to check it, because small bikes may have no gauge |
| Brakes | Both levers or pedals firm, not spongy or dragging |
| Throttle | Turns smoothly and snaps back closed when released |
| Mirrors, horn, lights | Working, because you will need them in the first minute |
| Helmet | Fitted and fastened before you pull away |

The engine stop switch is the one beginners miss. Honda's manual says that if the starter motor will not operate, you should check the starting sequence and make sure the engine stop switch is in the Run position. It also says not to use that switch except in an emergency, because stopping the engine while riding makes the bike unsafe. ## Starting with the electric starter

Many modern Cub-style bikes have a start button as well as a kick starter. Honda's Super Cub 50 specification lists a self-starter with kick-starter fitted, though older or lower-cost Cub-style bikes may have only a kick starter, and your rental's controls may sit in different places. If you are not sure, ask the shop to start it once while you watch. The sequence in Honda's Super Cub manual is:

1. Make sure the engine stop switch is in the Run position.
2. Turn the ignition switch on.
3. Shift the transmission to neutral and wait for the neutral indicator, if the bike has one.
4. With the throttle completely closed, press the start button.

Release the button as soon as the engine starts. Honda warns that if the engine does not start within about five seconds, you should turn the ignition off and wait ten seconds before trying again, so that the battery can recover voltage. Do not hold the button down for long periods, and do not rev the engine hard to warm it up. Honda advises against extended fast idling and snapping the throttle.

If the engine will not start, Honda's manual for fuel-injected models adds two throttle tricks. First, open the throttle very slightly, about 3 mm of movement without free play, and press the button. If that fails, open the throttle fully and hold the button for five seconds, then repeat the normal procedure. Treat these as notes for bikes with fuel injection. A carburetted older Cub may behave differently, so ask before experimenting.

## Starting with the kick starter

The kick starter is a lever on the right side of the engine that you swing down with your foot. The Honda scooter manual describes the technique plainly: with the throttle completely closed, start from the top of the stroke and kick through to the bottom in a rapid, continuous motion.

Some practical points for a first-timer:

- **Stand the bike on its centre stand** if it has one, or hold it upright and firmly with the brake applied. A bike on its side stand can topple as you kick.
- **Keep the throttle closed.** Kicking with the throttle open is one of the quickest ways to flood or stall an engine.
- **Use your body weight,** not just your leg. A short, half-hearted push does not turn the engine over.
- **Do not stamp repeatedly.** If three or four good kicks do nothing, stop and check the basics below.

## Finding neutral

Neutral is where most Cub starting problems begin. On many Honda Cub-style bikes the neutral position sits between first and second gear, reached with a half-click of the pedal, but this varies and it is general knowledge rather than something to rely on. The reliable ways are to look for the green neutral light, if the bike has one, or to test by rolling the bike gently with the engine off. In neutral the rear wheel turns freely. Our short guide to [understanding neutral on a Honda Wave](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/understanding-neutral-on-a-honda-wave/) describes the same idea for the Wave, which uses the same type of pedal.

## If it will not start: a short troubleshooting order

Work through these in order, because the cause is usually something simple.

1. **Engine stop switch** in the Run position.
2. **Ignition** fully on, and the key turned all the way.
3. **Neutral**, confirmed by a light or by rolling the wheel.
4. **Fuel**, because small tanks run low faster than you expect. Ask the shop how much was in the tank at handover.
5. **Throttle** closed during the first attempt, as Honda's procedure says.
6. **Rest period.** After repeated attempts, wait a short while. Honda's manual asks for ten seconds between electric starter tries.
7. **Ask for help.** Call the shop on 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969), open 09:00 to 21:00 daily, before you start taking panels off. Our guide to [what to do about breakdowns with a rental bike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-bike-breakdown-hanoi-what-to-do/) gives the wider steps.

Do not hand the bike to a roadside mechanic without telling the shop first, because the rental contract is with the shop.

## Pulling away for the first time

Once the engine is running and settled, sit on the bike from the left with at least one foot on the ground, check your mirrors, and make sure the stand is up. Honda's Super Cub manual lists the sequence before pulling away: indicate your direction with the turn signal, and check for oncoming traffic.

Use the pedal gently. Select first gear with a firm but unhurried movement of your left foot, ease the throttle open, and let the bike move off. If it lurches, you probably opened the throttle too quickly, or the pedal came up too sharply. Do not worry if the first attempts feel clumsy. A Cub is built so that ordinary riders learn it in an afternoon, but traffic is a poor classroom. Our guide to [avoiding lurching when changing gears](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-avoid-lurching-when-changing-gears/) explains the movement. If you can, practise in a quiet street first, such as those in [Long Bien and along the Red River](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/long-bien-red-river-first-ride/).

## Switching off and parking

Honda's parking routine is short. Stop the engine, put the bike on its stand on a firm level surface, turn the handlebar fully to the left if the stand and ignition lock require it, then turn the key to Lock. Honda also notes that leaving the ignition on with the engine stopped drains the battery. The shop does not supply an extra lock, so locking the steering matters, and you should park in guarded places. ## FAQ: starting a Cub 50cc

### Do I need to pull a clutch lever to start a Cub?

No. A Cub-style bike has a centrifugal clutch and a foot gear pedal and no clutch lever, so the habit is to check neutral and keep the throttle closed.

### Should I use the kick starter or the button?

Use the button if the bike has one and the battery is healthy. Use the kick starter if the button does nothing or the shop tells you to.

### How long can I hold the start button?

Honda says that if the engine does not start within about five seconds, turn the ignition off and wait ten seconds before trying again.

### Why does the bike lurch when I pull away?

Usually the throttle was opened too quickly or the gear pedal released too fast. Open the throttle gently and let the clutch take up the drive.

### Is a Cub 50cc easy for a beginner?

Many first-time riders find it approachable because it is light and has no clutch lever, but traffic still demands attention. Practise somewhere quiet first.
