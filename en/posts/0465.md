On a scooter, signal early with the indicator switch, keep both hands on the grips while you do it, and leave the signal on until the turn is finished. Article 15 of Law 36/2024/QH15 says that before changing direction the operator must observe the surroundings to ensure a safe distance with the vehicle behind, slow down and send out a signal indicating the direction, or give a hand signal where the vehicle has no direction-change lights, and that the signal must be used continuously during the change of direction. A scooter's indicator switch sits under your thumb, so you can signal without taking a hand off the bars. That matters, because Article 33 bans riders of motorcycles and mopeds from taking both hands off the handlebars while riding.

This guide explains what the law requires, how to use the indicator switch smoothly, when to signal, how to avoid the common mistakes of signalling late, cancelling early or not at all, what to do when the indicators do not work and how to check them on a rental scooter. It is general information based on the law as of October 2026, not legal advice.

## What the law says about signals

Three provisions of Law 36/2024/QH15 deal with signals.

- **Article 15(2), change of direction.** Before turning left, turning right or making a U-turn, the operator must observe the surroundings to ensure a safe distance with the vehicle behind, slow down and send out a signal for notifying the direction to be changed, or give a hand signal for vehicles that have no indicator lights, and gradually move to the lane closest to the direction of the change. The signal or hand signal must be used continuously during the change.

- **Article 13(2), lane changes.** The operator must send out a signal indicating the intention in advance, and observe the surroundings to ensure safe distances before changing lane.

- **Article 18(3), stopping and parking.** The operator must signal to notify other operators when leaving or entering a stop or parking space.

Article 14(5) adds that an overtaking vehicle must signal the direction to be changed, in addition to warning with lights or horn.

Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, as read on the Government portal, sets a fine of VND 600,000 to 800,000 for turning without observing, without a safe distance, without slowing down or without a signal, or for not keeping the signal on during the turn, and VND 200,000 to 400,000 for changing lanes without a signal or where it is not allowed. Check the current text if a figure matters to you.

## Why signals matter more on a scooter

A scooter is small and quick, and other road users have a short time to read what you are about to do. A signal gives them that time. In dense Hanoi traffic, where riders leave small gaps and many move at once, a clear, early signal is often the only warning. A late or missing signal turns your move into a surprise for the rider beside or behind you. ## Know your controls before you ride

Before you leave the shop, learn where things are.

1. **Find the indicator switch** on the handlebar, usually a small slider or rocker under the left thumb, with left and right positions and a centre position.

2. **Learn how it moves.** Press and release, or slide and return. Know which way is left.

3. **Learn how it cancels.** Many scooters do not cancel the signal automatically. You push the switch inward, or press it back to centre.

4. **Find the horn and the headlight switch,** so that you do not press the wrong one in a hurry.

5. **Check the dashboard indicator,** the arrow that blinks on the panel and the tick-tock you may hear.

## Keeping control while you signal

The aim is to signal without disturbing the steering or the brakes.

- **Use your thumb,** and keep your fingers on the grip and your hand on the brake lever.

- **Keep both hands on the bars.** Article 33(3) bans taking both hands off the handlebars. Using the switch keeps both on.

- **Do not look down** at the switch. Learn it by feel in a quiet place.

- **Signal on a straight, steady line,** before you slow or lean, not in the middle of the turn.

- **Leave your throttle hand alone.** Do not twist the throttle as you press.

- **Keep your eyes on the road,** and watch the dashboard only in a glance.

If you have gloves, practise with them on, because the switch feels different.

## When to signal

Signal before every change of direction or position.

| Situation | Signal |
|---|---|
| Turning left or right at a junction | Before you slow, early enough for traffic behind to see |
| Changing lanes | Before you move, and keep it on until you are in the new lane |
| Moving out around a parked car | Before you leave your line |
| Overtaking | Before you move out, with a flash of the light or the horn, as Article 14 requires |
| Leaving a roundabout | Before the exit |
| Pulling over to stop or park | Before you slow into the space |
| Pulling out from a kerb | Before you move off |

Article 15 says to signal, to slow down and to move gradually into the lane for the turn. In practice, the order is: look and check the mirror, signal, slow, position, turn.

## Signal early, but not too early

How early is early enough?

- **Early enough for others to react.** Traffic behind needs a second or two to see it and respond.

- **Not so early that it is misleading.** If you signal right at the first of several side streets, riders may think you are about to turn into the first one. Signal as you approach the one you want.

- **Not at the last second.** A signal and a turn at the same moment give no warning.

Count it in distance and time, not in rules. On a quiet street, a few seconds before the turn is a good guide. In heavy traffic, you may need to signal a little earlier and slow more gradually.

## Keep it on until the turn is finished

Article 15 says the signal must be used continuously during the change of direction. That means:

- **Do not turn it off as you start the turn.** Keep it on until you are through.

- **Do not cancel it halfway through a lane change.**

- **Cancel it once you are straight on the new road or in the new lane.**

- **Do not leave it on afterwards.** A signal left blinking tells the rider behind that you are about to turn again. Check the dashboard indicator.

On a scooter without self-cancelling indicators, make the cancel part of the turn: turn, straighten, cancel.

## Signalling on a roundabout and at junctions

At a roundabout, a signal tells the traffic waiting to enter which exit you are taking. Signal right as you approach your exit, and keep it on until you have left. ## Do not rely on others' signals

Local riders may signal late, or not at all, and some leave indicators on by mistake. So:

- **Treat a signal as a hint, not a promise.** Watch the front wheel and the rider's head and shoulders.

- **Treat a missing signal as a warning.** A rider who slows and drifts may be about to turn.

- **Do not turn on the strength of another rider's signal.** Check for yourself.

## Brake lights and hazard lights

- **Your brake light is a signal.** Tap the brake early when you slow down, so that the rider behind has warning.

- **Hazard lights** are for stopping because of a technical fault or other force majeure, as Article 18 describes. Do not use them as a general "I am going to do something" signal.

- **Horns.** Article 21 allows horns to warn of circumstances affecting safety and to announce an overtake, and bans continuous use. A short tap is enough.

## If the indicators do not work

If an indicator fails:

1. **Check the bulb and the switch,** and the dashboard indicator. A fast blink often means a bulb is out.

2. **Tell the shop** and ask for a repair or another bike. Nguyen Ha can be reached on 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969), open 09:00 to 21:00 daily.

3. **Do not ride in traffic without working signals** if you can avoid it. Article 15 contemplates hand signals only for vehicles that have no indicator lights, so a bike with failed indicators is a fault to fix, not a normal state.

4. **If you must move a short distance,** use extra care, slow down and give way. A brief arm signal is better than none, but keep control of the bike.

## Checks on a rental bike

At pickup:

- **Test left and right indicators,** front and back, and the dashboard repeaters.

- **Check that they cancel,** or learn how.

- **Check the brake light,** front and rear.

- **Check the horn.**

3. **Forgetting to cancel it.**

4. **Taking a hand off the bars.**

1. **Know where the switch is, and how it cancels.**

2. **Signal early, with the thumb, both hands on the grips.**

3. **Slow down, position, then turn.**

4. **Keep the signal on until the turn is finished.**

## FAQ: signalling on a scooter

### Do I have to signal for every turn?

Yes. Article 15 of Law 36/2024/QH15 requires a signal before changing direction, and Article 13 requires one before a lane change.

### How long must I keep the signal on?

Throughout the change of direction. Article 15 says it must be used continuously.

### Can I use a hand signal instead?

Article 15 contemplates hand signals for vehicles that have no direction-change lights. On a scooter with indicators, use the indicators, and keep both hands on the bars, because Article 33 bans taking both hands off the handlebars.

### What is the fine for turning without a signal?

VND 600,000 to 800,000 for turning without observing, a safe distance, slowing down or a signal, or without keeping the signal on, under Decree 168/2024/ND-CP. Check the current text.

### What if my indicators do not cancel?

Cancel them by hand with the switch after each turn, and check the dashboard indicator. Tell the shop if they will not turn off.

### Do I signal when I pull over?

Yes. Article 18 requires a signal when entering or leaving a stopping or parking space.
