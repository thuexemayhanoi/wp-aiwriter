Usually not safely, and often not at all. A typical automatic scooter such as a Honda Vision or Lead has no luggage rack. Its manufacturer sets small limits: about 10 kg in the under-seat box, 1.5 kg on the front hook and 3 kg on the rear grab rail, with a maximum of 15 kg of luggage on the Vision.

A full-size suitcase is heavier and bulkier than that, and Vietnam's road traffic law also bans riders from carrying bulky items and limits how far cargo may stick out. A daypack, a small cabin bag or a few shopping bags can work. A large suitcase, a surfboard or a box should travel another way. This guide explains the limits, shows where each kind of bag can go, spells out the cargo rule and lists practical alternatives for the days you arrive or leave with heavy bags.

## What "large luggage" means on a scooter

On a car, a large bag is one that does not fit the boot. On a small scooter, almost any bag bigger than a school backpack is large, because the bike was designed for a rider, perhaps a passenger, a helmet and a few personal items.

For this article, large luggage means any bag that:

- does not fit inside the under-seat storage with the seat closed,
- needs to be strapped on, held between your feet or propped against the handlebars, or
- is heavy enough that you notice the weight when you lift it.

That definition catches most wheeled suitcases, big travel backpacks, camera cases, boxes and sports gear. It leaves out a small daypack worn on your back or a lightweight bag in the storage box.

## What Honda's manuals say about load

Honda's Vietnamese owner's manuals are specific about loading, and the numbers are small.

The Vision manual says that the combined weight of the rider, passenger, luggage and accessories must not exceed 170 kg and that luggage must never exceed 15 kg. Its label lists the limits by position: 10 kg in the storage box, 1.5 kg on the front hook and front compartment and 3 kg on the rear grab rail. The Lead manual lists the same three position limits of 10 kg, 1.5 kg and 3 kg, and gives a maximum carrying capacity of 176 kg for the variants it covers.

The manual's warning is blunt. Overloading or loading badly can cause a crash. Carrying extra weight affects handling, braking and stability, and you should ride at a speed that suits the load. Honda also says to tie luggage down securely, balance it, keep it as close to the centre of the bike as possible and keep it away from the lights and exhaust.

A rental scooter may be a different variant from the one in the manual, so ask the shop for the limits on your bike. The point is that the manufacturer's numbers describe a bike for commuting and shopping, not for moving house.

## Where luggage can and cannot go

| Place | What the manuals say | What fits |
|---|---|---|
| Under-seat box | Up to 10 kg on the Vision and Lead | A helmet, a small bag, a rain jacket, a camera in a soft case |
| Front hook or front compartment | 1.5 kg | A light bag, a bottle, a phone |
| Rear grab rail | 3 kg | Almost nothing, and it is meant for the passenger's hands |
| Floor between your feet | Not given a limit | Only a small, soft, stable bag, and with care |
| On your back | Not a scooter limit, but it adds to the 15 kg figure | A daypack that does not shift |
| On the pillion seat, strapped | Not described as a luggage place | Risky; it blocks the passenger seat and shifts weight to the rear |
| Hanging from the handlebars | Not recommended | Anything; it affects steering |

The under-seat box is the safest place for weight. Honda says the Air Blade's is 23.2 litres and the Lead's is more than 37 litres, while the Vision's is smaller, commonly reported around 18 litres. A full-size cabin suitcase will not fit in any of them.

## Why a big bag on a scooter is a problem

Several problems stack up at once.

**Balance.** A scooter has a short wheelbase and small wheels. A heavy bag standing on the floorboard or tied behind the seat raises the centre of gravity or moves it backwards, so the front wheel feels light and the steering vague.

**Steering and visibility.** A bag on the floor can block your feet and your view of the front wheel. A bag on the pillion seat leaves no space for the passenger, if you have one.

**Braking.** More mass means a longer stopping distance, and the rear brake on most small automatics is a drum brake. Our guide to [front brake versus rear brake on small manual motorbikes](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/front-brake-versus-rear-brake-on-small-manual-motorbikes/) explains how braking balance shifts under load, and the principle is the same on a scooter.

**Hanoi traffic.** Narrow alleys, sudden stops and crowded junctions leave little room for a wide or wobbling load. Our guide to [crossing Hanoi intersections](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/crossing-hanoi-intersections/) describes the conditions you would be riding through.

**Parking.** A bike carrying a visible suitcase is an attractive target. Our guide to [parking a motorbike in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-motorbike-in-hanoi/) explains why guarded lots matter, and the shop does not supply an extra lock, so you would be leaving the bag with the bike.

## What Vietnam's road rules say about cargo

This is general information, not legal advice. As of October 2026, the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (Law No. 36/2024/QH15), in force since 1 January 2025, has two relevant provisions in Article 33.

First, riders of two-wheeled motorbikes and mopeds must not carry bulky items, and must not load goods beyond the limits the rules set. Passengers must not carry bulky items either.

Second, cargo must not extend more than 0.3 metres beyond the width of the luggage rack on each side or 0.5 metres behind the rack, as designed by the manufacturer, and the load height measured from the road must not exceed 2 metres.

These limits are written around a luggage rack, and many automatic scooters have none. How an officer would apply them to a scooter with no rack is for the authorities, not for this article to decide. The practical reading is cautious: if the load looks bulky, sticks out beyond the body of the bike or is strapped on in a way that you would not want to explain at the roadside, do not carry it. Penalties for breaking the rules on cargo are set separately by Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, and heavier ones apply if a load contributes to a crash.

The rules on helmets and documents still apply as usual, as covered in our guides to [helmet rules in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/helmet-rules-in-vietnam/) and [what documents to carry when riding](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/documents-to-carry-riding-in-vietnam/).

## What can reasonably go on an automatic scooter

Used sensibly, a scooter can carry plenty of everyday gear:

- A small daypack on your back, as long as it is not heavy enough to pull you backwards.
- A helmet, a rain jacket and a water bottle in the under-seat box.
- A soft shopping bag on the front hook, within its small limit.
- A small camera bag in the storage box.
- Your phone in a holder or the front compartment.

Pack the heaviest item in the box, at the bottom, and keep anything loose from touching the lights or exhaust. ## Better ways to move large bags

If you arrive in Hanoi with a suitcase or are moving between hotels, consider these:

1. **Leave the big bag at your hotel.** Many hotels and hostels will store luggage for guests, so ask before you collect a scooter. Take only what you need for the day.

2. **Pack a day bag and a base bag.** Keep the day bag light enough for the storage box and leave the rest at your accommodation.
3. **Use a taxi or ride-hailing car for the transfer,** then rent the scooter after you have dropped the bags. This is often the simplest solution on arrival and departure days.

5. **Choose another vehicle for a big load.** A larger scooter has more storage but is not a pickup truck. If you need to carry heavy cargo regularly, a different vehicle or a courier is the safer choice.

## Before you leave the shop with any luggage

Whatever you are carrying, take a few minutes:

1. **Test the under-seat box** with your actual bag and helmet. Close it fully and check the seat latches.
2. **Check tyre pressure** is correct for the load. The Vision and Lead manuals list the figures, and the shop can tell you the right number for your bike.
3. **Mount the bag low and central** and ride a short distance to feel the balance.
4. **Take the bag off** if the bike feels twitchy, pulls to one side or the steering feels vague.
5. **Do not hang anything from the handlebars,** where it could swing into the front wheel or block the controls.
6. **Ask the shop** if you are unsure. Staff know their bikes and can say what a model handles well. Call 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969), open 09:00 to 21:00 daily.

## FAQ: automatic scooters and large luggage

### Can I carry a wheeled suitcase on a Vision or Lead?

It is not a good idea. The manuals limit luggage to small weights in fixed places, and a suitcase is bulky. Use a hotel luggage room or a car instead.

### How much luggage can a Honda Vision carry?

Honda's Vision manual gives a maximum of 15 kg of luggage, with 10 kg in the storage box, 1.5 kg on the front hook and 3 kg on the rear grab rail.

### Can I carry a backpack on my back?

A small, light daypack is common, but it still counts towards the load and can pull you backwards if it is heavy.

### Can I put a bag on the floor between my feet?

Some riders do with small soft bags. It can block your feet and affect balance, so keep it light, low and stable, and stop using the spot if it gets in the way.

### Can the shop deliver the bike with my luggage?

Delivery is for weekly and monthly agreements within 10 km of Hoan Kiem Lake, and the airport is not covered. Ask the shop what it can arrange.

### Is a bigger scooter better for luggage?

It has more under-seat space, but it still has the same small limits on hooks and rails. Space is not the same as load capacity.
