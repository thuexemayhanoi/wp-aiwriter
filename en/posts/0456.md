A safe following distance is enough space behind the vehicle ahead to stop if it brakes suddenly, and in Vietnam it is a legal duty as well as good sense. Law 36/2024/QH15 requires operators to comply with the regulations on speed and on the minimum safe distance from the vehicle in front in the same lane. Circular 38/2024/TT-BGTVT, in force since 1 January 2025, defines the safe distance as the minimum distance between the vehicle behind and the vehicle immediately ahead in the same lane that avoids a collision if the vehicle in front suddenly slows or stops. The Circular sets fixed distances from 60 km/h upwards, and for speeds below 60 km/h it tells the operator to keep a distance suited to traffic density and the real situation. For a scooter in the city, a time gap of about two seconds in dry conditions, and more in rain, is a practical way to do that.

This guide explains the legal rule, gives a time-based method that works at city speeds, shows how the gap changes in rain, in stop-and-go traffic and behind buses and trucks, and covers what to do when someone is tailgating you. It is general information based on the law as of October 2026, not legal advice.

## What the law requires

Three texts matter.

**Law 36/2024/QH15, Article 12.** Drivers and operators must comply with the regulations on speed and on the minimum safe distance between their vehicles and the vehicles in front of them moving in the same lane or road section. They must also keep a speed suited to the conditions of bridges and roads, traffic density, terrain, weather and other influencing factors. The Minister of Transport sets the detail.

**Circular 38/2024/TT-BGTVT, Article 3.** The Circular defines the safe distance as the minimum distance between the vehicle behind and the vehicle immediately ahead, in the same lane, that avoids a collision if the front vehicle suddenly reduces speed or stops.

**Circular 38/2024/TT-BGTVT, Article 11.** On a dry, non-slippery, straight, level road with good visibility and no fog, the safe distance depends on speed:

| Operating speed | Safe distance |
|---|---|
| 60 km/h | 35 metres |
| Over 60 up to 80 km/h | 55 metres |
| Over 80 up to 100 km/h | 70 metres |
| Over 100 up to 120 km/h | 100 metres |

For speeds below 60 km/h, the Circular says the operator must actively keep a safe distance appropriate to the vehicle ahead, depending on traffic density and the actual situation. In rain, fog, on slippery surfaces, on winding or hilly roads and where visibility is limited, the operator must keep a distance greater than the values in the table, and greater than the value on any "minimum distance between two vehicles" sign. Where such a sign is posted, you must keep at least the distance it shows.

One more fact helps with planning. For mopeds, the legal class that covers 50cc bikes and most low-powered electric scooters, the Circular sets a maximum operating speed of 40 km/h on roads other than expressways, while larger motorcycles have higher general limits by road type. Ask the shop which class your bike belongs to, and see our guide to [speed limits for motorbikes in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/speed-limits-motorbikes-vietnam/).

## Turning the law into a habit: the time gap

Most scooter riding in Hanoi happens below 60 km/h, where the Circular leaves the distance to your judgement. A metre count is hard to judge on the move. A time gap is easier.

1. **Pick a fixed point** the vehicle ahead passes, such as a lamp post, a sign or a shopfront.

2. **Count the seconds** between the front vehicle passing it and you passing it. "One thousand and one, one thousand and two."

3. **Aim for at least two seconds** on a dry road in normal conditions. Increase it to three or more when conditions are poor.

The table shows what that means in metres. It is arithmetic, not a legal requirement.

| Speed | Distance covered per second | About 2 seconds | About 3 seconds |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20 km/h | 5.6 m | 11 m | 17 m |
| 30 km/h | 8.3 m | 17 m | 25 m |
| 40 km/h | 11.1 m | 22 m | 33 m |
| 50 km/h | 13.9 m | 28 m | 42 m |
| 60 km/h | 16.7 m | 33 m | 50 m |

At 60 km/h, the Circular's 35 metres is about two seconds, so the two-second gap is close to the legal figure where one applies. The gap shrinks in metres as you slow down, but it never disappears. At 20 km/h it is still around 11 metres, which is more than most riders leave in a crowded street, so expect to be somewhere between the ideal and what the traffic allows, and aim for the ideal whenever there is room.

## Why Hanoi makes this harder

In dense mixed traffic, the gap in front of you is also the gap other riders want. Scooters fill every space, and a rider who leaves a car's length gets cut in on. That is a reason to leave room, not a reason to close it. Space is your braking distance, your escape route and your view of the road. When someone fills it, drop back again, smoothly, and do not take it personally.

Some habits help:

- **Look through and beyond the vehicle ahead.** Watch the car two or three places in front, and the brake lights of vehicles you can see past or beside it.

- **Look for the reason traffic is slowing,** not only the brake lights.

- **Keep a gap to the side** as well as in front, where the road allows. Article 13 requires you to keep within one lane and to keep safe distances with vehicles in front, behind and on both sides when changing lanes.

- **Use both brakes,** smoothly, and tap the rear brake early to light the brake lamp for the rider behind.

## Rain and wet roads

The Circular is explicit: in rain, fog and on slippery surfaces, the distance must be greater. Riding guidance from rider-training sources says the same, with a rule of thumb that wet roads need roughly double the dry stopping distance, partly because there is less grip and partly because visibility is lower. In practice:

- **Double the time gap** to four seconds on a wet road.

- **Brake earlier, and smoothly,** with both brakes, while upright.

- **Avoid braking on painted markings and metal covers,** which are slippery when wet. Our guide to [braking safely on wet road markings](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/braking-safely-on-wet-road-markings/) covers the technique.

- **Do not follow so closely that spray hides the road.**

- **Remember the first minutes of a shower,** when the road can be slipperiest after a dry spell.

## Stop-and-go traffic

In slow traffic the distance changes, but the principle does not.

- **Leave room to see the rear wheel of the vehicle ahead** when you stop, so that you have space to pull out if it stalls.

- **Do not creep up** to the vehicle in front, and do not stop with your front wheel beside its rear wheel.

- **Stop smoothly,** and keep a hand on the brake.

- **Watch your mirror** when you stop, because the rider behind may not.

- **Keep your feet ready** to put down, and your balance.

## Behind buses, trucks and large vehicles

A large vehicle hides the road ahead and stops in unexpected places.

- **Leave a bigger gap,** because you cannot see past it.

- **Stay slightly to one side** so that you can see ahead and the driver can see you in the mirror.

- **Expect sudden stops** at bus stops, crossings and junctions.

- **Do not follow in a truck's spray or exhaust.**

## At lights and junctions

- **Stop behind the stop line,** and leave a gap to the vehicle ahead.

- **Do not follow another rider through an amber light.** The rule applies to you separately.

- **Leave room when turning,** because Article 15 requires a safe distance from the vehicle behind before a change of direction.

## When someone is tailgating you

A rider or driver that follows too closely behind you is dangerous, because a sudden stop by you becomes a collision for them.

1. **Do not brake hard** to "teach a lesson".

2. **Increase your gap** in front, so that you can slow gradually.

3. **Signal early,** and slow smoothly, so that the rider behind has time.

4. **Move to the right** if it is safe, and let them pass.

5. **Keep calm.** A confrontation helps no one.

## What can go wrong, legally

Following too closely can be an offence. Under Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, as read on the Government portal, not keeping a safe distance so that you collide with the vehicle ahead, or not keeping the distance shown on a "minimum distance between two vehicles" sign, carries a fine of VND 600,000 to 800,000 for motorcycle and moped riders, and turning without observing or without a safe distance from the vehicle behind carries the same range. If the lack of distance causes an accident, the fines are much higher and licence points are deducted. Check the current text if a figure matters to you. ## A quick checklist

1. **Count a gap of at least two seconds on a dry road.**

2. **Double it in rain.**

3. **Look through and past the vehicle ahead.**

4. **Leave more behind buses and trucks.**

5. **In stop-and-go traffic, stop with room to see its rear wheel.**

6. **Brake smoothly, and tap the rear brake early.**

7. **If someone tailgates you, increase your gap and let them pass.**

## FAQ: safe distance on a scooter in Hanoi

### What is a safe following distance in Vietnam?

For speeds from 60 km/h on a dry road, Circular 38/2024/TT-BGTVT sets 35 metres at 60 km/h and more as speed rises. Below 60 km/h, you must keep a distance suited to traffic density and the real situation.

### Does the gap change in rain?

Yes. The Circular says that in rain, fog and on slippery surfaces you must keep more than the standard distance. A common rule of thumb is to double it.

### What is the two-second rule?

Pick a fixed point, and count the seconds between the vehicle ahead passing it and you passing it. Two seconds or more is a reasonable minimum on a dry road.

### What if there is a "minimum distance" sign?

Keep at least the distance shown on the sign.

### Is following too closely an offence?

Yes, where it causes a collision with the vehicle ahead or breaks a "minimum distance" sign, and a higher fine applies if it causes an accident.

### What should I do if someone is tailgating me?

Do not brake hard. Increase your gap, signal early, slow gradually and let them pass when it is safe.
