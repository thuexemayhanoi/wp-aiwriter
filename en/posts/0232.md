After a wet ride, do not plug in straight away. Turn the bike off, wipe the seat, the charging port and the plug dry, let the battery cool for about 20 minutes, and charge only in a dry, ventilated place with a dry floor and a dry socket. If water has got into the port, the battery case or the charger, leave it unplugged and call the shop. Light rain on a sealed electric motorbike is normal, and the real danger is not the rain itself but charging with moisture in the wrong place. This guide covers what to do in the first half hour after a wet ride, where to charge when it is still pouring, what Hanoi's storm season changes and the signs that mean stop.

## What rain does and does not do to an electric motorbike

Electric two-wheelers are built to be ridden in the wet. London Fire Brigade says most e-bikes and e-scooters are designed to withstand light rain and splashes in normal use, but that lithium-ion batteries are not waterproof. Water that gets into a battery or the electrical parts can cause short circuits, corrosion and battery failure, and a battery that has been submerged should not be used.

Some makers advertise a much higher level of protection. In August 2026 ABS-CBN reported VinFast's claim that an IP67 rating means the motor and control unit can withstand submersion in half a metre of water for up to 30 minutes. That is a statement about specific parts of a specific range of bikes, reported in a different market, and it does not make a battery, a charging port or a plug safe to charge wet. Treat a rating as a reason to ride through rain with less worry, not as permission to charge a soaked bike.

The distinction is between riding and charging. Riding in rain sends water over sealed components. Charging puts mains electricity through a port, a plug and a cable, and those are exactly the places where moisture can hide.

## The first half hour after a wet ride

Do these in order. None needs tools.

1. **Switch the bike off** and take the key out.

2. **Move under cover.** A covered, dry spot with a dry floor, not a puddle by the kerb.

3. **Wipe the exterior dry.** Seat, panels and the area around the charging port. If there is a flap over the port, open it and look inside.

4. **Dry the plug and the cable end** with a clean cloth before they go anywhere near the port.

5. **Check the port.** Look for droplets, a film of water or sand and leaves. If it is wet inside, do not charge. London Fire Brigade advises drying the bike and battery if they become wet, and removing and drying the battery before charging if possible.

6. **Wait for the battery to cool.** Provincial fire-prevention advice from Gia Lai says to wait about 20 minutes after use before charging, rather than charging straight away. That applies to hot batteries after a long ride and is a sensible habit after a wet one, too.

7. **Charge only when everything is dry,** and only in a place you have checked.

If any step is doubtful, stop at that step. The shop would rather answer a call than inspect a damaged battery.

## Where to charge when it is still raining

Weather often decides the place for you. These are the sensible options, in order of preference.

- **Indoors in a dry, ventilated spot** that the hotel or host has agreed to, with someone present. Fire-prevention advice in Gia Lai recommends a dry, airy place and not a closed room, and a temperature range of about 0 to 35 degrees Celsius.

- **Under a roof but open to the air,** such as a covered courtyard or garage mouth, if the floor is dry and rain is not blowing in.

- **Not outdoors in the open.** An outdoor socket on a wet wall is a poor choice, and a cable lying in a puddle is worse.

- **Not in a closed bathroom, laundry or kitchen** where steam and splashes are common.

- **Not in a corridor or stairwell.** If something goes wrong there, it blocks the way out. London Fire Brigade advises keeping charging away from main through-routes and exits.

If the only place on offer is wet, delay the charge. A bike on 30 per cent that charges tomorrow morning is better than a bike charged in a damp corner tonight.

## Sockets, leads and the things around the bike

Rain brings wet clothes, wet floors and wet hands into the room. Check these before you plug in.

- **Dry hands, dry floor.** Do not plug in with wet hands or while standing in water.

- **Use the charger supplied with the bike.** London Fire Brigade advises using the charger supplied with the battery or one the manufacturer recommends. Do not swap in another because it is nearer.

- **Plug straight into a fixed wall socket where you can.** Avoid long extension leads and overloaded adaptors, which fire services flag as hazards in their charging advice.

- **Keep the cable off the floor** where water or wet shoes will reach it, and out of walkways.

- **Keep wet things away from the charger.** Raincoats, ponchos, umbrellas and wet helmets should not hang over or beside it. A poncho draped over a charging bike is a hazard, since covering batteries or chargers can cause overheating.

- **Keep flammable things away** such as cardboard, paper and fabrics.

## What Hanoi's storm season changes

Hanoi's rainy season runs from about May to October according to Vietnam Tourism's guide, and weather statistics put October rainfall at roughly 128 mm on average. Those are averages. Storms bring much more at once. In late September 2025, Typhoon Bualoi's rain caused flooding in the capital that residents described as the worst since 2008, and in early October 2025 Reuters reported Hanoi flooded again, with people pushing motorbikes through flooded streets after Typhoon Matmo.

For a renter, three things follow.

**Do not ride through deep water.** London Fire Brigade advises avoiding deep water and flooding. A bike that has been submerged, or in water above the battery or port, should not be charged or ridden. Report it to the shop immediately.

**Park high and early.** If a storm is forecast, put the bike somewhere that will not flood and do not leave a battery on the floor of a basement. Our guide to [parking a motorbike in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-motorbike-in-hanoi/) explains guarded lots, which are often at street level.

**Expect power cuts and surges.** A storm may interrupt the supply. Do not leave a charger plugged in and unattended while the lights flicker. Unplug the charger and the bike if the electricity cuts out, and do not plug in again until the supply is steady and the area is dry.

## Warning signs: stop and call the shop

Stop charging and unplug if you see, smell or feel any of these:

- the battery, charger or cable is unusually hot;
- the case is swollen, bulging, cracked or deformed;
- there is a smell of burning, chemicals or something sweet;
- the plug or port shows scorching, discolouration or melting;
- you see sparks or smoke;
- the display shows an error you cannot clear;
- the bike was submerged or the port was flooded.

London Fire Brigade lists unusual heat, swelling, odd smells and much-reduced performance as signs of a battery that may have been damaged by the weather. Gia Lai's fire-prevention department advises replacing a battery that is swollen or cracked. If the battery or the charger is smoking, burning or spitting, move people away, do not touch it, and call the fire and rescue emergency number given at the hotel or reception. Then tell the shop.

Call Nguyen Ha on 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969), open 09:00 to 21:00 daily, for anything short of an emergency. Our guide to [rental bike breakdowns in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-bike-breakdown-hanoi-what-to-do/) covers who does what.

## What not to do

- **Do not dry a battery with a hairdryer, heater or in direct sun.** Slow, ambient drying is safer. High heat stresses lithium batteries.

- **Do not wash the bike with a hose or a pressure washer.** London Fire Brigade warns against high-pressure water on e-bikes and e-scooters.

- **Do not open the battery, the charging port or the casing.** That is for the shop.

- **Do not charge a battery that has been submerged.** It should not be used.

- **Do not charge in a hurry because of the rain.** A flat battery is an inconvenience. A wet port is a risk.

- **Do not leave it charging overnight** because the weather has made you late. Fire-prevention advice in Vietnam and the UK is to stop charging before you sleep or go out.

## A quick routine for a wet day

**Before riding:** check the charge, the port cover and the plug seal. Take the charger back to the hotel dry.

**After riding:** switch off, move under cover, dry the bike and plug, check the port.

**Before charging:** wait about 20 minutes, check the place, check the socket and use the supplied charger.

**While charging:** stay nearby, check from time to time and keep wet clothes away.

**Before sleeping or going out:** stop the charge and unplug.

**Next morning:** look at the port and plug again. If there is any doubt, call the shop before riding.

## FAQ: charging an electric motorbike in the rain

### Can I charge an electric motorbike right after riding in the rain?

Not straight away. Dry the bike, the port and the plug, and let the battery cool for about 20 minutes, following provincial fire-prevention advice. Charge only in a dry place, and not if the port or plug is wet.

### Is it safe to charge outdoors under a roof?

It can be, if the area is dry, ventilated and the floor and socket stay dry. An open yard in the rain, a wet wall socket or a cable lying in water is not safe.

### What if the charging port got wet?

Leave it unplugged. Dry the exterior and let the area dry. If you cannot see inside or the port looks wet, call the shop. Do not use a heater to speed it up.

### What if I rode through a flooded street?

Tell the shop. Water above the battery or port is a reason not to charge or ride until it has been checked. A submerged battery should not be used.

### Does an IP67 rating mean I can charge in the rain?

No. A rating describes how well parts resist water, not whether it is safe to put mains power through a wet port. Reports of VinFast's IP67 claim concern the motor and controller on certain models.

### Is lightning a reason not to charge?

As general caution, do not charge during a storm if you would not be present to watch, and unplug if the supply is unstable. This is general guidance and not a manufacturer's instruction, so ask the shop.

### Can the shop recharge it for me?

Ask. Whether it can depends on the bike and the arrangement made at handover, where the shop confirms the battery level and charging plan for electric rentals.
