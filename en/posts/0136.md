For a week in Hanoi, pick the automatic scooter that fits your routine on day four, not the one that looks best on day one. A week is long enough for small annoyances to grow: a tank that needs filling every second day, a seat that tires you, a compartment that will not take your bag, tyres that lose pressure. It is also short enough that a small, easy bike usually beats a powerful one. This guide helps you decide by walking through a seven-day plan, what each model on the shop's automatic list is known for, how to budget fuel, which checks to repeat midweek, and what to ask when you book. Weekly packages are priced on their own, so confirm the current figure on the prices page.

## Start with what your week looks like

The right scooter depends on the shape of the week, so sketch it first. Most renters fall into one of three patterns:

- **City base.** You stay in or near the centre and ride short trips between sights, cafes and a workplace or class.
- **City plus day trips.** Most days are urban, but one or two are longer rides out of town, such as to a craft village or a mountain.
- **Work or study week.** You commute at fixed times, carry a bag, and ride in rain and dark as well as sun.

Write down the longest single day you expect, whether you will carry a second person or luggage, and whether you will ride after dark. Those three answers narrow the choice more than engine size does. For the longer trips in the second pattern, see our guide to [whether you can take a rental motorbike out of Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/take-rental-motorbike-out-of-hanoi/) before assuming anything.

## A simple way to choose among them

Ask which problem you most want to avoid:

- **Hard to handle and park?** Choose the lightest. On Honda's figures that is the Vision.
- **Tired by a long day?** Choose the steadier, larger-wheeled bike, such as the Air Blade, and plan stops.
- **Always carrying things?** Choose the Lead, which has the largest tank and a low, easy seat, and check its storage space yourself.
- **Anxious about a first scooter?** Choose the lowest seat and lightest weight you can find, and confirm you can put both feet down.

If two bikes tie, choose the one you can pick up from its stand without strain. A scooter you cannot handle comfortably at a standstill is not a good week-long partner, whatever the specification.

## Plan a seven-day rhythm

A deliberately planned first two days make the rest of the week easier. This is a sample, not a rule:

| Day | Suggested use | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Short loop near the shop, fill the tank, test brakes | Learn the bike while traffic is light |
| 2 | Central streets and parking practice | Get used to filtering and tight turns |
| 3-4 | Normal routine or sightseeing | The scooter should now feel familiar |
| 5 | Midweek check: tyres, brakes, lights | Catch a slow leak or a loose mirror |
| 6 | Longer ride, if planned | You know the bike well enough |
| 7 | Clean up, refuel to the agreed level, return on time | Avoid late-return charges |

If you are new to riding, spend the first day on quieter roads east of the river rather than the Old Quarter. Our guide to [riding in the Old Quarter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-old-quarter/) explains why that area rewards walking more than riding.

## Budget fuel across the week

Honda gives standard-test fuel consumption of 1.82 litres per 100 km for the Vision and 2.23 litres per 100 km for the Air Blade 160, with the Lead listed at 2.1 litres. Real city riding uses more, so treat these as lower limits. As a worked example, if you ride 40 km a day for six days, you cover 240 km. Even at double Honda's figure, the Vision would use roughly 9 litres across the week, which is fewer than two tank-fulls.

The shop's usual routine is to hand petrol bikes over with about 0.5 to 2 litres and to expect them back with about 0.5 to 1 litre; an empty tank at return costs VND 20,000. So plan a fill-up on the first day, again whenever the gauge reads low, and one last top-up before the return. Our guide to [fuel in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/fuel-in-hanoi-petrol-stations-rental-scooter/) explains pump etiquette and what to say.

## Midweek checks that keep a scooter safe

On a week-long rental, repeat a few quick checks at least once. Honda's tyre guidance says air pressure drops gradually over time, that under-inflation can be hard to spot by eye and that pressure should be measured with a gauge at least once a month, when the tyres are cold. A week is shorter than a month, but a rental has changed riders, so checking once near day five is sensible.

Look at three things:

1. **Tyres.** Cuts, bulges, nails and the tread-wear indicator. Honda says to replace a tyre immediately when the wear bar becomes visible.
2. **Brakes.** Honda's daily-check advice is to push the bike forward while holding the brake lever and pressing the pedal, to confirm both brakes hold, and to note unusual lever play.
3. **Lights and mirrors.** A cracked lens or loose mirror is easier to fix on day five than on day seven.

If anything seems wrong, do not adjust parts yourself. Honda's manuals tell riders to see a dealer when brake service is needed. Telephone the shop on 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969), open 09:00 to 21:00 daily.

Read the [prices page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/prices/) for the current picture.

Other points to plan around:

- **Deposit.** A cash deposit between VND 2,000,000 and VND 5,000,000 is separate from the rent, depending on the bike and contract. Our guide to [how the deposit works](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbike-rental-deposit-hanoi/) explains how it is returned.
- **Delivery.** Offered only for weekly and monthly agreements, within 10 km of Hoan Kiem Lake, at VND 50,000 per trip or VND 100,000 both ways. Beyond 10 km it is agreed in advance, and there is no airport service.
- **Helmet.** One basic helmet per rider is free.
- **Raincoat.** Not included for short rentals, so bring your own in the rainy months.
- **Extra lock.** Not provided. Lock the steering and park in guarded places.
- **Late return.** VND 30,000 per hour for one to five hours, and one extra rental day from six hours.

## Rain and heat across the week

Over seven days you may meet rain, heat or both. Honda's PCX manual notes that brakes lose effectiveness when wet and recommends applying them gently at low speed while riding to help them dry. Plan an easy first ride if it rains: brake earlier, avoid painted lines and manhole covers and keep a longer gap. Our article on [riding in Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) lists the streets that flood.

In hot weather, park in shade where you can, because a bike left in direct sun for hours can leave the seat uncomfortably hot. A guarded car park is better than a pavement for another reason too: our guide to [parking in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-motorbike-in-hanoi/) explains guarded lots and tickets.

## Questions to ask when you book

Before agreeing, ask the shop:

1. Which automatic model will I get, and can I see it before paying?
2. What is the weekly price for that bike, and what does it cover?
3. How much fuel is in the tank now, and where should I refuel?
4. When were the tyres and brakes last checked?
5. What happens if I get a puncture or a fault midweek?
6. Is delivery possible for my address, and what is its cost?
7. How and when do I return, and what time is the cut-off?

## FAQ: weekly automatic scooter rental

### Is a weekly package cheaper than seven days?

It is priced separately from the daily rate, and the shop gives ranges for each bike type. Ask for the actual figure for your bike.

### Which scooter is easiest for a first-timer?

Usually the lightest one with a low seat that lets you stand flat-footed. On Honda's figures, the Vision is the lightest and the Lead has the lowest seat.

### Do I need to check tyre pressure myself?

A quick look is enough. If a tyre looks soft, tell the shop rather than inflating it yourself.

### Can I extend the rental midweek?

Ask the shop. Do not assume an extension at the same rate, and tell them well before the return time.

### What if I want a day trip out of town?

Check the shop's terms first. Our guide to [riding to Tam Dao](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-to-tam-dao-by-motorbike/) shows what a closer mountain ride involves.
