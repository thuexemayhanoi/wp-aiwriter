The safest default is not to leave a rental electric scooter charging while you sleep. Fire services in the UK and the US advise charging only while you are awake and present, so that you can react at once if the battery or charger overheats, and the US Consumer Product Safety Commission says to be present when charging and never charge while sleeping. Manufacturers in Vietnam argue that certified batteries and chargers have protection that cuts the charge when the battery is full, and that fire risk is very low when they are used correctly. Both views can be true at once, but a rental is not your own vehicle, so you do not know the battery's age or the charger's history. This guide sets out the questions to ask, where and when to charge, what to do if you must use a night rate or a shared building, and the signs that mean stop. It is general information, not a guarantee.

## Is it safe to charge overnight at all?

The answer depends on whose advice you follow and what you know about the bike.

**What fire and safety agencies advise.** The UK government's battery safety guidance for e-scooter users says it is vitally important to charge only while you are awake and alert, so that you can respond quickly if a fire occurs, and not to leave batteries on charge while asleep or away from home. London Fire Brigade says never to leave a battery charging unattended or while you are asleep, and notes that most fires involving e-bikes and e-scooters happen while the battery is charging. The US CPSC's September 2025 public safety message says to always be present when charging, never while sleeping, and to use only the supplied charger.

**What manufacturers and sellers say.** VietnamNet reported in December 2025 that representatives of electric motorbike makers and a Yadea Vietnam sales agent said certified batteries have a battery management system with protection layers and automatic shutdown, that chargers carry overcurrent and overheat protection and cut off when full, and that fire is nearly impossible when the vehicle is placed and charged correctly. The same article quotes experts saying the real issue is whether parking and charging areas meet fire and electrical safety standards.

**How to reconcile them.** Protection systems reduce risk, they do not remove it. A damaged battery, a worn charger, a wrong charger or an overloaded circuit can defeat protection, and you cannot inspect a rental battery from outside. If something goes wrong at 3 a.m. while you are asleep, you will not notice the first warning signs. That is why the cautious advice is the better default for a borrowed vehicle.

## Questions to ask the shop before you leave

For electric bikes the shop confirms the battery level and charging arrangements at handover, so use that conversation.

- Does the manufacturer or the shop recommend a charging routine, and does it say anything about overnight?
- How many hours does a full charge usually take on this bike?
- Is this the original charger, and is there a spare?
- Can the battery be removed, and if so, may you carry it indoors?
- How much charge should you plan to have left at the end of the day?
- What should you do if the battery or charger gets hot or smells?

## Where you charge matters as much as when

Hanoi has a specific issue here. VnExpress International reported in December 2025 that the management of the HH Linh Dam apartment complex announced it would stop allowing electric motorbikes and bicycles in its basement, citing insufficient space and a lack of firefighting equipment, and that the local ward chairman directed the management to scrap the ban and instead designate charging areas. The same article says fire safety rules do not prohibit parking or charging electric vehicles indoors, but the lack of specific guidance has led many buildings to make their own rules. A Lao Dong report in July 2026 found many rented rooms and mini-apartments in Hanoi do not allow tenants to charge electric vehicles.

For you, that means three checks before you plug in:

1. **Ask the owner or manager.** Do not charge in a hotel, hostel or apartment without permission. Some places forbid it or restrict it to certain areas.

2. **Choose a place that is not an escape route.** Fire advice says not to charge on escape routes, in hallways or in communal areas, because a fire there would block the way out. London Fire Brigade also advises charging on hard, flat surfaces where heat can disperse and away from combustible materials.

3. **Avoid bedrooms and sleeping areas.** It is difficult to react to a problem you cannot hear or smell, and the NFCC says never to charge in a bedroom or hallway where it could block your escape.

## A safer routine if you cannot avoid evenings

You do not have to ride in the dark to avoid overnight charging. The aim is to finish charging while you are awake.

- **Charge in the early evening.** Plan to plug in after dinner and unplug before bed. Rest of World reported in 2023 that a full charge for many electric two-wheelers took up to three hours, and VinFast's FAQ gives battery charging times of about three hours to 90 percent and 4.8 hours to 100 percent per battery for its e-scooters. These are not figures for the shop's rental bikes, so ask the shop for the real time on yours.

- **Let it cool first.** Fire services advise letting the battery cool after use before charging.

- **Top up during the day instead.** A cafe stop, a lunch break or time at your accommodation while you work can add charge without a night-time session. Our guide to [charging an electric scooter in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/charging-an-electric-scooter-in-hanoi/) lists the places and the timing.

- **Do not leave it plugged in when full.** NFPA advises not to continue charging once the battery is full.

- **Set a reminder.** A phone alarm at the expected finishing time is a simple way to remember the plug.

If you need to leave the bike plugged in for a long time, for instance while you are out, the same advice applies: fire services say not to leave a charging battery while you are away from home.

## Checks before you start charging

- The charger is the one supplied with the bike.
- The cable and plug show no cuts, scorch marks or loose parts.
- The socket is solid, not hot or discoloured.
- There is no extension strip daisy-chained to another.
- Nothing covers the charger or battery.
- The place is dry, ventilated and away from exits and flammable items.
- You are going to be awake and nearby.

NFPA notes that e-bike and e-scooter battery fires have been associated with faulty charging equipment, improper charging practices and overloaded electrical circuits, so the electrical set-up deserves as much attention as the battery itself.

## Warning signs while charging

GOV.UK lists the signs a battery may be a fire hazard: extreme heat, bulging or swelling, hissing or cracking noises, a strong or unusual smell, failure to charge fully or unusually long charge times, and smoke. The NFCC adds lumps and a leaking charger.

If you notice any sign but there is no smoke or fire:

1. Switch off and unplug the charger if it is safe to do so.
2. If you can, carefully move the scooter or battery outside, away from people and flammable materials.
3. Call the shop on 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969), open 09:00 to 21:00 daily. Outside those hours, leave a message and keep the bike outdoors, away from buildings.

If there is smoke or flame, leave at once, alert others and call local emergency services. Do not fight a battery fire yourself, and do not go back for belongings.

Check the display, the charger and the battery. A battery that is swollen, very hot or smells wrong should not be ridden or charged again. Tell the shop, show photographs, and ask for a replacement. ## FAQ: charging a rental electric scooter overnight

### Do fire services allow overnight charging?

Fire services in the UK and the US advise against charging while you sleep. Local advice in Vietnam may differ, so ask the shop and your accommodation.

### Do modern chargers not stop when the battery is full?

Many have protection, and Vietnamese manufacturers say so. But protection can fail with damaged or mismatched equipment, and you cannot check a rental battery's condition from outside.

### Can I leave it charging when I go out for dinner?

Fire guidance says not to leave a battery charging unattended or while you are away from home. Plan to charge when you are in.

### Is charging in a hotel lobby or corridor acceptable?

Not without the hotel's permission, and not on an escape route.

### Can I charge outdoors?

A dry, covered, ventilated spot away from combustible material is better than a bedroom, but rain and wet connectors are their own hazard. Ask the shop what it recommends.
