For city commuting in Hanoi, the Honda Wave and the Honda Blade are close to the same motorbike. Both are semi-automatic 110 cc underbones with a four-speed gearbox, an air-cooled single-cylinder engine and the same size of fuel tank, and both do the job of crossing a congested city on a few litres of fuel. The differences are small and concrete: a couple of kilograms of weight, a few millimetres of ground clearance, the style of body, the choice of wheels and front brake, and the condition of the specific bike in front of you. This guide compares them using Honda Vietnam's own specification pages and owner's manuals, explains which differences matter for daily riding and tells you what to check when you rent. Nguyen Ha's manual fleet includes the Honda Wave and the Honda Blade, and the exact bike you receive is confirmed at booking.

## The comparison at a glance

These figures come from Honda Vietnam's product pages, checked in October 2026. The table compares the Wave Alpha 110, the model most people picture when they say "Honda Wave", with the Blade 110.

| | Wave Alpha 110 | Blade 110 |
|---|---|---|
| Engine | 109.2 cc, air-cooled, single cylinder | 109.1 cc, air-cooled, single cylinder |
| Peak output | 6.12 kW at 7,500 rpm | 6.18 kW at 7,500 rpm |
| Peak torque | 8.44 N·m at 5,500 rpm | 8.65 N·m at 5,500 rpm |
| Gearbox | Mechanical, four-speed rotary | Mechanical, four-speed rotary |
| Kerb weight | 96 kg | 98–99 kg by version |
| Dimensions (L x W x H) | 1,913 x 689 x 1,076 mm | 1,920 x 702 x 1,075 mm |
| Seat height | 770 mm | 769 mm |
| Ground clearance | 134 mm | 141 mm |
| Fuel tank | 3.7 litres | 3.7 litres |
| Honda's fuel figure | 1.72 L per 100 km | 1.85 L per 100 km |

The headline is how little separates them. The engines are the same size, the outputs differ by well under 1 kW and the weights differ by two or three kilograms. Honda's fuel figures are manufacturer test results, not real-world numbers, so do not treat the gap of about 0.13 litres per 100 km as meaningful in Hanoi traffic.

Honda also sells the Wave RSX FI, a fuel-injected 110 cc model. Its owner's manual gives a kerb weight of 97 or 98 kg depending on version, a 4.0-litre fuel tank, front and rear tyre sizes of 70/90-17 and 80/90-17 and a drum rear brake. If your rental is a Wave, ask whether it is the Alpha or the RSX FI, because the RSX FI's tank and fuel injection are the two differences you might notice.

## What actually differs for a commuter

**Weight and handling.** The Wave Alpha is the lightest of the three at 96 kg, and the Blade is two or three kilograms heavier depending on wheels and brakes. On a bike this light, the difference is hard to feel while riding, but it is noticeable when you push the bike backwards out of a tight parking row or lift it on a stand. A rider with a small frame who often parks in crowded rows may notice the lighter bike first.

**Ground clearance.** Honda lists 134 mm for the Wave Alpha and 141 mm for the Blade. Seven millimetres is small, but it is the difference that matters on high kerbs, speed bumps and flooded streets. In Hanoi's rainy season the extra clearance is a minor advantage for the Blade. It does not make the bike safe in deep water, and our guide to [riding in Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) explains why.

**Seat height.** At 769 mm and 770 mm the Blade and Wave Alpha are almost identical. A rider of average height has both feet near the ground on either.

**Body and styling.** The Blade is slightly longer and wider than the Wave Alpha, at 1,920 by 702 millimetres against 1,913 by 689 millimetres. For commuting this affects little beyond taste and how the bike fits in a crowded parking row.

**Wheels and brakes.** Honda lists Blade versions with cast wheels and with spoked wheels, and with a mechanical front brake or a front disc brake. Many riders find that a front disc gives better feel in the wet. Ask the shop which front brake the bike has, then test it as in our guide to [checking the brakes before leaving the rental shop](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/manual-bike-brakes-what-to-test-before-leaving-the-rental-shop/). The Wave RSX FI's rear brake is a drum, as its manual refers to brake shoes.

**Fuel tank.** The Wave RSX FI's 4.0-litre tank is the largest of the three, while the Wave Alpha and Blade share a 3.7-litre tank. At Honda's quoted consumption, any of these bikes covers a normal day of commuting without refuelling. Our guide to [fuel in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/fuel-in-hanoi-petrol-stations-rental-scooter/) explains where and how to refuel.

**Fuel injection.** The Wave RSX FI uses electronic fuel injection, while Honda's Wave Alpha and Blade pages do not list it. For a commuter the difference is small. Ask the shop what the bike in front of you has.

## Load and passenger limits

Honda's Wave manuals state that the bike carries the rider and one passenger, give a maximum load of 115 kg or 155 kg depending on the version and set a luggage limit of 5 kg. We did not retrieve the Blade's manual figures, so ask the shop for them and do not assume they match. Vietnamese law allows only one passenger on either bike in ordinary cases. Our guide to [how much luggage you can carry on a Honda Wave](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-much-luggage-can-you-safely-carry-on-a-honda-wave/) explains how to do the weight sum, and our article on [riding a manual motorbike with a passenger](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-a-manual-motorbike-with-a-passenger-in-hanoi/) covers what two-up riding changes.

For a commuter who rides alone with a work bag, none of this is a constraint. For a couple, check the figure for the bike you are offered before you sign.

## Which one for which commute

**A short daily hop through the Old Quarter and the lake area.** Either bike works. The lighter Wave Alpha is easier to manoeuvre in tight spaces, and the Blade's extra ground clearance helps over kerbs. Choose the one that is in better condition.

**A longer commute from the outer districts.** Both are comfortable. A rider covering longer distances may prefer a Wave RSX FI for its fuel injection and larger tank, or a Blade with a front disc for the braking feel.

**A commute with a passenger or a backpack.** Check the maximum load and the carrier of the specific bike. If you often carry two people, our comparison of [scooters for two riders](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/scooter-for-two-riders-and-luggage-hanoi/) may suit you better than either of these.

**A commute in the rainy season.** Prefer a bike with a front disc brake and good tyres, and add margin to every stop.

## Condition beats badge

The most important difference between two rental bikes is not the model. It is the condition: tyre tread, brake pads, chain tension, lights, the way the gearbox changes and whether the bike has been dropped. A well-kept Wave beats a tired Blade, and the reverse is also true. ## Licence and legal basics

As of October 2026, Article 57 of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (Law No. 36/2024/QH15), in force since 1 January 2025, puts both bikes in the A1 class, which covers two-wheeled motorbikes up to 125 cc. A foreign visitor needs a home licence with a motorcycle category plus an International Driving Permit. Under Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, riding without a valid licence is fined VND 2,000,000–4,000,000 for a motorbike of up to 125 cc. Our guide to [licence rules for foreigners in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbike-licence-rules-for-foreigners-in-vietnam/) explains which permits count. ## FAQ: Wave versus Blade

### Is the Honda Blade faster than the Honda Wave?

Not in any way you would notice in the city. Honda lists nearly identical outputs and torque for the Wave Alpha and the Blade, so condition and your own riding matter more.

### Which has more ground clearance, the Wave or the Blade?

Honda lists 141 mm for the Blade and 134 mm for the Wave Alpha. The difference is small.

### Which is lighter?

The Wave Alpha, at 96 kg, against 98–99 kg for the Blade depending on version.

### Do both bikes have fuel injection?

Honda's Wave RSX FI is fuel-injected. Honda's Wave Alpha and Blade pages do not list fuel injection. Ask the shop what the bike you receive has.

### Which is better in the rain?

Neither is built for deep water. A bike with a front disc brake and good tyres matters more than the badge, and the Blade's extra clearance is a minor plus.

### Which should I rent for a daily commute?

Choose the bike that is in the better condition and has the brakes you trust. The model differences are small.
