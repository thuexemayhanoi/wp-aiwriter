Con Son and Kiep Bac are two separate sites about 5 km apart, about 80 km from Hanoi by published estimates, and they pair well as a single motorcycle day: a mountain-and-pine pagoda linked to the poet-scholar Nguyen Trai and the monk Huyen Quang, and a riverside temple to the general Tran Hung Dao. They are living places of worship as well as monuments, and since July 2025 they are part of a UNESCO World Heritage property, so the best visit is a respectful, unhurried one outside festival peaks. This guide concentrates on the two sites themselves: what each is, how to order your visit, when the crowds arrive, and how a rider should behave on arrival. For the wider region, the thinner roads and the Thanh Mai pagoda, see our separate ride-planning guide on Chi Linh.

## What the two sites are

Official and government sources describe a heritage area made of two main parts: Con Son pagoda and Kiep Bac temple. The Cultural Heritage Department says the combined relic was ranked a special national monument by Decision 548/QD-TTg on 10 May 2012. In July 2025 UNESCO inscribed the Yen Tu - Vinh Nghiem - Con Son, Kiep Bac complex on the World Heritage List, according to Vietnamese state radio. Con Son and Kiep Bac are the part of that series most easily reached from Hanoi.

Administratively, this area was in Chi Linh city, Hai Duong province. Since the July 2025 merger, official Vietnamese sources describe it as part of Hai Phong city. Older signs and maps still say Hai Duong.

## Con Son pagoda and the hillside around it

Con Son pagoda, also called Chua Hun, was built in the 14th century according to the Cultural Heritage Department, though the National Authority of Tourism, quoting a heritage official, dates its origins to the 10th century with expansion under the Tran dynasty. Sources differ, so treat the origin story as layered. What is consistent is its importance: it is one of the three main centres of the Truc Lam Zen school, and the monk Huyen Quang, the school's third patriarch, spent his life there. The pagoda is laid out in royal style, with a half-moon lake and a three-door gate in front, a path to the bell tower and then the main hall.

Around the pagoda the heritage department lists features that make a walk worthwhile:

- **Thanh Hu cave,** a valley between Con Son and the Ngu Nhac range with the Con Son stream running through it.
- **Ngu Nhac mountains,** a range more than 4 km long with five peaks, the highest about 238 m.
- **The "fairy chessboard" (Ban Co Tien),** a fairly flat spot at the top of Con Son.
- **Con Son stream,** about 3 km long, with a run of small rapids and falls before it enters Con Son lake.

A national tourism-board article adds that the pagoda is full of carved animals and plants, and that the Nguyen Trai temple stands in the same relic area. Allow time to walk, not to ride; the shaded paths are the reason to come.

## Kiep Bac temple and the river

Kiep Bac temple is dedicated to Tran Hung Dao, the 13th-century general who commanded Dai Viet against Mongol invasions. According to the tourism board, he trained soldiers and stored provisions at this place, and the temple was built to honour him after his death. A VnExpress travel report says the temple looks out on the Thuong river, also known as the Luc Dau, which was the Binh Than landing in Tran times. A temple for the Confucian teacher Chu Van An stands next door, a pairing mentioned in both the tourism board's article and VOV's English-language report on the area.

Heritage staff quoted by state media describe customs that visitors may see, including rubbing elephant bones for good health and an entry and exit ritual at the temple gates. These are practices of worshippers, not performances. If you want to take part, watch what others do first and follow the example of local visitors.

## The best order for a motorcycle day

With about 5 km between them, the order matters less than the timing. A practical sequence, with times only a suggestion:

1. **Arrive at Con Son first, if you can.** The walk is the longest part and benefits from morning light and cooler air.
2. **Have lunch in the area,** where food stalls are plentiful on busy days.
3. **Ride to Kiep Bac in the early afternoon.** It is flatter, and the riverside setting is pleasant late in the day.
4. **Start the return by mid-afternoon.** With about 80 km to ride and a flat but busy highway, you do not want to be on it at dusk. Read our guide to [riding at night in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-at-night-in-hanoi/) if you are tempted to stay longer.

If you only have time for one, choose Con Son for the walk and the setting, or Kiep Bac for the history and the river.

## Festival days: when to go and when to avoid

The area runs on the lunar calendar. The Cultural Heritage Department says the Con Son festival begins on the 15th day of the first lunar month and runs to the end of that month, and the Kiep Bac festival is held from the 16th to the 20th day of the eighth lunar month. Hai Phong's tourism news says the 2026 autumn festival ran from late September to early October, with a lantern festival on the Luc Dau river. In 2019, Vietnamese state media reported about 120,000 visitors in six days during the autumn festival, which is a useful warning about crowds.

For a rider this has simple consequences:

- **Roads slow down** near the sites, with buses, cars and pedestrians sharing narrow lanes.
- **Parking fills early,** so plan to arrive early or walk the last stretch.
- **Services stretch thin,** and temples can feel more like fairgrounds.

If you want a quieter visit, choose a weekday outside the festival weeks. If you want the atmosphere, accept the crowds and plan for a slower day. Check the dates on the official heritage website nearer the time, as lunar dates shift each year.

## Temple manners for visitors on two wheels

A rider arrives in different clothes from a pilgrim. A few habits help:

- **Dress modestly.** Cover shoulders and knees; carry a light layer if you ride in shorts.
- **Take off your helmet and sunglasses** before entering a shrine, and keep your voice low.
- **Ask before photographing** worshippers or ceremonies; do not photograph people at prayer.
- **Do not touch offerings or statues.** Follow signs about where to remove shoes.
- **Leave your bike properly parked and locked.** The shop does not provide an extra lock, so lock the steering and use a guarded or marked parking area. Check any parking charge on the day; this guide does not quote one because it could not be confirmed.

## How long to allow, and what to carry

These are planning allowances, not published visiting times. Give Con Son pagoda and its paths two to three hours if you like walking, and Kiep Bac about an hour to ninety minutes, plus time to sit and eat. That is a long day once the ride is added, which is why an early start matters more here than at a city sight.

Pack light but sensibly:

- **Water and a snack,** because places to buy supplies are few once you are on the paths.
- **A thin rain layer.** The shop does not include a raincoat for short rentals, so bring your own if the forecast is uncertain.
- **Cash in small notes** for drinks, parking and offerings; do not count on card machines at small stalls.
- **Comfortable shoes** you can walk in on stone steps, which can be slick after rain.
- **A charged phone and power bank,** for maps and for calling the shop if needed.

If the weather turns heavy, postpone rather than push on. Wet stone paths and slick mountain roads are a poor combination.

## Getting there and back

The route is the same as for the wider Chi Linh trip: east from Long Bien on ordinary roads towards Hai Duong, then north into the heritage area. The key rule is to stay off expressways, because Law 36/2024/QH15 prohibits motorcycles and mopeds from them. ## FAQ: Con Son and Kiep Bac by motorcycle

### How far apart are Con Son and Kiep Bac?

About 5 km, according to the national tourism board.

### Which one should I visit if I only have time for one?

Con Son for forest walks and a hillside pagoda; Kiep Bac for the temple to Tran Hung Dao and the river setting.

### When are the festivals?

Con Son's runs from the 15th day of the first lunar month to the month's end; Kiep Bac's from the 16th to the 20th of the eighth lunar month. Check the official site for the current year.

### Is there an entrance fee?

Entry arrangements can change, and I could not confirm current fees, so check on the day at the gate or on the official website.

### Is Con Son in Hai Duong or Hai Phong?

Both names appear. Official sources now place it in Hai Phong city following the July 2025 merger of Hai Duong with Hai Phong.

### Can I take a passenger?
