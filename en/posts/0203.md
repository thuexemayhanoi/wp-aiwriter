A tourist on a rented 50cc bike is usually dealing with two separate insurance products, and neither protects the rider's own injuries by default. The first is the compulsory civil liability insurance that Vietnamese rules require the vehicle owner to hold, which pays third parties and passengers for damage the bike causes, not the rider and not the bike. The second is the traveller's own travel or medical policy, which may or may not cover moped riding depending on its wording. As of October 2026, the useful questions are whether the bike's compulsory certificate is valid, what it excludes, whether your own policy accepts a 50cc moped and what the rental contract says about damage and theft. This guide sets out those questions in the order to ask them and explains the reasons behind each. It is general information and not insurance advice.

## Question 1: Does this bike have a valid compulsory certificate?

Decree 67/2023/ND-CP, issued on 6 September 2023, requires owners of motor vehicles that take part in traffic in Vietnam to hold compulsory civil liability insurance, and its definitions of covered vehicles include mopeds, expressly including electric ones, in the property damage limits. The insurer issues a certificate for each vehicle to the owner, and the decree allows electronic certificates that follow the electronic transactions rules. Article 56 of the 2024 Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety, in force since 1 January 2025, adds that a driver must carry the insurance certificate, and Decree 168/2024/ND-CP sets a penalty range of VND 200,000 to 300,000 for a rider who has no valid certificate or does not carry it, under Article 18, clause 2, as reported by luatvietnam.vn. The previous range was lower.

For you as a renter, the practical consequence is simple: ask to see the certificate before you ride, and read the end date. The owner is the shop or whoever the shop rents the bike from, so the policy is in their name, not yours. A certificate that expired last month is the same as no certificate.

## Question 2: What does the compulsory cover actually pay for?

The decree defines the insured object as the owner's civil liability towards third parties and towards passengers. Article 7 says insurers must pay for non-contractual damage to health, life or property of third parties caused by the vehicle in operation, and for damage to the health or life of passengers on the vehicle. The liability limits are set per person per accident for injury and death, and per accident for property, and are different for motorcycles and mopeds than for cars. This guide does not quote the amounts, because decrees are amended and a decree dated 22 June 2026, Decree 220/2026/ND-CP, amends parts of Decree 67/2023, according to a listing on English luatvietnam; this guide has not reviewed what it changed, so check the current figures before relying on any number.

Put plainly:

- **A pedestrian or another rider you injure:** covered, up to the policy limit and subject to the exclusions.
- **Someone's parked car or shopfront you damage:** covered as property damage, up to the limit.
- **Your passenger's injury:** covered as a passenger.
- **Your own injuries as the rider:** not covered by this policy.
- **Damage to the rental bike:** not covered by this policy, so the contract decides who pays.
- **Theft of the bike:** not covered by this policy.

Our guide to [what travel insurance usually leaves out on a rental motorbike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/travel-insurance-motorbike-rental-vietnam/) explains the same split from the traveller's side.

## Question 3: What can make the insurer refuse?

Article 7 of Decree 67/2023/ND-CP lists cases in which the insurer is not liable. In the English text of the decree these include:

- intentional damage by the owner, the driver or the victim;
- a driver who intentionally flees the scene and fails to meet the owner's civil liability;
- a driver who does not meet the age condition of the road traffic law, or who has no licence, an invalid licence or one that does not suit a vehicle requiring a licence;
- indirect consequences such as loss of commercial value;
- property damage caused by a driver whose blood or breath alcohol is above the normal level set by the health ministry, or who uses banned drugs and stimulants;
- damage to property stolen or robbed in the accident;
- damage to special property such as gold, gems, valuable papers, antiques and money;
- damage caused by war, terrorism or earthquake.

Two points matter for a 50cc rider. First, a moped does not need a driving licence under the 2024 law, so the licence exclusion is aimed at vehicles that do need one, but the age condition still applies, and the minimum age to ride a moped is 16. A renter under that age is outside the cover. Second, the alcohol exclusion applies to property damage, and Vietnam's own drink-driving rules are strict, as our guide to [drink-driving rules for motorbike riders in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/drink-driving-rules-motorbike-vietnam/) explains. Whether the insurer pays in a particular case is up to the insurer and, if necessary, the courts, not this page.

## Question 4: Does my own travel policy accept a 50cc moped?

This is the question that surprises tourists most. Travel insurers write their motorbike clauses in different ways, and the common conditions include:

| Clause to look for | Why it matters on a 50cc bike |
|---|---|
| Engine size limit | Some policies cover only up to a given capacity; a 50cc is below most limits but check the wording on mopeds and scooters |
| Licence requirement | Some require a motorcycle licence even for small engines, although Vietnam does not require one for a moped |
| Helmet | Almost all require a properly fastened helmet |
| Alcohol | Any alcohol can void a claim |
| Passenger | A policy may cover only the policyholder, not a passenger |
| Activity wording | "Hazardous activities", "motorcycling" or "moped use" may be excluded or sold as an add-on |
| Country rules | The policy may say you must obey local rules, so illegal riding can void it |
| Medical evacuation | Whether it applies after a moped crash |

Our guide to [licence rules for foreigners in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbike-licence-rules-for-foreigners-in-vietnam/) explains the permits that bigger bikes require. A 50cc moped may satisfy Vietnamese rules without satisfying your insurer's, so the safest step is to email the insurer before you travel and ask: "I will ride a rented petrol moped of 50cc in Vietnam, with no motorcycle licence, wearing a helmet. Is that covered?" Keep the reply. A call centre's verbal answer is hard to prove later.

## Question 5: What does the rental contract say about damage and theft?

Because the compulsory policy ignores the bike itself, the cost of a scratched panel, a broken mirror or a stolen bike falls under the rental contract. Ask:

- Is there a stated excess or a fixed charge for damage, and where is it written?
- What happens to the cash deposit if the bike is damaged, and how is the amount decided? Our guide to [how a motorbike rental deposit works](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbike-rental-deposit-hanoi/) explains the usual logic, and Nguyen Ha states that the deposit is separate from the rent.
- Who pays for theft, and what must you do to avoid being at fault, such as locking the steering and parking in guarded places? Nguyen Ha does not provide an extra lock, so lock the steering and park in guarded places.
- Is a fall with no third party involved your cost entirely?
- Which accidents does the contract say void any protection?

Read the contract before signing. [Our explanation of the clauses in a Hanoi rental contract](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-motorbike-rental-contract-explained/) shows what each tends to mean. If the shop says "we have insurance", ask for the document and ask which risk it covers.

## Question 6: What do I do after a crash?

The sequence below is general, and the first priority is always safety and medical help.

1. **Check for injuries,** call for medical help if needed and move out of the traffic if possible.
2. **Call the shop** on 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969), open 09:00 to 21:00 daily. The owner is the one who deals with the compulsory insurer.
3. **Record the scene:** photographs of the vehicles, the plates, the road and any damage; names and phone numbers of other parties and witnesses.
4. **Do not agree to pay anything on the spot** or sign documents you cannot read without speaking to the shop and, if you are insured, your own insurer.
5. **Report to your own insurer** as your policy requires, and keep medical records and receipts.
6. **Keep the rental contract and registration photographs.**

Decree 67/2023/ND-CP says the insurance buyer or the insured must send a notice of an accident to the insurer within 5 working days, in paper or electronic form, which is why the shop needs to hear promptly. For a minor breakdown that is not an accident, see our guide to [what to do about lost keys, flat tyres and breakdowns](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-bike-breakdown-hanoi-what-to-do/).

## Extra considerations for 50cc riders

- **Passengers:** the compulsory cover includes passengers' health and life, but your travel policy may not. Our guide to riding a 50cc scooter with a passenger covers the safety side.
- **Age:** if a teenage family member will ride, the age rules matter for the cover as well as for the road rules; see our overview of [renting a 50cc scooter in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/renting-a-50cc-scooter-in-hanoi/).
- **Helmets:** the rules apply to mopeds as to motorcycles, and a missing helmet can void travel cover; one basic helmet per rider is included at Nguyen Ha.
- **Short rentals:** the compulsory certificate is annual and belongs to the bike, so it does not depend on how many days you rent.
- **Electric mopeds:** the decree names electric mopeds among covered vehicles, but your own policy may treat them differently.

## FAQ: insurance for tourists on 50cc bikes

### Is insurance compulsory for a 50cc moped?

Decree 67/2023/ND-CP covers motor vehicles including mopeds and electric mopeds, and a rider without a valid certificate faces a penalty under Decree 168/2024/ND-CP.

### Does the compulsory insurance pay for my own injuries?

No. It covers the owner's liability to third parties and passengers, not the rider.

### Does it cover damage to the rental bike?

No. Damage and theft are matters for the rental contract.

### Can my travel insurance refuse a claim on a 50cc?

Yes, if the policy excludes mopeds, requires a motorcycle licence, or you broke a condition such as helmet use or alcohol. Ask the insurer in writing.

### Do I need a certificate if I only ride in the city?

Yes. The certificate belongs to the vehicle and the duty applies wherever it takes part in traffic.

### What if the shop says insurance is included?

Ask to see the certificate and the end date, and ask what the contract says about damage, theft and your own injuries.
