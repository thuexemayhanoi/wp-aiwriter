An automatic scooter does not accelerate suddenly by itself. In nearly every case a surge comes from the right wrist, from a start sequence done in the wrong order, from a bump that jerks the grip, or from a throttle that is not returning properly. You can prevent almost all of it with four habits: test the throttle before you leave the shop, start with the throttle fully closed and a brake held, open the throttle slowly in stages rather than in one twist, and keep a relaxed wrist over bumps and out of corners. Honda's manuals say it in a sentence: twist the throttle slowly. This guide explains the situations where surges happen, what the manuals say about each, a simple practice routine, and what to do on the rare occasion the engine keeps revving when you want it to slow down.

## Why automatics can feel abrupt

On a manual or semi-automatic bike, the clutch or the gear lever sits between your wrist and the rear wheel, and a beginner learns to feather it. An automatic scooter has none of that. Honda's own comparison of scooters and motorcycles describes the typical scooter as having an automatic transmission with no clutch operation or gear shifting, which is why it is easy for a wide range of riders. The price of that simplicity is that the throttle is the main control, so the throttle needs more respect.

Three features are worth understanding:

- **The throttle is a twist grip.** Turning your wrist opens it, and anything that moves your wrist, such as a bump, a grab at the brake or a tense forearm, can open it too.
- **Small scooters are light.** A small scooter responds quickly, and a short, firm twist moves it forward faster than a new rider expects.
- **There is no gear to slip.** The response depends on the throttle alone, so it pays to learn where the scooter starts to pull by opening the throttle very slowly in a quiet place.

None of this means automatics are dangerous. It means the right wrist deserves the same care you would give a clutch.

## The moments when a surge happens

Most unexpected accelerations belong to a small set of situations. Recognising them in advance is half the cure.

| Situation | What goes wrong | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Pulling away from a stop | A full twist to "get going" | Open slowly and wait for the scooter to move before adding more |
| Rolling over a speed bump or pothole | The hand bounces and twists the grip | Relax the wrist, grip lightly and keep the forearm level |
| Leaving a corner | Throttle opened while still leaning | Honda's advice is to open the throttle slowly when the corner ends |
| Braking, then releasing | A hand that squeezes the brake may also twist the throttle | Close the throttle fully before braking, as Honda describes |
| Restarting after an Idling Stop pause | The engine restarts when the throttle is turned | Check the indicator; keep the throttle quiet until the engine runs |
| Pushing or manoeuvring with the engine on | The grip is twisted by accident | Switch off before moving the scooter by hand |
| Starting the engine | Throttle opened during start | Start with the throttle fully closed |

Of those, pulling away and bumps are the commonest. Slow, early input and a relaxed grip fix both.

## A twenty-second throttle check at the shop

The Honda Vision owner's manual includes a short throttle inspection that anyone can copy at handover, with the engine off. It asks you to check that the throttle turns smoothly from fully closed to fully open at every steering position, including full lock to each side, and that the free play at the grip flange is correct. For the Vision, the manual gives 2 to 6 mm of free play. If the throttle does not turn smoothly, closes by itself badly or has a damaged cable, it says to take the scooter to an authorised Honda dealer.

You can do the same check in four steps:

1. With the engine off, turn the throttle open and let go. It should snap back to closed without help.
2. Turn the handlebar fully left, then fully right, and repeat. It should still return smoothly in both positions.
3. Feel for a small amount of slack at the start of the twist. That is normal free play, not a fault.
4. Listen for scraping and look for a grip that sticks or a cable that looks kinked or frayed.

If anything is wrong, ask the shop to change the scooter. Other models, such as the Air Blade or the Lead, have their own figures, so ask what applies to yours. ## A calm start sequence

Honda's Vision manual sets out the sequence for starting and moving off. Followed in order, it removes most of the surprise.

1. **Close the throttle and hold the brake.** The manual says to start the engine with the throttle fully closed, and the training document says to squeeze a brake when pressing the start button.
2. **Press the start button and release it as soon as the engine runs.**
3. **If the engine does not start, do not rev it.** The manual says that if the engine has not started within about 5 seconds, turn the key off and wait 10 seconds before trying again, so that the battery voltage can recover. It also describes a gentle technique for a hard start, opening the throttle only about 3 mm beyond the free play, and it warns that revving the engine repeatedly can damage it.
4. **Raise the stand, sit from the left and keep one foot on the ground.** The manual describes mounting from the left side.

5. **Release the rear brake lock before you move.** The manual says to make sure it is fully released to prevent the rear wheel dragging.
6. **Look ahead, indicate and check for traffic.** Honda lists turn signals and a traffic check before pulling away.
7. **Open the throttle slowly.** To accelerate, increase throttle gradually, as the manual says. To slow down, release it.

If you want more detail on switches and what each one does, our scooter guides cover that in turn. Here the key point is order: throttle closed, brake held, start, then gentle input.

## Open the throttle in stages, not in one twist

A habit that prevents most surges is to treat the throttle as having three stages.

- **Stage one:** take up the free play and open a small amount until the scooter begins to roll.
- **Stage two:** hold that position and let the scooter settle at a gentle speed.
- **Stage three:** add a little more only when you are moving steadily and the road ahead is clear.

Honda's training document describes the same idea for turns: reduce speed before the bend, then increase throttle slowly as you leave it. It also says to keep a relaxed riding posture, with the shoulders loose, elbows relaxed and slightly in, and hands wrapped round the grips. A tense wrist tends to over-twist.

## Bumps, drains and rough patches

A speed bump, a drain cover or a rough patch can make your hand bounce on the grip, and if your wrist is rigid, the bounce becomes a twist. Three habits help:

- **Close the throttle before the bump.** Roll over it with the throttle closed, then open again after the rear wheel is over.
- **Keep your grip light but secure.** Your fingers should hold the grip without crushing it.
- **Keep your forearm level.** A wrist that is bent up or down is more likely to turn the throttle when the front wheel drops.

This matters most when you are loaded with a passenger or bags, because the scooter then reacts more slowly and your correction may come late.

## Idling Stop and restarts, if your scooter has it

Some Honda scooters, including versions of the Vision, Lead and Air Blade, have an Idling Stop system that switches the engine off when you stop and restarts it when you turn the throttle. The manuals warn of the following, and they are relevant to this topic:

- When the Idling Stop indicator is flashing, do not leave the scooter. If you must, turn the key off. The manual says the engine can start suddenly if the throttle is turned.
- To restart, check that the indicator is flashing and then turn the throttle. If the indicator is not flashing, the system cannot restart the engine.
- The system can be switched on or off with a switch, so you can ask the shop to show you where it is.

Whether your scooter has this system depends on the version, so ask at handover. If you do not like the idea of the engine restarting under your wrist, ask to be shown the switch and use it in a quiet place before you ride.

## If the engine keeps revving

A throttle that will not close is rare, and the manual treats it as a fault for a dealer. If it ever happens, the general advice is simple, and this is general guidance rather than a Honda quotation:

1. **Do not panic and do not twist harder.** Let go of the grip and see whether it returns.
2. **Use both brakes together.** Honda describes closing the throttle and braking with both brakes, and an engine's power is usually less than a firm brake can hold.
3. **Move to the side if you can and stop.** Turn the key off or use the engine stop switch if the scooter has one.
4. **Do not ride on.** Call the shop on 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969), open 09:00 to 21:00 daily. Our guide to [what to do about lost keys, flat tyres and breakdowns](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-bike-breakdown-hanoi-what-to-do/) explains what to expect.

## A practice drill before the city

If you are new to automatics, spend ten minutes somewhere quiet before you enter heavy traffic. A wide, low-traffic stretch near the river is a good choice, and our guide to an [easy first ride around Long Bien and the Red River](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/long-bien-red-river-first-ride/) suggests one. Then try these exercises:

- **Slow roll:** pull away with the throttle barely open, travel 20 m at walking pace and stop with both brakes.
- **Stop-start:** repeat ten times, focusing on smooth input.
- **Throttle off over a bump:** find a gentle bump and roll over it with the throttle closed.
- **Corner exit:** make a slow turn, then open the throttle only after the bend.

## FAQ: avoiding sudden acceleration

### Why does my scooter jump forward when I pull away?

Usually because the throttle was opened too far, too fast. Open it slowly until the scooter begins to roll, then add more.

### Is free play in the throttle normal?

Yes. The Honda Vision manual gives 2 to 6 mm of free play at the grip flange. A throttle that sticks or does not close needs a dealer's attention.

### Should I use the brakes while I accelerate?

No. Close the throttle and then brake. Honda's manual says to fully release the throttle and use both brakes together.

### Can the engine start suddenly with Idling Stop?

Honda's manual says that when the Idling Stop indicator is flashing, the engine can start suddenly if the throttle is turned, so turn the key off if you leave the scooter.

### What if the throttle does not return?

Let go, use both brakes, stop safely, turn the key off and call the shop. Do not ride on.

### Does a passenger make surges worse?

A passenger makes the scooter heavier and slower to respond, so late corrections are more likely. Use even smaller, earlier inputs.
