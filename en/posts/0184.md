A Cub 50cc has gears but no clutch lever. You change gear with your left foot on a shift pedal while the engine's automatic centrifugal clutch does the work a clutch lever would do on a conventional motorcycle. The rider closes the throttle, presses the pedal to select the next gear, then opens the throttle again, and the clutch re-engages by itself. A typical Cub has three or four forward gears with neutral at the bottom of the pattern. That makes it easy to learn once the rhythm is clear, and it is why the Cub has been a beginner's machine for decades. This guide explains the parts involved, the usual shift pattern, how to pull away, change up and change down, what the neutral lamp tells you, which mistakes beginners make and what to ask the shop at handover. Patterns differ between models, so always confirm with the shop on the actual bike.

## What makes a Cub different

Honda's account of the Super Cub says its automatic centrifugal clutch was designed to free riders from complicated clutch and gear-shifting operations. The idea was said to come from a wish to give delivery riders a bike they could handle with one hand free. Reference works describe the result as a clutchless, foot-shifted gearbox: the clutch is slaved to the shift lever, so that pressing the lever disengages the drive for a moment, and a centrifugal clutch engages when the engine speed rises.

Cub-style 50cc bikes in Vietnam follow the same pattern, although many are copies by other makers, and details vary. Our guide to [renting a 50cc scooter in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/renting-a-50cc-scooter-in-hanoi/) describes the Cub as a small semi-automatic with a foot shift and no clutch lever. The practical effect for a beginner is that you will not stall by releasing a clutch lever too quickly, because there isn't one. What you must learn is when to ease off the throttle and how to press the pedal firmly.

## The controls you will use

- **Right hand:** the throttle grip and the front brake lever.
- **Right foot:** the rear brake pedal.
- **Left foot:** the shift pedal, which is often pivoted so that it has a toe end at the front and a heel end at the rear.
- **Left hand:** no clutch lever. There may be switches for indicators, lights and horn.
- **Dashboard:** a neutral lamp, and on some bikes a gear position display.

Honda's Super Cub 125 owner's manual describes a four-speed gearbox with a heel-and-toe shifter, where you shift up by lifting with your toe or pressing down with your heel, and shift down by pressing the pedal down with your toe. That manual is for a 125cc model, and a 50cc may use a simpler pedal. An owner's manual for a Cub-style copy sold abroad describes the same principle in plain words: press the front of the shifting lever to select first gear, press the front again for second and so on, and to go down, press the rear of the lever. Ask the shop to show you which way the pedal works on your bike before you leave.

## Neutral, and how the pattern runs

On Honda's Super Cub, neutral sits at the bottom of the pattern, with first, second, third and, on four-speed models, fourth above it. Honda's history pages note a Cub with an N-1-2-3 pattern, so three-speed versions exist. A gear lamp on the dashboard shows neutral. The copy bike's manual notes that the neutral lamp goes out when you shift into any other gear and that the gear lamps light according to the gear selected.

The Super Cub 125's manual adds a detail worth knowing: a dash symbol appears on its gear display when the transmission was not shifted properly. If you think a gear has not engaged, the dashboard is a quick check, if the bike has one. On an older Cub with only a neutral lamp, a bike that moves off with the lamp lit is in neutral and will not drive.

Because the pattern differs by model, spend one minute at the shop with the bike on its stand. Ask a staff member to press the pedal through the gears while you watch the lamp and the pedal, so you can see how far it travels and where neutral is.

## Pulling away step by step

1. **Start in neutral.** The neutral lamp should be on. Hold the front brake.
2. **Select first gear.** With the engine at idle, press the pedal once as instructed for first. You should feel a small clunk and the lamp should go out.
3. **Check around you and signal.** Look over your shoulder, because a Cub is a small bike and drivers are not always watching for it.
4. **Open the throttle gently.** Release the brake as the engine note rises. The centrifugal clutch engages and the bike moves off.
5. **Keep the first few metres smooth.** An abrupt throttle on a 50cc makes the bike lurch.

## Changing up

For a smooth change up:

1. **Build a little speed in the current gear.** Do not change too early, or the engine will struggle, and do not rev it until it screams.
2. **Ease the throttle slightly.** You do not need to close it fully on every change; many riders just lift a little.
3. **Press the pedal firmly and decisively.** Honda's owner's manual says improper shifting can damage the engine, the transmission and the drive train, and a lazy half-press may fail to engage the gear.
4. **Open the throttle again smoothly.** The clutch takes up the drive.

Honda's Super Cub 125 manual gives recommended shift points of roughly 20 km/h from first to second, 30 km/h from second to third and 40 km/h from third to fourth. These figures are for a different model, with a different engine and gearing, so a 50cc will differ, and a moped in Vietnam has its own speed limit. The general lesson is that you change up when the engine sounds like it is working hard, not when it is revving high. Listen to the bike.

## Changing down

Changing down is the part beginners find least natural, because you do it as you slow:

1. **Close the throttle and brake gently** to slow.
2. **Press the pedal to select a lower gear** as the bike slows, as the model requires.
3. **Release the throttle smoothly** after the change, not with a jerk.

Honda's manual for the Super Cub advises avoiding hard braking and rapid down-shifts, because sudden braking can reduce stability. Taking several gears at once, or downshifting while braking hard, can make the rear wheel hop. Slow first, shift next. Where the bike has a rotary pattern, some models allow a drop from a higher gear to first in one movement when stopped, but do not assume this. ## Stopping at a junction

At a red light you have two choices. You can stay in first with your hand on the front brake, which lets you move off at once, or you can select neutral and hold the brake. Many riders prefer first gear in dense traffic in order to be ready, and neutral on a long wait. Either way, remember that when you move off after a stop you must be in first gear, not neutral, and check the lamp.

A common beginner mistake is to forget that the bike is in neutral and to open the throttle without moving, which makes a loud revving noise and leaves you stationary in the road. Look at the lamp, press the pedal to first gear and try again.

## Common beginner mistakes

| Mistake | What happens | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Half-pressing the pedal | Gear does not engage, bike revs without moving | Press firmly and fully |
| Changing up too early | Engine labours, bike hesitates | Wait for a little more speed |
| Revving too high before changing | Noisy and wasteful | Change when the engine sounds busy |
| Slamming down through the gears | Rear wheel hops, bike lurches | Slow first, then shift |
| Opening the throttle in neutral | Revving with no movement | Check the lamp, select first |
| Coasting with the engine off | Can damage the transmission, Honda warns | Keep the engine running |
| Jabbing the throttle after a change | Lurch | Open smoothly |

## When the gears do not seem right

If the pedal will not move, if the bike jumps out of gear, if the lamp does not match what the bike is doing or if you hear a loud clunk on every change, report it. These can have simple causes, such as a pedal that is too stiff or bent, or a gear that needs a firmer press. They can also point to a mechanical fault. ## FAQ: Cub 50cc gears

### Does a Cub have a clutch?

Yes, but it is automatic. There is no clutch lever. A centrifugal clutch engages as the engine speed rises, and the shift pedal operates the gearbox.

### How many gears does a Cub 50cc have?

Many Cubs have three or four forward gears, plus neutral. Check your bike, because models differ.

### Where is neutral?

On Honda's Super Cub it is at the bottom of the pattern, and a lamp shows it. Ask the shop to show you on your bike.

### Can I stall a Cub?

You can if the engine is loaded too hard in too high a gear, but with no clutch lever, you will not stall by releasing a lever too fast.

### Is a Cub harder than an automatic scooter?

It takes a few minutes to learn the rhythm. After that many beginners find it easy. If you do not want to shift, choose an automatic.

### Should I coast with the engine off?

No. Honda warns that coasting or towing for long distances with the engine off can damage the transmission.
