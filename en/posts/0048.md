The end of a motorbike rental is a short ceremony with a few moving parts: you arrive before the agreed time, the bike is inspected against the record made at pickup, the fuel is checked, any extra charges are settled and the cash deposit comes back. Done well it takes ten minutes. Done badly, usually because the pickup record was vague or the rider is in a hurry for a flight, it becomes an argument about a scratch nobody can date. This guide walks through the return step by step, using the published terms of Nguyen Ha Motorbike Rental in Long Bien as the worked example, and explains what to do when you are running late, want to return early, or come back with a problem. It is general information, not legal advice.

## Start the return at pickup

The return is decided on the day you collect the bike. The condition record made at handover, with photographs, the odometer reading and the fuel level, is the baseline against which the bike is judged when it comes back. Anything not on that record is assumed to have happened during your rental.

So the first rule of a smooth return is a thorough pickup. Walk round the bike with the staff member, photograph every panel, the mirrors, the seat, the exhaust, both wheels and the instrument cluster showing the mileage, and ask for existing marks to be written on the contract. Our [10-minute rental scooter inspection checklist](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-scooter-inspection-checklist/) gives the full routine. Keep the photos on your phone until the deposit is back in your hand; they are your evidence if a mark is disputed.

Note the exact handover time as well, because the rental day is counted from it.

## When the bike is due back

One rental day is 24 hours from handover; shorter use is charged as a full day. A bike collected at 10:00 on Tuesday for three days is due at 10:00 on Friday. Weekly and monthly packages are priced as their own products rather than day-by-day, so the end time is the time written in the contract, not an approximation.

Nguyen Ha is open from 09:00 to 21:00 every day. If your return would fall outside those hours, for example before an early flight, agree a different time when you book or at least a day ahead. Do not assume someone will be at the shop at 06:00, and do not leave the bike outside a closed shop with the key in a letterbox unless that has been explicitly agreed, because the bike remains your responsibility until it is handed over and checked.

For renters on a weekly or monthly agreement, delivery and collection within 10 km of Hoan Kiem Lake is available at VND 50,000 per trip or VND 100,000 both ways; beyond 10 km it is agreed in advance. There is no airport service. If you want the bike collected from your hotel rather than ridden back, arrange it in advance and be present for the inspection.

## Fuel: what the tank should look like

Petrol bikes at Nguyen Ha are handed over with roughly half a litre to two litres in the tank and are expected back with roughly half a litre to one litre. The shop is not asking for a full tank, and it will not refund fuel you leave in it. The only charge is for returning the bike with an empty tank, which costs VND 20,000 at Nguyen Ha. Our guide to [fuel in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/fuel-in-hanoi-petrol-stations-rental-scooter/) explains where to fill up and how far a small tank goes.

Read the gauge on the day before the return rather than on the morning itself; small scooters have an optimistic needle, and a "quarter" on a Honda Vision can be very little actual petrol. Topping up a litre at the last station before the shop takes two minutes and removes the question entirely.

Electric scooters are different: the battery level and charging arrangements are confirmed at handover, and the same level is what the shop will look at on return. Our article on [renting an electric scooter in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/renting-an-electric-scooter-in-hanoi/) covers charging expectations.

## The damage walk-round at return

At the shop, staff do the same walk-round in reverse: compare the bike against the pickup record and the photographs. Stand with them and do it together rather than handing over the key and walking off to find a taxi. Point out anything you know about, such as a scuff from a low-speed drop or a cracked mirror, before they find it. Honesty at this stage usually costs less than a discovered mark, and it keeps the conversation factual.

Three categories come up:

- **Ordinary wear.** Tyres lose tread, brake pads wear, paint dulls. A fair contract and the Civil Code's general approach treat this as the owner's cost, not the renter's.
- **New cosmetic damage.** A fresh scratch, a dented panel or a broken indicator lens. Expect a charge for parts and labour. Ask to see the part and, where possible, a repair receipt.
- **Mechanical damage or loss.** A seized engine from riding with no oil, a bent fork from a crash, a lost key or registration card. These are the expensive outcomes and the contract will normally hold you responsible.

If staff claim a mark you believe was already there, show the pickup photo. If the photo settles it, the charge should be withdrawn; if it does not, agree a reasonable figure rather than a standoff, and get the agreed amount written on the contract. Our page on [what the clauses in a Hanoi rental contract mean](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-motorbike-rental-contract-explained/) explains how damage clauses are usually worded.

## Settling extra charges

Besides fuel and damage, the items that can appear at return are late-return charges, parking or towing costs incurred during the rental, and traffic fines that arrive after the fact. Nguyen Ha's published terms are specific on the first: a return between one and five hours late is charged at VND 30,000 per hour, and from six hours late one extra rental day is charged at the bike's rate. Parking and fines during the rental are the rider's responsibility.

A traffic fine can reach the registered owner of a bike after the rental has ended. A shop that receives one can match it to the renter from the contract dates. It is worth asking at return how the shop handles a camera fine that arrives later, and giving a contact that still works after you leave Vietnam. Our article on [traffic fines that catch foreign riders out](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/traffic-fines-vietnam-foreign-riders/) explains how fines are issued.

Ask for a written note of every deduction. A receipt or a line on the contract showing the amount and reason is normal and protects both sides.

## Getting the deposit back

The cash deposit at Nguyen Ha is generally VND 2,000,000 to 5,000,000 depending on the bike and the contract, paid separately from the rent at pickup. Once the bike has been inspected, the fuel checked and any extra charges agreed, the shop settles the deposit under the contract, less any agreed deduction. If nothing is late, missing or broken, that takes a few minutes. Ask at pickup how and when the deposit is returned, so the answer is written in the contract and not assumed.

When the money is handed back, count it and keep the contract copy showing that the deposit was settled. Our guide to [how a motorbike rental deposit works in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbike-rental-deposit-hanoi/) explains why a cash deposit is the normal arrangement and why you should never leave your passport instead.

Rent paid for unused days is not refunded on early return. The deposit, by contrast, always comes back once the bike is returned in the agreed condition and any charges are settled.

## Running late

Being late is the most common return problem and the easiest to manage. Message or call the shop on 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969) before the deadline, not after it. An agreed later time is a change to the contract; ask for the new time and any extra rent to be confirmed in a chat message so both sides have a record.

If you are stuck in traffic on the way back, the late-return charge still applies by the contract, but shops that have been told in advance are generally more flexible about the first hour than shops that have been left guessing. If you cannot reach the shop at all, keep riding safely rather than speeding to beat the clock; a VND 30,000 hourly charge is a better outcome than a collision.

Returning the bike after closing time without prior agreement means the handover will happen the next morning, and the bike stays your responsibility overnight. Park it in a guarded lot and keep the ticket; our guide to [parking a motorbike in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-motorbike-in-hanoi/) explains how guarded parking works.

## Returning early

Returning early is allowed at any time within opening hours, but unused rent is not refunded. If your plans change and you want to shorten a weekly or monthly rental, speak to the shop as soon as you know; the published rule is no refund, and anything more generous is a matter of goodwill rather than entitlement. Returning a bike a day early to avoid a late return the next morning is often sensible, especially before a flight.

## Returning with a problem

Sometimes the return is complicated by something that happened on the road: a breakdown, a puncture, a drop, a lost key. The order of priorities is safety, then the bike, then the paperwork. Our article on [lost keys, flat tyres and breakdowns with a rental bike in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-bike-breakdown-hanoi-what-to-do/) explains what to do at the roadside. For the return itself:

- Tell the shop before you arrive what happened and where.
- Bring any receipts for repairs or towing you paid for; some shops reimburse agreed repairs to their own bike.
- Do not attempt to hide a drop. The scuffs are obvious to staff who see the same models every day.
- If the bike cannot be ridden back, agree collection and who pays for it before the bike is moved.

A lost key or registration card is handled as a loss: expect a charge for replacement, and ask for the amount in writing.

## A return checklist

The night before:

1. Check the fuel gauge; top up a litre if it reads near empty.
2. Confirm the return time and that it falls within 09:00–21:00, or that a different time is agreed.
3. Gather the key, the registration card, the insurance slip, the helmet and the contract.

At the shop:

1. Stand with staff for the walk-round and compare against your pickup photos.
2. Point out anything you know about.
3. Agree any deduction and get it written down.
4. Receive the deposit in cash and count it.
5. Keep your copy of the contract and your photos for a few weeks in case a camera fine surfaces.

## FAQ: returning a rental motorbike in Hanoi

### Does the tank need to be full when I return the bike?

No. At Nguyen Ha the bike is handed over with about half a litre to two litres and should come back with about half a litre to one litre. Only an empty tank is charged, at VND 20,000.

### What happens if I return the bike late?

Between one and five hours late the charge is VND 30,000 per hour; from six hours late one extra rental day is charged. Call the shop before the deadline if you can see you will be late.

### Will I get a refund if I return the bike early?

Unused rent is not refunded under Nguyen Ha's published terms. The deposit is returned in full once the bike is checked and any charges are settled.

### How is damage assessed at return?

The bike is compared against the pickup record and photographs. Ordinary wear is not charged; new damage is charged at the cost of parts and labour, which you can ask to see.

### Can I return the bike outside opening hours?

Only by prior agreement. Otherwise the handover happens when the shop opens, and the bike remains your responsibility until then.

### What if a traffic fine arrives after I have left Vietnam?

A fine can reach the shop as the registered owner after you leave, and the shop can match it to you from the contract dates. Ask how the shop handles that and leave a working contact.
