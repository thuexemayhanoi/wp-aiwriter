To start an automatic scooter, make sure the side stand is up, turn the ignition to On (or wake the smart key system), squeeze the rear brake lever with your left hand, keep the throttle fully closed and press the start button once with your right thumb, releasing it as soon as the engine fires. There is no gear lever, no clutch and no kick starter to think about. What confuses people is not the sequence but the cluster of switches around it: the ignition positions that also open the seat and fuel cap, a start button that sits near the horn and turn signal, a small Idling Stop switch that makes the engine go quiet at traffic lights, and a smart key that behaves differently from a metal key. This guide lays out the switches, the order, what each warning means and what to do when the engine will not start. It is general information, so follow the shop's demonstration for your own bike.

## Three kinds of key, one idea

Honda's Vietnamese owner's manuals describe the ignition switch with the same set of positions on the Vision, Lead and Air Blade. Rentals may have a metal key or a smart key, and the shop will tell you which:

- **Metal key.** The ignition switch has positions for Off, On and Lock. Many Honda scooters also include a position for the seat or the fuel cap, often marked SEAT or SEAT FUEL. On a Vision the manual notes that the key can be removed in Off or Lock. On some models you push the key in before turning it to unlock it.
- **Smart key (a fob).** The system lets the bike recognise a fob carried in your pocket or bag, so there is a knob, a button or a switch rather than a key to turn. Honda's Lead manual gives a working range of about two metres, and the Air Blade manual warns that anyone holding the fob within range can start the bike or unlock the seat, even through a wall or window.
- **Anti-theft alarm.** Some bikes also sound an alarm, which is cancelled by the fob or by unlocking the ignition.

Ask the shop which one you have, whether the fob needs a battery swap and how to switch the system on and off, and take a photograph of the key or fob and the way it is carried. If the bike has a smart key and the fob battery is weak, say so before you leave.

## Map of the switches

| Control | Where | What it does | Common mix-up |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ignition switch | Centre of the dashboard or under the handlebar | Off, On, Lock, plus seat or fuel cap | Turning to the seat or fuel position instead of On |
| Start button | Right grip area | Cranks the engine | Pressing it with the throttle open |
| Rear brake lever | Left handlebar | Brakes the rear wheel, and enables the starter on some models | Using the right lever instead |
| Front brake lever | Right handlebar | Brakes the front wheel | Treating it as the starter permission |
| Idling Stop switch | Handlebar switch cluster | Turns the auto-stop system on or off | Thinking the bike has stalled when it stops at lights |
| Turn signal switch | Left grip area | Left, right and cancel | Pressing the horn instead |
| Horn button | Left grip area | Horn | Hitting it at the same spot as the cancel |
| Dip switch | Left grip area | High or low beam | Leaving the beam on high |
| Rear brake lock lever | Next to the rear brake lever | Parking lock | Leaving it locked when riding off |

The exact positions differ by model, so treat the table as a checklist of things to find rather than a map. Honda's Lead manual, for example, lists the start switch, the Idling Stop switch, the high and low beam switch, the horn and the turn signal switch together on its page of handlebar switches. That is the cluster to get familiar with before you ride.

## The start-up sequence

Follow the same order every time. Honda's PCX manual gives the sequence, and the Vision manual confirms the last step.

1. **Put the scooter where you want it.** Straight, off the kerb, with space in front.
2. **Check that the side stand is up.** Many Honda scooters will not start with it down. The Air Blade manual says the engine cannot start with the side stand lowered.
3. **Wake the bike.** Turn the key to On, or switch on the smart key system and keep the fob on you.
4. **Wait for the dashboard to settle.** Watch the warning lights. Honda's Vision manual notes that the PGM-FI warning light (MIL) should go off.
Now the part that involves your hands.

5. **Squeeze the rear brake lever.** The PCX manual says the starter motor only works while the rear brake lever is squeezed and the side stand is up. Other models may differ, but squeezing the lever is a good habit anyway, because it stops the scooter from creeping.
6. **Keep the throttle completely closed.** Honda's Vision manual says to press the start button with the throttle fully closed.
7. **Press the start button.** Release it as soon as the engine starts.
8. **Let the engine settle.** Wait a few seconds before you move.

The scooter should now be idling. Keep the brake lever squeezed while you sit up, check the mirrors and look around.

## The one rule about the start button

Do not hold the button for a long time. If the engine does not start within about five seconds, turn the ignition off and wait about ten seconds before trying again, which Honda's Metropolitan manual and the BeAT manual give as a way to recover battery voltage. Repeated cranking drains the battery, so ask the shop rather than trying again and again.

## Idling Stop: when a running engine goes quiet

Many Honda scooters have an Idling Stop system, and it is a common source of worry. It is designed to cut fuel use and noise by stopping the engine briefly when you halt, for example at a red light, and to restart it when you open the throttle. The Vision manual describes a switch with two positions, IDLING STOP and IDLING.

What riders should know:

- **It does not mean the bike has died.** The dashboard indicator shows the system is active.
- **Restart is simple.** Open the throttle gently and the engine starts again. Do not press the start button.
- **It only works under conditions.** The Air Blade manual lists them: side stand up, engine started with the start button, engine warm enough and a speed above 10 km/h.
- **It can be switched off.** The switch to the IDLING position disables it. Honda notes that with a weak battery, you should switch it off.
- **Do not drop the side stand while it is stopped.** The PCX manual warns that the engine will not restart that way.

If you would rather the engine kept running at every stop, ask the shop how to turn the system off, and set it before you leave. Turning it off is a convenience for a nervous first-timer, not a requirement.

## Smart key specifics

If your bike has a smart key, these points reduce confusion:

- **Carry the fob close.** The Lead manual gives a working range of about two metres.
- **Do not leave it on the bike.** Anyone within range can start it, and Honda says this applies even through a wall.
- **Know the off switch.** Many bikes have a way to switch the system off for parking, such as the ignition in Lock, and to turn it on again.
- **Learn the alarm.** Pressing the fob button cancels the alarm on models that have one.
- **Ask about the battery.** A fob with a weak battery may fail to start the bike.

The seat, fuel cap and steering lock can all be controlled by the same knob. A first test in the shop, with staff beside you, is to unlock the seat, close it, lock the steering and start the bike once.

## If the engine will not start

Work through this list calmly, and stop if you are unsure:

| Check | Why |
|---|---|
| Side stand up? | Many scooters cut the engine with it down |
| Ignition on, not at seat, fuel or lock? | The wrong position leaves the system dead |
| Smart key fob with you and switched on? | The bike cannot read a fob left behind |
| Rear brake lever squeezed? | Some scooters need this to start |
| Throttle fully closed? | The throttle open prevents a normal start |
| Rear brake lock released? | A locked brake makes the rear wheel drag |
| Fuel in the tank? | Check the gauge |
| Dashboard lights as expected? | A warning light needs the shop |

Honda also describes a procedure for a flooded or hard-to-start engine, which involves the centre stand and a specific throttle opening. The Lead manual says to stand the bike on its centre stand, squeeze the rear brake lever, open the throttle fully and press the start button for about five seconds. That is a manual-based procedure rather than an everyday habit, so ask the shop first and do not experiment. ## Switching off and locking

When you stop, turn the ignition to Off. If you want the steering secured, turn the handlebar fully left and turn the ignition to Lock, as Honda's Vision manual describes, then take the key or fob. Our shop does not provide an extra lock, so the advice is to lock the steering and park in guarded places. If you are stopped at the roadside and want the engine off briefly, use the ignition rather than the horn or a button you are unsure of.

## FAQ: starting an automatic scooter

### Do I need to squeeze the brake to start?

On some models, yes. The Honda PCX manual says the starter only works with the rear brake lever squeezed and the side stand up. It is a good habit anyway.

### Why does the engine stop when I stop at a light?

It may be the Idling Stop system. Open the throttle and it restarts.

### Why will the engine not start when I press the button?

Check the side stand, the ignition position, the fob, the brake lever and the throttle, then ask the shop.

### Is there a kick starter?

Not on a typical modern automatic scooter. Our guide to the [kick starter on an older rental bike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-use-the-kick-starter-on-an-older-rental-motorbike/) covers machines that have one.

### Can I leave the fob in my bag?

Yes, but keep it with you and away from the bike when parked.

### How long should I hold the start button?

Only until the engine starts. If it does not start within about five seconds, stop and wait.
