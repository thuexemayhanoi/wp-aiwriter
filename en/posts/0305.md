To reach the Thang Long Water Puppet Theatre on a rental bike, aim for the streets around the north-east corner of Hoan Kiem Lake, leave the bike in a guarded lot a few minutes away and walk to 57B Dinh Tien Hoang Street, because from Friday evening to Sunday night the streets around the lake close to vehicles and the theatre's own street sits inside that area. The theatre states that it performs every day of the year, with five to seven shows daily, and a show lasts around 50 minutes. The practical job is therefore to pick a show, plan parking before the lake closure begins and think about how you will get the bike home after the final curtain. This guide takes that in order.

## The address and what is around it

The theatre's own website gives its address as 57B Dinh Tien Hoang, Hoan Kiem, Hanoi, and describes itself as the only theatre in Asia performing water puppetry continuously 365 days a year, with five to seven performances a day. Vietnam Airlines lists the same address and gives the length of a performance as 50 minutes. Dinh Tien Hoang is the street along the eastern side of Hoan Kiem Lake, so you are looking for the lakeside building rather than a side lane. One travel guide warns that a few other venues use similar names, so search a map app for the street address and not just the words water puppet.

From a rider's point of view, you are heading into one of the most heavily managed streets in the city. Our [Hoan Kiem area page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hoan-kiem/) gives the neighbourhood context.

## Check the weekend lake closure first

The streets around Hoan Kiem Lake have operated as a walking space at weekends since 2016. The national tourism administration's English notice of the opening lists Dinh Tien Hoang among the streets in the pedestrian zone, and the Hanoi culture department says the lake area runs from 19:00 on Friday to 24:00 on Sunday. VnExpress's later guide repeats that pattern, and its description of the 2026 pilot says motorbikes and cars are barred from the Hoan Kiem Lake pedestrian area from 19:00 to midnight on Fridays, Saturdays and Sundays.

The two descriptions differ slightly in what they say about Saturday and Sunday daytime. To be safe, plan as though Dinh Tien Hoang is not open to your bike from Friday evening until Sunday night, and look for barriers, signs and wardens before you commit to a route. Our guide to [where to park near Hoan Kiem Lake](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/where-to-park-a-scooter-near-hoan-kiem-lake/) explains the walking-street logic in more detail.

For a weekday visit none of this applies in the same way, but streets around the lake are still busy, and many are one-way.

## Pick a show before you pick a route

Show times matter because they decide when you need to be parked and when you will collect the bike.

- The theatre says there are five to seven performances daily.
- One guide lists seven slots on Monday to Saturday between 13:45 and 21:15, and an extra Sunday morning show.
- Other guides list five slots between 15:00 and 20:00.
- The theatre's site has a ticket-booking page, and one travel guide says the schedule is updated weekly.

Because these sources disagree and the schedule can change, use the theatre's own booking page for the date you want and do not rely on a printed itinerary. A show that ends at 20:00 or 21:15 is the sort of thing to take into account when you plan your parking lot's closing time and the shop's opening hours, which run from 09:00 to 21:00 daily.

Ticket tiers vary by seating category. This guide does not quote prices; the theatre's booking page shows what is available for your date.

## Planning your arrival

Work backwards from the show:

1. **Show time.** Choose the performance.
2. **Seating time.** Plan to be at the door about 20 to 30 minutes before, as one guide suggests for photography and seating. Another guide notes that latecomers can disturb others once the pool performance is under way.
3. **Walk from the lot.** Allow five to ten minutes on foot, more on a busy evening.
4. **Parking time.** Allow at least a quarter of an hour to find a spot.
5. **Ride time.** Add the ride in Hanoi traffic, which is slower than the map suggests at rush hour.

If you are coming from the east bank of the Red River, set your map app to motorbike mode, check the route against the one-way system and allow extra time at the river crossings and the approaches to the Old Quarter. Our guide to [crossing Hanoi intersections](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/crossing-hanoi-intersections/) is worth a read before the first big junction.

## Where to leave the bike

You should not expect to stop at the theatre's door. Dinh Tien Hoang is a lake-side street, and on weekends it is not for bikes. Guides to the walking street name parking on streets such as Hai Ba Trung, Ly Thai To, Ngo Quyen, Quang Trung and Le Phung Hieu, which lie a short walk from the lake. These come from tourism guides rather than an official list, so treat them as areas to look in, not guaranteed spaces.

Under Article 18 of Law 36/2024/QH15 on road traffic order and safety, in force since 1 January 2025, a rider who parks in a city street must keep close to the right-hand kerb in the direction of travel, no more than 0.25 metres away, without obstructing pedestrians or traffic. The same article forbids stopping or parking on pedestrian crossings, at pick-up and drop-off points and within 5 metres of a junction. That matters at the lake, where crossings and junctions are frequent.

A few practical points:

- Use a guarded lot or ticketed parking point. Our guide to [parking a motorbike in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-motorbike-in-hanoi/) explains tickets and attendants.
- Ask what time the lot closes before you leave the bike for an evening show.
- Keep the ticket safe, lock the steering and take the key.
- Photograph the bike and the lot sign, as in our [pre-ride inspection routine](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-scooter-inspection-checklist/).

## The return trip: collecting the bike after dark

The last show can end late, and that changes your ride home. Hanoi at night has different hazards from daytime, with lower visibility, tired riders and drivers, and wet roads in the rainy season. Read our guide to [riding at night in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-at-night-in-hanoi/) before an evening plan, check that the bike's lights work before you leave the shop, and consider whether an earlier performance suits you better.

If your final show finishes during the Friday to Sunday restriction window, the lake streets will be pedestrian, so walk to the lot rather than trying to ride to the theatre door to meet someone. If you are tired or unsure, leave the bike in the lot overnight only if the lot allows it, and check the rental terms first. Do not leave it on the street. If the bike must be back at a particular time, remember the shop's hours and that late-return charges apply, which are explained on the [prices page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/prices/).

## Rain, helmets and what to wear

The rental includes one basic helmet per rider. It does not include a raincoat for short rentals, so bring your own in the wetter months, and read our guide to [riding in Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/). Helmet rules are summarised in our guide to [helmets in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/helmet-rules-in-vietnam/).

A theatre visit is not a hiking trip, but the walk from a lot can be longer than the map suggests. Wear shoes you can walk in, and carry the helmet by its strap if the theatre will not store it. If your seat is in a small auditorium, a bulky bag on the floor is awkward, so leave non-essentials in the bike's luggage space only if the lot is secure, and carry valuables yourself.

## Making an evening of it

The lake is one of the most pleasant places in the city for a walk before or after a show. Vietnam Airlines notes that the theatre is within walking distance of other landmarks such as the Opera House, and Ngoc Son Temple sits on the lake itself. You might combine the show with a meal from our [street food loop](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-street-food-loop-by-scooter/), and our [guides index](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/guides/) lists more routes. If you intend to see two attractions in one evening, plan the order around the vehicle restriction and the lot's closing time.

## Alternatives if the bike is a hassle

If the weekend closure makes the bike awkward, ride to a lot well outside the lake area and walk or use a taxi for the last stretch. On Friday to Sunday evenings the simplest plan is to leave the scooter parked and travel on foot. For a daytime or weekday visit the bike is more straightforward.

## FAQ: reaching the Water Puppet Theatre by bike

### Where exactly is the theatre?

At 57B Dinh Tien Hoang Street, Hoan Kiem, next to Hoan Kiem Lake, according to the theatre's website and Vietnam Airlines.

### Can I ride right up to the door?

Not on weekend evenings, when the streets around the lake close to vehicles. Even on weekdays you should expect to park nearby and walk.

### How long is the show?

Vietnam Airlines gives 50 minutes. Plan about an hour including seating.

### How many shows are there per day?

The theatre says five to seven. Other guides list different time slots, so use the theatre's booking page.

### Do I need to book ahead?

The theatre offers online booking, and guides recommend it for busy dates. Availability is not something the bike shop can promise.

### Is there guaranteed parking?

No. Use a guarded lot and ask about closing time.
