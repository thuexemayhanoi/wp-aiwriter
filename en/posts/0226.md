Choose an electric bicycle if your riding will be short, slow and local, such as a loop around West Lake or an easy ride along the Red River, and you like the idea of pedals as a back-up. Choose an electric scooter if you want to cover more ground, keep up with Hanoi's traffic, carry a bag or a passenger, or ride for several days without thinking about effort. The two are different vehicles in law and in the saddle, not two sizes of the same thing. This guide compares them on the points that decide a rental, from speed and range to weight, charging and cost, and ends with a checklist of questions to put to the shop before you pick one.

## What the two vehicles actually are

Vietnam sorts electric two-wheelers by pedals, motor power and top speed. A VnExpress International explainer on the national technical standards says an electric bicycle must have pedals like an ordinary bicycle, a top speed of 25 km/h, a motor of no more than 250 W and a weight under 40 kg. An electric motorbike, by contrast, has no pedals, a motor of up to 4 kW and a top speed of up to 50 km/h, and is built more like an automatic scooter.

| | Electric bicycle | Electric scooter (moped class) |
|---|---|---|
| Pedals | Yes | No |
| Motor | Up to 250 W | Up to 4 kW |
| Top speed | About 25 km/h | Up to 50 km/h by design |
| Typical weight | Under 40 kg | Roughly 100 kg with battery (examples below) |
| Feel in traffic | Slower than the flow | Close to the flow in the city |
| Fits | One rider | Often one or two, ask the shop |

Nguyen Ha's electric group covers three kinds of vehicle: electric bicycles, 50cc-equivalent electric scooters and some VinFast electric motorbikes. The bike you receive is confirmed when you book. A VinFast model that goes faster than 50 km/h is a motorcycle in law, which changes the licence you need, so our guide to [electric bikes in Hanoi: rules, speed limits and where you can ride](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/electric-bikes-in-hanoi-rules/) is the place to check the class before you commit. Sources describe the licence position for electric mopeds differently, so ask the shop to confirm it for the exact bike.

## Ten questions that settle the choice

Answer these honestly and the choice usually makes itself.

| Question | Points to bicycle | Points to scooter |
|---|---|---|
| How far will you ride in a day? | Under about 20 km, in short hops | More than that, or an uncertain distance |
| Will you carry a passenger? | No | Yes, if the model allows it |
| Do you have luggage? | A small bag | A day bag or shopping |
| Are you crossing bridges and long straight roads? | Not often | Yes |
| Do you want exercise? | Yes, with assist | No |
| Do you want the bike indoors at night? | Yes, it is lighter | Hard to carry |
| Are you nervous in traffic? | A slow bike can feel safer on quiet streets | A faster bike can feel safer on busy ones |
| How long is your stay? | A few days | A week or a month |
| Will your hotel allow charging? | Easier: a smaller battery | Needs a socket and several hours |
| Do you want to leave the city? | No | Short trips only, if the shop agrees |

The table is a guide, not a rule. The row that most often changes the answer is distance, which the next section covers.

## Range: real numbers and why they differ

Range is where the two vehicles are hardest to compare, because makers measure it in different ways.

For electric bicycles, one Hanoi e-bike operator states that its own bikes travel up to 80 km with pedal assist or 60 km using the throttle only. That is a claim about that company's bikes, not about a rental at Nguyen Ha, and it depends on the rider's weight, the terrain and how much you pedal.

For scooters, the published figures come from VinFast's test at a constant 30 km/h. They are not for the rental bike you will ride, but they show the scale of the difference between models:

- **VinFast Feliz S:** a 3.5 kWh battery and a quoted range of about 198 km, with a top speed of 78 km/h.
- **VinFast Feliz Neo:** a 2.0 kWh battery and about 114 km, with a top speed of 60 km/h.
- **VinFast Evo with a single 1.5 kWh battery:** about 85 km, per VinFast's own specification page.

Real city riding gives less than the test figure, because stop-and-go traffic, a passenger and hills all use energy. For a day of sightseeing, the point is to choose a vehicle with comfortable headroom, not one that will be at 10 percent by the afternoon. If you only plan 15 to 25 km of easy riding, either vehicle has plenty. If you plan a long loop, ask the shop for the real-world range of the specific bike and add a margin.

## Speed, flow and where you sit in traffic

A bicycle limited to 25 km/h is slower than most of the traffic around it. On quiet lakeside roads and in the early morning that does not matter, and many riders enjoy it. On a main road in the afternoon, you will be overtaken constantly, and you need to ride at the right edge of the flow and keep your line.

A scooter limited to a design speed of 49 or 50 km/h can keep up with most city traffic, so you are part of the stream, not an obstacle in it. That makes some riders feel safer and others more exposed. Our guide to [crossing Hanoi intersections](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/crossing-hanoi-intersections/) explains how traffic flows and how to merge, and it applies to either vehicle. For the narrow streets in the centre, our guide to [riding in Hanoi's Old Quarter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-old-quarter/) explains why a slow and light vehicle can be an advantage, and when walking is the better choice.

## Weight, parking and handling

Weight is the quiet difference. An electric bicycle under 40 kg can be wheeled through a hotel lobby, lifted a step and parked inside a narrow hallway. A scooter cannot. The published weights of VinFast scooters, including the battery, are about 99 kg for the Feliz Neo and 110 kg for the Feliz S, which means you park it on its stand and need a guarded place to leave it.

Weight also affects handling. A heavier scooter feels planted at speed but needs care when pushing it backwards or on a slope. A bicycle is easy to turn and manoeuvre, but its small wheels and light frame make potholes and rough patches more noticeable. Neither is difficult for a competent rider, but a new rider should try pushing and turning the bike at the shop before riding away. Our guide to [parking a motorbike in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-motorbike-in-hanoi/) explains guarded lots and the costs of parking badly.

## Charging and where the battery lives

Both vehicles need a plug. The difference is the time and the place.

Published full-charge times for VinFast scooters are about 6 hours for the Feliz S, about 5 hours 20 minutes for the Feliz Neo and about 4.5 hours for a single-battery Evo. A smaller e-bike battery typically charges faster, in a few hours, but ask the shop for the real figure for the bike you receive. That means the scooter should be charged when you are present, and not overnight if your hotel advises against it. Our guide to [charging an electric scooter in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/charging-an-electric-scooter-in-hanoi/) explains the options, and a bicycle usually fits more easily into a hotel's arrangements because its battery is smaller and may be removable.

Nguyen Ha confirms the battery level and the charging arrangement at handover for every electric model, so photograph the display at pickup and again at return.

## Which one for which traveller

- **A nervous first-timer who wants a quiet start:** an electric bicycle on lakeside roads and the east bank, such as the easy route in our guide to [Long Bien and the Red River](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/long-bien-red-river-first-ride/).
- **A couple sightseeing across the city:** a scooter, if the model allows a passenger, because of speed and carrying capacity.
- **A solo traveller staying a month:** a scooter for range, or a bicycle if your daily distance is short and you value exercise.
- **Someone who wants to ride out to a nearby town:** probably neither. Ask the shop first whether an electric rental can leave the city, and read [can you take a Hanoi rental motorbike out of the city](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/take-rental-motorbike-out-of-hanoi/).
- **A rider who wants gears and a clutch:** neither. See the [motorbikes page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbikes/) for petrol options.

## Before you ride away on either

1. **Ask which class the vehicle is** and whether pedals, a number plate and a speed limiter are fitted.
2. **Read the battery level** and photograph the display.
3. **Ask how far it will really go** with your weight and your plan.
4. **Ask where to charge** and how long it takes.
5. **Test the brakes** at walking speed, both front and rear.
6. **Check the helmet,** which is included free, one basic helmet per rider.
7. **Check the lights and horn,** especially on a quiet vehicle that pedestrians will not hear coming.

## FAQ: electric bicycle or scooter

### Which is easier for a first-time rider in Hanoi?

An electric bicycle is slower and lighter, which suits quiet streets and a gentle start. A scooter is closer to the flow of traffic, which suits busy roads. Try the shop's forecourt before you decide.

### Do I need a helmet on an electric bicycle?

Yes. A helmet is expected on an electric bicycle, as on a scooter, and the shop includes one basic helmet per rider.

### Which goes further on one charge?

It depends on the model. Published test figures for VinFast scooters range from about 85 km to about 198 km, and one Hanoi e-bike operator quotes up to 80 km with pedal assist. Real-world figures are lower, so ask the shop.

### Can I carry a passenger?

An electric bicycle is built for one rider. A scooter may carry two if the model and the shop allow it, so confirm before you go.

### Which is cheaper to rent?

The shop publishes weekly and monthly electric rates and asks you to enquire for a day rate. Ask for the rate for the exact bike.
