On a 50cc moped rental in Hanoi you carry two sets of papers: your own and the bike's. Your own are an identity document such as your passport, the rental contract and, if you have them, your home licence and International Driving Permit. The bike's are its registration certificate and its compulsory vehicle liability insurance certificate, which Law 36/2024/QH15 lists among the papers a driver must carry. What a moped rider does not need, as of October 2026, is a driving licence, because the law exempts mopeds from the licence requirement. This guide goes through each document, who normally holds it, how to check that it belongs to the bike you are riding, how to store it and what to do at the handover, at a roadside check and on return.

## Two groups of documents

It helps to sort the papers by whose they are.

| Group | What it includes | Who normally holds it |
|---|---|---|
| Yours | Passport or other identity document, the rental contract, optional home licence and IDP | You |
| The bike's | Registration certificate, compulsory insurance certificate, any inspection certificate that applies | The shop provides it with the bike |
| The rental record | Receipt for the deposit, handover photographs, the shop's phone number | You and the shop |

The distinction matters because visitors often assume the whole burden is theirs. For the bike's papers, your job is to check that they exist and match the machine, not to produce them yourself.

## What the law lists for a driver

Article 56 of Law 36/2024/QH15 says that a driver on the road must carry the vehicle's registration certificate, a licence suited to the vehicle, the technical safety and environmental inspection certificate where one applies and the certificate of compulsory civil liability insurance for motor vehicle owners. The same article exempts moped riders from the licence requirement and asks them instead to know the traffic rules, have the skill to control the vehicle and meet the age and health conditions.

Two readings follow. First, the licence is the one item a moped rider can leave out. Second, the vehicle papers are the ones the law names for everyone, so they should travel with the bike. How strictly each paper is checked on a small moped at a roadside stop is a practical question that a shop can answer from experience. If a driver's papers have been integrated into an electronic identity account, the same article allows them to be shown that way, which is a convenience mainly for residents.

## Your identity document

The rental contract needs an identity document. Nguyen Ha Motorbike Rental checks and photographs identity documents and does not keep the originals, according to the shop's published practice. Ask at booking which document the shop wants and whether a copy is acceptable.

For carrying it on the road, a visitor has two sensible options: a passport carried in a waterproof pouch, or a clear copy plus a photograph on the phone while the original stays in the hotel safe. Which of those the shop and your own risk tolerance prefer is your decision. Whatever you choose, keep the rental contract with it so that you can show who is responsible for the bike.

## The bike's papers: what to look at before you ride

At handover, ask to see the registration certificate and the insurance certificate, and check four things:

1. **The plate number matches** the number on the bike.
2. **The engine size** on the registration is 50 cm3 or less, or the electric motor is within the moped limit. This confirms the bike is genuinely a moped. A larger engine would make it a motorcycle, and a licence would then be needed.
3. **The insurance certificate is in date.** Ask the shop to show the period it covers.
4. **The owner named on the papers** is the shop or the person you are dealing with.

Then photograph the papers, the plate and the bike. If the shop wishes to keep the originals with the bike, ask for copies to be placed in the storage box. ## Optional: your home licence and IDP

You do not need a licence for a genuine moped, but carrying one does no harm. If you hold a motorcycle licence and a 1968 Vienna Convention permit, bring both. They are useful if you later decide to rent a larger scooter, and they answer questions if a shop or a checkpoint asks. Our guide to [what documents to carry when riding in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/documents-to-carry-riding-in-vietnam/) covers the full set for larger motorbikes, and our guide to [licence rules for foreigners](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbike-licence-rules-for-foreigners-in-vietnam/) explains which permits Vietnam accepts.

Do not assume an IDP can stand in for a licence you do not hold. An IDP reflects the categories on the home licence it accompanies, and carrying a permit that does not match the bike adds nothing.

## How to carry and store everything

- **Use a waterproof pouch or a zipped pocket.** Hanoi rain arrives suddenly, and wet paper becomes illegible.
- **Keep photographs on your phone** of the passport photo page, the contract, the bike's papers and the plate, in a folder you can open without mobile data.
- **Do not leave papers in the under-seat box** of a parked bike. Take them with you.
- **Store the originals you are not carrying** in the hotel safe.
- **Write down the shop's number** on paper as well as in your phone. Nguyen Ha's is 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969), open 09:00 to 21:00 daily.

## At the shop, at a roadside stop and on return

**At the shop.** Show your identity document, sign the contract, check the bike's papers, photograph everything and keep a copy of the contract. Take the helmet the shop provides and check that it fits.

**At a roadside stop.** Pull over where you are directed, keep calm and have the papers ready. Show the contract, your identity document and the bike's papers. If you are asked for a licence you are not required to hold on a moped, say politely that the bike is a 50cc moped and show the registration, which records the engine size. Do not argue at the roadside. Call the shop if the situation is unclear.

**On return.** Hand back the bike and any copies of its papers the shop gave you, and get back your deposit and any document the shop was holding. Our guide to [returning a rental motorbike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/returning-your-rental-motorbike-hanoi/) covers the fuel, damage and deposit checks.

## If a paper is missing or looks wrong

Do not ride away and hope for the best. A few common problems and sensible responses:

- **No registration certificate with the bike.** Ask the shop to produce it or a copy before you leave. A bike without its papers is a problem for the owner first, but it becomes yours the moment you are stopped.
- **The plate or engine number does not match the paper.** Point it out politely and ask for another bike or a corrected document. Do not accept an explanation you cannot check.
- **The insurance certificate has expired.** Ask for a current one. Compulsory cover that has lapsed protects nobody.
- **The engine size is not stated or is above 50 cm3.** The bike may not be a moped. Ask what class it belongs to, and if you hold no licence, ask for a bike that clearly is a moped.
- **Different names on different papers.** This can be innocent, for example when a shop rents a bike registered to a family member, but ask the shop to explain it in writing in the contract.

A shop that welcomes these questions is usually a shop that keeps its papers in order. A shop that becomes evasive has told you something useful.

## A short checklist

- Passport or identity document, or the arrangement agreed with the shop
- Rental contract and deposit receipt
- Registration certificate and compulsory insurance certificate for the bike, or copies
- Optional: home licence and 1968 IDP
- Phone with photographs of all the above
- Shop's phone number, written down
- Helmet, supplied with the bike

## FAQ: documents for a 50cc rental

### Do I need a driving licence for a 50cc moped?

Under Law 36/2024/QH15 the licence requirement does not apply to moped riders, who must be at least 16, know the traffic rules and be able to control the vehicle. Individual shops can set their own conditions, so ask when you book.

### Which of the bike's papers should be with it?

The registration certificate and the compulsory civil liability insurance certificate, plus an inspection certificate where one applies. The law lists these for drivers in general, and the shop should be able to show you the ones for your bike.

### Should I carry my original passport or a copy?

Either can work, depending on the shop's policy and your comfort. Ask the shop what it accepts, and keep a photograph of the photo page on your phone in either case.

### What if I am stopped and asked for a licence?

Explain that the bike is a 50cc moped, show the registration and the contract and call the shop. Keep the exchange calm and polite.

### Are the documents different for an electric scooter?

If the scooter falls within the moped definition, the licence exemption is the same. Check the motor rating and design speed, and read our guide to [electric bikes in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/electric-bikes-in-hanoi-rules/).
