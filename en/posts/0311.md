You can ride a scooter on the Red River dyke roads in Long Bien, because many of them work as ordinary public roads, but they behave differently from city streets. The top of a dyke is narrow and often raised above the fields and houses beside it, trucks and buses use it, and the law lets the authorities close or restrict it when the river is high or the embankment is damaged. The practical rules are simple: obey the weight and no-entry signs, stay off the dyke slopes and the riverbed side, give heavy vehicles room, and skip the route in a flood warning, in heavy rain or after dark if you are new to it. This guide explains what a dyke is, which dyke roads a renter from Long Bien is likely to meet, what Vietnamese dyke law says, and how to ride them calmly.

## What a dyke road is, and why it matters to a rider

The Red River is held back through Hanoi by earth embankments, called dykes, that protect the city and its farmland from floods. Vietnam's Law on Dykes defines a dyke as a structure that blocks river flood water and treats the dyke, its revetments, the culverts through it and its supporting works as one protected system. The consolidated text of the law was published in 2025 on the thuvienphapluat.vn legal database.

That has two consequences for riders. First, the dyke is infrastructure with its own rules, so what is acceptable on a street may not be acceptable here. Second, the dyke carries a road in many places because the crest is a convenient raised line. Engineering standards say that when a dyke crest is used as a road, a sign must state the limit for motor vehicles, so the dyke is not damaged or destabilised. The Hanoi People's Committee has also told the transport department to put up weight-limit signs on all dyke routes and to stop overloaded vehicles.

You will not be in danger just because you are on a dyke. You will be on a road that is shared with heavy traffic, in a place where the authorities may close access for reasons you cannot see from the saddle.

## The dyke roads a Long Bien rider is likely to meet

Nguyen Ha Motorbike Rental is in Bo De Ward, Long Bien, on the east bank of the Red River, so the dyke is part of the local landscape. Several routes involve it.

- **The approach to Chuong Duong Bridge.** News reports during the 2025 closure of Long Bien Bridge describe vehicles packed along Xuan Quan Dyke, the main approach to Chuong Duong Bridge, and jams on the Ngoc Thuy dyke area stretching almost two kilometres. These are also the diversion roads that carry riders when Long Bien Bridge is closed.

- **The riverside route to Bat Trang.** Travel guides to the pottery village tell riders to cross Chuong Duong or Vinh Tuy Bridge and then follow the Red River dyke road south. One guide describes the route as Chuong Duong Bridge, Hong Tien Street, the Long Bien-Xuan Quan dyke road and then Giang Cao Road. Our [guide to Gia Lam and Bat Trang by scooter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/gia-lam-east-bank-bat-trang-by-scooter/) covers the destination.

- **Local trips along the river.** The [Long Bien area page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/long-bien/) lists places nearby, and the [first-ride guide to Long Bien and the Red River](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/long-bien-red-river-first-ride/) describes a gentle introduction to the east bank.

A map app will usually treat a dyke road as any other road. It will not tell you about a weight limit, a flood closure or the day's heavy-truck traffic. Look at the signs.

## What the dyke law says that matters to you

Vietnam's Law on Dykes lists acts that are banned. Two are relevant to visitors:

- **Using a motor vehicle over the permitted weight on a dyke** is prohibited. This is aimed at trucks, not scooters, but it explains why the crest has signs and sometimes barriers.
- **Using a motor vehicle on a dyke when a no-entry sign is up** is prohibited when the dyke has an incident or there is a flood, storm or high water, with exceptions for inspection vehicles, dyke defence, defence and security, ambulances and fire engines.

The same law gives the ministry in charge of agriculture the job of setting the weight limits and the sign designs. In practice you will see round weight-limit signs and, when needed, barriers or officers at an access point. If you meet one, treat it as a legal order, not a suggestion. Our [guide to Vietnam's traffic fines for foreign riders](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/traffic-fines-vietnam-foreign-riders/) explains what happens when a rider ignores a sign.

The 2024 Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety also applies on a dyke road. Slower vehicles keep to the right, reserved lanes stay reserved, and you must stop and park only where you do not endanger others and close to the right-hand edge. It also bars stopping on a bend or near the top of a rise where your bike cannot be seen. On a dyke crest, those two rules rule out most casual photo stops.

## Truck traffic, dust and the surface

Reports about Hanoi dykes mention overloaded trucks running day and night, sand and building-material storage near the embankment, and damage to the crest. A 2018 report on dykes in Hanoi described these as long-running problems, and the city's 2015 order to the police and transport department was to put up more weight-limit signs and stop overloaded vehicles. The detail is dated, but it tells you what the traffic can be like: heavy, slow and sometimes dusty.

What that means in practice:

- **Give trucks room.** A loaded truck throws up grit, takes the middle of a narrow crest and needs a long distance to stop.
- **Do not overtake on a blind rise or a bend.** The crest can hide an oncoming vehicle.
- **Watch the edge.** Gravel, mud tracked from side roads and soft shoulders are common at the sides of a raised road, and a wheel off the edge can be hard to recover on a small bike.
- **Expect sudden crossings.** Local roads, farm tracks and ramps join the dyke at angles, and residents on bikes may enter without stopping.
- **Protect your eyes and lungs.** One guide to the Bat Trang route warns that the road can be dusty and recommends sunscreen and protective clothing.

## Flood season: when the dyke is not a place to be

The Red River's high-water period runs through the summer and autumn. In September 2024, Hanoi's People's Committee sent an urgent order to focus on flood response and dyke safety after the river level at Chem rose to a level the newspaper described as unprecedented. That was an unusual event, but it shows how fast conditions can change.

Rules of thumb for the season:

1. **Check the weather and flood notices before you go.** If a warning is in force, choose a different route.
2. **Obey every no-entry sign and barrier.** The law restricts motor vehicles on a dyke during a flood or incident, and the sign may be there for a reason you cannot see.
3. **Do not ride onto the riverbed side or the mid-river sandbars.** A tourism guide to Long Bien Bridge advises against the island paths in the wet season or after rain, and against being near the bridge in typhoon conditions.
4. **Do not drive through standing water.** See our guide to [riding through standing water](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-a-manual-motorbike-through-standing-water/).
5. **If it starts to rain hard, stop in a safe place and wait.** Our [guide to riding in Hanoi's rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) covers the basics.

## Night, light and visibility

Dyke roads outside built-up areas are often less well lit than city streets and sit above the land beside them, so a rider can lose sight of the edge. A scooter's headlight may not show a pothole in time. Unless you know the route, treat the dykes as a daylight option. Our [night-riding guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-at-night-in-hanoi/) explains the problems of low visibility and how to plan around them.

## When to skip the dyke

Skip it if there is a flood or storm warning, if the weather is wet and you are a beginner, if you will be riding after dark or if you are carrying a heavy load. There is almost always an alternative on the city streets of Long Bien, even if it is slower. ## FAQ about riding near the Red River dykes

### Can tourists ride on the Red River dyke roads in Long Bien?

Yes, where the road is open and signed for motor vehicles. Follow weight-limit and no-entry signs, and expect truck traffic.

### Why would a dyke be closed to motorbikes?

The Law on Dykes allows restrictions on motor vehicles using a dyke when there is a flood, storm or an incident with the dyke, with exceptions for inspection and emergency vehicles. Obey the sign.

### Is it safe to stop on the dyke for a view of the river?

Only where there is a safe pull-off and you are not blocking the road, on a bend or on a rise. The road law does not allow stopping where you endanger others, and the crest is narrow, so it is often better to keep going to a proper stop.

### Is the dyke the best route to Bat Trang?

Many guides recommend it because it follows the river south from the bridges. Check the signs, avoid it in bad weather and compare options in our Bat Trang guide.

### Should I ride on the sandbars or the riverbank at low water?

No. Water levels change, the ground can be soft and such areas are not roads.
