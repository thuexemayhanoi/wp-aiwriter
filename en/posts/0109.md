The Honda Lead is the scooter in Hanoi built around storage. Honda Vietnam describes an under-seat box of more than 37 litres that can take two large full-face helmets and other personal items, with a movable divider and a light inside. That makes it the easiest rental scooter for a helmet and a shopping bag at the same time, but it does not remove the limits: Honda's manual allows 10 kg in the box, only 1.5 kg on the hook and in the front pocket together, and 3 kg on the rear grab rail, and it warns that some helmets will not fit whatever the litre figure says. This guide explains how the box is laid out, how to arrange helmets and bags in it, how heavy a shopping trip can be, what the Smart Key warning about phones means, and how to test the box at the shop before you rely on it.

## How the Lead's storage is laid out

The Lead has four storage points, each with its own limit in Honda's Lead 125 owner's manual (November 2025 edition):

| Place | What it is | Honda's maximum |
|---|---|---|
| Under-seat box | The big compartment, with divider and light | 10 kg |
| Front compartment | A lidded pocket below the handlebar, with a latch | 1.5 kg (shared with the hook) |
| Hook | Above the fuel cap, for one light bag | 1.5 kg (shared with the front compartment) |
| Rear grab rail | The passenger handle at the back | 3 kg |

Honda's page for the Lead ABS says the front compartment, which is deeper and larger on the newer versions, takes a 500 ml bottle and has a USB Type-C port. The under-seat box is listed as "up to over 37 litres", and Honda's 2013 announcement of the first Lead 125 in Vietnam already described a 37 litre space able to hold two full-face helmets, with a footnote that storage may not be possible depending on the size or shape of the helmets.

Older Leads exist, and a rental bike may not be the latest version. Ask the shop which Lead you are given and test the box on that bike.

## The divider: two layouts

Honda's manual says the divider's position can be changed according to what you are storing, and that it forms two zones in the box. The manual also says to place the helmet according to the divider position, with the front of the helmet facing the front of the bike, and to fit the divider firmly into its slots.

In practice this gives you two layouts to try: one that favours the helmet area and one that favours a bag or box. Which is better depends on the shape of your helmet and the bag you carry, and Honda's manual shows the two positions only in pictures. Try both with your own helmet before you ride away. Honda stresses that some helmets, because of their size or design, cannot be stored, so it is the helmet in your hand that matters.

## Helmets: one, two or none

A full-face helmet is the problem case on most scooters. Honda says the Lead's box can hold two large full-face helmets, but with the caveat that it depends on helmet size and shape. For a visitor this suggests three outcomes:

1. **A basic rental helmet** is likely to go in the box, but test it; the shop supplies one basic helmet per rider, free.
2. **Your own bulky or full-face helmet** may or may not fit, so test it.
3. **Two helmets** for a rider and a passenger may fit, but only if the divider and shapes cooperate. Otherwise one is worn and one is carried.

The helmet hook under the seat is for use only while parked, according to Honda. Do not ride with a helmet hanging from it. ## Shopping bags: what works and what does not

The big box is what makes the Lead attractive for market trips and supermarket stops. A shopping bag can go straight in, provided it is not heavy and does not leak.

**Works well:**

- Soft carrier bags with light goods such as bread, fruit or clothes.
- A small cool bag, if the weight stays modest.
- Items in a sealed container, so a spill does not reach the box lining or papers.

**Does not work well:**

- **A heavy load on the hook.** Honda's limit of 1.5 kg for the hook and front compartment together is low. A bag with a couple of bottles of water can exceed it. Honda warns against hanging bulky items on the hook because they can slip off or catch your legs.
- **Rigid items that stop the seat closing.** Check that the seat latches.
- **Wet or dripping items without a bag.** Water in the box can damage papers and other contents, according to Honda's cleaning advice.
- **Strong-smelling food** in a box that also holds your helmet.

Our guide to [carrying a passenger and luggage on a Lead or Vision](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/scooter-for-two-riders-and-luggage-hanoi/) covers the total load, and the Lead manual gives a maximum of 176 kg for rider, passenger, luggage and accessories together on most of the versions it lists.

## How heavy can a shopping trip be?

10 kg sounds generous, but it is a ceiling for the box, not a target. A rough way to think about it: a few kilograms of fruit, a small bag of groceries and a helmet are comfortable. Several large water bottles, rice and tinned goods may reach the limit quickly. If you are in doubt, buy less or split the purchase, because Honda says an overload affects steering, braking and stability. Place the heaviest item low in the box and near the middle, and keep a bag from sliding forward.

Because the box sits beneath the seat, a loaded box also affects how the bike feels when you push it off the stand or park it on a slope. Take care the first time.

## Smart Key and phones: a specific warning

Many current Leads use a Smart Key. Honda's manual warns not to leave the key fob inside the box, because with the ignition on anyone could ride the bike away without the fob. It also says not to put mobile phones or other radio-emitting devices in any storage box, since their radio signals can disturb the Smart Key system.

So for a Lead with that system:

- Keep your phone in your pocket or bag, not in the box.
- Never leave the fob or the emergency key in the box when you close the seat. The manual says to take care not to forget the key inside.
- If the bike does not respond to the key after you load the box, check that your phone is not inside.

If your rental has an ordinary key, these cautions may not apply, so ask the shop what kind of key the bike has.

## The box light and the seat

The Lead's box has a light that Honda says comes on for about 20 seconds when the ignition is turned off and on for about 60 seconds when you press the alarm button on the fob, which helps at night. To open the seat on the Smart Key version, you turn the ignition knob to the SEAT FUEL position and press the SEAT side of the switch. After loading, press down on the rear of the seat and lift it slightly to check that it has latched.

## A two-minute test at the shop

1. **Open the seat** and look at the box. Is it clean and dry? Does the light work?
2. **Fit your helmet** with its front facing the front of the bike. Close the seat gently.
3. **Move the divider** to its other position and try again.
4. **Put in your usual bag** and check that the seat latches.
5. **Open the front compartment** and check its lid latches.
6. **Check the hook** is firm.
7. **Photograph the inside** for your own record. Our [10-minute inspection guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-scooter-inspection-checklist/) lists the other checks.

## FAQ: Honda Lead storage

### How big is the Lead's under-seat box?

Honda Vietnam says up to over 37 litres, with a divider, and it can hold two large full-face helmets depending on their size and shape.

### How much weight can go in the box?

Honda's manual gives a maximum of 10 kg.

### Can I hang a shopping bag on the hook?

Only a light one. The hook and the front compartment share a 1.5 kg limit.

### Can I keep my phone in the box?

Not on a Smart Key bike. Honda says not to put mobile phones in any storage box because they can disturb the key system.

### Will two helmets fit?

Honda says two large full-face helmets can fit, but not every helmet will. Test yours at the shop.

### Is there a light in the box?

Yes on the version Honda lists. It switches on briefly when you turn the ignition off or press the alarm button.
