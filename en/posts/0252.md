A rented electric scooter is built to ride in ordinary rain, but its water resistance has limits that a renter must know before the first downpour. Most VinFast electric motorbikes are rated IP67 for the motor and battery, which means the parts survive temporary immersion in clean, still water in a laboratory test. The maker's stated figure is about 0.5 m of water for up to 30 minutes. That is not a promise about muddy, moving flood water, and it is not permission to wade through a flooded street. What a renter must check is therefore practical: which rating the model carries, whether the seals and covers look intact, how the bike behaves after rain, and what the shop wants you to do about the charger. This guide covers each in turn, using manufacturer guidance and an October 2026 report on electric motorbikes in the rainy season.

## What the IP code means

IP stands for Ingress Protection, a classification of how well an enclosure keeps out solids and water, set by the international standard IEC 60529. VnExpress, reporting on 14 October 2026, explains the code for readers: in IP67 the 6 means complete protection against dust, and the 7 means the device withstands temporary immersion at a depth of one metre for 30 minutes without harmful water entering.

Two points from that report matter more than the numbers. First, the test is done in fresh, clean, still water. Real flood water is muddy, carries rubbish and moves, and the report notes that a moving vehicle churns it and that the waves from other vehicles can push water into places not designed for direct force. Second, manufacturers say the protection can weaken with age and use, and advise against exposing the equipment to pressurised or fast-flowing water.

So treat IP67 as a good buffer against rain and splashes, not as a rating for flood riding.

## Not every model has the same rating

VinFast's customer pages show that the rating depends on the model. A VinFast FAQ about its older e-scooters says they use an IP57 motor and IP67 batteries, able to keep working when submerged to 0.5 m for up to 30 minutes. VinFast's pages for the Feliz S, the Klara S and the Vento S all describe IP67 and the same 0.5 m figure. The Feliz 2025 page lists IP67 for the motor, and a 2026 report on VinFast's newer swappable-battery models mentions IP67 for several of them.

These are VinFast figures. Nguyen Ha's electric group also includes electric bicycles and 50cc-equivalent electric scooters, which may be from other makers and have different ratings, and the bike you receive is confirmed when you book. So ask the shop:

- What is the stated water rating for this model's motor, battery and charging port?
- Is the rating on the manufacturer's documentation or only on a sticker?
- Has this bike been through a flood, or been repaired after water damage?

If the shop does not know, treat the bike as suitable for rain only and avoid any standing water.

## What to check at handover

A rating is only as good as the seals that keep it. Spend two minutes with the bike before you ride off, together with a staff member:

1. **The charging port cover.** It should close fully, with no cracks, and the rubber lip should be present.
2. **The boot and any battery compartment.** The lid should seat evenly. If the bike has a second battery in the boot, look at its connector area and the rubber around it.
3. **Cables and connectors.** Look for loose, cracked or crushed rubber boots and any green or white corrosion, which suggests that water has got in before.
4. **The display.** Check for fogging, moisture under the screen or odd flickering.
5. **Switches and the key area.** They should operate cleanly, with no sticking.
6. **The underside and footboard area.** Dried mud lines on the underside and battery casing can show that the bike has been in deep water.
7. **Warning lights.** A fault light at start-up should be explained, not ignored. Our guide to [what to check before you ride off](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-scooter-inspection-checklist/) shows the general inspection, and this list adds the water-specific items.

Photograph anything that looks worn, and ask the shop to note it. If the charging port cover or a seal is damaged, ask for another bike.

## Riding in rain

Rain itself is the case that the ratings are built for. VnExpress states that most electric motorbikes can be ridden in ordinary heavy rain without concern for the electrical system, and notes an advantage over petrol bikes: an electric scooter has no exhaust pipe or air intake through which water can reach the engine.

The risks in rain are the same as for any scooter, and our guide to [riding in Hanoi's rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) covers them:

- **Slippery road paint and manhole covers.** Brake early and gently.
- **Visibility.** Wipe the visor, and use lights.
- **Standing water hiding potholes.** You cannot see the hole, which is a danger on any bike.
- **Water on the footboard.** A wet shoe on a wet floor is a slip risk.

Park under cover if you can. After a ride, wipe the seat, the display and the footboard.

## Flooded streets: the manufacturer's advice

Hanoi's streets can flood in heavy rain. VinFast's guidance, as summarised by VnExpress, is that riders should avoid flooded roads in the first place, limit travel in bad weather, choose safe routes, avoid fast-flowing water and park the bike in a high, dry place.

If you have to cross water, VinFast's advice is to check the depth first and cross only if the water does not rise above the bike's ground clearance. VinFast lists ground clearance of about 135 mm for the Feliz S and about 141 mm for the Feliz 2025, which is a shallow puddle, far below the 0.5 m immersion figure. Keep the speed low and steady, do not accelerate suddenly or make a bow wave, and never stop, reverse or park in flood water.

An older VinFast article from 2023 gives a simpler rule of thumb: avoid water deeper than half the height of the wheel, since water can seep through the wiring at the wheel hub. Either way, the answer for a renter is the same: do not ride through flooded streets. Choose another route, wait, or ask the shop to collect the bike. Our guide to [riding a manual motorbike through standing water](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-a-manual-motorbike-through-standing-water/) explains why even shallow standing water hides hazards, and its points apply to electric bikes as well.

## After water: what never to do

If the scooter has been through deep water, or sat in a flood:

- **Do not start it, keep riding or charge it.** VinFast's guidance, as reported in October 2026, is to switch the bike fully off and turn off its switches.
- **Do not charge while the charging port is wet,** or immediately after the bike has been through water.
- **Do not wash the bike with a pressure hose,** especially near the motor, battery, charging port and connectors. This follows from the general warning against pressurised water.
- **Do not open the battery compartment or try to dry parts with heat.**
- **Do call the shop** and describe what happened. VinFast's advice is to contact the manufacturer for support and to have the bike carried to a workshop on a transport vehicle, where technicians check the electrical system, battery and motor before it is used again.

## Charging and wet weather

Water and charging go together badly. The charger and the port should be dry, indoors or under cover, and away from splashes and rain. Our guide to [charging an electric scooter in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/charging-an-electric-scooter-in-hanoi/) explains where to charge and why not to leave a charge unattended. A bike that has just come in from rain should be wiped down, and the port dried, before you plug in. In September 2026 fire-prevention advice said that the area for charging should be dry, ventilated and clear of heat and flammable material.

## Wear, seals and long rentals

Water resistance is not permanent. VinFast recommends replacing the motor seals every 30,000 km or three years, whichever comes first, to limit the risk of water entering. A rental bike may be well within that, but for a week or a month of use, you can help by:

- avoiding pressure washing and deep puddles;
- reporting a loose cover, a cracked boot seal or a damp compartment at once;
- storing the bike under cover, and not outside in a downpour, when you can;
- telling the shop about any warning light, moisture or odd noise after rain.

## FAQ: electric scooter water resistance

### Can I ride a rental electric scooter in heavy rain?

Yes, for ordinary rain, according to the October 2026 report on VinFast models, but ride carefully and avoid flooded streets.

### What does IP67 mean?

It means complete dust protection and protection against temporary immersion in clean, still water in a test. VinFast states about 0.5 m for up to 30 minutes.

### Can I ride through a flooded street?

VinFast advises against it. If there is no alternative, the water must not exceed the bike's ground clearance, and you should ride slowly and steadily without stopping.

### Can I wash a rental electric scooter?

Wipe it with a damp cloth. Avoid pressure hoses and avoid spraying the motor, battery, port and connectors.

### Is it safe to charge after rain?

Only when the bike and the port are dry, and with the supplied charger. If the bike was in deep water, do not charge it, and call the shop.

### Do all electric scooters have the same rating?

No. Ratings differ by model and maker. Ask the shop for the rating of the bike you are given.
