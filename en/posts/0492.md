On a sloped or uneven street, the safest parking decision is often to walk a little further to a flatter, firmer spot. If you cannot, park legally first, then make the bike stable: Honda's published parking advice says a motorcycle is safest parked facing uphill, because facing downhill can let the bike roll forward and collapse the side stand, and a side stand pointing downhill or into soft or muddy ground can sink or let the bike fall. Article 18 of Law 36/2024/QH15 also sets rules for where and how a vehicle may stop and park, and it requires a driver to use the parking brake or take other safety measures before leaving a parked vehicle.

This guide explains what makes a street unsafe to park on, how Article 18 applies, how to choose a spot, which way to face, how to deal with camber, kerbs, soft ground and drains, what to do with the stand and steering, how to check stability before you walk away and what to do if the bike falls. For a semi-automatic bike on a slope, see also our guide to [safely parking a semi-automatic motorbike on a slope](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/safely-parking-a-semi-automatic-motorbike-on-a-slope/). This guide is general information based on the law and published parking advice as of October 2026, not legal advice.

## What makes a street unsafe to park on

Hanoi streets are rarely flat or smooth, and a bike on its stand needs a stable base.

- **Slope.** Gravity adds a force that pushes the bike to roll or tilt, and on a side stand it changes the lean angle.

- **Camber.** Many roads are crowned, with the middle higher than the edge, so a bike parked near the edge leans towards the gutter.

- **Soft ground.** Verges, dirt, sand, wet paving and loose gravel let a stand sink.

- **Uneven surfaces.** Cobbles, paving blocks, broken slabs and potholes tilt the bike or leave the stand on an edge.

- **Slippery surfaces.** Oil, wet leaves, mud and smooth tiles let the stand slide.

- **Drains and manhole covers.** A stand foot can slip into a gap or on to a metal surface.

- **Kerbs and ramps.** A kerb can leave one wheel higher than the other, and a ramp adds a slope.

- **Crowded rows.** One bike that falls can push others over.

Honda's parking advice for motorcycles states the problem clearly: a two-wheeler cannot stand on its own, so it relies on a side stand or a centre stand, and on a slope it may not be possible to park in the correct position, which can make it fall over. Honda advises avoiding slopes where possible.

## What the law says about parking

Article 18 of Law 36/2024/QH15 sets rules that apply to every parked bike.

- **Parking brake or other safety measures.** When parking on the road, the operator may leave the vehicle only after using the parking brake or taking other safety measures. Vehicles parked on steep roads must steer toward the roadside with the wheels chocked.

- **Where to park.** On streets, park close to the right roadside or sidewalk in your direction of traffic, with the nearest wheel no more than 0.25 metre from the roadside or sidewalk, and without obstruction or danger to people and vehicles. On roads, use wide roadsides or land outside the carriageway, or park close to the right edge.

- **Where not to park.** You may not stop or park on the left side of one-way roads, on bends or near the crests of slopes with obscured visibility, on bridges, under overpasses, on pedestrian crossings, at intersections or within 5 metres of their edges, at pick-up and drop-off points, in front of gates and within 5 metres of both sides of gates, on road sections wide enough for only one motor vehicle lane, within the railway safety scope, where you obscure signs and signals, and on the roadway or sidewalks in contravention of law.

- **Signal.** Send a signal when leaving or entering the parking space, and do not affect pedestrians or vehicles.

Under Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, as read on the Government portal, stopping or parking on a pedestrian crossing, at a junction or at a pick-up point carries a fine of VND 400,000 to 600,000, and stopping or parking on a bridge a fine of VND 600,000 to 800,000. Check the current text before relying on a figure. Our guide to [who is responsible for parking and traffic fines](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/who-is-responsible-for-parking-and-traffic-fines/) explains how a parking notice reaches a rental.

The law and Honda's advice can pull in different directions. The law asks you to park at the right in your direction of traffic, which may mean facing downhill, while Honda says facing uphill is safer. If the lawful spot faces downhill on a slope, do not squeeze the bike into it. Find a flatter lawful place, or a guarded lot.

## Choosing the spot

1. **Look for flat first.** A level kerb, a side street or a parking lot is better than a slope. Our guide to [parking a motorbike in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-motorbike-in-hanoi/) explains how guarded lots work, and our guide to [how to find legitimate guarded parking near tourist sights](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-find-legitimate-guarded-parking-near-tourist-sights/) lists what to look for.

2. **Check the surface.** Prefer asphalt or concrete. Avoid soft earth, loose gravel, wet leaves, oil, and paving that is cracked or uneven.

3. **Check the tilt.** Stand back and look at the bike's lean on its stand. If it leans much more or much less than on level ground, move.

4. **Avoid camber and the gutter.** A bike parked in the gutter may lean the wrong way.

5. **Avoid drains and covers.** Do not put the stand foot on or beside a grate or manhole.

6. **Avoid kerb cuts and ramps.** A bike parked across a ramp is both unstable and in the way.

7. **Think about the exit.** Pushing a heavy bike backwards uphill is hard. A rider-guide on parking on hills advises thinking about how you will get the bike out before you park, ideally so that you can ride out.

8. **Stay clear of traffic and of crowds of bikes.** A bike knocked by another may fall.

9. **Choose a place that is lawful and visible.** Our guide to [how to park a larger scooter on a narrow Hanoi street](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-park-a-larger-scooter-on-a-narrow-hanoi-street/) and our guide to [parking a lightweight 50cc bike in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-lightweight-50cc-bike-in-hanoi/) give more detail.

## Which way to face on a slope

Honda's published parking advice for motorcycles gives a clear order of preference.

| Position | What happens | Advice |
|---|---|---|
| **Front facing uphill** | The bike's height is the same on both sides; stopping with a low gear or the parking brake is more secure | Safest of the three |
| **Front facing downhill** | If the bike moves forward, the side stand may collapse and the bike fall | Avoid; Honda recommends facing uphill instead |
| **Side stand on the downhill side** | The bike leans more than on level ground; hard to stand upright; may fall on muddy or soft ground | Avoid |
| **Side stand on the uphill side** | The bike is nearer the vertical and can fall easily if hit from the stand side | Avoid |

A rider guide to hill parking adds that the ideal is to park facing uphill, that the steeper the slope the more uphill the bike should face, and that on slight inclines it is safer to be in gear, though a scooter has no gear. It also warns that putting the front wheel against the kerb facing downhill may leave you pulling the bike backwards uphill to leave.

## The stand and the ground

- **Use the side stand on firm ground,** with the bike's weight properly on it, and check that the foot does not sink.

- **Do not put the centre stand on a slope or on uneven ground.** Honda's training advice for a manual bike says not to use the main stand on sloping or uneven ground, and our guide to [safely parking a semi-automatic motorbike on a slope](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/safely-parking-a-semi-automatic-motorbike-on-a-slope/) explains why.

- **Put something firm under the foot** if the surface is soft or has a gap, only if it is flat, wide and will not shift. A thin or round object can slip.

- **Check the lean.** The bike should lean a little onto the stand, not stand upright and not lean too far.

- **Test by pushing gently.** Push the bike gently from the stand side and the other side. If it moves, rocks or slides, find another place.

- **Turn the handlebars to the side your manual advises** and lock the steering. Honda's manual for the Wave RSX FI advises turning them fully left on a side stand. Ask the shop for the right position for the bike you rent.

## Dealing with common street problems

| Problem | What to do |
|---|---|
| **Camber towards the gutter** | Park on the flatter part; avoid the very edge; check the lean |
| **Cobbles or paving blocks** | Find a flat block, or move; do not put the foot between blocks |
| **Soft verge or dirt** | Avoid; if you must, use a firm flat base under the stand |
| **Wet or oily surface** | Avoid; the stand can slide |
| **Drain or manhole cover** | Keep the stand clear of grates and metal covers |
| **Kerb with a gap** | Park clear of it, so the stand does not fall into the gap |
| **Ramp or entrance** | Do not park across it; it is unstable and blocks access |
| **Slope in the lawful direction facing downhill** | Do not park there; find a flatter lawful place |
| **Strong wind** | Park sheltered, and check the bike when you return |

## Before you walk away

1. **Switch off the engine,** remove the key and lock the steering.

2. **Check the stand is fully down** and the bike leans firmly on it.

3. **Apply any parking brake or brake lock** your bike has. Our guide to [how to prevent your scooter from rolling while parked](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-prevent-your-scooter-from-rolling-while-parked/) explains how.

4. **Look at the position:** clear of kerb cuts, gates, crossings and other bikes.

5. **Do a gentle push test.**

6. **Take your helmet and valuables.** The shop's published terms say no separate lock is provided, so lock the steering and park in guarded places. Our guide to [how to transport a helmet on a scooter while parked](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-transport-a-helmet-on-a-scooter-while-parked/) explains where to leave it.

7. **Photograph the position** if the area is unfamiliar, so you can find it again and show where it was left.

## If the bike falls or rolls

- **Do not try to catch it.** A bike is heavy, and trying to hold it can injure you.

- **Check for damage, fuel or oil leaks,** and for anyone hurt or any other vehicle damaged.

- **Lift it carefully,** with the engine off, as the manual advises.

- **Take photographs** of the position and the damage.

## A quick checklist

1. **Prefer flat, firm, dry ground, and walk further if needed.**

2. **Park lawfully: right side, within 0.25 m of the roadside, clear of crossings and junctions.**

3. **If you must park on a slope, face uphill and avoid a side stand on the downhill side.**

4. **Check the lean, and do a gentle push test.**

5. **Lock the steering, and use any parking brake.**

6. **Avoid drains, covers, cobbles, verges and ramps.**

7. **Take your helmet and valuables.**

8. **Photograph the spot, and tell the shop if anything goes wrong.**

## FAQ: parking on slopes and uneven streets

### Which way should I face when parking on a hill?

Honda's advice is to face uphill, because the bike is more stable and a downhill-facing bike can roll forward and collapse the side stand. If the lawful spot faces downhill, find another place.

### Can I use the centre stand on a slope?

Honda's training advice for a manual bike warns against using the main stand on sloping or uneven ground.

### Does Vietnamese law say how to park on a steep road?

Article 18(2) of Law 36/2024/QH15 says vehicles parked on steep roads must steer toward the roadside with the wheels chocked, and that an operator may leave a parked vehicle only after using the parking brake or taking other safety measures.

### Can I park on the left side of the street?

On one-way roads, no. On other streets, park close to the right roadside in your direction of traffic, within 0.25 metre of it.

### What if the ground is soft?

Avoid it. A stand can sink, and the bike can fall.

### Who pays if the bike falls?

That depends on the agreement. Photograph the damage and call the shop.
