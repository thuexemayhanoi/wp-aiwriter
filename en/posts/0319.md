The Ho Chi Minh Mausoleum area is not closed to scooters as such, but a scooter cannot take you to the door. The complex is a protected state relic site with barriers, security checks and morning-only visiting hours, so the practical plan is to park in a legitimate lot outside the controlled area and walk the last stretch. As of October 2026 the mausoleum itself is closed for its annual maintenance, from 4 September to 2 November 2026, and is due to reopen on Tuesday 3 November. After that it receives visitors on Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Saturday and Sunday mornings only. This guide explains the three kinds of restriction a rider meets (site security, visitor rules and city traffic arrangements), the timing problem with a rental shop that opens at 09:00, and how to organise the day.

## Check the closure before you plan anything

Every year the mausoleum closes for roughly two months of periodic restoration. For 2026 the Mausoleum Management Board announced a closure from 4 September to 2 November, with public visits resuming on 3 November. News agencies VNA and Tuoi Tre reported the dates in August 2026, and the Communist Party of Vietnam's news site, in its defence section, carried the same notice. During the closure neither Vietnamese nor foreign visitors are received.

If you are reading this before 3 November 2026, you cannot go inside, though the surrounding square and gardens may still be reachable on foot. Do not rely on an old blog that says the mausoleum "opens every morning". Check the current notice, because the closure dates move from year to year; in 2024 it ran from 10 June to 12 August, for example.

## The visiting schedule at a glance

The schedule below follows the 2026 notice as summarised by the Vietnamese legal portal Thu Vien Phap Luat and by Tuoi Tre.

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Days open | Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Saturday, Sunday, mornings only |
| Closed | Mondays and Fridays |
| Exceptions | Opens on 19 May, 2 September and the first day of Lunar New Year even if they fall on a Monday or Friday |
| Warm season, 1 April to 31 October | 07:30 to 10:30; Saturdays, Sundays and holidays until 11:00 |
| Cool season, 1 November to 31 March | 08:00 to 11:00 on weekdays; weekends and holidays run a little later |
| Annual closure | About two months; in 2026, 4 September to 2 November |

Reports differ on the exact weekend closing time in the cool season, so confirm it close to your date. Plan to arrive well before closing: one visitor guide notes that the last entry is effectively earlier than the stated closing time when the line is long.

## Three layers of restriction a rider meets

### 1. Site security: barriers, checks and no riding in

The mausoleum belongs to what Vietnamese law calls the Ho Chi Minh Mausoleum Relic Area. Decree 161/2025/ND-CP, which implements the 2025 ordinance on its management and protection, says the Ministry of Defence arranges monitoring systems, warning signs and protective barriers, and carries out security checks on people, objects and vehicles entering the protected area. In plain terms: expect fences, guards and controlled gates. The fact that pedestrians walk through a gate does not mean a scooter may follow.

Take the same approach as our [Ba Dinh by motorbike guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/ba-dinh-by-motorbike/): treat any barrier, cone or officer as final, and look for an authorised parking place instead of an entrance.

### 2. Visitor rules: they apply to you, not your bike

The rules for visitors concern people. Summaries of the official rules, and the main visitor guides, agree on these points:

- **Modest dress.** Knees and shoulders covered; no shorts, vests or tank tops; shoes that cover the feet.
- **Bags go to storage.** Large bags and professional cameras are left at the baggage counter, and everyone passes through a security gate.
- **Quiet, single file.** Visitors walk in a line, do not push, do not put hands in pockets and remove hats at the door.
- **Phones and cameras.** Reports differ: some say a phone that is switched off may be carried, others say phones are collected. Follow the instruction given on the day.
- **No food, drink or smoking** in the mausoleum area.

Riders are often dressed for the road, not for a memorial. Our guides on [motorbike footwear for Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbike-footwear-for-hanoi-comfort-versus-protection/) and [riding in sandals](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-a-semi-automatic-bike-in-sandals-practical-safety-risks/) explain why closed shoes make sense on a bike anyway, which happens to satisfy the mausoleum rule too.

### 3. City traffic arrangements around Ba Dinh

Hanoi's low-emission zone plan covers nine wards inside Ring Road 1, and Ba Dinh is one of them. In 2026 only the first phase is running, and it applies to Hoan Kiem ward, not Ba Dinh. The weekend-evening vehicle ban of the first phase covers the Hoan Kiem Lake walking area, the Old Quarter and the night market, which are a different part of the city. The city's own timetable extends the zone to the whole of Ring Road 1 in 2028 to 2029, when motorbikes that do not meet emission level 3 are due to be restricted. As of October 2026, a petrol rental scooter can still ride the roads around Ba Dinh; ask the shop about the bike's emission standard if you plan to rent beyond 2027.

Separately, ceremonies and state events can bring temporary road closures to central Hanoi at short notice. A diversion is not a problem if you simply take it, and the signs on the day decide.

## Where the scooter stays

We could not verify a named official visitor car park for the mausoleum, so be sceptical of anyone who calls a particular lot the "official" one. Use the same method as in our [parking guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-motorbike-in-hanoi/):

1. Choose an attended lot, not a stretch of pavement.
2. Keep the claim ticket and check the closing time, since the lot must outlast your visit.
3. Park so that the bike does not block a gate, a crossing or a junction. Law 36/2024/QH15, in force since 1 January 2025, forbids stopping or parking on a pedestrian crossing and within five metres of a junction, and on city streets it requires you to keep close to the kerb on the right.
4. Lock the steering, take the helmet and key, and do not leave documents on the bike.
5. Photograph the bike and the entrance of the lot.

If the officer on duty tells you to move on or to take another road, comply. The same law lists failing to obey the instructions of an on-duty officer among prohibited acts, and it ranks an officer's commands above traffic lights and signs when they conflict. In other words, a signal from a guard beats the arrow on your phone.

## The timing problem: the shop opens at 09:00

Here is a practical trap that guides about the mausoleum rarely mention. Nguyen Ha's shop in Long Bien is open from 09:00 to 21:00 daily. The warm-season visiting window starts at 07:30 and ends at 10:30; the cool-season window runs from 08:00 to 11:00. If you collect a scooter at 09:00 and then ride across the Red River, you would arrive at the end of the queue time or after it. The shop is at Alley 5 Nguyen Van Cu Street, Bo De Ward, Long Bien District.

Options:

- **Visit the mausoleum first by taxi, ride-hailing or bus,** then collect the scooter and use it for the rest of the day. This is the simplest plan.
- **Ask whether the bike can be collected the evening before.** The shop's terms treat one day as 24 hours, so an evening handover may work, but ask the shop on 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969) and confirm in writing. Do not assume it.
- **Choose another day for the scooter,** and treat the mausoleum as a morning on foot.

Hanoi has made subsidised city bus routes inside Ring Road 1 free from 1 July 2026 to 30 June 2027, though the city says the policy does not cover commercial or tourist buses. That can be a cheap way to reach the area without parking. Read the [prices page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/prices/) and the [rental guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-guide/) for how hire periods work.

## A sample plan for a visit day

1. **Before 07:00:** reach Ba Dinh by taxi or bus. Do not carry a rucksack or any food.
2. **07:30 to 10:30 (warm season) or 08:00 to 11:00 (cool season):** queue and visit. Allow time for security and bag storage.
3. **Late morning:** walk the grounds, then eat nearby.
4. **Afternoon:** collect the rental scooter from Long Bien and ride to another area, for example the lakeside loop in our [West Lake guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/tay-ho-west-lake-by-scooter/).

If you do bring a scooter, ride in with long trousers, closed shoes and a covered shoulder layer in your bag, park well away from the barriers and walk.

## FAQ: scooters and the mausoleum area

### Can I ride a scooter up to the Ho Chi Minh Mausoleum?

Not to the entrance. The area is protected and controlled, with barriers and security checks. Park in a legitimate lot and walk.

### Is the mausoleum open now?

As of October 2026 it is closed for annual maintenance until 2 November 2026 and reopens on 3 November. Check the Management Board's current notice before you plan.

### Which days is the mausoleum open?

Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Saturday and Sunday mornings. It is closed on Mondays and Fridays, apart from 19 May, 2 September and the first day of Lunar New Year.

### Is there a ban on petrol scooters in Ba Dinh?

Not as of October 2026. The first phase of the low-emission zone applies to Hoan Kiem ward. Wider restrictions are scheduled for later years, so ask the shop about the bike if you rent long term.

### Can I wear shorts and sandals into the mausoleum?

No. Guides and official summaries require modest clothing, and closed shoes are advised. Pack long trousers.

### What if a guard tells me to ride another way?

Follow the instruction. Officers' commands outrank signs and signals, and a closed road is not negotiable.
