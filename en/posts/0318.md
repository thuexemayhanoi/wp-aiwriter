The Vietnam National Museum of History is a good scooter stop if you plan it as a weekday daytime visit and park once. It occupies two buildings in Hoan Kiem, at 1 Trang Tien and at 216 Tran Quang Khai, and one ticket covers both. The official site gives opening hours of 08:00 to 12:00 and 13:30 to 17:00, which means a lunchtime closure that suits a scooter day well. The museum's own pages do not describe a visitor motorbike park, so the safest plan is to leave the bike in a guarded lot nearby and walk in. The weekend-evening vehicle ban that Hanoi started in the Old Quarter on 1 July 2026 begins at 19:00, long after the museum has shut, so it rarely affects a museum visit. This guide covers the two buildings, the hours, the approach by scooter, parking and the practical rules inside.

## Two buildings, one visit

The museum's official site lists a single address with two parts: No. 1 Trang Tien and No. 216 Tran Quang Khai, both in Hoan Kiem. The ticket page says admission covers both museums. The first building, at 1 Trang Tien, presents Vietnamese history from the prehistoric period to the Nguyen dynasty and 1945. The second, at 216 Tran Quang Khai, takes the story from the middle of the nineteenth century to the present.

Why this matters on a scooter: you can visit one building or both, and the order is up to you. Most visitors who want the whole story start at Trang Tien and follow the timeline to Tran Quang Khai. A short visit to one building also works and leaves time for the rest of the day.

Do not assume that one parking spot serves both entrances. Plan to park once and walk, or choose a lot that sits between them after looking at a map on the day. If you have only a morning, picking a single building is more comfortable than shuttling a scooter between two streets in Hoan Kiem traffic.

## When to go: hours, lunch break and Mondays

The Opening Time page on the museum's site gives two sessions: morning from 08:00 to 12:00 and afternoon from 13:30 to 17:00. The gap between 12:00 and 13:30 is when the galleries close, so it is a natural time to collect the scooter, eat and return, or to leave the bike parked while you have lunch within walking distance.

Monday is where the official pages disagree. The museum's home page says it opens every day except Monday. The Opening Time page says it opens every day except the first Monday of every month, and that it still opens if a public holiday falls on a Monday. Because the two statements differ, treat Monday as uncertain. If your only free day is a Monday, check the museum's site or ask your hotel to confirm before you ride across the city. The site also says the Lunar New Year schedule is announced separately.

A few practical timing points:

- **Arrive close to 08:00 or just after 13:30.** These are the quietest moments for galleries, and for parking.
- **Allow time for both sessions only if you want both buildings.** A full visit to both can fill a day with the lunch break in the middle.
- **Leave by 17:00 at the latest,** since the afternoon session ends then, and a dusk ride back across the river is harder than a daylight one. Our [guide to riding at night in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-at-night-in-hanoi/) explains why.

## Getting there by scooter

Nguyen Ha's shop is at Alley 5 Nguyen Van Cu Street, Bo De Ward, Long Bien District, on the east side of the Red River. The museum is in central Hoan Kiem on the other side of the river. A scooter makes the trip easy in daylight outside the peak hours that the city itself uses in its traffic plans: 06:00 to 09:00 and 16:00 to 19:30.

On the approach, keep three things in mind.

1. **Use a legal route, not just the shortest line on the map.** Central Hoan Kiem has several one-way streets. Our [Old Quarter riding guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-old-quarter/) covers the narrow streets to the north, and our guide to [crossing Hanoi intersections](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/crossing-hanoi-intersections/) explains how junction traffic flows.
2. **Ride on a weekday or a weekend morning or afternoon.** On Fridays, Saturdays and Sundays from 19:00 to midnight Hanoi bars motorbikes, motorcycles and cars from the core zone of the Old Quarter and the lake walking area, apart from military and public-security vehicles.
3. **Pick a parking plan before you start,** as the next section describes.

If you would rather not ride into the centre, the museum's own Accessibility page lists buses No. 02, 34, 44, 49 and 51 as serving the museum. Hanoi has also made subsidised bus routes free within Ring Road 1 from 1 July 2026 to 30 June 2027, though the city says this does not apply to commercial or tourist buses. Ask the shop about the cleanest way to combine a scooter ride with a bus if you prefer not to park in the centre.

## Drop-off and parking: what is known and what is not

Here the official information is limited, so it helps to be exact.

**What the museum says.** The visiting rules page tells visitors to leave luggage in the lockers near the doorway. It does not give details of a visitor motorbike park, so you should not assume one exists.

**What the city says.** As part of the low-emission zone pilot that started on 1 July 2026, the city's Department of Construction arranged four transfer points for dropping off and picking up passengers. One of them is on Tran Khanh Du, in front of the History Museum. The same plan lists 220 car parks and parking points around the pilot area. These are passenger transfer points, so a scooter should not be left there for hours. Use them for a quick drop-off if you are with a passenger, then park properly.

**What this means for you:**

- Look for an attended lot with a ticket system, as our [parking guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-motorbike-in-hanoi/) describes, and keep the claim ticket until you collect the bike.
- Confirm the closing time of the lot before you walk away. The museum closes at 17:00, but you may also want dinner.
- Do not leave the scooter on a pavement because others have. Law 36/2024/QH15, in force since 1 January 2025, bans stopping or parking on pedestrian crossings and within five metres of a junction, and it requires parking close to the kerb on the right-hand side of the direction of travel on city streets.
- Lock the steering, take the key and do not leave the helmet or documents on the bike.
- Take a photograph of the bike and the lot entrance, in case you need to describe where you parked.

## Inside the museum: practical rules

The museum's visiting regulation page sets out a short list:

- Tickets bought at the box office are shown to the ticket collector at the entrance of the exhibition hall.
- Groups and visitors who want a guide should go to the Information Room at the museum's entrance at No. 1 Trang Tien.
- Anyone who wants to record video or take professional photographs should send an official letter and contact the Information Room first.
- No weapons, flammable, explosive or toxic items are allowed in the exhibition halls.
- Luggage goes in the lockers near the doorway, except money and jewellery.
- There is no smoking, eating, noise or touching of objects and displays.

The ticket page also lists a separate media fee for cameras, with a higher fee for video. If you plan to use anything more than a phone, read the page first.

For a scooter rider the locker rule is the useful one. A rental scooter has little secure storage, so do not carry a rucksack full of valuables to the museum. Take what you need for the day, leave the rest at your hotel and use the museum lockers for a jacket or small bag. Take the helmet with you rather than leaving it hanging on the bike, and ask at the desk whether it can go in a locker.

## Choosing your day: heat, rain and traffic

The museum is a good wet-weather alternative to a longer ride, since the exhibition is indoors. If a storm is forecast, read our guide to [riding in Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) before you choose to ride at all. Hanoi's streets flood in places, and a museum visit is the kind of plan that can wait for a better day.

In the heat, the morning session is more comfortable for the ride and for the walk between buildings. If you are riding in from Long Bien, a sensible pattern is to leave after the morning rush, reach the museum for the late-morning gallery time, have lunch nearby during the 12:00 to 13:30 break and finish before 17:00.

## FAQ: the National Museum of History by scooter

### Is there parking at the Vietnam National Museum of History?

The museum's own pages do not describe a visitor motorbike park. The city lists many parking points around the area, so use an attended lot nearby and keep the ticket.

### What are the opening hours?

The official site lists 08:00 to 12:00 and 13:30 to 17:00. It is closed on Mondays according to the home page, or only on the first Monday of the month according to the Opening Time page, so check before a Monday visit.

### Does one ticket cover both buildings?

Yes. The ticket page says admission covers the museums at 1 Trang Tien and 216 Tran Quang Khai.

### Does the weekend vehicle ban affect a museum visit?

Rarely. The ban in the core zone runs from 19:00 to midnight on Fridays, Saturdays and Sundays, and the museum closes at 17:00. It matters if you stay in the area for dinner.

### Can I leave my bag on the scooter?

It is better not to. The museum provides lockers near the doorway, and a rental scooter has little secure storage.

### Can I use the Tran Khanh Du transfer point to park?

No. It is a passenger drop-off and pick-up point, not a parking area.
