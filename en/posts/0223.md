Before you book a 50cc scooter, ask the shop about seven things: who may ride it (age and what ID is needed), what the bike really is (engine size, maximum speed, model), exactly what the price includes, how the deposit works, what the fuel and late-return rules are, which papers and insurance go with the bike, and what happens if it breaks down or is damaged. Asking takes five minutes by message or phone and prevents most of the surprises that show up at the counter. This guide gives the questions in the order they matter, explains why each one is worth asking, and notes what the rules say as of October 2026 so that you can recognise a clear answer. Where the facts come from Nguyen Ha Motorbike Rental, they are stated as the shop's own terms; for any other shop, ask the same things.

## Group 1: Who can ride it?

**1. What is the minimum age to rent?**
Vietnam's Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (Law 36/2024/QH15), effective 1 January 2025, sets 16 as the minimum age for riding a moped in Article 59. A shop may set its own conditions on top of that, especially for younger riders, so ask what they are and whether a parent or guardian has to sign.

**2. Do I need a driving licence for a 50cc scooter?**
Under the law, no. Article 56 says moped riders must know the road rules, be able to control the vehicle and meet the age and health requirements, and it does not require a licence class for them. That is a legal point, though. Ask whether the shop still wants to see your licence or an international permit, and say honestly what you hold.

**3. Which documents will you check?**
At Nguyen Ha, identity documents and licence are checked and photographed for the contract, and originals are not kept. If a shop asks to keep your passport as security, that is worth a second thought, and our guide to [what documents to carry when riding in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/documents-to-carry-riding-in-vietnam/) explains what you should carry yourself.

**4. Can a second person ride with me?**
Article 33 allows a moped to carry one person, and a second one only in listed cases, such as a child under 12 or a person who is elderly or disabled. Ask how the shop applies that, and do not assume that two adults are fine.

## Group 2: What is the bike, exactly?

**5. Is it truly a 50cc, and which model is it?**
In law a moped is a vehicle with a design speed of no more than 50 km/h and a petrol engine no larger than 50 cubic centimetres, or an electric motor no larger than 4 kW. Ask for the engine size, the model and the year. A bike that is bigger than that is a motorcycle, and the licence rules change. At Nguyen Ha the 50cc group is made up of Cub 50cc bikes and 50cc scooters, and the specific bike you get is confirmed at booking.

**6. Is it a Cub-style semi-automatic or a twist-and-go scooter?**
The two ride differently. A Cub-style bike has a foot gear shift and no clutch lever, and a scooter is automatic. Choose the one you are comfortable controlling, and ask whether you can see it before booking.

**7. How fast does it go, and what is the legal limit?**
A moped is limited to 40 km/h on the road under the speed regulation in force since 1 January 2025, which our guide to [renting a 50cc scooter in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/renting-a-50cc-scooter-in-hanoi/) explains. If your plans include roads where traffic moves at 50 or 60 km/h, ask yourself whether that is right for you.

**8. Can I see recent photos of the actual bike?**
A shop that lists a model but not a plate or a recent photo may hand you a different one. A photo of the actual bike is a reasonable request.

## Group 3: What does the price include?

**9. What is the daily, weekly and monthly price, and what is a "day"?**
At Nguyen Ha the 50cc group is VND 200,000 per day, VND 1,000,000 per week and VND 2,000,000 per month, shown on the [prices page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/prices/). One day is 24 hours, and shorter use is charged as a full day. Weekly and monthly packages are priced on their own, not as the day rate multiplied. The final price is confirmed on the actual bike before the contract is signed.

**10. What comes with the bike?**
One basic helmet per rider is included at no charge. A raincoat is not included on short rentals and may be considered for monthly ones, and no extra lock is provided, so lock the steering and park in guarded places. Ask also about a phone holder, a rear box or luggage space if you need them, since the shop will tell you what it has.

**11. Is delivery possible?**
Delivery is available only for weekly or monthly agreements, within 10 km of Hoan Kiem Lake, at VND 50,000 per trip or VND 100,000 for both ways, and beyond 10 km by agreement. There is no airport service.

**12. Are there any other charges I should know about?**
Ask for the list of charges in writing: late return, empty tank, damage, cleaning, cancellation. Surprises are rarely about the daily rate. They are about the extras.

## Group 4: The deposit, fuel and late return

**13. How big is the deposit, and in what form?**
The cash deposit at Nguyen Ha is VND 2,000,000 to VND 5,000,000, depending on the bike and the contract. It is separate from the rent, and this is not a no-deposit service. Ask when and how it is returned. Our guide to [how a rental deposit works in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbike-rental-deposit-hanoi/) explains the usual pattern.

**14. How much fuel will the bike have, and how should I return it?**
Petrol bikes are handed over with about 0.5 to 2 litres and come back with about 0.5 to 1 litre. An empty tank at return costs VND 20,000. A small bike has a small tank, and our guide to [fuel in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/fuel-in-hanoi-petrol-stations-rental-scooter/) explains where to fill up.

**15. What happens if I return the bike late?**
A late return is charged at VND 30,000 per hour for 1 to 5 hours, and from 6 hours it counts as one extra rental day. Early return does not refund unused rent. Ask what the cut-off time is in practice and whether the shop must be told in advance.

**16. What are the opening hours for pickup and return?**
Nguyen Ha is open 09:00 to 21:00 daily. If you land late or leave early, ask whether the shop can arrange a handover time.

## Group 5: Papers, insurance and responsibility

**17. Does the bike have its registration and compulsory insurance certificate?**
Decree 67/2023/ND-CP requires owners of motor vehicles, including motorbikes and mopeds, to hold compulsory civil liability insurance, and the insurer issues a certificate for each vehicle, which may be electronic. Article 56 of the Road Traffic Law lists the registration certificate and the compulsory insurance certificate among the papers drivers carry. Ask the shop to show you both. This cover is third-party cover for the owner's liability and is not the same as cover for your own injuries or for the bike.

**18. What does my travel insurance say about mopeds?**
Check this yourself before you travel, since a policy may exclude motorbikes or require a licence even when Vietnamese law does not. Our guide to [travel insurance and motorbike rentals](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/travel-insurance-motorbike-rental-vietnam/) explains the common gaps.

**19. Who pays for damage, and how is it assessed?**
Ask what counts as damage, how it is priced, and who decides. A clear contract should say. Our explanation of [what the clauses in a Hanoi rental contract mean](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-motorbike-rental-contract-explained/) covers the wording, and a walk-around video plus photos at pickup is the best record to keep.

**20. What if it is stolen or I lose the key?**
Ask what you owe and what you must do first. Locking the steering and parking in a guarded place reduces the risk.

## Group 6: Using the bike

**21. Where can I ride it?**
A moped is not allowed on expressways, and a slow bike is a poor fit for national roads and long days out. Ask whether the shop allows it outside Hanoi, and see our guide on [taking a rental motorbike out of the city](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/take-rental-motorbike-out-of-hanoi/).

**22. What happens if it breaks down or the battery fails?**
Get a phone number and ask who comes, how long it takes and who pays. The shop's number at Nguyen Ha is 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969). Our guide to [lost keys, flat tyres and breakdowns](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-bike-breakdown-hanoi-what-to-do/) explains the steps.

**23. Is the bike fit for night riding?**
Ask whether the headlight and tail light work, and test them before leaving. Our guide to [riding at night in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-at-night-in-hanoi/) explains lights and visibility.

## A one-message version

If you want to keep it short, send this before you book:

> Hello, I would like to rent a 50cc scooter for [dates]. Please tell me: the exact model and engine size, the minimum age and documents you check, the price for my dates and what it includes, the deposit and how it is returned, the fuel and late-return rules, whether the bike has its registration and compulsory insurance certificate, and who I call if it breaks down.

A good shop answers clearly and without irritation. A vague or evasive reply is useful information too.

## FAQ: booking a 50cc scooter

### Do I need to book in advance?

Ask the shop. Availability is confirmed when you book, and the bike you receive depends on what is free.

### Is a 50cc scooter cheaper than a 110 cc one?

Not necessarily. Its advantage is the licence rule, not the price. See the price page for current figures.

### Can I rent without a motorcycle licence?

A 50cc moped needs no driving licence under Vietnamese law, but you must be at least 16. Shop conditions and your travel insurance may differ, so ask both.

### Should I pay a deposit in cash or leave a passport?

Ask what the shop's contract requires. At Nguyen Ha the deposit is cash, and originals of your documents are not kept.

### What if I want to extend the rental?

Ask before you book how extensions are priced and whether the bike will still be free.

### Where can I see the full price list?

On the prices page, and the rental guide explains the usual steps.
