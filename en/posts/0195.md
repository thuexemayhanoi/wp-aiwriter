A slow moped does not need a lesser helmet. A 50cc bike in Hanoi is held to a low top speed, yet a crash at city speed can still throw a rider onto kerb stone, a kerbside post or tarmac, and a helmet protects only if it stays on and sits in the right place. That is a question of fit, not of speed. The practical test takes about a minute: the helmet sits level and snug on your head, does not rock or twist when you shake your head, has cheek contact without pain, and has a chin strap that is fastened with about two fingers of space under the chin. This guide explains why low speed does not relax those requirements, what Vietnam's rules and Honda's training advice say, how to check a rental helmet, and when to ask for another one.

## Why a lower top speed does not relax the helmet requirement

It is tempting to treat a 50cc bike as closer to a bicycle than a motorcycle. The law does not. As of October 2026, the Road Traffic Order and Safety Law (Law 36/2024/QH15, in force since 1 January 2025) says that riders and passengers of two-wheeled motorcycles and mopeds must wear a helmet that meets the national technical regulation and must fasten the strap correctly. A moped is the same as any other motorbike in that respect, and the rider's age or the lack of a driving licence for a qualifying moped changes nothing.

The reason is physical. A moped in Hanoi is surrounded by cars, buses and faster motorbikes, it has small wheels, and it may be ridden by someone new to the city. A fall at modest speed can still involve the head hitting the ground. Honda's owner's manual for its 50cc Ruckus scooter calls the helmet the most important piece of riding gear and says helmets significantly reduce the number and severity of head injuries. It adds that a helmet should fit the head comfortably and securely, which is the part riders overlook.

Our guide to [helmet rules in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/helmet-rules-in-vietnam/) covers the rule itself. This page is about the next question: given that you must wear one, how do you make sure it will do its job?

## What the national standard says about size and sizing

Vietnam's current helmet regulation, QCVN 2:2021/BKHCN, took effect for helmets placed on the market from 1 January 2024, and helmets certified under the older 2008 version could stay on sale until the end of 2024. The Ministry of Science and Technology's summary on the national standards agency's site notes that it covers helmets for motorcycles, mopeds, electric bikes and similar vehicles. That means the helmet on a rented 50cc bike should be one made to the same family of rules as for any motorbike.

Two details from that summary are useful for a renter. First, sizes are defined by head circumference, nine sizes in all from 460 mm to 620 mm, so children's heads and large adult heads are both covered. A helmet that is clearly too big is not simply a comfort issue; it is outside the range intended for that head. Second, the common half-head and three-quarter helmets are tested for impact at a velocity of roughly 5.8 to 5.95 metres per second on the larger sizes, which works out to around 21 km/h. That figure is a laboratory drop speed rather than a safe riding speed, but it shows that helmets are designed for the kind of impacts that happen at urban speeds.

## The fit check in five steps

Honda Vietnam's safe-riding training guide, published on its site in 2026, describes how to choose and wear a helmet: open the strap, put the helmet on, turn it left and right to make sure it suits the head, fasten the buckle, then check that two fingers fit between your chin and the strap. NHTSA's motorcycle-helmet advice and Bell's fitting guide add the remaining checks. Combined, they make a short routine.

1. **Position.** Put the helmet on level, with the brow edge low on the forehead, not tipped back like a hat.
2. **Crown and cheeks.** It should feel equally snug around the crown and the cheeks. Bell describes cheek pads that sit tight against the cheeks without biting.
3. **Twist test.** Fasten the strap, grip the helmet and twist it left and right. If the skin of your forehead moves with it, the fit is good; if the helmet slides over the skin, it is too big.
4. **Strap.** Fasten the buckle and check for about two fingers of space under the chin, as Honda says. The strap should lie flat, not twisted.
5. **Shake test.** Nod and shake your head firmly. A helmet that rocks forward over the eyes or backwards off the forehead needs a different size or pad.

If you cannot get these five right, do not accept the helmet and ask for another size.

## What to inspect on the shell, foam, strap and label

Honda Vietnam's guide lists what to look at on any helmet before wearing it. The shell should be smooth, thick, hard and not easily broken. The shell rivets should not be metal and should not protrude more than 2.5 mm. The foam liner should be thick and should not sink when you press it. The strap and the buckle should be strong and able to bear a firm pull. The quality label should be complete and correctly applied. The guide also says not to use a helmet that has been cracked or hit hard.

The US safety agency NHTSA gives a similar picture of a poor helmet: a thin shell, no stiff foam lining and flimsy plastic buckles. Its examples are about the US market, so the label to look for here is the Vietnamese conformity marking and not an American sticker. But the structural clues carry over.

A quick table to use at the shop counter:

| Part | Looks right | Ask for another helmet if |
|---|---|---|
| Shell | Smooth, firm, no cracks | Cracks, deep gouges or a shell that flexes |
| Foam | Thick, springs back | Compressed, crumbling or loose |
| Strap | Flat, sturdy, no fraying | Frayed, twisted permanently or stitching loose |
| Buckle | Clicks shut and holds under a pull | Slips, sticks or looks cracked |
| Label | Present and readable | Missing or peeled off |
| Fit | Passes the twist and shake tests | Rocks, pinches or tips |

## Visors, hair, glasses and other small fit problems

At moped speeds the small details cause most of the trouble. A tinted or clear visor on a helmet that has one can fog in rain and hot weather; the QCVN summary notes that lightly tinted visors must carry a note that they are for daylight use only, so do not wear a tinted visor in the evening. A visor that sits crooked or scratches ahead of your eyes is not a safe choice either.

Long hair pushed into a bun at the nape can lift the back of the helmet. A ponytail pulled through a gap at the back may be better. Glasses should fit under the helmet without being pushed into the temples, and a thick face mask or cap can change how the helmet seats. Try the helmet with the same items you will wear on the ride. If you wear a cloth cap, a bandana or a thick scarf, take it off before you check the fit and then put it back and check again; if the helmet no longer feels secure, drop the extra layer.

Heat matters too. A helmet that is uncomfortably hot is one you will be tempted to take off at a stop or leave unfastened. Choose the lightest shape that is certified, and plan a rest in shade.

## Wearing it every time, including short hops

The most common helmet failure on a moped is not a poor helmet but a missing or unfastened one. A fast five-minute trip to a cafe, a corner shop or the next street is where people say "it is only round the corner" and leave the strap undone. The law treats an unfastened helmet as incorrectly worn, and a helmet that is not fastened will not stay on in a fall.

Make it a habit: helmet on, buckle clicked, tug on the strap, then start the engine. Every time you stop and take the helmet off, do the same on the way back. For a passenger, check their helmet in the same five steps; a helmet that is too large on a smaller person is a common problem. Our guide on [child passengers on a motorbike in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/child-passengers-motorbike-vietnam/) explains the extra points for children.

## A two-minute check before you ride away

Use this list at the counter, and again after any stop where the helmet was off:

- Helmet sits level, brow edge low, no gap at the forehead.
- Twist test: forehead skin moves with the helmet.
- Shake test: no rocking.
- Chin strap flat, about two fingers of space.
- Buckle clicked and holds under a pull.
- No cracks, dents, loose foam or frayed webbing.
- Visor clean, undamaged and suited to the light.
- Hair, glasses and mask do not break the seal.

## FAQ: moped helmets

### Do I need a full-face helmet on a 50cc moped?

The rule asks for a helmet that meets the national regulation and is correctly fastened; it does not specify a full-face type for a moped. Honda's guide lists half-head, three-quarter and full-face helmets as acceptable styles. A full-face helmet protects the jaw and face more, but is hotter and heavier. Choose whatever certified helmet you will wear properly every ride.

### Is a helmet that "fits loosely" acceptable if the strap is tight?

No. A tight strap does not make up for a loose shell. If the helmet rocks or slides when you twist it, the shell is too large and will move in a fall. Ask for a smaller size or an additional pad.

### How tight should the chin strap be?

Honda Vietnam says two fingers should fit between the chin and the strap. The strap should be snug but should not choke you or force your jaw shut. A strap that hangs or dangles below the chin is too loose.

### Should I replace a helmet after dropping it?

Honda's guide says not to use a helmet that has been cracked or hit hard. A helmet dropped on a hard floor may be fine, but a helmet that has been in a crash should be retired. At a rental, tell the shop about a helmet that has taken a knock.

### Can the passenger share my helmet size?

No. Every person on the bike needs a helmet of the right size for their own head. A passenger on a 50cc bike is also covered by the helmet rule.
