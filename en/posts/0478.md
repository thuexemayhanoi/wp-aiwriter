A phone in a secure mount, set up before you move and glanced at like a mirror, is a reasonable way to navigate on a scooter in Hanoi. A phone in your hand is not. Article 9 of Law 36/2024/QH15 prohibits holding in hands and using phones or other electronic devices while operating a vehicle on the road. Vietnamese legal commentary reads this to mean that a phone fixed on a holder, which the rider only looks at to find the way, is outside the ban, while holding and using it is an offence. The reported fine under Decree 168/2024/ND-CP is VND 800,000 to 1,000,000 with 4 licence points deducted, rising to VND 10,000,000 to 14,000,000 and 10 points if it causes an accident.

This guide explains what the law allows, what to look for in a mount, how to fit one on a rental without damaging it, how to set up navigation before you ride, how to use a screen without losing your attention, how vibration, heat and rain affect a phone, why earphones are a separate problem and what to do with the mount when you return the bike. It is general information based on the law and published guidance as of October 2026, not legal advice.

## What the law allows and bans

| Act | Position under Vietnamese law | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Holding the phone while riding and using it | Banned | Article 9 of Law 36/2024/QH15 |
| Phone fixed in a mount, only looked at for navigation | Not the banned act, according to Vietnamese legal commentary, but you must still ride safely | Legal Q&A published by LuatVietnam, Thu Vien Phap Luat and Tuoi Tre |
| Wearing earphones or using other sound devices | Banned, except hearing aids | Article 33 of the Law |
| Using an umbrella while riding | Banned | Article 33 of the Law |
| Any act that affects traffic order and safety | Banned | Article 33 of the Law |

The commentary adds a warning that matters for riders: even where a mounted phone is not itself an offence, it must not distract you or block your view. A police officer or a court looking at an accident will ask whether you were riding with proper attention, not only whether the phone was in a holder.

Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, quoted in Vietnamese legal Q&A, groups using an umbrella, using a sound device and holding and using a phone in the same clause of Article 7, with a fine of VND 800,000 to 1,000,000 and 4 licence points deducted. If the offence causes an accident, the legal Q&A reports a fine of VND 10,000,000 to 14,000,000 and 10 points. Check the current text of the decree if a figure matters to you, and see our guide to [how to verify the current fine for a traffic offence](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-verify-the-current-fine-for-a-traffic-offence/).

The shop's published terms do not list a phone holder among the items provided, and they say nothing about fitting your own. ## What to look for in a mount

| Feature | Why it matters on a scooter |
|---|---|
| **A firm clamp on the handlebar or mirror stem** | A phone that moves or falls can hit the road, a wheel or a pedestrian |
| **A grip that holds the phone on all sides** | Pothole shocks and braking can pull a phone out of a weak holder |
| **A position clear of the instruments, warning lights and mirrors** | You need the speedometer and the mirrors as much as the map |
| **A position that does not touch the brake lever, throttle or cables** | The bars must turn fully and the controls must move freely |
| **An angle you can read without tilting your head** | A small movement of your eyes costs less than a big one |
| **A rain cover or waterproof case** | A wet touchscreen is hard to read |
| **A way to take the phone off quickly** | You should not leave it on a parked bike |

Do not use a suction cup on a curved or painted surface, and do not rest a phone on the footboard or the seat storage. Do not put it in a pocket where you must pull it out at each junction.

## Vibration and your phone

A handlebar mount passes the engine's vibration to the phone. Apple has published a support note, as reported by technology news sites, saying that high-amplitude vibration from high-power motorcycle engines can degrade the optical image stabilisation and closed-loop autofocus in some iPhone cameras, and that it does not recommend attaching an iPhone to such motorcycles. The note says mopeds and scooters generate comparatively lower-amplitude vibration, but recommends a vibration-dampening mount for them and says to avoid regular use for prolonged periods. Other phone makers have not published identical statements in the sources we read, although riders have reported similar camera problems on other brands.

For a short rental, the practical steps are modest:

- **Use the mount for the trip, not for a whole day.** If you only need the map for part of the route, take the phone off the bars afterwards.

- **Consider a dampened mount** if you use the phone's camera for work.

- **Keep an old or spare phone for navigation,** if you have one.

## Set up before you move

Most distraction comes from changing the route on the move, so do it at the kerb.

1. **Enter the destination before you start the engine,** and check the route overview.

2. **Check the type of road.** Map apps may send you onto a road that motorcycles may not use. Our guides to [how to check if a navigation app suggests an expressway](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-check-if-a-navigation-app-suggests-an-expressway/) and [motorbike navigation near Hanoi: toll roads and detours](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbike-navigation-near-hanoi-toll-roads-and-detours/) explain what to look for.

3. **Download offline maps** if you will leave the city. Our guide to [how to use offline maps for countryside rides near Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-use-offline-maps-for-countryside-rides-near-hanoi/) explains how.

4. **Set the screen to stay on and to be bright enough,** since glare in sun makes you stare harder.

5. **Turn off notifications** that pop up over the map, such as messages and calls.

6. **Set the app to avoid options you cannot use,** such as highways, if it has that setting.

7. **Charge the phone first,** or carry a power bank in a pocket. Do not let a charging cable dangle near the wheel or the controls.

8. **Know your first two turns,** so that you do not need to look at once.

## How to use the screen while riding

The law's ban is on holding and using a phone. The safety rule is wider: your eyes should be on the road almost all the time.

- **Treat the map like a mirror.** A quick glance, then eyes back up.

- **Do not touch the screen while moving.** Vietnamese commentary treats a fixed phone that you only look at as lawful, and touching it to zoom or search is both unsafe and a step towards the banned act of using it.

- **Do not read text.** Street names, messages and long instructions need a stop.

- **Plan the next turn early.** Look at the map before a junction, not in it.

- **If you are lost, stop.** Our guide to [when to stop and check a map instead of looking down](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/when-to-stop-and-check-a-map-instead-of-looking-down/) explains where to pull over and how.

- **Let a passenger navigate.** A passenger who is not operating the vehicle can read the map and tell you the turns. Our guide to [riding with a passenger: balance and communication](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-with-a-passenger-balance-and-communication/) explains how to agree signals.

## Why earphones are not the answer

Voice directions through earphones may sound safer than looking at a screen, but Article 33 of the Law bans sound devices for riders other than hearing aids, and earphones also hide the horns and engines you need to hear. Do not wear earphones or headphones. Use your eyes at a stop, or ask a passenger to read the route.

## Heat, sun and rain

- **Heat:** A phone in strong sun may dim its screen or stop charging. A shade from the handlebar cover, a lower brightness or a break in the shade can help. Do not leave a phone in a hot mount while you park.

- **Rain:** Use a waterproof cover, or take the phone off the bars and put it in a dry pocket. Wet fingers do not work on a screen, and rain on the glass hides the map. Our guide to [riding in Hanoi in the rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) explains how to adjust.

- **Night:** A bright screen can hurt your night vision. Dim it, and use dark mode if the app has one.

## When you park and when you return

- **Take the phone with you when you park.** A mount with a phone in it advertises that the bike is not secured.

- **Remove the mount before you return the bike,** and check for marks on the handlebar, mirror stems and cables. Photograph the area to show that it is as you received it.

- **Tell the shop about any mark** you notice, and keep your photographs. The shop's published pages say to keep copies of the signed agreement and the handover photos. ## A quick checklist

1. **Ask the shop, photograph the handlebar and fit a firm, removable mount.**

2. **Keep the instruments, mirrors and controls clear.**

3. **Set the route, the road type and the screen before you move.**

4. **Glance like at a mirror, and never hold or touch the phone while moving.**

5. **Do not wear earphones.**

6. **Protect the phone from heat, rain and long vibration.**

7. **Stop to re-route or to read anything.**

8. **Take the phone and the mount away when you park or return.**

## FAQ: phone mounts on scooters

### Is it legal to use a phone on a mount while riding in Vietnam?

Article 9 of Law 36/2024/QH15 bans holding and using a phone while operating a vehicle. Vietnamese legal commentary says a phone fixed on a holder that you only look at for navigation is not the banned act, but you must still ride safely.

### What is the fine for holding a phone on a motorbike?

Legal Q&A reports VND 800,000 to 1,000,000 and 4 licence points under Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, and VND 10,000,000 to 14,000,000 and 10 points if it causes an accident. Check the current text.

### Can I wear earphones for directions?

No. Article 33 bans sound devices for riders, except hearing aids.

### Will a mount damage my phone's camera?

It might. Apple has said that vibration from high-power motorcycle engines can degrade some iPhone cameras, and recommends a dampened mount for mopeds and scooters and avoiding prolonged use.

### Does the rental shop provide a phone holder?

The published terms do not list one. Ask the shop before you rely on it, and ask before fitting your own.

### What should I do if the app tells me to turn onto a banned road?

Do not follow it. Stop at a safe place and check the road. Our guide to [how to check which roads restrict motorbikes](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-check-which-roads-restrict-motorbikes/) explains how.
