The practical answer for Hanoi Railway Station is to treat it as a place to drop a person, not to leave a rental scooter for a long trip. The station has two entrances, the main one at 120 Le Duan Street and a second, used by northbound trains, on Tran Quy Cap Street. Ticket and travel sites say there is parking for motorbikes at the station, but we could not find a current official notice of its location, hours or fees, so confirm on the spot before relying on it. If you are away for more than a few hours, returning the bike first is usually cheaper and safer than leaving it. This guide separates what is verified from what is not, explains the rules for a quick drop-off, shows how train times collide with the shop's opening hours and offers a plan for each kind of trip. It is general information as of October 2026.

## Two entrances, two streets

Hanoi Railway Station, known locally as Ga Ha Noi, is at 120 Le Duan Street in Hoan Kiem, with the station area in Cua Nam ward. Two travel sources agree on how the station is organised:

- **Gate A, the main entrance on Le Duan Street,** serves southbound trains such as the Reunification Express to Hue, Da Nang and beyond.
- **Gate B, on Tran Quy Cap Street,** serves northbound trains, including those for Lao Cai and Sapa. Local Vietnam's station guide says that to reach it you cross the level crossing off Le Duan to the north of the station and follow the road to the left.

For a rider this matters before parking, not after. If your train leaves from the Tran Quy Cap side, parking at the Le Duan front adds a walk with luggage. Check the gate printed on your ticket and plan the approach to that street. The station is open around the clock, and the distance to the centre of the Old Quarter is under two kilometres according to Local Vietnam, which is why many people walk or take a short ride-hail instead of bringing a scooter.

## What is verified about parking at the station

Honest sorting of the evidence helps here.

**Reported but not verified in detail.** Omio's station listing says parking is available for motorbikes and cars at the station but gives no location, fee or hours. A ticket-booking site for Vietnam Railways describes a motorbike and car park with guards and cameras. Both are commercial listings, not station notices, so use them as a hint rather than a promise.

**Not found.** We could not find a current official page that gives the position of the station's motorbike lot, its opening hours, its overnight rules or a fee table. If you read a specific price in a forum post or video, treat it as unconfirmed.

**A dated warning that still teaches something.** In January 2013 the newspaper Tien Phong reported complaints that a parking operator at the station charged several times the printed ticket price. The station chief said at the time that parking areas inside the station grounds were run under the station's management, that overnight parking was negotiated with the customer, and that a ticket must still be issued. That report is more than a decade old and the operators and prices will have changed, but the lesson is current: ask the price before you leave the bike, and take the ticket.

## What to do at any lot near the station

Because we cannot name an approved lot, use a method that works at any one.

1. **Look for the signs of a real operation:** a defined area, an attendant, posted hours and a posted price.
2. **Ask the price and the closing time before you park,** and ask whether overnight parking is allowed. The most convenient spot is useless if it locks its gate before your train returns.
3. **Take the ticket and keep it.** Check that the number matches the plate.
4. **Lock the steering and take the key,** and carry the helmet. The shop does not supply an extra lock, so the steering lock and a guarded place are your protection.
5. **Photograph the bike and the lot** when you leave it.
6. **Never leave documents, a bag or valuables** on the bike.

## Should a rental scooter be parked at the station at all?

Think about why you are going. A bike left at a station while you ride a train is a bike that is still being rented.

| Your plan | What usually makes sense | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Drop a friend or collect one, a few minutes | Pull over briefly outside, then leave | No parking needed; see the drop-off rules below |
| A train trip of a few hours | Park at an attended lot, keep the ticket, check closing time | Cheaper than returning, but you carry the risk |
| An overnight or multi-day train trip | Return the bike, or leave it at a guarded place at your lodging | A rental day is 24 hours and the shop's policy is that early return does not refund unused rent, so ask what is best for the dates |

## Dropping someone off outside the station

If you only need to stop, the rules of Law 36/2024/QH15, in force since 1 January 2025, are straightforward.

- **Stopping is temporary.** The law defines a stop as standing still for as long as needed to let someone on or off or to load. While stopped you should not switch off the engine or leave the rider's position, apart from the limited cases the article lists.
- **Do not stop within five metres of a junction,** or on a pedestrian crossing.
- **Do not stop at a designated passenger pick-up or drop-off point,** for example a bus stop.
- **On city streets, stop beside the kerb on the right,** with the nearest wheel no more than 0.25 metres from it, so as not to obstruct others.
- **Do not leave the bike to follow your passenger inside.** A longer stop is parking, and it belongs in a lot.

If you have a passenger and luggage, read [how much luggage you can carry on a Honda Wave](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-much-luggage-can-you-safely-carry-on-a-honda-wave/) and the guide to [scooters for two riders and luggage](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/scooter-for-two-riders-and-luggage-hanoi/). A rucksack and a suitcase on a small bike is a hard load to ride with.

## Train times against shop hours

Trains run through the night. On one September 2026 day, a ticketing site listed 43 trains at the station with the first at 02:17 and the last at 23:41. The rental shop, however, is open from 09:00 to 21:00. That gap creates real problems for a rider:

- **An early train** means the bike cannot be returned at the shop on the morning of travel. Return it the day before, or ask the shop whether another arrangement is possible.
- **A late arrival** means the shop is closed when you get back. If you left the bike at a lot, check that the lot is open, and ask what you owe the shop for a late handover. The shop's late-return rule is VND 30,000 per hour for the first one to five hours and an extra rental day from six hours, and it should be confirmed before you sign.
- **A night ride** across the city after a late train is harder than the same ride by day. Our [night riding guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-at-night-in-hanoi/) explains the problems.

A simple rule: plan the train and the bike separately, and write down the hours of each.

## Luggage and security

The station has self-service lockers in the Gate A and Gate B halls, according to a Vexere guide, so a day pack and a bag can be left there instead of on the scooter. Check the current price and payment method on the day. A scooter is a poor store for luggage: rental bikes have little secure storage, and a lock cannot protect a bag. Carry valuables with you and leave nothing under the seat.

## The low-emission zone and the station area

Hanoi's low-emission zone pilot began on 1 July 2026, and its first phase covers parts of Hoan Kiem ward around the lake and Old Quarter, not the station area. The roadmap says the second phase, from 1 January 2027, extends to the Hoan Kiem and Cua Nam wards, and the reports describe that phase as aimed at petrol ride-hailing motorbikes and at cars that do not meet emission standards. Wider limits on motorbikes below emission level 3 are scheduled from 2028. As of October 2026 a petrol rental scooter can still be ridden and parked around the station. If you rent for a long period that runs past 2026, ask the shop about the bike's emission standard.

The nearby Train Street area has its own access issues; see our [Train Street guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/visiting-hanoi-train-street-why-parking-and-access-rules-matter/) before you combine it with a station visit.

## FAQ: parking near Hanoi Railway Station

### Is there motorbike parking at Hanoi Railway Station?

Ticket and travel listings say yes, but we could not find an official notice with the location, hours or fees. Confirm on arrival and take the ticket.

### Which gate should I go to?

Gate A at 120 Le Duan Street serves southbound trains, and Gate B on Tran Quy Cap Street serves northbound trains such as those to Lao Cai. Check your ticket.

### Can I drop a friend at the station and leave?

Yes, as a brief stop. Keep five metres from junctions, stay on the right beside the kerb and do not leave the bike.

### Should I park a rental scooter at the station for a multi-day trip?

Usually not. Ask the shop about returning it, since a rental day is 24 hours and early return does not refund unused rent.

### Can I put my rental scooter on a train?

Not without the shop's written permission. See the guide on taking a rental bike out of Hanoi.

### Does the low-emission zone affect the station?

Not in 2026. The pilot's first phase is in the lake and Old Quarter area. The second phase, from 1 January 2027, extends to Cua Nam ward.
