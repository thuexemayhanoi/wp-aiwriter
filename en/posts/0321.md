Hanoi has several weekend walking spaces, and they run on different clocks, so "where do I leave my scooter" has no single answer. The short version for the busiest one, the Hoan Kiem Lake and Old Quarter area, is this: since 1 July 2026 motorbikes, motorcycles and cars are barred from the core zone from 19:00 to midnight on Fridays, Saturdays and Sundays, so you park outside the perimeter before 19:00 and walk in. Hanoi's government news portal reports 220 parking lots and points arranged around the pilot area, four passenger transfer points and free subsidised buses inside Ring Road 1 until 30 June 2027. This guide sets out which walking spaces exist, how the 2026 vehicle ban fits over them, where a scooter can wait, and how to avoid a night that ends with a scooter locked inside a closed zone or a shop that has closed.

## Several walking spaces, several schedules

The Hoan Kiem and Old Quarter area is really two connected walking spaces, and the city treats them separately. When Hoan Kiem ward changed the schedule for the 30 April to 1 May holiday in 2026, the lake space opened continuously from 07:00 on 30 April to midnight on 3 May, while the Old Quarter space kept its normal Friday to Sunday evening hours. That tells you the two are managed independently.

| Walking space | What it covers | Usual schedule reported |
|---|---|---|
| Hoan Kiem Lake and surroundings | Streets around the lake, among them Dinh Tien Hoang, Hang Khay, Le Thai To (the lake side), Trang Tien, Le Lai, Le Thach, Lo Su and Hang Dau | From 19:00 on Friday to midnight on Sunday |
| Old Quarter | Hang Dao, Hang Ngang, Hang Duong, Dong Xuan, Hang Khoai and Hang Giay, with later additions such as Cau Go, Hang Be, Hang Bac and Dao Duy Tu | 19:00 to midnight on Friday, Saturday and Sunday |

The schedules above come from the 2016 launch announcement published by the national tourism authority, from a Vietnam Airlines travel guide and from Vietnamese news coverage. Holiday and event adjustments are common, so confirm the details for your date.

## The 19:00 line: what the 2026 pilot changed

On 1 July 2026 Hanoi began the first phase of its low-emission zone pilot. The city's plan, as reported by the government news portal and VOV, divides the Hoan Kiem pilot into two zones.

- **Zone 1** includes the lake walking space, the Old Quarter and the Hoan Kiem night market. The city keeps its walking-space arrangement and, from 19:00 to midnight every Friday, Saturday and Sunday, bans motorbikes, motorcycles and cars from circulating, except vehicles managed by the Ministry of Defence and the Ministry of Public Security.
- **Zone 2** covers the roads around the Old Quarter and the lake. It restricts fossil-fuel coaches and cars with more than 16 seats during peak hours, 06:00 to 09:00 and 16:00 to 19:30.

News reports name the streets bounding Zone 1 as Trang Tien, Hang Khay, Le Thai To, Hang Dao, Hang Ngang, Hang Buom, Ma May, Hang Bac, Hang Mam, Nguyen Huu Huan and Ly Thai To. Before this change the rule of thumb was simply to avoid the barriers; now the closure is also a scheduled vehicle ban, so a scooter still inside the perimeter at 19:00 is in a zone that has been closed to it. Earlier drafts proposed different hours, and some websites still repeat them, so rely on the 19:00 to midnight rule reported at launch.

The same plan covers every weekend night, not only the busiest ones. See our [Hoan Kiem area page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hoan-kiem/) and [Old Quarter page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/old-quarter/) for the neighbourhood context.

## Where a scooter can wait: the outer ring

The city's plan does not name one official scooter car park for the walking streets. What it does give you is an approach.

**Parking points.** The Department of Construction, working with the wards, arranged 220 parking lots and points around the low-emission area as of 25 June 2026. Lao Dong's photo report from July 2026 showed examples on Trieu Quoc Dat and Quan Su streets and a lot at the Ring Road 1 gateway on Tran Khat Chan. Treat these as examples of the type of place to look for, not as a promise of free spaces on your night.

**Park-and-ride lots.** The city also plans ten transfer lots, in places such as Long Bien, Tran Khat Chan, Tran Khanh Du, Tran Quang Khai and Yen Phu, so that residents can leave a private vehicle and continue by bus or urban rail. These are planned, not guaranteed to be open yet, so check before you rely on one.

**Passenger transfer points.** Four points serve drop-off and pick-up: Ba Trieu between Hang Khay and Hai Ba Trung, Tran Nhat Duat between Cao Thang and O Quan Chuong, Tran Quang Khai between Hang Mam and Hang Thung, and Tran Khanh Du in front of the History Museum. They are for dropping people, not for leaving a scooter all evening.

Earlier visitor guides suggested parking on streets near the lake such as Hai Ba Trung, Quang Trung, Ngo Quyen and Ly Thai To. Ly Thai To is on Zone 1's boundary, so confirm that a given spot is outside the closed area. Our guide to [where to park a scooter near Hoan Kiem Lake](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/where-to-park-a-scooter-near-hoan-kiem-lake/) explains how guarded lots, tickets and photographs work, and our general [parking guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-motorbike-in-hanoi/) covers claim tickets and what to do if the bike is missing.

### A quick test for a spot

1. Is it clearly outside the Zone 1 perimeter, and does the route to it avoid the closed streets before 19:00?
2. Does it have an attendant, a ticket and posted hours that run past the time you plan to leave?
3. Is the bike on a legal part of the street? Law 36/2024/QH15 bars stopping or parking on a pedestrian crossing and within five metres of a junction.
4. Can you walk to the lake or the market in a reasonable time?

## Three ways to do an evening without riding into the closure

**1. Park outside before 19:00 and walk.** This is the classic plan. Arrive in daylight or early evening, park in a guarded lot on the outer ring, and enter on foot. It costs you the walk each way and gives you the cleanest result.

**2. Leave the scooter at your hotel and use buses or bicycles.** The city made subsidised bus routes inside Ring Road 1 free from 1 July 2026 to 30 June 2027, though not commercial or tourist buses. It also placed 44 public bicycle stations with 456 bikes in Hoan Kiem and Cua Nam wards. Our [riding at night guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-at-night-in-hanoi/) explains why leaving the bike behind is often the easiest night.

**3. Ride a taxi or ride-hail to a transfer point.** Drop-off points sit on the edge of the zone. This is the plan for a group that wants to eat and drink freely.

## Match the evening to the shop's hours

Nguyen Ha's shop at Alley 5 Nguyen Van Cu Street, Bo De Ward, Long Bien District, is open from 09:00 to 21:00 daily, and one rental day counts as 24 hours. The weekend walking street runs until midnight. So a night out can end long after the shop has closed, which means you are responsible for the bike overnight.

- **If you return the bike before 21:00** you can enjoy the evening without it, but you also give up the ride home.
- **If you keep the bike,** you need a secure place to leave it overnight; a hotel with a guarded bike area, for example. Ask before you book.
- **If you return it late,** the shop's late-return rule is VND 30,000 per hour for the first one to five hours and one extra rental day from six hours. Ask about the exact terms before signing. ## If you plan to eat and drink

Street food and beer are part of the walking-street evening. Law 36/2024/QH15 bars riding with any alcohol in the blood or breath, so the safe plan is not to ride after drinking. Our [drink-driving guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/drink-driving-rules-motorbike-vietnam/) explains the rule. Decide your transport home before the first drink, not after, and consider walking, a taxi or ride-hailing.

## Other weekend walking spaces in Hanoi

The Hoan Kiem area is the best-known, but not the only one. Travel guides published in 2025 list several others, each with its own schedule, which you should confirm before you go:

- **Tran Nhan Tong and Thien Quang Lake** in Hai Ba Trung, with a reported schedule that starts on Saturday morning and runs to midnight on Sunday.
- **Ngoc Khanh Lake** in Ba Dinh, reported as open on Friday evening and on Saturday and Sunday until 22:00.
- **Trinh Cong Son** beside West Lake in Tay Ho, a roughly one-kilometre walking street. Our [West Lake by scooter guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/tay-ho-west-lake-by-scooter/) covers the lakeside loop.
- **Dao Ngoc and Ngu Xa** in Ba Dinh, a night food street.

The 2026 vehicle ban described above applies to the Hoan Kiem pilot zone, not to these places. Some of them may have their own barriers, so read the signs on arrival.

## FAQ: weekend walking streets and your scooter

### Can I ride a scooter into the Hoan Kiem walking street at night?

Not from 19:00 to midnight on Fridays, Saturdays and Sundays. Since 1 July 2026 motorbikes, motorcycles and cars are barred from the core zone in those hours, with exceptions for military and public-security vehicles.

### Is there one official place to park for the walking streets?

Not that we could verify. The city arranged many parking points and planned transfer lots around the area. Use an attended lot outside the perimeter.

### Does the ban apply to electric scooters?

Reports describe the ban as applying to motorbikes, motorcycles and cars without exempting electric models. Do not assume an exemption.

### What if the road is closed for an event?

Follow the diversion. Hanoi has announced temporary closures around the lake for events, so check the day's notices.

### Are walking streets elsewhere in Hanoi covered by the same ban?

No, the ban belongs to the Hoan Kiem pilot zone. Other walking spaces have their own schedules.
