Ask for an extension before your current rental period ends, ideally a day or more ahead, by phone or message during the shop's opening hours, and do not treat the new period as agreed until the shop has confirmed the price, the new return time and which bike you will keep. At Nguyen Ha in Long Bien the published terms say that any extension must be agreed before the existing period ends, including price and return arrangements, and that telling the shop you are late does not waive a late-return charge. This guide explains when to ask, what to include in the request, what to confirm in the reply, what can stop an extension from working and what to do if the shop cannot extend the same bike.

## Why the timing of the request matters

An extension is a change to the agreement you signed, so it needs the shop's consent. The shop's terms say so directly: an extension must be agreed before the existing period ends. If you simply keep the bike without asking, the shop treats it as a late return, which at the published reference rate is VND 30,000 per hour for hours one to five and one extra rental day from six hours late. Letting the shop know you are running late is appreciated but does not remove that charge.

There is also a practical reason to ask early. The shop's bikes are scheduled, and availability changes daily, so the model is confirmed when you book rather than promised ahead. If another renter is due to collect your bike, an extension on the same bike may be impossible, but the shop can only tell you that if it hears from you with time to spare. Asking a day ahead leaves room for a swap, whereas asking an hour before the due time leaves almost none.

Remember how the period is counted. A rental day is 24 hours from the handover time written in the contract, so your due time is a specific hour on a specific date, not simply "the end of the day". Check the contract copy for that hour before you write to the shop. Our [rental guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-guide/) explains how the handover time anchors everything.

## When to ask

There is no published deadline in days, so use judgement and ask as soon as the need becomes likely rather than certain.

- **As soon as your plans change.** A new flight date, a trip extension or a weather change all justify a message the same day.
- **A day or more before the due time** if you can. This gives the shop time to check its schedule.
- **During opening hours.** The shop is open 09:00 to 21:00 daily, and the booking contact is 0334 699 969 by phone or Zalo, or +84 334 699 969 on WhatsApp. A message sent late in the evening may only be seen the next morning.
- **Not at the last minute.** If the due time is 10:00 and the shop opens at 09:00, a request made at 09:45 is too late to arrange anything calmly.

If you are unsure whether you will need more time, it is better to ask early and withdraw than to wait. Our guide to [choosing a rental period when plans may change](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/choosing-a-rental-period-when-your-travel-plans-may-change/) helps you decide how long a period to ask for.

## What to include in your request

A good request lets the shop answer in one reply. Include:

1. **Your name and the bike.** The model and, if you have it, the plate or contract reference.
2. **The current due time.** The date and the hour from your contract.
3. **The new return date and time.** Be specific, for example a date and an hour, and not "a couple more days".
4. **A fallback.** Whether you would accept a different bike if yours is booked.
5. **A question about price.** How the extra days would be charged.
6. **A question about payment.** When and how the extra rent is paid.

A short message might read: "Hello, I am renting a scooter and the contract says it is due back at 10:00 on the 14th. Can I keep it until 10:00 on the 17th? Please tell me the price for the extra days, whether the same bike is available and how I pay." It names the facts the shop needs, and it asks the questions you need answered.

Written messages leave a record. If you speak by phone, follow up with a short message that repeats what was agreed.

## What to confirm in the reply

Do not stop at "yes". The terms speak of price and return arrangements, so confirm both.

| Point | What to ask | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| New due time | The exact date and hour | Late charges count from the agreed time |
| Price of the extra days | Per day, or as a package | Weekly and monthly packages are priced on their own, not as the daily rate multiplied |
| Which bike | The same one or a replacement | Condition and fuel checks start again if the bike changes |
| Payment | How and when | Avoids a surprise at the return |
| Deposit | Whether anything changes | The cash deposit of VND 2 million to 5 million is set by the bike and the contract |
| Contract | Whether the new time is written down | The signed contract is what finally applies |

The shop does not publish a standard price for extensions, so treat the figure as something to ask, not something to assume. In particular do not assume the extra days will be charged at the same rate as the original period. The published rules say weekly and monthly packages are individually priced, so a few extra days may be quoted on the daily rate while a larger extension may be quoted as a week. Our guide to [weekly and monthly rental questions](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/weekly-monthly-motorbike-rental-hanoi/) lists what long-term renters should ask.

## Extending by a few days or by a week

A short extension and a long one raise different questions.

### A day or two

Ask for the daily price and for confirmation that the extra day runs for 24 hours from your existing due time. A shorter extension still counts as a day, so do not expect a part-day price. Never plan to use the late-return scale as if it were an extension option: it is a charge for lateness, and the shop describes it that way.

### A week or more

Ask whether a weekly package is available for the extra period and how it compares with counting days. For a stay that keeps growing, the shop's own advice applies: a weekly package that can be extended is often better than a month paid up front. Our guide on [choosing a manual motorbike for a week-long rental](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/choosing-a-manual-motorbike-for-a-week-long-hanoi-rental/) is useful if your rental has turned into a longer one.

## When an extension is not possible

Sometimes the answer will be no, or yes for a different bike. Reasons can include another booking for the same bike, a bike that needs servicing or a request that cannot be matched to available dates. In that case:

- Ask whether a similar bike is free for the extra period, and whether the swap can be done at the shop at a convenient time.
- If a swap happens, repeat the handover routine: brakes, lights, tyres, mirrors, bodywork, fuel level and photographs. Our [10-minute inspection checklist](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-scooter-inspection-checklist/) covers it.
- Return the original bike on time. Do not keep it past the due time hoping for a later yes.
- If you cannot continue with the shop, plan the end of the rental and the return of the deposit under the contract.

## The legal background in brief

Under Article 481 of Vietnam's Civil Code, the lessee must pay rent in full and on time as agreed, and under Article 482 the lessee must return the leased property in the condition in which it was received, normal wear and tear excepted. The code does not set a specific procedure for extensions, so what you agree with the shop and write down is what matters in practice. That is also why the shop's FAQ describes its terms as published in October 2026 and says that the contract for your specific bike is what finally applies.

## After the extension is agreed

- **Update your own notes.** Write the new due time next to the handover time.
- **Keep the confirmation.** A screenshot or a copy of the message is enough as a record.
- **Pay as agreed.** If payment is due at a set time, do not leave it to the return.
- **Plan fuel and return.** Petrol bikes come back with about 0.5 to 1 litre, and an empty tank at return costs VND 20,000. Our guide on [returning your rental motorbike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/returning-your-rental-motorbike-hanoi/) explains the checks at the end.
- **Ask again if plans change again.** A second extension is a new agreement and needs the same early request.

The shop's [contact page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/contact/) gives directions and the [prices page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/prices/) shows the reference rates.

## FAQ: extending a motorbike rental

### Can I extend by message instead of in person?

The shop takes bookings by phone, Zalo and WhatsApp, so a message is a reasonable way to ask. Keep the reply as a record.

### Is an extension guaranteed?

No. Availability changes daily and the same bike is not promised.

### How much does an extension cost?

The shop does not publish a fixed extension price. Ask for the price per day or per package when you request it.

### What if I ask after the due time has passed?

The shop's terms say an extension must be agreed before the period ends, so a late request risks the late-return charge.

### Does the deposit change when I extend?

Ask. The deposit is set by the bike and the contract, and the shop will say whether anything changes.

### Will I be asked to swap bikes?

Possibly. If so, inspect the replacement as you would at the original handover.
