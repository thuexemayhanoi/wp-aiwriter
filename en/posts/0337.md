To find legitimate guarded parking near a Hanoi tourist sight, decide where you will park before you ride, choose a lot that has a defined area off the carriageway, an attendant, a posted price and time basis, and a ticket or electronic receipt, and treat anyone who runs into the street to wave you in as a warning sign. In 2026 the city has been moving licensed lots to electronic, cashless collection, which makes a proper lot easier to recognise than before. This guide is about finding and judging a place, not about how parking works in general: it covers what changed in 2026, a checklist for the entrance, red flags, a way to search in advance, sight-by-sight patterns, and what to do if a charge looks wrong.

## What changed in 2026

According to a Bao Ve Phap Luat report of 28 June 2026, Hanoi's People's Committee Plan No. 198/KH-UBND, a programme called "Do xe minh bach - Chuyen doi so Thu do" (transparent parking, digital transformation of the capital), requires parking lots and points to apply technology for management and electronic payment from 1 July 2026. The report says Hanoi's Department of Construction counts about 3,000 licensed paid parking lots and points, and that many lots had already been taking cashless payment by QR code or automatic plate recognition for more than a year.

A few practical details from that report:

- Fees are calculated automatically, and staff cannot adjust them. The operator VETC says charging by time block is common, often 60 minutes per entry, so a short stop can be billed as a full block.
- If a traffic account lacks funds, a QR code can be used at the parking point.
- Transactions are stored electronically, and a user can look up the history and download an e-invoice in the operator's app or website.
- VETC says a billing error reported online is refunded within 48 hours of verification.

This followed a pilot reported by Vietnam+ in July 2024, which ran from 15 April to 17 June across nine inner districts including Hoan Kiem and Long Bien at 64 designated parking areas, where nearly 90 per cent of transactions were cashless.

A separate VietNamNet report of 12 October 2026 describes a draft decision that Hanoi was consulting on. It would require motorbike lots to use technology, allow QR or other cashless payment, and require price, time basis and payment method to be posted at the entrance and the payment point.

It would also require a receipt, and complaints to be taken through at least two channels with wrong charges returned within three working days, and keep cash as an exception when the system fails. It is a draft, so do not quote it as a rule. Even so, it shows the direction: a legitimate lot should be able to show you its price and give you a record.

Not every lot is fully digital yet, and the sources describe requirements for licensed points, not for the informal spaces beside them. Use the checklist below instead of assuming.

## The entrance checklist

Before you leave the bike, look for these six things. You do not need all of them in every lot, but a place with none should not get your key.

1. **A defined parking area off the carriageway.** Bikes are in rows in a marked space or yard, not strung along a pavement or the road edge.
2. **A posted price and time basis.** The sign should say what you pay and for how long. The draft decision would require this at the entrance and the payment point.
3. **An attendant who records the bike.** A ticket, a numbered tag, a plate scan or an electronic receipt that ties your bike to a record.
4. **A way to pay that you can see.** A QR code, card reader or posted payment method. If they say the system is down, that is a flag to be polite and extra careful.
5. **Opening hours and a closing time.** Ask before you walk away.
6. **A contact point for complaints.** A phone number or a notice on the sign. The draft decision describes at least two complaint channels.

When the lot is part of a building, hospital or shopping centre, the operator and rules may differ from a street lot, but the same questions apply.

## Red flags that mean keep riding

- **A tout steps into the road or waves you down.** A 2019 DTiNews report described unlicensed lots around the Hoan Kiem pedestrian area at weekends, with staff rushing onto the street to invite riders and charging several times the printed ticket price. It is old reporting, but the pattern is the one to avoid.
- **No price is visible.** In March 2026 a Hanoi tourism department official told VnExpress International that failing to display prices publicly is a fraudulent practice, and said the department had set up a tourist hotline.

- **The price is quoted only after you park.** Ask first.
- **"Park here, I will watch it" with no ticket.** A casual promise from someone with no record is not a service.
- **Bikes are on the pavement or in front of arches or gates.** See the section on where you may stop at all.
- **They ask for the key, helmet or documents to be left with them.** A claim ticket or tag is enough. Do not leave your passport, licence or rental contract with a lot.

## Where you may stop at all

Article 18 of Law No. 36/2024/QH15 on Road Traffic Order and Safety, which took effect on 1 January 2025, sets out where vehicles may not stop or park. In the English translation published on luatvietnam.vn, the list includes pedestrian crossings, intersections and the 5 metres from their edges, in front of the gates and within 5 metres of both sides of gates of agency headquarters that have driveways, road sections wide enough for only one lane of vehicles, and the railway safety scope. It also bars parking on the carriageway and pavements in contravention of the law. On streets, the nearest wheel must not be more than 0.25 metre from the roadside or pavement edge.

Article 69 allows traffic police to remove a vehicle that is parked in breach of these rules and is causing obstruction, and the operator or owner pays the removal charge. So a convenient pavement gap is not just disliked by residents; it can cost you the bike for a while. This is a general summary of an unofficial English translation; the Vietnamese text controls, and current signs and officers' instructions take priority.

## How to search before you ride

Choose the lot at your desk, not at the sight.

1. **Check the sight's own information.** The Hoa Lo Prison Relic's visitor rules, for example, say to park only at the designated lot, so look on the official site or ask at the entrance. Where an official site names a lot, prefer it.
2. **Search a map for parking near the sight.** Use the terms "motorbike parking", "bai do xe" and "diem trong giu xe". Switch to street view or satellite view to see whether there is a real entrance, a barrier or a yard, and check the listed hours.

3. **Look at two or three options, not one.** Pick one that is a few minutes on foot from the sight rather than the one right at the gate.

4. **Ask your accommodation.** Hotels and homestays usually know which lots nearby are attended.
5. **Ask the shop.** Nguyen Ha's shop at Alley 5 Nguyen Van Cu Street, Bo De Ward, Long Bien District can be reached on 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969), 09:00 to 21:00 daily, but do not expect it to run parking for you or to know every lot. Its role is to rent you the bike.
6. **Save the lot as a pin and note the closing time.** Take a photograph of the entrance sign when you arrive.
7. **Have a second choice.** If the first lot is full, move on rather than squeezing in elsewhere.

Maps and reviews are not licences. Treat a high rating as one data point and trust what you can see on the spot more.

## Paying and keeping proof

- **Ask the price and time basis before you hand over the bike.** If they quote one price and then another, ride away.
- **Keep a small amount of cash as a backup,** and ask whether QR or card is accepted. Our guide to [paying for fuel, parking and tolls as a visitor](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/paying-fuel-parking-tolls-hanoi-visitor/) covers payment methods.
- **Keep the ticket or e-receipt until you are on the bike and away.** The tag number is how they find your bike.
- **Photograph the price sign, the ticket and the bike** with your plate visible.
- **Lock the steering and take the key.** The shop does not provide an extra lock, so the steering lock and an attended place do the work.
- **Take your helmet and valuables.** One basic helmet per rider is included in the rental, but you are responsible for it. Do not leave it on the mirror.
- **Do not leave documents in the lot.** The ticket is enough.

## If a charge looks wrong

1. **Stay calm and polite.** Arguing at a gate rarely helps.
2. **Point to the posted price and ask for the receipt.** For a cashless charge, note the transaction details.
3. **Take photographs** of the sign, the ticket and the amount.
4. **If a system operator is shown,** use its online reconciliation. VETC says billing errors reported through its app or site are refunded within 48 hours of verification.
5. **For a clear overcharge,** note the place, time and photographs and report it to the tourist hotline mentioned by the Hanoi tourism department, or to the local ward authority.
6. **Tell the shop** if the dispute involves the rental bike or if the bike is missing.

If a bike is not where you left it, do not assume theft. ## FAQ: guarded parking near Hanoi sights

### How can I tell a licensed lot from an informal one?

Look for a marked area off the carriageway, a posted price and time basis, an attendant who records the bike and a ticket or receipt. Touts in the road are a warning.

### Do Hanoi parking lots take cashless payment now?

Reports say licensed lots were required to apply technology and electronic payment from 1 July 2026, and many already used QR codes. Keep some cash as a backup.

### Can I park on the pavement if other bikes are there?

No. Other bikes are not a permission, and a bike parked in breach of the rules can be removed at the owner's cost.

### Will the shop find parking for me?

It can give general advice by phone, but it is a rental shop, not a parking service, and it does not run parking at tourist sights.

### What if a lot has no price sign?

Ask the price and time basis first. If you do not get a clear answer, go to another lot.

### How much time should I allow to find parking?

Plan a few extra minutes at busy sights and have a second choice, especially in the evening and on weekends.
