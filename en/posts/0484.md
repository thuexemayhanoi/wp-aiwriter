A helmet protects you only if it fits snugly, sits level and low on your forehead, and is fastened every time. Put it on with the strap open, pull it down over the back of your head, check that it sits about two fingers above your eyebrows, close the chin strap so that no more than two fingers fit between strap and chin, and then test it: shake your head, and try to roll the helmet forward off your head from the back. If it moves independently of your skin, or comes off, it is too big or too loose. Law 36/2024/QH15 requires the driver and every person carried on a motorcycle or moped to wear a helmet that meets the national technical regulation and to fasten the chin strap properly (Article 33).

This guide explains how to choose a size and shape, how to put the helmet on, how to adjust the strap, the tests to run before every ride, the most common mistakes, what to check on a rental helmet, how to care for it and what the law and the fines say about fastening. It is general information based on the law and published fitting guidance as of October 2026, not legal or medical advice.

## Why fit matters

The World Health Organization says correct helmet use can reduce the risk of death in a crash by more than six times and the risk of brain injury by up to 74 per cent. The word "correct" is doing the work. A helmet that is too loose can rotate, tip back and expose the forehead, or come off in a fall. A helmet that is too tight or poorly shaped for your head causes pressure points that make you want to take it off or ride with it undone. A strap hanging loose does nothing at all, although it looks like protection.

Helmet-fitting guides make the same points: the helmet should be snug without causing pain, should not move on your head when you shake it, and should stay in place when you open your mouth wide. The standard also matters. ## Step 1: choose the size and shape

1. **Measure your head.** Use a soft tape around the widest part of your head, about an inch (2.5 cm) above your eyebrows, or use a string and measure it against a ruler.

2. **Read the size chart for that helmet.** Sizes differ between brands, so use the chart, not the size you wore last time.

3. **Think about shape.** Heads are rounder or more oval, and a helmet that matches yours will fit evenly. If one spot hurts after a few minutes, the shape may be wrong, not just tight.

4. **Expect a new helmet to feel snug.** Guides note that the lining compresses with use, often by a noticeable amount in the first 15 to 20 hours, so a helmet that feels very slightly tight at first may become comfortable. A helmet that is loose when new will only get looser.

5. **Try on before you buy,** and wear it for several minutes. ## Step 2: put it on

1. **Open the strap fully** and hold the strap ends, one in each hand.

2. **Spread the helmet's opening** and pull it on from the front, rolling it over the back of your head, since the back is usually the widest part.

3. **Set it level.** The helmet should sit low on the forehead, with the front edge about two fingers above your eyebrows and the eyeport lined up with your eyes. It should not tilt back like a hat or forward over your eyes.

4. **Check the cheek pads.** They should touch your cheeks firmly, without squeezing your jaw. There should be no gap between your forehead and the lining.

5. **Check your sight lines.** Look left and right. The helmet should not block your side view.

6. **Move your hair out of the way.** Tie long hair back, so it does not push the helmet out of place or whip across your eyes.

7. **Put glasses on before the helmet** if you wear them, and check that the arms do not press into your temples.

## Step 3: fasten the strap

1. **Pull the strap snug under your chin,** with the side straps coming down just in front of and below your ears. Fitting guides describe the side straps forming a V under each ear.

2. **Leave a gap of no more than two fingers** between the strap and your chin. Passenger-safety guides advise pulling the strap as tight as you can without choking.

3. **Fasten the buckle fully.** A quick-release buckle should click, and a double D-ring strap should pass through both rings and back through the first one, and be pulled tight.

4. **Tuck the loose end** into the keeper, so it cannot flap in your face or catch.

5. **Check that the strap is flat,** not twisted, and that it lies against the skin and does not slide forward onto your chin tip.

6. **Open your mouth wide.** The helmet should resist lifting. If it rides up, tighten the strap.

A memory aid in several guides is the "2-2-2" check: about two fingers above the eyebrows, no more than two fingers between strap and chin, and the side straps forming a V about two fingers below the ears. Use it as a quick test, not a replacement for the shake and lift tests.

## Step 4: test it every time

| Test | How to do it | What you want |
|---|---|---|
| **Shake test** | Fasten the strap, hold the helmet and move it side to side and up and down | Your skin moves with the helmet; it does not slide over your head |
| **Chin-bar test** | Grip the chin or the edge of the helmet and move it | Your cheeks move with the helmet, not the helmet over your cheeks |
| **Lift or roll-off test** | With the strap fastened, put a hand at the back edge and try to roll the helmet forward off your head | It stays on, and does not expose your forehead |
| **Open-mouth test** | Open your mouth wide | The helmet does not rise |
| **Nod test** | Nod and tilt your head | The helmet stays fixed; the strap stays put |
| **Vision test** | Turn your head each way | You see the side mirrors and the road without moving the helmet |

If the helmet fails the lift test or turns on your head, it is too big or the strap is too loose. Try a smaller size or a different shape. ## Common mistakes

- **Riding with the strap undone** or hanging loose, which looks like wearing a helmet but offers little protection and is an offence.

- **Wearing the helmet tipped back,** which exposes the forehead.

- **Choosing a helmet that is too big,** so it moves, makes noise and may come off.

- **Twisting the strap** or leaving the buckle half closed.

- **Wearing a cap, hood or thick scarf under the helmet,** which changes the fit and can make it loose.

- **Using a helmet that was dropped from a height, or that has a cracked shell or frayed strap.** Our guide to [what to check if a helmet has been dropped](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-to-check-if-a-helmet-has-been-dropped/) explains the inspection.

- **Sharing a helmet between people with different head sizes** without re-fitting it.

- **Wearing a decorative or novelty helmet** that does not meet the standard.

## Check a rental helmet before you leave

The shop's published terms say one basic helmet per rider is included at no charge, and that both sides inspect brakes, tyres, lights, mirrors, bodywork, accessories and the fuel level before you leave. Add the helmet to your own check.

1. **Look at the shell** for cracks, deep gouges and loose parts.

2. **Look at the strap and the buckle,** for fraying, stiff or broken fastenings.

3. **Look at the lining,** for crumbling or compressed foam.

4. **Check the size** against your head, and ask for another if it fails the tests.

5. **Check the visor,** if there is one, for scratches that blur your view.

6. **Ask whether it has been cleaned.** If you are uneasy about hygiene, ask for a spare or carry a thin cap, but test the fit again with the cap on.

7. **Take a photograph** of the helmet at handover, so any later damage is clear. Our guide to [rented motorbike accessories: helmet, mirrors and phone holder](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rented-motorbike-accessories-helmet-mirrors-and-phone-holder/) explains how to record it.

## Care

- **Store it in a cool, dry place,** out of the sun.

- **Set it down carefully,** not on a seat where it can roll off.

- **Clean it gently** with mild soap and water, and let it dry. Do not use strong solvents.

- **Check it regularly** for cracks, loose parts and a worn strap.

- **Replace it** after a crash or a hard impact, if it shows damage, or if the lining has worn out.

## What the law says about fastening

Article 33 of Law 36/2024/QH15 requires drivers and persons carried on two-wheeled motorcycles, three-wheeled motorcycles or mopeds to wear crash helmets up to the national technical regulations and to fasten the chin strap properly. Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, as read on the Government portal, sets a fine of VND 400,000 to 600,000 for a rider who wears no helmet or does not fasten the strap, and the same range for carrying a passenger without a helmet or with the strap undone, with exclusions for certain cases. Check the current text of the decree before relying on a figure. ## A quick checklist

1. **Measure, read the chart, choose a snug size.**

2. **Roll it on, level and low, two fingers above the eyebrows.**

3. **Strap snug, two fingers at most under the chin, buckle closed, end tucked.**

4. **Run the shake, lift and open-mouth tests.**

5. **Check the shell, strap and lining before every rental.**

6. **Do it again every time you put it on.**

## FAQ: fitting and fastening a helmet

### How tight should a motorbike helmet be?

Snug on the cheeks and forehead, with even pressure and no sliding when you shake your head. A new helmet should feel snug, since the lining compresses with use.

### How tight should the chin strap be?

No more than two fingers between the strap and your chin, and tight enough that the helmet does not lift when you open your mouth. Not so tight that you cannot breathe or swallow.

### How do I know if my helmet is too big?

If it moves independently of your skin, rolls forward off your head in the lift test or rises when you open your mouth, it is too big.

### Do I need to fasten the strap on a short trip?

Yes. Article 33 of Law 36/2024/QH15 requires the chin strap to be fastened properly, and a short trip can end in a fall.

### Can I use a cap under the helmet?

A thin cap may be acceptable, but test the fit with it on, since it changes the fit. Avoid anything thick or bulky.
