If you ride an automatic scooter at home, a Honda Wave is not a harder machine, but its controls are in different places and two of your habits will let you down. The brake you used with your left hand is now a pedal under your right foot, there is a gear lever under your left foot instead of a second brake lever, and the engine does not shut itself off or restart when you twist the throttle. There is no clutch lever to squeeze, which surprises many people in the opposite direction. This guide maps each hand and foot control on a Wave against what an automatic scooter such as a Honda Vision does, uses Honda's own owner's manuals for the facts, and ends with a calm first five minutes. It is about controls and habits; gear-change technique is covered in our other guides.

## Your hands and feet: the map in one table

On an automatic scooter both hands work the brakes and both feet rest on a flat floor. On a Wave the work is shared differently.

| Body part | Honda Vision (automatic scooter) | Honda Wave (semi-automatic) |
|---|---|---|
| Right hand | Throttle and a brake lever | Throttle and the front brake lever |
| Left hand | A brake lever | No lever, but still the indicator, horn and dip switches |
| Left foot | Rests flat on the floor | Works the gear lever |
| Right foot | Rests flat on the floor | Works the rear brake pedal |

Honda's Vision manual says to release the throttle and use "both the front brake lever and the rear brake lever" at the same time. The Wave RSX FI manual, by contrast, lists a front brake lever and a rear brake pedal. That one difference is the most important to rehearse, because a hand that reaches for a left lever that is not there wastes a vital half-second.

Use both brakes together, as Honda advises for both bikes, and practise finding the rear pedal with your right foot before you move.

## Habit 1: the left hand squeezes nothing

A rider used to a scooter will reach for the left lever by reflex when something happens. On a Wave there is no lever there. Hold the left grip normally and learn the switches around it. Before moving off, press the indicator, horn and dip switch once each so your thumb knows where they are.

The same applies to the other side of the scooter habit. Because there is no clutch lever either, the bike does not need your left hand for pulling away. The clutch works automatically. Practise keeping the left hand loose rather than tense.

## Habit 2: your left foot now matters

The gear lever sits under your left foot. Honda's manual says the gearbox has four forward gears, and that you should always ease off the throttle before changing gear, press the lever lightly with the toe until it moves down, and avoid resting your foot on the lever while riding because it can damage the shifting and clutch mechanisms.

That last point is easy to forget for a rider whose left foot has always lived flat on a floor. Keep the foot on the footrest, move the toe only when you shift, and bring it back. Close-toed shoes make this far easier; our piece on [riding a semi-automatic bike in sandals](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-a-semi-automatic-bike-in-sandals-practical-safety-risks/) explains why.

The shift pattern is also unfamiliar. The Wave has a rotary four-speed pattern: with the bike stopped you can go from fourth gear straight round to neutral, but while moving you cannot go from fourth to neutral directly. Our guide to [Honda Wave gears](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/honda-wave-gears-starting-shifting-and-stopping-smoothly/) takes you through starting, shifting and stopping in order, so we do not repeat it here.

## Habit 3: the right foot is now a brake

On a scooter the floor is a place to put your feet. On a Wave the right footrest area includes the rear brake pedal, so a foot placed carelessly may press or hover over it. Honda gives a free-travel figure of 20 to 30 mm at the pedal end for the RSX FI, which is why the pedal should feel firm after a short, easy press. If it feels very long or very soft, say so; our guide to [checking the rear brake on a Honda Wave](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/checking-the-rear-brake-on-a-honda-wave/) explains what is normal.

Learn the pedal's position by feel with the bike on its stand, then rest your boot beside it, ready.

## Starting: different checks, different order

A scooter often will not start unless you hold a brake lever, and the engine may stop itself at traffic lights. The Vision's official page says its idling stop system can switch the engine off when the bike has been halted for more than 3 seconds and starts it again when you twist the throttle, and its manual says the starter works only when a brake lever is squeezed and the side stand is up.

A Wave works differently, and Honda's manual for the Wave RSX FI describes the sequence:

1. Turn the ignition to On.
2. Shift to neutral, shown by the N indicator.
3. Release the throttle fully and press the start switch.

The Wave manual describes no idling stop system, so the engine keeps running at the lights. A rider who expects it to stop will be puzzled; a rider who twists the throttle expecting it to restart will find nothing happens, because it never stopped. The manual also describes a kick starter as a fallback. Do not try it unless the shop shows you; our guide to [the kick starter on an older rental motorbike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-use-the-kick-starter-on-an-older-rental-motorbike/) covers it.

## Reading the dashboard

The Wave RSX FI's dashboard shows the gear position, numbered from 1 to 4, and an N light for neutral. The fuel gauge needle on the red mark means about 1.2 litres remain, according to the manual, out of a 4.0 litre tank. That is a useful fact if you are used to a scooter's digital bar.

If the neutral light and the gear display disagree, or you cannot find neutral, do not force anything. Our [neutral guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/understanding-neutral-on-a-honda-wave/) and the [dashboard lights guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/semi-automatic-motorbike-dashboard-lights-explained/) describe the signals.

## Keys, seat and storage: smaller details that catch scooter riders

Several things around the bike differ from a modern scooter:

- **Key.** The Vision is sold with a Smart Key system on the version Honda lists. The Wave RSX FI uses an ordinary ignition key with a steering lock and a safety shutter over the keyhole, which Honda tells riders to close after parking.
- **Seat.** On the Wave RSX FI the seat opens by pressing the key down at the ignition and lifting the seat.
- **Storage.** Honda limits the Wave RSX FI's under-seat box to 5 kg. The Vision's under-seat box is rated for 10 kg and it also has a front compartment with a very light 1.5 kg limit. Scooter riders are used to bigger space; our guide to [how much luggage a Wave can carry](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-much-luggage-can-you-safely-carry-on-a-honda-wave/) explains the figures.
- **Helmet holder.** Both manuals say the hook under the seat is for use when parked only. Never ride with a helmet hanging from it.

## Three slips scooter riders make in the first hour

**Reaching for a lever that is not there.** In an emergency the left hand closes on the grip and nothing happens. The fix is rehearsal: with the bike on its stand, press the right-foot pedal and the right-hand lever together twenty times.

**Pulling away in the wrong gear.** A scooter never asks which gear it is in. Glance at the gear display before every pull-away, especially after a stop, so that you start in first and not in a higher gear left over from the last block.

**Stopping in gear with the foot off the pedal.** At a red light, keep the right foot on the rear brake pedal or on the ground, not floating, and keep the toe ready for the lever. The bike will not roll away on its own as long as you hold it, but a relaxed scooter habit of letting the bike creep is a poor one here.

## What does not change

Plenty of what you know still applies. The throttle works the same way: smooth in and smooth out. Both brakes are used together. Indicators come before moves. Honda's manual for the Wave advises hands on the bars and feet on the footrests, avoiding loose clothing that could snag a part of the bike, and wearing a proper helmet. Look well ahead, and ride the bike as if the other road users will not see you. Our guide to [crossing Hanoi intersections](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/crossing-hanoi-intersections/) is worth reading before the first busy junction.

## Your first five minutes on the bike

Do this before you leave the shop's side street:

1. **Sit with the bike on its stand.** Find the front lever, rear pedal, gear lever and switches by feel.
2. **Start it** in neutral with the shop beside you. Watch the N light.
3. **Practise one smooth pull-away** on a quiet, flat strip, then stop.
4. **Repeat it three times,** and on the last one change up once before stopping.
5. **Brake from a walking pace** with both brakes at once.

## FAQ: Wave controls for scooter riders

### Is there a clutch lever on a Honda Wave?

No. You change gear with your left foot and the clutch works automatically, so there is no lever on the left handlebar.

### Where is the rear brake on a Wave?

It is a pedal under your right foot, not a lever on the handlebar.

### Can I start a Wave in gear?

Honda's manual tells you to start from neutral. Use the N light to check.

### Does the engine shut off at traffic lights?

The Wave RSX FI manual describes no idling stop system, so expect the engine to keep idling.

### Which foot do I use to change gear?

The left foot, using the toe. Do not rest it on the lever while riding.

### Can I hang my helmet on the hook while riding?

No. Honda says to use the helmet hook only when the bike is parked.
