On a day trip, refuel when the gauge reaches about half, and treat the red mark as the signal to stop at the very next station. A Honda Wave or Yamaha Sirius carries only 3.7 to 4.0 litres of petrol, and once you allow for hills, wind and a passenger, a full tank covers roughly 150 to 200 kilometres. That is enough for most day trips out of Hanoi, but not for a return to the farther destinations, and the countryside has fewer stations than the city. This guide turns the manufacturers' figures into a simple refuelling rule, shows what that means for the routes riders actually choose, explains where to fill up and what to do if the tank runs dry. For fuel costs and a litres-per-trip formula, see our guide to [estimating a fuel budget for a Honda Wave](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/honda-wave-fuel-economy-how-to-estimate-a-trip-budget/). This article is about timing.

## The rule in two lines

1. **Fill before you leave the city,** so every trip starts from a full tank.
2. **Top up at about half a tank,** and never wait for the red mark.

Everything below explains why those two lines work and how to adapt them.

## What the gauge and the tank tell you

Honda's Wave RSX FI manual gives a specific figure for the fuel gauge: when the needle is on the red mark, about 1.2 litres remain in a 4.0-litre tank. That is roughly 30 per cent. At Honda's quoted 1.56 litres per 100 km, 1.2 litres is theoretically enough for about 77 km, but real riding uses more. Small-bike fuel gauges can also be slow to respond and can jump on bumps or hills, so the needle is a guide, not a precise measure.

Tank sizes in the fleets we see:

| Model | Tank | Honda's or Yamaha's figure | Theoretical range | With a 30% allowance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Honda Wave RSX FI | 4.0 litres | 1.56 L per 100 km | about 256 km | about 197 km |
| Yamaha Sirius FI | 4.0 litres | 1.65 L per 100 km | about 242 km | about 186 km |
| Honda Wave Alpha 110 | 3.7 litres | 1.72 L per 100 km | about 215 km | about 165 km |
| Honda Blade 110 | 3.7 litres | 1.85 L per 100 km | about 200 km | about 154 km |

The 30 per cent allowance is our planning assumption for traffic, hills, wind, a passenger and luggage, not a measured figure. It gives a realistic full-tank range of about 150 to 200 kilometres. A sensible refuelling interval is a little over half of that, so about 90 to 120 kilometres, which is where the half-tank rule comes from.

## What that means for popular day trips

Our route guides give approximate distances from central Hanoi. Using them with the ranges above:

| Trip | Distance one way | Round trip | Refuel plan |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ba Vi National Park, with a 12 km summit road | about 60 km | about 120 km plus the climb | Fill before leaving; top up in Son Tay before the climb or on the way back |
| Tam Dao, with a 13 km hairpin climb | about 70 to 85 km | about 140 to 170 km | Fill before leaving; top up at Vinh Yen before the hill road |
| Ninh Binh and Tam Coc | about 95 to 105 km | about 190 to 210 km | At least one refuel, ideally in Ninh Binh before the return |
| Mai Chau | about 135 to 150 km | about 270 to 300 km | Two refuels on a day trip, or plan an overnight |
| Ha Long Bay | about 155 to 170 km | over 300 km | Two refuels; a long day, so consider whether to go |

The pattern is simple. Up to about 70 km one way, a full tank and a top-up on the way back is enough. From about 95 km one way, you need a planned stop. Beyond 130 km one way, plan two stops and check the bike's tyres and chain as well. ## Why not wait for the red mark

It is tempting to run the tank down to save a stop. Three reasons to resist:

- **Hills use more fuel than the figure suggests,** and a long climb with a nearly empty tank can starve the engine when fuel sloshes away from the pickup. Honda's manual says a clogged filter can make the engine run unevenly, and a low tank on a slope is the same kind of problem.
- **Stations thin out in the hills,** and a town you expected to have one may have only a roadside seller.
- **Running dry on a mountain road or a busy national road is dangerous** as well as annoying, because you end up pushing a bike beside traffic.

A top-up at half a tank costs a few minutes and removes all three risks.

## Where to refuel

- **In Hanoi before you leave.** Fill at a station on your way out, not at the last one on the edge of the city if you are in a hurry.
- **In the last large town before a climb,** such as Son Tay for Ba Vi or Vinh Yen for Tam Dao, as our route guides describe.
- **At stations you can see.** Use a station with a pump and a board showing the price, not a roadside bottle seller, if you have a choice. Bottle sellers are common in rural areas and can be a convenience, but you cannot check the quality.
- **Before the return leg.** Fill at your destination, so you start back with a full tank.

## How to refuel safely

Honda's manual tells owners how to fill the tank: switch off the engine, open the seat, unscrew the fuel cap anticlockwise and remove it, and fill no higher than the lower edge of the filler neck. Refit the cap and tighten it clockwise, with the arrow marks lined up, and close the seat. In practice:

- **Switch off the engine and step off the bike.**
- **Do not smoke or use a phone near the pump.**
- **Do not overfill.** Honda says not to fill above the lower edge of the filler neck. Fuel in the neck can spill on the engine or the exhaust.
- **Check that the cap is tight** before you ride off.
- **Count your change** and keep the receipt if you want one.

## What to do if you run dry

If the engine starts to cough and lose power, pull over at once and switch off. On a small bike, you may have a few hundred metres of fuel in reserve, but not many. Options:

1. **Push the bike to the nearest station or house.** A small bike is light enough to push for a short distance.
2. **Ask a local for a bottle of petrol.** Roadside stalls and houses often sell it. Pay a fair price and use a clean funnel.
3. **Call the shop** on 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969), open 09:00 to 21:00 daily, and describe where you are.

After adding fuel, the engine may take a few tries to restart. Honda's manual says that if the engine does not start within five seconds with the electric starter you should switch off, wait ten seconds and try again. ## FAQ: refuelling on a day trip

### How often should I refuel on a day trip?

At about half a tank, which on most small bikes means every 90 to 120 km. Do not wait for the red mark.

### What does the red mark mean?

On the Honda Wave RSX FI, Honda says about 1.2 litres remain at the red mark. Refuel at the next station.

### How far can a Wave go on a full tank?

On Honda's figures, over 250 km in theory, but plan on 150 to 200 km in real riding.

### Can I buy petrol from a roadside stall?

Many riders do, especially in rural areas. A station with a pump is more reliable if you have the choice.

### What should I do if I run out of fuel?

Pull over, push the bike to a station or house, buy a bottle of petrol if needed and call the shop if you are far from help.

### Do I have to return the bike with a full tank?

Not at Nguyen Ha. Bikes come back with roughly 0.5 to 1 litre, and only an empty tank is charged.
