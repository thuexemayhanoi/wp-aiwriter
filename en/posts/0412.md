If your return time changes, contact the shop as soon as you know, say exactly how much later or earlier you will be, and ask for the new time to be confirmed in writing. At Nguyen Ha in Long Bien a return 1 to 5 hours late is charged at VND 30,000 per hour, a return 6 hours or more late costs one more rental day, advance notice does not waive the charge, and an early return does not refund unused rent. Because the shop is open 09:00 to 21:00 daily, a change that pushes you towards or past closing time needs an arrangement before it happens. This guide sorts the common changes into cases, explains what to do in each, covers returns outside opening hours and delays caused by the bike, and lists what to keep as a record.

## Start by naming the kind of change

The right response depends on the kind of change, so identify it before you pick up the phone.

| What has changed | Typical cause | First step |
|---|---|---|
| You will be an hour or two late | Traffic, a long meal, a slow start | Tell the shop now and give a new time |
| You will be several hours late | A longer excursion, a missed connection | Check whether you are crossing the 6-hour mark and consider an extension |
| You need another day | Plans or weather changed | Ask for an extension before the period ends |
| You can return earlier than planned | Shorter trip, early departure | Return in opening hours; do not expect a refund |
| The new time falls outside 09:00 to 21:00 | Late flight, delayed bus | Ask the shop what is possible before the day |
| You cannot ride the bike back | Breakdown, puncture, accident | Call the shop and follow its instructions |

If your plan has really become a longer rental, read our guide on [how to ask for an extension on a motorbike rental](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-ask-for-an-extension-on-a-motorbike-rental/), because an extension agreed in advance is a different thing from a late return.

## Contact the shop first, with numbers

Phone or message the shop on 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969), by phone, Zalo or WhatsApp, between 09:00 and 21:00. A useful message states the original due time, the new time you expect, the reason in a few words and the question you want answered. For example: "My scooter is due at 14:00 today. A delay means I expect to arrive at about 16:30. Please confirm what that means for charges and whether you will still be at the shop."

Giving a clear time does two things. It lets the shop plan the bike's next booking, and it lets you work out where you stand against the published thresholds. A vague "a bit late" helps neither side. Keep the reply, or write down the name of the person and the time of the call.

The shop's published terms are clear that advance notice does not automatically waive the agreed charge. Telling the shop is appreciated and helps planning, but it is not an exemption.

## If you will be a few hours late

The published reference is VND 30,000 per hour for a return between one and five hours after the agreed time. The shop does not publish how a part-hour is counted, so ask when you call whether, for example, an extra twenty minutes counts as a full hour. The short version is that each hour of delay is a separate unit of VND 30,000 until the sixth hour.

Two practical points apply here.

- **Count from the agreed time, not from when you set off.** The due time is a specific hour in the contract, and the delay is measured from that hour.
- **Decide early whether to cross the sixth hour.** Once the delay reaches six hours, the published rule is one extra rental day at the daily rate of that bike. If you are going to be five and a half hours late, think about whether an agreed extension would be a better arrangement than a late return.

## If you will be six hours late or more

At six hours or more, the published reference is one more rental day, charged at the daily rate of that bike. In that situation the question changes from "how late am I" to "do I want another day". If you do, ask for an extension and get the terms agreed. If you do not, return the bike as soon as you can and ask the shop what applies. The published terms do not spell out how charges are counted beyond the first extra day, so ask rather than guess. Our guide on [choosing a rental period when plans may change](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/choosing-a-rental-period-when-your-travel-plans-may-change/) can help you decide how long a period to ask for.

## If you can return earlier than planned

Returning early is allowed in the sense that you can bring the bike back, but unused rent is not refunded. The shop writes this into the contract so that both sides know the bike's schedule from the start. If your plans shorten, do not hurry to return the bike at the cost of a rushed handover check. Bring it back during opening hours, with the fuel it should have, and expect the bike to be compared with the handover record: condition, fuel and time. Our guide on [returning your rental motorbike in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/returning-your-rental-motorbike-hanoi/) describes that check and how the deposit is settled.

There may be a reason to return earlier all the same: a flight, a long journey or simply tiredness. Whether the shop can do anything with the remaining time is for the shop to say, so ask politely, but do not count on a refund.

## If the new time is outside opening hours

The shop's public hours are 09:00 to 21:00 every day. If a delayed bus, a flight or a long excursion means you will arrive after 21:00, ask the shop what can be arranged before you are in that position. The published terms do not describe a night return, so treat the answer as a matter for agreement.

Some precautions apply whatever the answer.

- **Never leave the bike or the key unattended** at a place that has not been agreed with the shop.
- **Do not assume a drop-off is acceptable.** Ask whether it is, and in what form.
- **Do not leave the deposit to chance.** The deposit is settled at the return once the bike has been checked, so an unattended return leaves the check and the settlement undone.
- **Move the return earlier if you can.** If a late return is likely, returning the bike before you collect luggage or travel onward may remove the problem. There is no airport delivery or collection, so the shop is the return point.

## When the bike is the reason

Sometimes the delay is the bike's fault: a puncture, a fault in the drive or a bike that will not start. Call the shop first, describe where you are and what has happened and follow its instructions. According to the shop's FAQ, a technical fault that did not come from the way the bike was used is the shop's to deal with, and it will check, repair or swap the bike as the situation allows. Damage from a collision, an accident or misuse is priced by its cause, the actual condition of the bike and the contract. Ask how the cost of any roadside repair is handled before you agree, and keep the receipt. Our guide on [lost keys, flat tyres and breakdowns](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-bike-breakdown-hanoi-what-to-do/) lists the steps.

If a breakdown makes you late, say so in the first call and ask how the shop will treat the delay, because the cause is part of the picture. Do not assume the late scale applies automatically or that it does not apply; ask and write down the answer.

## The legal background in brief

Vietnam's Civil Code, Article 482, requires a lessee to return leased property in the condition in which it was received, normal wear and tear excepted, and provides that the lessee bears the risk to the property during a period of delay. In plain terms, if you keep a bike past the agreed time, the responsibility for what happens to it stays with you. Article 481 requires rent to be paid in full and on time as agreed. The detail is set by your contract, which is why the shop's FAQ says the contract for your specific bike is what finally applies.

## What to keep as a record

1. **The original due time** from your contract copy.
2. **The message or call note** with the new time you gave and the shop's reply.
3. **Photographs of the bike and the fuel gauge** at the return, taken in the shop.
4. **Any receipt** for a roadside repair.
5. **Confirmation of the charge** before you pay it, in writing if possible.

## FAQ: when your return time changes

### Do I have to tell the shop if I will be late?

Yes, as early as you can. It helps the shop plan, although it does not waive the charge.

### Does telling the shop in advance cancel the late charge?

No. The published terms say advance notice does not automatically waive the agreed charge.

### What if I am only a few minutes late?

The shop does not publish how a part-hour is counted. Ask when you call.

### What if I want to keep the bike for another day?

Ask for an extension before the period ends and get the price and new return time agreed.

### Can I return the bike after 21:00?

The published hours are 09:00 to 21:00. Ask the shop in advance whether anything can be arranged.

### Can I return early and get some rent back?

No. Unused rent is not refunded.
