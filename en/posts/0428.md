To check whether a rental allows an out-of-city trip, ask the shop directly before you leave, name the destination, the dates and the number of riders, read the contract for any area, distance or use clause and get the answer in writing, in a message or on the paper itself. Do not rely on a travel article, a friend's experience or a verbal "no problem" at the counter. A bike that can physically make the trip is not necessarily one the shop has agreed to supply for it, and the agreement should cover the things that go wrong on the road: breakdowns, late return, accidents and who pays.

This guide gives a step-by-step way to check: what to ask, which parts of the contract to read, how to word a message, how to record the answer, what red flags to look for and what to do if the answer is no or "it depends". It builds on our guide to [whether you can take a Hanoi rental motorbike out of the city](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/take-rental-motorbike-out-of-hanoi/) with a practical checklist.

## Why a written answer matters

Vietnam's Civil Code (Law No. 91/2015/QH13), in force since 1 January 2017, treats a motorbike rental as a lease of property. As of October 2026, the lease articles, which we read in the text published by the Tuyen Quang Department of Justice, say that the lessee must use the property in accordance with its function and the purpose agreed, and that if it is used otherwise, the lessor may terminate the contract and claim damages (Article 480). They also say the lessee must return the property in the condition received, apart from natural wear (Article 482), and must compensate if it is lost or damaged (Article 479).

The Code does not say whether a trip outside the city is within the agreed use. That is up to the contract and the shop's practice. If the shop has not agreed to a trip, and something goes wrong far from Hanoi, you could find yourself arguing about whether you were allowed to be there. A written answer settles that question before it matters. This is general information, not legal advice.

## Step 1: decide exactly what you plan

You can only ask a clear question if you have a clear plan. Write down:

- **The destination or route,** such as Ninh Binh, Ba Vi, Tam Dao or Mai Chau.

- **The dates and the time you will leave and return.**

- **The number of riders,** and whether you will carry luggage.

- **The kind of roads,** paved national roads or mountain roads.

- **Whether you will stay overnight** away from Hanoi.

## Step 2: ask the shop, before you book if you can

Ask before you pay, because the answer may change which bike you want or whether you rent at all. Nguyen Ha can be reached on 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969), open 09:00 to 21:00 daily, and on WhatsApp on +84 334 699 969. Its contact page asks renters to get in touch before travelling to the shop so that the bike, the price and the deposit can be confirmed, so raise the trip at the same time.

Nguyen Ha's published terms do not set out a general rule on riding outside Hanoi, so the answer comes from the shop and the contract. Ask these questions.

1. **Is a trip to [destination] on [dates] allowed with this bike?**

2. **Is there any area or distance limit?**

3. **Do any extra terms apply** to a trip outside the city, such as a larger deposit, a different price or a daily distance limit?

4. **What happens if the bike breaks down** far from Hanoi: who do I call, and how is the bike recovered?

5. **Who pays for recovery,** a repair or a replacement bike in that case?

6. **How are late return and extra days handled** if I am delayed?

7. **Is the bike suitable for those roads,** and is there a better bike for the trip?

8. **Does the shop need anything from me** before I go, such as a route, a phone number or a check-in?

## Step 3: read the contract for the relevant clauses

Even if the shop says yes, read the paper. Look for these.

| Clause | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Use or permitted area | A statement that the bike may be used only in Hanoi, or within a distance |
| Prohibited use | Wording about roads, off-road riding, other provinces |
| Rental period | How a day is counted: the published terms say a day is 24 hours |
| Late return | The published terms say VND 30,000 per hour for 1 to 5 hours, and one extra day from 6 hours |
| Breakdown and recovery | Who recovers the bike and who pays |
| Damage and loss | Liability, deposit use, any excess |
| Insurance | What the bike has, and what it excludes |
| Contact | A number you can reach outside the city |

If the contract is silent on a point, ask and have the answer added. Our guide to [reading a motorbike rental agreement in English](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/reading-a-motorbike-rental-agreement-in-english/) explains how to add notes before you sign, and our explanation of [what the clauses in a Hanoi rental contract mean](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-motorbike-rental-contract-explained/) covers the usual wording.

## Step 4: get the answer in writing

A verbal yes is easily forgotten. Ask for the answer in a form you can keep.

- **A message** from the shop that names the destination, the dates and the conditions.

- **A line on the contract** such as "Trip to Ninh Binh on 25 October agreed", with initials.

- **A photograph** of the page if the form has no space, with the staff member's initials visible.

If the shop prefers to answer by phone, send a message afterwards that summarises what was said: "Thank you for confirming by phone that a one-day trip to Ninh Binh on 25 October is fine, with return by 20:00. Please let me know if I have misunderstood." Keep any reply.

## A message you can adapt

"Hello, this is [name]. I would like to rent a [bike type] from [date] to [date]. I plan to ride to [destination] on [date], leaving about [time] and returning about [time], with [number] riders and [luggage]. Could you confirm that this trip is allowed with this bike? Are there any conditions, such as a distance limit or a different deposit? What should I do if the bike breaks down there, and who pays for recovery? Thank you."

Keep it short, and ask the shop to reply in writing.

## Step 5: check the road side of the question

Permission from the shop is one question. Whether you may ride the road is another. A route approved by the shop can still include roads closed to motorbikes. Under Law No. 36/2024/QH15, in force since 1 January 2025, ordinary motorbikes may not use expressways, and some ring roads and bridges have restrictions. Navigation apps tuned for cars may route you onto them. Our guide to [Hanoi's ring roads and highways](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-ring-roads-highways-motorbikes-allowed/) explains how to plan a legal route. Download an offline map, and watch the road signs.

- **Your licence and IDP** must cover the class of bike, wherever you ride. Our guide to [documents to carry when riding in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/documents-to-carry-riding-in-vietnam/) lists what to have.

## Step 7: plan for a breakdown away from the shop

Ask for the shop's plan for a breakdown outside Hanoi before you go, not after. Useful points include:

- the number to call, and whether the shop can reach you outside its opening hours;

- how the bike would be recovered, and how long that might take;

- whether you would be given another bike or helped to return;

- what you should pay for yourself and what you should leave to the shop;

- how you would record a repair, as our guide to [how to keep receipts for repairs on a rental motorbike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-keep-receipts-for-repairs-on-a-rental-motorbike/) explains.

## Red flags

- **The shop will not answer clearly,** or says "no problem" without discussing conditions.

- **The contract forbids leaving Hanoi** but the staff say it is fine. Get the contract changed or the permission written down.

- **No number is given** for a breakdown outside the city.

- **The shop will not say** who pays for recovery.

- **A much larger deposit or a surprise charge** is added at the last moment, without an explanation.

- **The bike is in poor condition** for a long trip. Our guide to [questions to ask about a high-mileage manual rental bike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/questions-to-ask-about-a-high-mileage-manual-rental-bike/) explains what to check.

## If the answer is no, or "it depends"

- **If no,** ask what the shop would recommend, such as a nearer destination, a different bike or renting nearer your destination. Our guide to [whether to ride to Ha Giang or rent there](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-to-ha-giang-ride-or-rent-there/) explains why that can be better.

- **If it depends,** ask exactly on what, and get the conditions in writing.

- **If you go anyway without agreement,** you take the risk of a dispute, extra charges or a refusal to help in a breakdown. We do not recommend it.

## A quick checklist

1. **Write down the destination, dates, riders and roads.**

2. **Ask the shop before you pay.**

3. **Ask about limits, conditions, breakdown, recovery and late return.**

4. **Read the contract for area and use clauses.**

5. **Get the answer in writing.**

6. **Check the road side: expressways and restrictions.**

7. **Check your licence and insurance.**

8. **Plan for a breakdown, and keep the paper trail.**

## FAQ: checking an out-of-city trip

### Can I take a rented bike outside Hanoi?

Ask the shop. Nguyen Ha's published terms do not set a general rule, so confirm your trip in writing.

### How do I get permission in writing?

Send a message naming the destination, the dates and the number of riders, and ask the shop to reply with its decision and any conditions.

### What if the contract says Hanoi only?

Ask the shop to change it or to write the permission on the contract, or choose a different trip.

### Does the shop's permission cover every road?

No. Motorbikes are banned from expressways and some roads, whatever the shop says.

### What if the bike breaks down far away?

Call the shop, and ask in advance how recovery would work and who pays.

### What happens if I am late back from a trip?

The published terms say VND 30,000 per hour for 1 to 5 hours late and one extra rental day from 6 hours. Ask for a margin.
