If a scooter pulls to one side on a test ride, the cause is usually one of five things: the road, your own posture, the tyres, a dragging brake or a crooked front end or load. The first two are harmless and easy to test. The last three are faults that the shop should look at before you ride away. A quick way to sort them out is to ride the same short stretch in both directions, at a steady low speed, with relaxed hands. If the bike drifts the same way on the same road in both directions, the road is probably the cause. If it drifts toward the same side whichever way you ride, suspect the machine. A scooter that needs constant steering effort to hold a straight line should not be rented as it is. This guide explains each cause, gives a simple test and tells you what to say to the shop.

## Start with the road and the wind

Many roads are built with a slight crown, higher in the middle and lower at the edges, so that rain runs off. Ride along the right-hand edge, as you should in Vietnam, and the surface tilts slightly toward the kerb. A light scooter on small wheels can feel that as a gentle drift to the right. It is normal, it affects every vehicle and it reverses if you ride the opposite way along the other edge.

Wind has the same effect. A crosswind from the side, a gust between buildings or the blast of a passing truck will push a light scooter sideways. Test on a calm stretch, away from large vehicles, and notice whether the drift disappears when the wind drops.

Ruts and worn grooves in the asphalt can also steer a narrow tyre. If the drift comes and goes with the surface, it is the road and not the scooter.

## Rule out your own posture

The rider can cause more drift than any mechanical part. Honda's riding guide recommends relaxed shoulders, loose elbows, a round grip on the handlebar, hips centred on the seat and a gaze far ahead. Three habits commonly produce a pull:

- **Uneven grip.** Pressing harder on one grip steers the front wheel slightly toward that side.
- **Leaning or sitting off-centre.** A rider sitting a little to one side shifts the balance.
- **Twisting to look over a shoulder.** Honda's training document warns not to turn the whole body or shoulders when checking behind, because the handlebar can turn with them and the bike changes direction suddenly. Turn the head only.

Before you blame the scooter, sit squarely, relax your hands, look ahead and ride straight for a few seconds. If the pull vanishes, it was you. Mirrors that are badly set can tempt a rider to lean; our guide to [adjusting mirrors on a rental](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-adjust-mirrors-on-a-honda-wave-rental/) helps.

## Tyres: pressure, wear and damage

Tyres are the first mechanical suspect. Honda's maintenance page says that every motorcycle has a specified tyre pressure, that both low and excessive pressure are bad, and that tyres should be checked for cracks, scratches and wear using the tread wear indicator marked with a triangle. Pressure should be measured when the tyres are cold, at least once a month, and the correct figure is on a label on the frame or in the owner's manual.

As an example, the Honda Vision manual lists 200 kPa at the front and 225 kPa at the rear. The Lead 125 manual lists 175 kPa at the front and 225 kPa at the rear. Your bike's label is the one that counts.

What a tyre can do to steering:

- **A soft front tyre** makes the bike feel heavy, slow to turn and inclined to wander.
- **A tyre worn flat across the middle or on one side** changes how it rolls.
- **A bulge, a bubble or a cut** can make the tyre steer unevenly and is a safety risk. Honda's guidance is to stop using a tyre with cracks or scratches.
- **A slow puncture** can make a tyre softer after a short ride than it was at the shop.

If the pull gets worse as you ride, stop and press the tyres with your thumb. A tyre that has visibly lost shape needs the shop.

## A dragging brake

A brake that does not release fully acts like a hand on one side of the bike. Honda's brake pages explain that pads and shoes grip a rotating surface and wear thinner over time, which is why brake checks belong in every pre-ride inspection. A front disc brake that drags can pull the bike toward the side of the caliper; a rear drum that drags makes the engine feel weak and the scooter sluggish.

Signs of a drag:

- The scooter feels as if it is running uphill on the flat.
- It rolls poorly when pushed with the engine off.
- A hub or disc feels unusually warm after a short ride. Honda warns not to touch the engine, exhaust, brakes or other hot parts until they cool, so hold the back of your hand near it rather than touching it.
- The lever has no free play, or one lever feels much tighter than the other.

If you suspect a drag, tell the shop. Do not adjust a brake yourself.

## A crooked front end or a bent part

If the handlebar is not centred when the front wheel points straight ahead, you will steer constantly to compensate and the scooter will feel as if it pulls. Look at the bike from the front and from above while it stands upright: the grips, mirrors and headlight should look symmetrical.

A bent handlebar, fork, wheel or frame is usually the result of a drop or a hit. Honda's manual says that after a fall you should check the tightness of key bolts, the handlebar, controls, brakes and wheels, ride slowly, and take the bike for a proper check because damage may be invisible. Honda's wheel page adds that a damaged wheel bearing can make a bike fail to go straight or make the handlebar sway. A loose or worn steering head bearing, which Honda lists among regular checks, can also make steering feel notchy or drifting.

If a rental scooter has a bent look or a history of drops, ask for a different one. Our guide to [what to do after dropping a rental bike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-to-do-after-dropping-a-rented-semi-automatic-bike/) explains the other side of that.

## Load and passenger

Uneven weight can steer a scooter by itself. A heavy bag on the hook or one side of the footboard pulls the bike toward its side. Honda's manuals limit the hook and front compartment to 1.5 kg on the Vision and Lead and warn against hanging bulky goods that could slip or foul your legs. A passenger who leans the wrong way or sits tight against one side has the same effect, and Honda's manuals tell passengers to lean in the same direction as the rider in turns and keep their feet on the pegs. Our guide to [riding two-up with luggage on a Lead or Vision](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/scooter-for-two-riders-and-luggage-hanoi/) covers loading.

For the test ride, ride alone with the box empty and nothing on the hook. If the pull is gone, load was the cause.

## A straight-road test you can repeat

1. **Pick a flat, quiet, straight road** with no traffic and a good surface, ideally one you can ride in both directions.
2. **Check the tyres first.** Look for flat or bulging tyres and ask the shop to confirm the pressures.
3. **Sit squarely and relax your grip.** Keep both hands on the grips at all times. Never take your hands off the bars to test a pull.
4. **Ride at a steady 20 to 30 km/h** for a few seconds and note which way the bike drifts, if any.
5. **Turn round and repeat in the opposite direction** on the same side of the road.
6. **Repeat gently with no brake and with light braking.** A pull that appears only under braking points to the brakes.
7. **Try a slightly higher speed** if the road and traffic allow it, because some faults only show above a certain speed.

Write down what you notice. "Drifts left in both directions at 30 km/h, gone when I brake" is a precise report.

## Reading the pattern

| What you notice | Likely cause | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Drifts to the right on one road, to the left coming back | Road camber | Normal |
| Drifts only in gusts | Wind | Normal; ride more slowly |
| Drifts the same way in both directions | Tyre, brake, front end or load | Ask the shop to check |
| Heavy, wandering feel from the front | Soft front tyre | Check pressure |
| Pulls only when braking | One brake gripping harder | Report it |
| Scooter feels as if it runs uphill | Dragging brake | Report it |
| Handlebar looks off-centre | Bent or misaligned front end | Ask for another bike |
| Shake and pull together | Wheel or bearing problem | Stop and report it |

This table is general mechanic knowledge, not a Honda specification.

## What to say, and when to refuse the bike

Describe the symptom plainly: "On a flat road at about 30 km/h it drifts to the left in both directions. Could someone check the tyres and brakes, or give me another scooter?" A competent shop will check pressure and look at the brakes and front end, or swap the bike. If it is a road effect, they will tell you so. If it is mechanical, do not accept "it is normal" when the bike needs steady correction to hold a line. ## FAQ: scooter pulling to one side

### Is it normal for a scooter to drift slightly to the right?

A very slight drift toward the kerb on a crowned road is normal. If it persists on a flat, level surface and in both directions, ask the shop to check the bike.

### Can low tyre pressure make a scooter pull?

A soft front tyre makes steering feel heavy and unstable, and a very soft one can make the bike wander. Check the pressure against the label.

### Can a bent handlebar cause a pull?

Yes. A bent or off-centre handlebar, fork or wheel usually follows a drop. Ask for another bike.

### Could the brakes be the problem?

A dragging brake can slow the scooter and pull it to one side. It shows as heat or a sluggish roll.

### Why does it pull only when I brake?

One brake may be gripping harder than the other, or a front component may be worn. Report it to the shop.

### Should I take my hands off the bars to test it?

No. Keep both hands on the grips at all times. Use repeated runs in both directions instead.
