Nam Tu Liem is the part of western Hanoi built around My Dinh National Stadium, the National Convention Center and a grid of wide modern boulevards, and a rental scooter is a reasonable way to see it on an ordinary day. On an event day it is a different story: the roads around the stadium are closed or restricted for hours, tens of thousands of people leave at once and parking becomes the real challenge. The best approach is to treat the area as two places, a quiet, spacious district for normal days and a crowd-control zone on match and concert nights. This guide explains how the area is organised after the 2025 reshuffle of local government, what police restrictions have looked like for big matches, how to choose between parking, ride-hailing and the metro, and how to get home safely afterwards.

## Where "Nam Tu Liem" is now

Until mid-2025 Nam Tu Liem was a district with ten wards, including My Dinh 1, My Dinh 2, Me Tri, Phu Do, Cau Dien, Tay Mo, Dai Mo, Xuan Phuong and Phuong Canh. Under Hanoi's reorganisation of commune-level units, which took effect on 1 July 2025, the district level disappeared and its area was regrouped into four wards: Tu Liem, Xuan Phuong, Tay Mo and Dai Mo. The stadium, according to the current Wikipedia entry, lies in Tu Liem Ward, formerly Nam Tu Liem District.

For a visitor this is mostly a labelling matter. Tickets, hotel pages and many maps still say "Nam Tu Liem" or "My Dinh", and drivers know the area by those names. The stadium's address is on Le Duc Tho Road. Wikipedia puts the venue about 10 km north-west of central Hanoi.

## What the stadium area is like on an ordinary day

My Dinh National Stadium was inaugurated on 2 September 2003 and holds 40,192 seats. It is the centrepiece of the National Sports Complex, and the stadium site alone covers around 17.5 hectares according to its builder, Xuan Mai Corporation. It hosted the opening and closing ceremonies of the 2003 Southeast Asian Games and is home to the national football team.

On a normal weekday the surroundings are wide, tidy and mostly quiet, with space between the large buildings. A listing for the stadium on a Hanoi tourism site names several nearby landmarks: the Vietnam National Convention Center and the Hanoi Museum, each roughly 1.8 km away, and a hotel under 2 km away. Those distances make the area suitable for a short half-day: ride out, look at the stadium exterior, visit one of the large institutions, and ride back.

If you simply want to see the stadium, go on a day with no event, in daylight, and park in a managed lot rather than at the roadside.

## Event nights: what the restrictions look like

For large events, the Hanoi traffic police publish a plan. A recent example from VietnamNet, dated 26 August 2026, concerned the second leg of the ASEAN Cup final between Vietnam and Thailand, kicking off at 8 pm. The plan ran from 2 pm until midnight, covering the period before, during and after the match. It temporarily banned trucks with a designed gross weight of 1.5 tonnes or more and passenger vehicles with 16 seats or more from a list of roads around the stadium, and it said other vehicles would also face restrictions on those roads and on a frontage road of Thang Long Boulevard.

The roads named were Le Quang Dao, Le Duc Tho, Tan My, Me Tri, Tran Huu Duc, Ham Nghi, Nguyen Co Thach, Do Xuan Hop, Nguyen Hoang and Chau Van Liem. Through traffic between National Highway 32 and Thang Long Boulevard was advised to use Provincial Road 70 or the elevated section of Ring Road 3.

For a rider this contains three lessons:

1. **Restrictions start hours before kick-off,** not at the final whistle. A match at 8 pm may mean controls from the afternoon.
2. **The plan names roads, not a single gate.** Even if you are only passing through, your usual route may be filtered.
3. **Alternative routes are written for general traffic.** The elevated Ring Road 3 advice does not tell you whether motorbikes are allowed on every ramp; follow the signs, and read our guide to [Hanoi's ring roads and highways](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-ring-roads-highways-motorbikes-allowed/).

Scale is also worth bearing in mind. VOV reported attendance of about 70,000 across the two Blackpink concert nights at My Dinh in July 2023. A crowd that size leaves by the same few roads at the same moment.

## Choosing how to arrive: park, ride-hail or take the metro

You have three realistic choices on event day, and each suits a different traveller.

| Option | Good for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Ride the rental scooter and park | Daytime visits and small events | Road filters, no verified public lot, long exit queues |
| Ride-hailing or taxi | Evening matches and concerts | Pick-up points moving away from the stadium, surge crowds |
| Metro Line 3 plus a short last leg | Visitors based in the city who want to avoid the closures | The stations are not at the stadium gate; plan a walk or short ride |

Hanoi Metro Line 3, the elevated line from Nhon to Cau Giay, opened to the public on 8 August 2024. Its stations are Nhon, Minh Khai, Phu Dien, Cau Dien, Le Duc Tho, Vietnam National University, Chua Ha and Cau Giay. It runs along the National Highway 32 corridor, so it serves western Nam Tu Liem rather than the stadium itself. Check a current map before relying on it for a specific gate.

## Parking near the stadium and other big venues

No official public statement listing where motorbike riders should park on event days turned up in this research, so treat any specific "free lot" you read online with caution. What you can rely on are general rules and habits.

Road-traffic Law 36/2024/QH15, in force since 1 January 2025, forbids stopping or parking at junctions or within 5 metres of them, in front of organisations' gates, on pedestrian crossings, at passenger pick-up points and wherever the street is only wide enough for one motor-vehicle lane. Street parking must be along the kerb on the right in your direction of travel, with the nearest wheel within 0.25 metres. As of October 2026, those points are in Article 18 of the published text. The practical meaning is that an unmanaged corner near an event is the wrong place to leave a rental scooter, however many others are parked there.

Better habits:

- **Use a staffed, ticketed lot or a venue's own motorbike park** where the organisers provide one, and keep the ticket.
- **Park facing out** so you can leave without reversing against a crowd.
- **Lock the steering and take your helmet and valuables** with you. The shop does not provide an extra lock, so choose a supervised place.
- **Photograph where you parked,** including a street sign or building, so you can find the bike in the dark.
- **Do not block a gate, a driveway or a delivery entrance.**

For a different kind of crowd-day etiquette, see [where to park a scooter near Hoan Kiem Lake](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/where-to-park-a-scooter-near-hoan-kiem-lake/).

## Riding home after an event

Leaving a stadium on a motorbike is the part many visitors underestimate.

1. **Decide your route before the final whistle.** Use the map while you still have a signal; mobile data often slows down in a crowd.
2. **Wait.** Staying for 30 to 45 minutes in a cafe or on a quiet pavement usually costs less time than sitting in the first surge of traffic.
3. **Avoid the roads named in the police plan** until the restrictions end.
4. **Use full lights and a visible helmet,** and keep a larger gap than usual; riders around you will be tired and may weave.
5. **Do not drink and ride.** Vietnam's rules on alcohol and driving are strict; read our guide to [drink-driving rules for motorbike riders](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/drink-driving-rules-motorbike-vietnam/) before an evening event.
6. **Consider leaving the scooter and taking a ride-hailing car** if you are tired, wet or unsure. A late pick-up is cheaper than a crash.

## A calmer Nam Tu Liem outing

When there is no event, a short loop is easy to plan:

- Approach from the city in the late morning, avoiding the commuter peak.
- Ride to the stadium area, look at the exterior and the surrounding plazas, and take photographs from the pavement.
- Choose one of the nearby institutions, such as the convention centre or the Hanoi Museum, and use its own parking if it has any.
- Eat locally and ride back before the evening peak.

Check opening hours of any institution before you go, because large venues often close for events and for set-up days.

## FAQ about Nam Tu Liem by scooter

### Is Nam Tu Liem still a district?

No. Hanoi's district level was abolished from 1 July 2025 and the old Nam Tu Liem area is now split into four wards: Tu Liem, Xuan Phuong, Tay Mo and Dai Mo. Everyday speech and many maps still use the old name.

### Can I ride a scooter to My Dinh National Stadium on match day?

You can try, but expect road restrictions starting hours before kick-off and heavy crowds afterwards. For evening matches a ride-hailing car or the metro plus a short walk is usually less stressful. Check the traffic police announcement for the specific event.

### Is there guaranteed motorbike parking at the stadium?

An official public motorbike lot for event days could not be verified. Ask the organiser, look for supervised lots and avoid parking at junctions, gates or crossings.

### How far is the stadium from the centre of Hanoi?

About 10 km north-west of the centre, according to Wikipedia. Journey time depends heavily on the time of day.

### Does Metro Line 3 stop at the stadium?

No. The line runs along the National Highway 32 corridor with stations at Nhon, Minh Khai, Phu Dien, Cau Dien, Le Duc Tho and others. You would need a short additional leg.
