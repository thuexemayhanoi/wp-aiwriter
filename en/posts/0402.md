To read a motorbike rental agreement in English in Vietnam, first find out which language the signed version is in, then read it in a fixed order: the parties and the bike, the dates and money, the condition and damage wording, the return terms and last the signature block. If the paper is in Vietnamese, or in both languages, ask which version counts, have the staff walk you through the key lines and write any English notes on the paper itself before you sign. This guide is about the reading process and the language problems, such as translated terms, money shorthand and date formats. It is not a clause-by-clause explanation and it is not legal advice.

## Start with one question: which language is the real one?

Rental shops in Hanoi use different kinds of paper. Some give you a printed bilingual contract. Some use a Vietnamese form with a short English summary. Some use a handwritten note. Each creates a different risk, and you can reduce it with a single question asked at the counter: "Which version do we both sign, and is there an English copy that matches it?"

Then ask the second question: "If the two versions differ, which one counts?" The sources reviewed did not turn up a Vietnamese rule that settles this for tourist rentals, so the safest approach is practical. Have the answer written on the paper, in a line such as "the Vietnamese version prevails" or "both versions have equal effect", and keep the English translation you were given. An English copy that the shop has handed over is, at least, evidence of what both sides understood when the deal was made.

At Nguyen Ha, identity documents and the licence are checked and photographed for the contract, and the final price is confirmed on the actual bike before the contract is signed. Ask the staff which language the contract is in and whether they can go through it with you in English; this guide cannot say what a particular shop's paper looks like.

## What Vietnam's Civil Code says about contract wording

A few general rules from the Civil Code 2015 (Law No. 91/2015/QH13) help when the wording is unclear. They are not a substitute for advice on a specific dispute.

- **Form.** Article 119 says a civil transaction may be made verbally, in writing or through specific acts. A rental can therefore be valid without a long document, which is one reason a written sheet is worth having.
- **Interpretation.** Article 404 says that where terms are unclear, they are read according to the wording and the parties' intentions before and after the contract was made. Where a term is difficult to understand, custom at the place of contracting is used, and where a party in a stronger position inserts disadvantageous content for the weaker party, the contract is read in favour of the weaker party.
- **Standard forms.** Article 405 covers contracts prepared by one side on a standard form. Unclear terms are read in favour of the party who receives the form, and terms that exempt the provider from liability or increase the other side's liability may be ineffective unless otherwise agreed. Whether a shop's sheet counts as a standard form is a question for a lawyer.
- **Appendices.** Article 403 says an appendix has the same effect as the contract, but must not contradict it. A handwritten addition or a separate list of marks may therefore matter as much as the printed text.

The lesson is practical: unclear wording can be argued about, so remove the doubt before you sign.

## A reading order that works

Read the paper in five passes, ticking each as you go.

1. **Who and what.** Names, the shop's details, the bike's model and plate number. Compare the plate to the bike in front of you.

2. **When and how much.** Handover time, return time, rent, deposit and any fees. Check each figure against what you were told.

3. **Condition.** What the paper says about the bike's state and whether any marks are listed.

4. **Responsibility.** Damage, loss, theft, fines, fuel, late and early return.

5. **Signatures and copies.** Who signs, in how many copies and who keeps which.

Most of the trouble is in passes 2 and 4. Our guide to [what the clauses in a Hanoi rental contract mean](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-motorbike-rental-contract-explained/) explains those clauses one by one, so this article concentrates on reading them across a language gap.

## Money, dates and numbers: where translation goes wrong

Even a perfect translation can mislead if the formats differ. Check these three.

**Money shorthand.** In everyday Vietnamese, "k" often means thousand and "tr" means million, so "30k" is 30,000 dong and "2tr" is two million. Vietnamese writing also uses a dot as a thousands separator, so "2.000.000" is two million, not two. Ask for amounts to be written out in full with "VND", and in words if the paper allows.

**Date order.** Vietnamese dates are normally day, month, year. "05/11" is 5 November, not 11 May. Write the month as a word when you fill in the paper.

**Time.** Use a 24-hour clock and an exact return time. Nguyen Ha counts one rental day as 24 hours from handover, and late-return charges are VND 30,000 per hour for one to five hours, then one extra rental day from six hours. A return time written only as "evening" is not enough.

## Translation tools: use them, but do not rely on them alone

A phone camera translator is a good way to get the gist of a Vietnamese paper. It can still stumble on handwriting, stylised print and legal phrases. Follow these habits.

- Translate **each clause that mentions money, damage, loss, fines or the deposit** and read the English out to the staff to confirm.
- If two translations of a sentence differ, mark it and ask.
- Do not rely on a translation of a sentence you do not understand to protect you; ask for the meaning to be written in English next to it.
- Photograph the signed page before you leave, with the translations if you made them.

A translation does not change the paper's own meaning, and a Vietnamese word may carry a nuance the English one does not, such as the difference between ordinary wear and damage.

## Wording that deserves a second look

Some phrases appear often enough that it pays to pause when you see them. Ask about each one, rather than assuming it is bad.

| Wording | Why it matters | A good question |
|---|---|---|
| "Customer accepts the bike in perfect condition" | Locks in that no marks existed | Can existing marks be listed first? |
| "Customer is responsible for all damage" | Could include wear | Does that exclude normal wear? |
| "Deposit is non-refundable" | May apply to the whole amount | Under what conditions is it kept? |
| "Repair cost determined by the owner" | Gives the shop sole control | Can I see the part and the receipt? |
| "Customer pays all fines" | May include fines before the rental | From which date and time? |
| "Documents held by the shop" | Includes passport risk | Is it a photocopy, or the original? |
| "Customer must return at the original place" | Ties you to one address | What if I need another location? |
| "Verbal changes are invalid" | Promises made at the counter do not count | Can they be added in writing? |

If you ask these politely, the answers tell you a lot about the shop, and the written additions protect both sides. Our guide to [returning your rental motorbike in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/returning-your-rental-motorbike-hanoi/) shows how damage questions play out at handback.

## Blanks, crossings-out and handwriting

Make sure nothing is left open. A blank line can be filled in later, so write "none" or draw a line through it. If something is crossed out, ask both sides to initial the change. Handwritten additions, such as a note about an existing scratch, should be in ink, dated and initialled, and they should be on both copies. Under Article 403 of the Civil Code, an appendix has the same effect as the contract, provided it does not contradict it, which is a good reason to keep every added page together.

## Ten minutes before you sign

1. Ask which language is the signed version and which counts if they differ.

2. Check the plate number against the bike.

3. Check handover and return times and the 24-hour rule.

4. Confirm rent and deposit as separate lines, in full figures.

5. Ask what the deposit can be used for and when it comes back. Our guide to [how a motorbike rental deposit works in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbike-rental-deposit-hanoi/) explains the usual pattern.

6. Look for any clause on damage, loss, fines and fuel, and ask for plain-English meaning.

7. List existing marks and photograph them.

8. Write corrections on both copies and initial them.

9. Sign only when you have a copy or a clear photograph of every page.

10. Save the photograph with the date and the shop's phone number, 0334 699 969.

## FAQ: reading a rental agreement in English

### Is a verbal or handwritten rental agreement valid in Vietnam?

The Civil Code says civil transactions can be verbal, written or by specific acts, so a short handwritten note can be valid. A clear written record is still better evidence.

### Which version counts if the English and Vietnamese differ?

No general Vietnamese rule was found in the sources reviewed that settles it for tourist rentals. Ask the shop and have the answer written on the paper.

### Can I use my phone to translate the contract?

Yes, as a guide. Confirm every clause about money, damage and the deposit with the staff, and keep the translation.

### What does "Party A" and "Party B" mean?

It is a drafting habit: usually the lessor and the lessee. Check which label is yours.

### What should I do if I am pressed to sign quickly?

Ask for a few minutes to read, or step aside and photograph the pages. A fair shop will let you.
