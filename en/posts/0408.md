Choose a 50cc bike when one of these applies: you hold no licence that covers a motorcycle, the rider is 16 or 17, you want the lightest and gentlest machine while you learn Hanoi traffic, your riding will stay slow and local, or you will mostly be pushing the bike through tight parking. Choose a larger scooter when you hold a suitable licence and want to keep pace with traffic, carry a passenger or bags, or leave the inner city. In Vietnam a 50cc moped is treated differently from a motorcycle: under Law 36/2024/QH15 it can be ridden from age 16, no licence class is attached to it, and on ordinary roads it is held to 40 km/h. That is both its great advantage and its limit.

## Situation 1: you hold no motorcycle licence

This is the strongest reason to take a 50cc bike. Vietnam's Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety defines a moped as a two- or three-wheeler with a design speed no higher than 50 km/h and, if petrol-powered, an engine of no more than 50 cm3. The licence classes in Article 57 begin with class A1 for two-wheeled motorcycles up to 125 cm3, so a moped is outside them and its rider is not asked for a motorcycle licence class. The rider must still understand the rules of the road and have riding skill, and every other rule applies.

The shop may still ask what you hold and which rules it applies to its own rentals, so say clearly that you have no motorcycle licence when you ask about availability. Your travel policy may also set its own conditions, which is a separate question from the law. Our guide to [renting a 50cc scooter in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/renting-a-50cc-scooter-in-hanoi/) covers the rules in more detail, and our overview of [licence rules for foreigners](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbike-licence-rules-for-foreigners-in-vietnam/) explains the permits behind larger bikes.

## Situation 2: the rider is 16 or 17

The Law sets age 16 as the minimum for driving a moped, and age 18 for the licence classes A1, A, B1, B and C1. A teenager on a family trip can therefore ride a moped but not a larger scooter. Shops often have extra conditions for renters under 18, such as involving a parent or guardian, so raise the age in your first message. Ask which bikes are in the 50cc group, because a 110cc scooter is not the same thing even if it looks similar. Check the engine size on the actual bike, as our guide on [how to confirm the exact model you will receive](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-confirm-the-exact-model-you-will-receive/) explains.

## Situation 3: you want the gentlest start

Weight and power make a difference to a nervous beginner. For scale, Honda Vietnam lists the Vision, a popular 110cc scooter, at 94 to 97 kg depending on the version, with a seat height around 761 to 785 mm, and the Air Blade 160 at 113 kg with a 775 mm seat. These are manufacturer figures for new bikes, not for a 50cc rental, and this article has no comparable manufacturer figure for the 50cc bikes in the rental fleet, so ask the shop how the 50cc bike feels to push and park. In general, a smaller engine means softer acceleration, and that is the thing a new rider wants while learning to balance, brake and read traffic.

Gentle does not mean safe by default. Helmets are required for riders and passengers on mopeds as on motorcycles, and traffic around you will still be faster than you are.

## Situation 4: your riding is slow, short and local

If your plan is a coffee run, a market visit or a loop around West Lake, a 50cc is enough. Hanoi's central streets often move slowly, and a lighter bike is easier to turn in a narrow lane and to squeeze into a parking row. Our guides to [riding in Hanoi's Old Quarter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-old-quarter/) and to [Long Bien and the Red River as a first ride](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/long-bien-red-river-first-ride/) describe the kind of riding in which a small bike copes well.

## Situation 5: parking and handling matter more than speed

In a city where a bike has to be walked into guarded lots and tight kerb lines, a light machine is easier to manage. If you will park many times a day, or you are a smaller rider who finds a heavier scooter awkward to push, the 50cc has an advantage that has nothing to do with the law. Read our guide to [parking a motorbike in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-motorbike-in-hanoi/) to see what a parking day involves.

## Four situations where a larger scooter is the better choice

### You will carry a passenger or luggage

A moped can carry a passenger within the law's limit for two-wheeled vehicles, but a small engine and small wheels leave little margin once there are two adults and a bag. Our guide to [scooters for two riders and luggage](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/scooter-for-two-riders-and-luggage-hanoi/) looks at what a 110cc automatic can handle.

### You expect to ride on faster roads

Circular 38/2024/TT-BGTVT sets a maximum operating speed of 40 km/h for mopeds and similar vehicles on roads other than expressways. Bridges and dyke roads around the Red River often carry quicker traffic, and a bike at its limit is the slowest thing on the road. Our guide to [speed limits for motorbikes in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/speed-limits-motorbikes-vietnam/) sets out the figures for other bikes, and the guide to [ring roads and highways](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-ring-roads-highways-motorbikes-allowed/) shows where motorbikes may go at all.

### You plan a day trip

A slow bike turns a half-day outing into a long day. Honest planning matters more than the sticker on the engine. If you are heading out of the city, read our guides to [Ninh Binh by motorbike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-to-ninh-binh-by-motorbike/) and [Tam Dao](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-to-tam-dao-by-motorbike/), then pick a bike that suits the route.

### You hold a licence that covers a bigger bike

If you already hold a suitable licence, the main reason to take a 50cc disappears, and the usual advice is to take a 110cc or 125cc automatic. Check the version: Honda Vietnam lists the Air Blade 160 at a cylinder capacity of 156.9 cm3, which is above the 125 cm3 limit of class A1 in Article 57, so a "larger scooter" is not always a bike every licence covers. Ask the shop for the engine size before you decide.

## A five-question test

Answer yes or no, then count.

1. Do I lack a licence that covers a motorcycle, or am I aged 16 or 17?
2. Will I ride mostly within the city on slow streets?
3. Will I travel alone, or with a light bag?
4. Do I want the lightest bike while I learn?
5. Am I happy with a top speed of 40 km/h on ordinary roads?

If you answered yes to questions 1 and 2 and at least two others, the 50cc is a good fit. If question 1 is no and you answered no to 2, 3 or 5, a larger scooter will suit you better.

## FAQ: choosing a 50cc over a larger scooter

### Can I ride a 50cc bike with no motorcycle licence?

Under Law 36/2024/QH15, as of October 2026, a moped carries no licence class, and a person aged 16 or over may ride one. Every other traffic rule applies, and the shop may have its own conditions.

### Is a 50cc bike cheaper than a larger scooter?

Not at Nguyen Ha, where 50cc bikes are VND 200,000 per day, at the top of the range for automatics.

### How fast can a 50cc bike legally go?

On ordinary roads the maximum operating speed for mopeds is 40 km/h under Circular 38/2024/TT-BGTVT.

### Is a 50cc bike safer for beginners?

It is gentler, but the helmet rule and traffic risks are the same. A beginner should choose quiet streets and avoid rush hour.

### Can two adults ride a 50cc bike?

The law has limits on passengers for two-wheeled vehicles, and a small bike is slow and unstable with two adults. A larger scooter is more comfortable if your licence allows it.
