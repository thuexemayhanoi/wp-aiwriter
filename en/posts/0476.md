At a railway level crossing in Hanoi, stop to the right behind the stop line whenever a guard signals, a red light flashes, a bell rings or the barrier is moving or down, and at a crossing with no guard, barrier or signal, stop behind the line, look both ways and cross only when no train is coming. That is Article 24 of Law 36/2024/QH15. For a scooter, add two physical rules: cross the rails as close to a right angle as you can, and never stop on the tracks. A rider who goes through a closed crossing risks far more than a fine: railway safety authorities elsewhere note that a train can take a mile or more to stop, and it cannot swerve.

This guide explains the legal rules, the fine reported for motorcycles and mopeds, how to read the three kinds of crossing, how to cross rails safely on small wheels, what to do if the bike stalls or gets stuck and why Hanoi's famous Train Street is not a place to treat as an ordinary street. It is general information based on the law and published guidance as of October 2026, not legal advice.

## What Article 24 requires

Article 24 of Law 36/2024/QH15, on traffic at railway crossings and bridges, sets out three rules.

1. **When a signal says stop.** When the railway guard gives a command, the red light is flashing, the bell is ringing, or the road barrier is being lowered or has been lowered, road users must stop to the right of their direction of traffic, behind the stop line.

2. **When there is no guard or signal.** When approaching a crossing with no guard, barrier, bell or signal, road users must stop to the right, behind the stop line, observe both sides, and cross only when no railway vehicle is coming.

3. **When a vehicle is stuck.** If a vehicle on the road is damaged, has an accident or spills goods onto a crossing or bridge, and cannot be moved at once out of the railway safety scope, the operator and people present must immediately give a signal to stop the train and take safety measures.

Other provisions of the Law apply nearby. Article 12 requires operators to slow down or stop at road-railway intersections. Article 14 bans overtaking at road-railway intersections. Article 18 bans stopping or parking within the railway safety scope.

## The fine

Railway crossing offences are penalised under a railway-sector decree rather than the main road traffic decree. Vietnamese news reports on Decree 81/2026/ND-CP, in force from 15 May 2026, say that Article 13 sets a fine of VND 4,000,000 to 6,000,000 for a rider of a motorcycle or moped, including an electric one, who passes the barrier while it is moving, crosses when the red light is on, or ignores the signs, markings or guard's instructions at a crossing. The same reports give much higher figures for cars. Read the current text of the decree if the exact figure matters to you.

The fine is the smaller risk. The Quang Ngai provincial police portal, quoting railway-sector statistics, reported 116 cases of drivers deliberately crossing level crossings in the first quarter of 2025 alone, and warned that crossing a closed barrier is one of the most dangerous behaviours on the road.

## Three kinds of crossing

| Kind of crossing | What you see | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Guarded or barriered, with lights and bell** | Barrier arms, flashing red lights, bell, sometimes a guard | Stop behind the line when the lights flash, the bell rings or the barrier moves. Wait until the barrier is fully raised and the lights stop |
| **Signal only** | Lights and bell, no barrier | Stop when the lights flash or the bell rings. Do not cross because it looks clear |
| **No guard, barrier or signal** | Warning sign, stop line, track | Stop behind the line, look both ways, listen, and cross only when it is clearly safe |

Do not assume which kind you are approaching. Some crossings change over time, and a signal may fail. Treat every crossing as one where a train may come.

## Before you reach the tracks

**1. Slow down early.** Article 12 requires you to slow down or stop at road-railway intersections. Rough rails can also jolt you at speed.

**2. Look and listen.** Take off any earphones, since the law already bans sound devices while riding (Article 33), and listen for a horn or the bell. Modern trains can be quiet, and buildings can hide them.

**3. Do not overtake near the crossing.** Article 14 prohibits overtaking at road-railway intersections.

**4. Check for space on the far side.** Cross only if there is room to clear the whole crossing without stopping. A queue of vehicles ahead may leave you stuck between the rails. Road-safety authorities advise waiting until you can cross completely.

**5. Stop on the right, behind the line.** Do not creep forward over the stop line, and do not stop in a place that blocks other vehicles or pedestrians.

## Crossing the rails on small wheels

Rails are made of steel, sit slightly above the road and sometimes leave gaps beside them. Motorcycle safety guidance from road authorities and rider-training bodies agrees on a few points.

- **Cross as close to a right angle (90 degrees) as possible.** A shallow angle can let a front wheel slip along the rail or into the groove beside it. A Canadian provincial motorcycle handbook advises crossing at an angle between 45 and 90 degrees and, if the tracks run alongside your path, moving far enough away to cross at an angle, then making a quick, sharp turn, not edging across.

- **Do not ride along the rails.** If the road and the tracks run parallel for a stretch, the safest position is not between or beside the rails but a path that lets you cross them squarely.

- **Be gentle when it is wet.** Wet steel is slippery. Avoid sudden braking, accelerating or leaning on the rails in rain. Our guides to [riding in Hanoi in the rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) and [braking safely on wet road markings](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/braking-safely-on-wet-road-markings/) explain how to adjust.

- **Do not shift gears on the crossing.** A United States state transport department's railroad safety page advises not changing gear while crossing, since a missed shift can leave you stalled. On an automatic scooter this matters less, but the principle holds: keep your inputs steady.

- **Keep your balance upright.** Stay seated, keep your eyes on the far side and hold the bars lightly. If the crossing is rough, rise slightly on the footboard only if you can do so without losing control.

- **Look for a second train.** On a crossing with more than one track, a train passing on one track can hide another on the next. Wait until you have a clear view before moving.

## If the barrier starts to come down while you are on the crossing

Keep going and clear the tracks. Do not stop or reverse. Railway safety guidance in other countries makes the same point: if the signals begin while you are already on the crossing, continue across.

## If you stall or get stuck

Article 24(3) of the Law requires the operator and people present to signal to stop the train and take safety measures. In practice:

1. **Get yourself and any passenger off the bike and away from the tracks at once.** Move well clear, and do not stand in line with the rails, since a train overhangs the track and the force of a collision can throw wreckage.

2. **Alert the guard if there is one,** and warn other people to stay back.

3. **Call 112.** It connects to the police, fire and ambulance services. Our guide to [emergency numbers and accident reporting in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/emergency-numbers-and-accident-reporting-in-vietnam/) explains what to say.

4. **Do not try to restart the bike or lift it clear if a train is near.** A scooter is replaceable, you are not.

5. **Call the shop afterwards.** Nguyen Ha can be reached on 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969), open 09:00 to 21:00 daily. Our guide to [where to stop safely after a motorbike breaks down](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/where-to-stop-safely-after-a-motorbike-breaks-down/) explains how to choose a safe spot when you can move.

## Parking near tracks

Article 18 bans stopping or parking within the railway safety scope. Do not leave a scooter at a crossing, in front of a barrier or beside the rails, even briefly for a photograph. Our guide to [motorbike parking near Hanoi railway station](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbike-parking-near-hanoi-railway-station/) explains where to leave a bike near the station.

## Hanoi's Train Street

The stretch of railway through Phung Hung and Tran Phu is a working railway between homes, not an ordinary street, and access rules have changed over time. Do not ride or park on it, do not step over a barrier and do not stand or sit on the rails for a photograph. Our guide to [visiting Hanoi Train Street: why parking and access rules matter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/visiting-hanoi-train-street-why-parking-and-access-rules-matter/) explains the rules and safer alternatives.

## A quick checklist

1. **Slow down and take off earphones before the tracks.**

2. **Stop right, behind the line, if there is any signal, guard or barrier movement.**

3. **At an unguarded crossing, stop, look both ways and listen.**

4. **Cross only if you can clear the whole crossing.**

5. **Cross as square to the rails as you can, steadily, without shifting or braking.**

6. **Watch for a second train.**

7. **Never stop, park or overtake on or near the tracks.**

8. **If stuck, get away, call 112 and tell the shop afterwards.**

## FAQ: railway crossings and scooters

### Do I have to stop at every railway crossing in Vietnam?

You must stop whenever there is a guard's command, a flashing red light, a bell or a moving or lowered barrier. At a crossing with none of these, Article 24 requires you to stop behind the line, observe both sides and cross only when no train is coming.

### What is the fine for crossing a closed barrier on a motorbike?

Vietnamese news reports on Decree 81/2026/ND-CP say a fine of VND 4,000,000 to 6,000,000 for motorcycles and mopeds, including electric ones. Check the current text.

### Can I overtake at a level crossing?

No. Article 14 bans overtaking at road-railway intersections.

### What angle should I cross the tracks at?

As close to 90 degrees as you can, so the wheels do not slip along the rail or into the gap beside it.

### What if the barrier comes down while I am on the crossing?

Keep going until you are clear. Do not stop or turn back.

### Can I park near the tracks?

No. Article 18 bans stopping and parking within the railway safety scope.
