Painted road markings can be very slippery when wet, so the safest way to brake on them is to avoid doing it: slow down before you reach the paint, brake in a straight line with the scooter upright, use both brakes gently and release them before you cross. Rider-training and touring guidance agrees on the main points. Painted lines, arrows and crossing stripes, along with manhole covers, drain grates and metal expansion joints, give much less grip in the wet, and a sudden or hard brake on them can lock a wheel. Wet roads also roughly double dry stopping distances as a rule of thumb, and the first minutes of rain after a dry spell are often the slipperiest. In Hanoi that matters, because the places where you brake most, such as stop lines, crossings and junction arrows, are covered in paint.

This guide explains why wet paint is slippery, where you meet it, how to plan and brake step by step, how to cross markings you cannot avoid, what your rental bike's brakes and tyres need and what the law asks of riders in rain. It is general information based on the law and on published riding guidance as of October 2026, not legal advice.

## Why wet markings are slippery

Painted surfaces are smoother than the asphalt around them, and a film of water on top of paint takes away more of the grip you rely on. Published guidance describes it plainly:

- A rider-training columnist writes that painted lines on a wet road are "almost as bad as ice" and advises avoiding any lean on painted lines such as safety zones and crosswalks, and braking on them only with extreme caution.

- A 2026 touring-rider guide says painted markings (lines, arrows, crossings) are slick when wet, and that riders should try not to brake, accelerate or turn on top of them. It lists manhole covers, drain grates and metal expansion joints as equally slippery.

- A rain-riding guide from a rider-gear company adds that painted lane markings, metal bridge joints, manhole covers, railroad tracks and polished intersections can become extremely slippery in rain.

The same sources point to two other facts. The first minutes of rain after a dry spell are the worst, because oil, rubber and dust on the surface are lifted by the water and make a greasy film. And wet roads lengthen stopping distances, with a common rule of thumb of roughly double the dry distance.

## Where you meet wet markings in Hanoi

- **Stop lines and pedestrian crossings** at traffic lights. This is where you brake hardest.

- **Direction arrows** painted on the lanes at junctions.

- **Lane lines,** especially wide, solid or hatched areas.

- **Kerb and parking markings.**

- **Painted islands and channelising areas** at junctions.

- **Bus-lane or reserved-lane symbols.**

- **Metal covers and drain grates,** which are not paint but behave the same way.

## What the law asks in rain

Article 12 of Law 36/2024/QH15 requires operators to keep a speed suited to the road, traffic density and weather, and to observe the surroundings and slow down or stop in rainy or windy conditions, and when there is fog, smoke, dust or a slippery or muddy road surface. Article 20 requires front lights to be on from 18:00 to 06:00 and in rain or other conditions that limit visibility. Circular 38/2024/TT-BGTVT adds that in rain, fog or on slippery surfaces, you must keep a greater distance from the vehicle ahead than the standard values. At a traffic light, Article 11 says that on amber you stop behind the stop line, and on red you stop. Our guide to [how to follow a safe distance in Hanoi traffic](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-follow-a-safe-distance-in-hanoi-traffic/) explains the distance rule.

These duties fit the technique. You are required to slow down, and slowing down early is exactly what makes braking on paint unnecessary.

## Step 1: slow down early, and read the road

The best braking on wet paint is the braking you finish before you reach it.

1. **Look ahead for markings,** especially stop lines, crossings and arrows.

2. **Ease off the throttle early,** and let the scooter slow before the paint.

3. **Use your mirror** and your brake light so that the rider behind is not surprised.

4. **Increase your following distance.** In the wet, give yourself at least double the usual gap.

5. **Plan the stop,** so that you stop behind the stop line with the front wheel clear of the crossing stripes.

## Step 2: brake gently, upright and in a straight line

If you must brake near paint:

- **Brake before the paint, while the bike is upright and moving straight.**

- **Squeeze, do not grab.** Apply both brakes smoothly and progressively, and build the pressure as grip allows.

- **Use both brakes.** Our guides to [automatic scooter braking: front and rear controls](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/automatic-scooter-braking-front-and-rear-controls/) and [front brake versus rear brake on small manual motorbikes](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/front-brake-versus-rear-brake-on-small-manual-motorbikes/) explain how the two work together.

- **Release the brakes before you cross a marking,** and roll across with a steady throttle, then brake again on clear asphalt if you need to.

- **Do not brake while leaning.** Finish slowing before a turn, and then turn smoothly.

- **Do not stab the brake at the last moment.** A sudden input is what breaks traction.

On a semi-automatic bike, using engine braking by easing off the throttle and changing down smoothly is a gentle way to shed speed. ## Step 3: cross markings you cannot avoid

Sometimes you have to ride over paint, for example crossing a wide stripe or arrow at a junction.

- **Cross as upright and straight as possible,** at a right angle where you can.

- **Hold a steady, light throttle,** and do not accelerate or brake on the paint.

- **Keep your grip relaxed,** so that a small slip does not turn into a big steering input.

- **Do not change direction on the paint.**

- **Look past it,** to where you want to be after it.

- **Choose a line between markings** where there is a gap, and where it is safe to do so, but do not swerve into other traffic to avoid paint.

The same applies to manhole covers, drain grates and metal joints.

## Stop lines, crossings and lights

At a traffic light, the stop line and the crossing stripes are the places where you most often brake on paint.

- **Slow early,** and stop with the front wheel behind the line, as Article 11 requires on amber and red.

- **If the road is wet, stop a little earlier** and more gently than you would in the dry.

- **Do not creep forward** onto the stripes.

- **Start gently** when the light changes, because the paint is just as slippery for acceleration as for braking.

- **Watch for pedestrians,** who may cross on the stripes. Article 12 requires you to slow down or stop at crossings and where pedestrians cross.

## The first minutes of rain, and heavy rain

Two situations deserve extra care.

- **The start of a shower after a dry spell.** Published guidance describes the first 10 to 15 minutes as the worst, because oil and grime float to the surface. Ease right off, or stop in a safe place for a few minutes.

- **Heavy rain and standing water.** Water on the road can hide markings, potholes and debris, and fast riding through it can reduce grip. Slow down, keep a steady throttle and do not brake hard in it. ## If the wheel starts to slide

1. **Stay calm,** and do not grab more brake.

2. **Ease off the brake slightly,** if you are braking, until the wheel grips again, and keep the bike upright.

3. **Keep your eyes up,** and look where you want to go.

4. **Do not steer sharply.**

5. **Once you are stable,** brake gently on clear asphalt, or roll to a stop.

If you fall, check for injuries and follow our guide to [what to do after a minor collision on a rental scooter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-to-do-after-a-minor-collision-on-a-rental-scooter/).

## A quick checklist

1. **Slow down before paint, not on it.**

2. **Brake upright, straight and gently, with both brakes.**

3. **Release the brakes before you cross a marking.**

4. **Cross at a right angle, with a steady throttle.**

5. **Stop behind the line, and do not creep onto the stripes.**

6. **Take extra care in the first minutes of rain.**

7. **Double your following distance.**

8. **Check brakes and tyres before you ride.**

## FAQ: braking on wet road markings

### Why are painted lines slippery in the rain?

Paint is smoother than asphalt, and a film of water on it removes much of the grip. Rider-training and touring guides describe wet painted lines as extremely slippery.

### Should I brake on a pedestrian crossing in the rain?

Avoid it. Slow down before you reach the stripes, and stop behind the line without braking hard on the paint.

### How much longer does it take to stop in the wet?

A common rule of thumb is roughly double the dry distance. Treat it as a minimum.

### Is the first rain the most dangerous?

Often, yes. Oil and dust build up in dry weather and float up when rain starts, so the first 10 to 15 minutes can be especially slippery.

### Do I need to avoid manhole covers too?

Yes. Metal covers, grates and expansion joints are as slippery as paint when wet.

### What should I do if the wheel slides?

Ease off the brake slightly, keep the bike upright and look ahead, and do not steer sharply or brake harder.
