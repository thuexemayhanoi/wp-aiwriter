When a thunderstorm arrives suddenly in Hanoi, stop riding as soon as it is safe, park the scooter properly and get inside a sturdy building. The US National Weather Service puts the rule simply: if you can hear thunder, you are close enough to be struck by lightning, no place outside is safe and the right shelter is a substantial building or, failing that, a hard-topped vehicle with the windows up. Stay inside until the storm has moved away and thunder is no longer audible, and for at least 30 minutes after the last clap, as the Service's local guidance advises. A scooter offers no protection at all, and riding on open roads, bridges and riverbanks during a storm is the worst place to be.

This guide explains what to do in the first minutes, where to shelter and where not to, how to park a rental scooter, what to do if you cannot find a building, how to handle strong wind, flooding and fallen branches, what to do if someone is struck and how to restart after the storm. It is general information based on published weather-safety guidance and Vietnamese traffic law as of October 2026, not medical or legal advice.

## Recognise the warning signs early

Lightning risk is highest when a storm is overhead, but the National Weather Service notes that vulnerability is often highest as storms approach and depart, because people do not take shelter early enough or leave shelter too soon. Warning signs for a rider include:

- **Dark, towering clouds** building quickly, especially on a hot, humid afternoon.

- **Distant thunder.** If you can hear it, you are within range.

- **A sudden drop in temperature** and a cool gust of wind.

- **Large, scattered raindrops,** followed by a downpour.

- **Flashes of lightning,** even if you cannot yet hear thunder.

At the first sign, start looking for shelter. Do not wait for the rain, and do not try to outrun the storm. ## What counts as safe shelter

The National Weather Service's guidance is consistent across its local offices.

- **A sturdy, fully enclosed building** is the safest place. In Hanoi that means a shop, cafe, restaurant, hotel lobby, office or station with a solid roof and walls.

- **A hard-topped vehicle with the windows up** is the second choice, for example a car or a bus.

- **No place outside is safe.** If you cannot reach a building or a vehicle, you can only slightly reduce your risk.

Places that look like shelter but are not:

- **Under a tall, isolated tree.** The Service advises never sheltering under tall, isolated trees. The tree keeps you dry but raises the risk of being struck.

- **Small sheds, awnings, carports, open garages and partially enclosed structures.** These are not adequate shelter.

- **Next to tall objects** such as towers, fences, telephone poles and power lines.

- **On a bridge or an elevated place.** The Service advises getting off elevated areas.

- **Near water.** Get away from lakes, ponds and rivers. In Hanoi that includes lakeside and riverbank paths.

- **Under an overpass.** A rider-gear guide warns that stopped vehicles and low visibility under an overpass can create another hazard. Article 18 of Law 36/2024/QH15 also bans stopping and parking under overpasses and on bridges, except in places where it is allowed.

## Step 1: slow down, signal and get off the road

1. **Check your mirror and signal** before you slow down.

2. **Slow smoothly,** and brake gently, because the road may already be wet and slippery. Our guide to [braking safely on wet road markings](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/braking-safely-on-wet-road-markings/) explains why.

3. **Choose a building** you can reach within a minute or two, and head for its front.

4. **Turn on your headlight** if you are still moving. Article 20 of the Law requires front lights in rain and bad weather.

5. **Do not stop in the traffic lane.** Article 18 requires stopping and parking close to the right roadside, without causing obstruction or danger, and with a signal when you enter or leave a stopping place.

## Step 2: park the scooter properly

A rental scooter is someone else's property, so park it with care.

- **Park off the carriageway,** on a guarded lot or in front of the building you are entering, close to the right edge. Our guide to [parking a motorbike in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-motorbike-in-hanoi/) explains guarded lots and tickets.

- **Do not park under trees,** where branches may fall, or under signs and loose structures.

- **Do not park on a footpath crossing, a driveway or a pedestrian crossing.** Article 18 bans stopping and parking on pedestrian crossings and within 5 metres of intersections.

- **Lock the steering and take the key,** and keep the helmet with you. The shop's published terms say no separate lock is provided, so lock the steering and park in guarded places.

- **Put the seat down and close any compartment** so that water does not get in.

## Step 3: go inside, and stay inside

- **Go in at once.** Do not stand at the doorway watching the storm, and keep away from windows and glass doors.

- **Stay away from plumbing and electrical equipment** where you can. The Service notes that lightning can travel through wires and metal pipes.

- **Wait until the storm has passed.** The National Weather Service advises staying inside until the storm has moved away and thunder is no longer audible. Its local guidance says to stay for at least 30 minutes after the last clap of thunder.

- **Use a count if you wish.** One office notes that counting the seconds between a flash and the thunder, with about five seconds to a mile, shows how far away the strike was. If you see a flash and instantly hear thunder, take shelter immediately.

## If you cannot reach a building

If you are on an open road with no building in reach, you cannot make yourself safe, but you can reduce the risk.

1. **Get off the scooter,** and away from it. Metal vehicles do not protect riders on two wheels.

2. **Stay away from tall objects** and from metal fences, poles, power lines and signs.

3. **Do not shelter under a tall isolated tree.** The Service says this raises vulnerability.

4. **Get off elevated ground,** including bridges and embankments, and move to the lowest place you can reach safely, away from water.

5. **Do not lie flat.** The Service notes that lying flat creates more surface area for ground current to travel through.

6. **Get into a hard-topped vehicle** if one stops, such as a bus or a car.

This is damage limitation, not safety. Planning your day around the weather is the real defence.

## Strong wind, flooding and falling branches

Thunderstorms bring more than lightning.

- **Wind.** Gusts can topple parked scooters and drop branches and signs. Stay away from trees and hoardings. If you must ride in wind, our guide to [riding on bridges during strong winds](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-on-bridges-during-strong-winds/) explains how.

- **Flooding.** Heavy rain can flood streets quickly. Do not ride or park in deep water, and do not try to cross water you cannot judge. ## If someone is struck or injured

Lightning strikes can cause cardiac arrest and other serious injuries.

1. **Call for help at once.** The national emergency number is 112, and the ambulance number is 115. Decree 200/2025/ND-CP, in force since 23 August 2025, created the 112 call centre for incidents and emergencies, free from fixed and mobile phones, 24 hours a day.

2. **Move the person to a safer place** only if the area is dangerous, and only if you can do so safely.

3. **Start first aid** if you are trained and the person is not breathing. The National Weather Service's tips note that a person apparently killed by lightning can often be revived by prompt resuscitation, and that if several people are struck, you should treat those who appear dead first.

4. **Stay with the person** until help arrives.

## After the storm

- **Wait.** Do not leave shelter at the first break in the thunder.

- **Check the scooter.** Look at the lights, the mirrors, the tyres and the seat, and look for fallen branches or debris on it.

- **Wipe the seat and the controls,** and dry your hands and helmet.

- **Ride slowly for the first stretch.** The road may be slippery, with debris, standing water and new potholes. Our guide to [dealing with potholes on a rental motorbike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/dealing-with-potholes-on-a-rental-motorbike/) explains how.

## A quick checklist

1. **If you hear thunder, stop riding.**

2. **Get inside a sturdy building, not under a tree, a shed or an overpass.**

3. **Park the scooter properly, lock the steering and take the key.**

4. **Stay inside until thunder has stopped, and for at least 30 minutes after the last clap.**

5. **If caught outside, avoid tall objects, elevated ground and water.**

6. **Call 112 or 115 if anyone is hurt.**

7. **Ride slowly afterwards, and report any problem to the shop.**

## FAQ: thunderstorms and scooter riding in Hanoi

### Is it safe to keep riding in a thunderstorm?

No. If you can hear thunder, you can be struck. Stop and shelter in a sturdy building.

### Where is the safest place to wait?

A sturdy, fully enclosed building, or a hard-topped vehicle with the windows up. Avoid tall isolated trees, small sheds, awnings, bridges and overpasses.

### How long should I wait?

Until the storm has moved away and thunder is no longer audible, and for at least 30 minutes after the last clap, according to the National Weather Service's local guidance.

### Can I shelter under an overpass?

Not safely. It is not adequate lightning shelter, and stopping there can create a traffic hazard. Article 18 of Law 36/2024/QH15 bans stopping and parking under overpasses except where allowed.

### What if I am caught in the open?

Get away from the scooter and from tall objects and water, get to low ground and do not shelter under an isolated tree. This only slightly reduces the risk.

### Who do I call if someone is struck?

112 for the national emergency centre, or 115 for an ambulance. Start first aid if you are trained.
