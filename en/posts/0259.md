An electric scooter has three different speed numbers, and renters often mix them up: the design speed the maker built the bike for, the cap set by the riding mode you have selected, and the legal speed limit for its class. On VinFast scooters, the maker's own page describes two modes, Eco and Sport, with Eco holding the older models to about 30 km/h and Sport giving the strongest acceleration. A speed-limited variant such as the Evo Grand Lite is capped by design at 48 km/h, while the Evo Grand is built for 70 km/h. Whatever the dial says, a moped-class scooter has a legal ceiling of 40 km/h in Vietnam. This guide explains each setting, what changes and what does not, and a short test to run at the shop. It is general information as of October 2026, based on VinFast material that describes particular models rather than every bike on offer.

## Three speeds that are not the same thing

1. **Design speed.** The top speed the manufacturer built the vehicle to reach. Vietnamese law uses it to decide the class: a moped must have a design speed of no more than 50 km/h and, if electric, a motor of no more than 4 kW.
2. **Mode or limiter speed.** A software or controller cap that a riding mode applies on a given day. It can be lower than the design speed, but it does not change the vehicle's class.
3. **Legal speed limit.** What the traffic rules allow on the road. Circular 38/2024/TT-BGTVT, in force from 1 January 2025, sets a maximum operating speed of 40 km/h for mopeds and similar vehicles outside expressways. Motorcycles follow the general tables.

A rider who knows which of the three is limiting them is far less likely to be surprised. A scooter in Sport mode may be able to do more than the law allows, and a scooter in Eco mode may be slower than the traffic around it, but only the third number is an offence if you exceed it.

## What Eco and Sport do on VinFast scooters

VinFast describes the modes on its own site. According to the manufacturer's page on Eco mode, its electric motorbikes have a switch to move between ECO and SPORT, and the rider can change mode while riding.

- **Eco** is described as the mode for gentle acceleration and saving energy. On the older models named on that page, Eco holds the vehicle steady at 30 km/h and does not let it exceed that limit. The page says the newer Theon model has an Eco mode with a higher cap of 50 km/h. The same page says Eco lowers energy use across the vehicle, including dimmer lights and instrument display, while still maintaining visibility.
- **Sport** is described as the stronger mode, giving the best acceleration, suited to climbing a slope or speeding up.
- **Adjustable limits.** The page adds that users can set a lower or higher speed limit for each mode using the phone app.

Those details come from a page dated 2021 and describe the models it names. Newer VinFast scooters may behave differently, so treat the page as an explanation of the idea and not a specification of the bike you will ride. A VnExpress review of the Evo200 gives a user's description of the feel: Sport mode delivers modest response in the first part of the throttle and a strong, steady push between about 20 and 60 km/h, while Eco softens the initial surge to help people new to electric scooters. The same review remarks that the Eco and Sport switch is placed so that the rider has to stretch a thumb to reach it, which is worth noticing before you leave the shop.

## Why some models have a built-in cap

Two VinFast models from the July 2025 specification sheet show how a cap is built in rather than selected:

| Model | Top speed on the sheet | Nominal motor | Peak motor |
|---|---|---|---|
| Evo Grand Lite | 48 km/h | 1,500 W | 1,900 W |
| Evo Grand | 70 km/h (one rider of 65 kg) | 1,500 W | 2,250 W |

The motors have the same nominal rating, but the Lite is sold as a lower-speed version. A trade report on VinFast scooters with removable batteries adds that the Lite versions are aimed at riders without a driving licence and that both the Evo Grand and the Evo Grand Lite offer Eco and Sport modes. A built-in cap like this is part of the design and counts as the design speed, unlike a mode that can be switched off. The class logic that follows from a built-in cap is covered in our guide to [electric bikes in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/electric-bikes-in-hanoi-rules/).

## The legal ceiling on top of the setting

For a moped-class scooter, the legal maximum is 40 km/h wherever mopeds may travel, according to Article 7 of Circular 38/2024/TT-BGTVT. That is lower than the 48 km/h cap of a Lite model and lower than the speed many moped-class scooters can reach. If your scooter's display reads 45 km/h on a flat road, you are above the legal limit even though the bike is behaving as built.

For a motorcycle-class electric scooter, the general table applies. In built-up areas the maximum is 60 km/h on dual carriageways and one-way roads with two or more motor lanes, and 50 km/h on two-way roads and single-lane one-way roads. Signs override the tables. Our [speed limits guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/speed-limits-motorbikes-vietnam/) lists the details for petrol and electric motorbikes, and most Hanoi traffic keeps you well below those numbers anyway.

## Why a lower mode does not change the class

A mode is something you choose and can usually change; the design speed is something the maker fixed. The law's moped definition is written around the design speed. A scooter built for 70 km/h is a motorcycle even if you ride it in a mode that holds it to 30 km/h, which means the licence rule does not become easier because the dial is set low. Our guide to [electric bikes in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/electric-bikes-in-hanoi-rules/) explains the classes. If a shop tells you that you may ride a faster scooter without a licence because it is set to Eco, ask to see the vehicle's registration and the maker's specification, and weigh the answer against what you have read here.

## Speed, mode and range

Speed affects how far you can go. VinFast's own range figures are measured at a steady 30 km/h under its test conditions, and its page quotes the Klara S as reaching up to 120 km in Eco mode at that speed, so they are best-case numbers. Riding faster in Sport mode, carrying a passenger or climbing hills will shorten the distance. If you are planning a longer outing, build in margin; our guide to [charging an electric scooter in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/charging-an-electric-scooter-in-hanoi/) covers charging options.

## A short test at handover

Before you leave the shop, with staff beside you:

1. **Find the mode switch** and reach it with the bike stationary and then at walking pace. Check that you can change mode without looking down for long.
2. **Read the display** to see which mode is active when the bike powers up, and ask whether it resets to a default each time.
3. **Ask whether the speed limits were changed** in the app for this bike, and who controls the settings.
4. **Try the throttle gently** in a quiet area. Note how strongly the bike responds in each mode, and choose the gentler one for your first kilometre.
5. **Check the brakes** at low speed, and make sure you understand that the bike can accelerate quickly from a standstill.
6. **Check the battery level** and what the shop records at handover. The shop's practice for electric vehicles is to confirm battery level and charging at handover.
7. **Use our [inspection checklist](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-scooter-inspection-checklist/)** for the general walk-around.

## Riding tips for a first electric ride

These are general suggestions and not manufacturer instructions:

- Start in the gentler mode and move to the stronger mode only when you are used to the throttle.
- Roll on the throttle smoothly. Electric motors deliver torque instantly, and a sudden twist can surprise a new rider.
- Leave extra space in Hanoi traffic. Quiet scooters are harder for pedestrians to hear, so use the horn and your eyes rather than assuming people know you are there.
- Watch your own speed. The display may read higher than the legal limit before you notice.
- Plan around the roads where two-wheelers are barred; our guide to [Hanoi ring roads and highways](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-ring-roads-highways-motorbikes-allowed/) helps.

## What not to do

Do not try to remove or bypass a speed limiter on a rental, and do not change the app limits unless the shop has told you to. A limited bike modified to go faster is no longer the bike described in its paperwork, and doing so would almost certainly breach the rental contract. If the speed on offer is not enough for your route, choose a different bike instead.

## FAQ: electric scooter speed settings

### What is the difference between Eco and Sport mode?

On VinFast scooters, Eco is for gentle acceleration and saving energy, and Sport gives the strongest acceleration. VinFast's page says the older models in Eco are held to about 30 km/h.

### Does Eco mode make a scooter legal without a licence?

No. The class depends on the design speed and motor power. A mode setting does not change them.

### What is the speed limit for an electric moped?

40 km/h under Circular 38/2024/TT-BGTVT, on roads other than expressways.

### Can I change the speed limit in the phone app?

VinFast says users can set lower or higher limits for each mode in the app. On a rental, only do so if the shop has told you to.

### Why is my display showing more than the legal limit?

The scooter may be capable of more than 40 km/h. The limit applies to you, not to the machine, so watch the display.

### Does riding faster reduce my range?

Yes. VinFast's range figures are measured at 30 km/h, and faster riding, hills and extra load all reduce range.
