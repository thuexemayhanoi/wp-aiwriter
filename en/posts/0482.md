Riding with a passenger works when both people share one plan: the rider controls the bike, the passenger stays still and relaxed, and both agree a few simple signals before they move off. The extra weight changes how a scooter starts, brakes and turns, and one published safety guide notes that riding double can add 20 to 30 per cent to the bike's weight. Law 36/2024/QH15 allows a two-wheeled motorcycle or moped to carry only one person, or two in four listed cases such as a child under 12, and requires every person on board to wear a helmet with the strap fastened (Article 33). Within the law, balance and communication decide whether the ride feels easy or frightening.

This guide explains the legal limits, what to agree before you set off, how to mount and dismount, how the passenger should sit and move, how to signal without earphones, how the rider should adjust braking, turning and slow-speed control, what to do in an emergency and what to tell the shop. It is general information based on the law and published rider guidance as of October 2026, not legal advice.

## What the law says

Article 33 of Law 36/2024/QH15 sets the main rules for motorcycles and mopeds.

- **Number of people.** The driver may carry only one person, or no more than two persons when transporting a sick person to emergency care, escorting a person who has committed a violation, carrying children under 12, or carrying old and weak people or people with disabilities. Our guide to [child passengers on motorbikes in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/child-passengers-motorbike-vietnam/) covers the child rule in detail.

- **Helmets.** The driver and the persons carried must wear helmets that meet the national technical regulation, with the chin strap properly fastened. Our guide to [helmet rules in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/helmet-rules-in-vietnam/) explains the standard.

- **What a passenger may not do.** A passenger may not carry bulky objects, use an umbrella, cling to or tow other vehicles, stand on the saddle or cargo rack, or sit on the steering wheel.

- **What the driver may not do.** The driver may not take both hands off the handlebars, carry people standing on the bike, or use sound devices other than hearing aids.

Penalties are set in Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, so read the current text for the figure that applies. Our guide to [traffic fines in Vietnam that catch foreign riders out](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/traffic-fines-vietnam-foreign-riders/) lists the common ones.

## Check the bike and the load

1. **Check that the bike has a proper passenger seat and footrests.** Some bikes fold their footrests away, so make sure they are down before the passenger gets on.

2. **Ask the shop about the maximum load.** The owner's manual states the maximum load for the model, and the shop can tell you. Do not exceed it.

3. **Check the tyres and brakes.** The [rental guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-guide/) lists the handover checks, and published guidance suggests checking tyre pressure for the extra load. Ask the shop what pressure it recommends for two people.

4. **Choose a bike with room.** Our guides to [choosing a scooter with space for a passenger helmet](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/choosing-a-scooter-with-space-for-a-passenger-helmet/) and [riding a 50cc scooter with a passenger: safety factors](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-a-50cc-scooter-with-a-passenger-safety-factors/) help you choose.

5. **Pack light.** A heavy bag on one person's back shifts the balance. Our guide to [why loose scarves and bags are dangerous on motorbikes](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/why-loose-scarves-and-bags-are-dangerous-on-motorbikes/) explains how to carry things.

## The briefing: five minutes before you move

Safety guides for riders and passengers agree that a short talk beats a long apology. Agree these points.

| Topic | What to agree |
|---|---|
| **Mounting** | The passenger gets on only after the rider says so, and gets off only after the rider says so |
| **Feet** | Feet stay on the footrests at all times, including at stops; never put a foot down to steady the bike |
| **Hands** | Hold the rider's waist, the grab rail or the strap, whichever is firmer |
| **Leaning** | Sit upright and move with the bike; do not lean on your own in a turn |
| **Stillness** | No sudden moves, no turning round, no photographs while moving |
| **Signals** | A tap on the shoulder means slow down; two taps mean stop; a squeeze means all is well; agree your own set |
| **Discomfort** | Say so at once; the rider will stop |
| **Destination** | The rider shares the route and the first stops |

One safety guide advises the rider to tell a nervous passenger that the bike leans to turn and that this is normal, and to tell the passenger to look over the rider's shoulder on the inside of the turn, which puts their weight in the right place.

## Mounting and dismounting

1. **The rider gets on first,** holds the bars, puts both feet down and holds the front brake. With a side stand, check that the bike is firm.

2. **The passenger waits for the signal,** then steps up, keeping their weight centred and not pulling the bike towards themselves. Passenger guides describe the left side as the customary side to mount from.

3. **The passenger puts both feet on the footrests** and finds the grab rail or the rider's waist.

4. **Both check that nothing hangs loose,** such as scarves, straps, ponchos or bags.

5. **The rider moves off gently,** and the passenger gives a thumbs-up or a tap when ready.

6. **To get off,** the rider stops, puts both feet down and holds the brake, and the passenger steps off on the signal.

Watch out for the exhaust and the hot parts of the bike. Our guide to [handling a scooter pillion passenger in slow traffic](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/handling-a-scooter-pillion-passenger-in-slow-traffic/) explains how to manage stop-start traffic.

## How the passenger should sit and move

- **Sit close to the rider.** Published guidance says that a passenger sitting too far back can make the front feel light and affect steering.

- **Stay upright and relaxed.** The passenger should move with the bike, not against it.

- **Look over the rider's shoulder** in the direction of the turn. A passenger guide says this puts the weight where it belongs.

- **Keep the feet on the footrests.** Never put a foot down to stop the bike, since that can pull it over.

- **Brace on braking.** When the rider brakes, the passenger's weight moves forward. Press the feet on the footrests and use the thighs to stay back, so as not to push the rider forward.

- **Keep your arms where the rider can feel them.** Hold the waist or the grab rail, not the rider's arms or the handlebars.

- **Do not use umbrellas, hold bulky items or stand up.** Article 33 bans them.

- **Do not use the phone,** except to show a map when the bike is stopped, and do not photograph over the rider's shoulder. Our guide to [when to stop and check a map instead of looking down](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/when-to-stop-and-check-a-map-instead-of-looking-down/) explains how a passenger can help with navigation.

## Communication without earphones

Wind and traffic make speech hard on a scooter. Do not use earphones, since Article 33 bans sound devices for drivers other than hearing aids and earphones hide the sounds you need to hear. Use signals.

- **Taps on the shoulder:** agree what one tap and two taps mean.

- **Squeezes or a thumbs-up:** all is well.

- **A pointing hand:** to show a hazard, such as a hole or a vehicle, at the side. Passenger guides encourage passengers to help look out for dangers.

- **Speak at stops,** when the engine is idling, to check how the passenger feels.

- **Plan stops:** agree where you will stop for water or a rest.

If the passenger is a child, give simple signals and keep rides short. Our guide to [children on scooters: what to verify before riding](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/children-on-scooters-what-to-verify-before-riding/) explains what to check.

## How the rider should adjust

The passenger changes the bike's balance, braking and acceleration.

1. **Start gently.** A smooth throttle avoids a lurch that throws the passenger back.

2. **Brake earlier and softer.** Braking distance increases with the extra weight, as safety guides note, so leave more distance. Our guide to [how to follow a safe distance in Hanoi traffic](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-follow-a-safe-distance-in-hanoi-traffic/) explains how.

3. **Use both brakes together,** and brake before the bend, not in it.

4. **Take turns more slowly,** with a smaller lean, and keep a steady throttle through them.

5. **Be careful at slow speed.** A scooter with two people is more top-heavy. Look where you want to go, and keep your feet ready.

6. **Leave extra room when overtaking.** Our guide to [safe overtaking decisions on two-wheel vehicles](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/safe-overtaking-decisions-on-two-wheel-vehicles/) explains how.

7. **Be gentle on rough surfaces.** Our guide to [dealing with potholes on a rental motorbike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/dealing-with-potholes-on-a-rental-motorbike/) explains how to take them with a passenger.

8. **Avoid conditions that push your limits,** such as heavy rain, strong wind and night riding when you are new to carrying someone.

## Practice before you go

If you have not carried a passenger before, spend ten minutes in a quiet, empty place such as a wide car park, with the passenger's helmet on. Practise moving off, stopping, turning at walking pace and braking gently. Do not take a first-time passenger straight into Old Quarter traffic. Our guide to [riding in Hanoi's Old Quarter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-old-quarter/) explains why a narrow street is the wrong place to learn.

## If something goes wrong

- **If the passenger shouts or taps twice,** slow down and stop at the next safe place.

- **If the bike wobbles,** ease off the throttle, keep the bars steady and brake gently.

- **If the passenger falls or is hurt,** stop, switch on hazard lights if you have them and call 112. Our guides to [what to do after a minor collision on a rental scooter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-to-do-after-a-minor-collision-on-a-rental-scooter/) and [emergency numbers and accident reporting in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/emergency-numbers-and-accident-reporting-in-vietnam/) explain the steps.

## A quick checklist

1. **Count heads: one passenger, or two only in the cases the law lists.**

2. **Check the helmets, the footrests and the load.**

3. **Brief the passenger: mount, feet, hands, stillness, signals.**

4. **Rider on first, passenger on the signal.**

5. **Move off gently, brake early, take turns slowly.**

6. **Passenger sits close, stays upright, looks over the inside shoulder.**

7. **Use taps, not earphones.**

8. **Stop whenever either of you asks.**

## FAQ: riding with a passenger

### How many people can ride on a scooter in Vietnam?

Article 33 of Law 36/2024/QH15 allows one passenger, or two in four listed cases, including children under 12. Never more than two.

### Must the passenger wear a helmet?

Yes. Article 33 requires the driver and persons carried to wear helmets that meet the standard, with the strap fastened.

### Should the passenger lean in turns?

The passenger should sit upright and move with the rider, looking over the inside shoulder, not leaning independently.

### Can the passenger use the phone for directions?

Better to stop and check. If the passenger reads the map, they should do it carefully and tell the rider, and the rider must not hold a phone while riding.

### Can we use earphones to talk?

Do not wear earphones. Article 33 bans sound devices for drivers except hearing aids. Use taps and stops.

### Does a passenger make the bike harder to stop?

Yes. The extra weight lengthens braking distance, so brake earlier and more gently.
