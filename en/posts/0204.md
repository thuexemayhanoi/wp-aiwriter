A 50cc scooter can carry a rider and one passenger under Vietnamese rules, but with very little power in reserve, so the sensible approach is to treat it as a short-distance, low-speed arrangement and to confirm that your particular bike is built for two. The 2024 road traffic law allows a two-wheeled motorcycle or moped to carry one person in addition to the rider, with a short list of exceptions for more, and the moped speed cap of 40 km/h applies whoever is on board. The real limits are physical: a small engine hauling two adults accelerates slowly, climbs poorly, needs more room to stop and leaves less margin on a wet or crowded street. This guide explains the rules as of October 2026, the mechanical and human factors that change with a passenger, how to brief the passenger, and when a larger scooter is the better choice.

## What the rules allow

Article 33 of Law 36/2024/QH15 on Road Traffic Order and Safety, in force since 1 January 2025, covers motorcycles and mopeds together. Its first clause says that the rider of a two-wheeled motorcycle or moped may carry only one person, with these exceptions, in which up to two people may be carried:

- taking a sick person for emergency care;
- escorting a person who has committed an offence;
- a child under 12;
- an elderly or disabled person.

So a rider with one adult passenger is within the rule, and a rider with two adults is not unless an exception applies. The same article requires the rider and the passenger to wear a helmet that meets the national standard, correctly strapped. It forbids the rider from, among other things, steering sitting sideways, standing, turning round to face backward, letting the foot or stand scrape the road, or carrying bulky items, and it forbids the passenger from carrying bulky items, using an umbrella, holding on to or pushing other vehicles, standing on the seat or luggage rack or sitting on the handlebars. Loads must not extend more than 0.3 metres each side of the rack or 0.5 metres behind it, nor rise above 2 metres from the road.

Two other rules matter for 50cc riders. A moped is limited to 40 km/h under Circular 38/2024/TT-BGTVT on roads other than expressways, according to a legal summary of that circular; our guide to [speed limits for motorbikes in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/speed-limits-motorbikes-vietnam/) sets out the full table. And the minimum age to ride a moped is 16. Our overview of [renting a 50cc scooter in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/renting-a-50cc-scooter-in-hanoi/) explains the moped definition, and the [helmet rules guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/helmet-rules-in-vietnam/) covers the strap and standard.

## Is this bike meant for two?

The rule above says what the road law allows. It does not say what the manufacturer designed the bike to do. Honda Global's table for the Japanese-market Super Cub 50 Pro lists seating for one person, for example, while many 50cc scooters sold elsewhere have a two-person seat and footrests. Models differ, so check the bike in front of you:

- Is there a **pillion seat** long enough for an adult, or a short single seat?
- Are there **passenger footrests** that fold down, so the passenger's feet are off the ground?
- Is there a **grab rail or strap** behind the seat for the passenger to hold?
- Is there a **rear rack** you may be tempted to sit on? A rack is for luggage and not a seat.
- What does the shop say about this bike and a passenger? Ask directly, and do not assume.

A bike without footrests or a grab rail is a single-rider bike for practical purposes. Honda's Wave RSX FI manual, for a 110cc model, tells riders to instruct the passenger to hold the rear grab rail or the rider's waist and keep their feet on the footrests. A bike on which that is not possible is not set up for a passenger.

## Power and weight: what a small engine does with two people

The numbers show the gap. Honda Global lists the Super Cub 50 at a maximum output of 2.7 kW and torque of 3.8 N·m, and the Super Cub 110 at 5.9 kW and 8.5 N·m. The 110 has more than twice the output of the 50. Other 50cc bikes will not be identical, but the comparison is typical of the class: half the engine, with the same two adults on top.

What that means in daily use:

- **Slow pull-away.** Starting from a junction takes longer, and you will be passed by 110cc and 125cc scooters.
- **Poor climbing.** Bridges, flyovers and ramps can slow a 50cc with two adults almost to walking pace.
- **Little reserve for emergencies.** If you need to accelerate out of trouble, there is not much to give.
- **More engine strain.** A small engine at full throttle for a long time runs hot and wears faster.
- **Higher fuel use** than a solo ride on the same route, though you should check how much the bike actually uses.

Our guide to [Honda Lead and Vision for two riders with luggage](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/scooter-for-two-riders-and-luggage-hanoi/) describes how a larger scooter copes with the same load.

### Weight limits

Honda's Wave RSX FI manual says that overloading affects handling, braking and stability and lists a maximum load figure for the model, together with a limit for the storage box. It also says to tie luggage down securely, balance it and keep it near the centre of the bike. A 50cc bike has its own figure, usually on a label or in the handbook. Ask the shop for it. Two adults, helmets and a bag can reach a bike's limit quickly, and exceeding it is a safety problem, not just a legal one.

## Braking, balance and stability

The Motorcycle Safety Foundation's guidance on riding with a passenger lists several effects that apply to any motorcycle and are stronger on a light one:

- The passenger adds weight and moves independently, which changes handling.
- A passenger tends to slide forward in quick stops and may bump the rider's helmet.
- Starting from a stop may need more throttle.
- Braking sooner and with greater pressure may be required.
- Extra weight over the rear tyre can make the rear brake more useful in hard stops.
- Braking distance rises on a downhill.
- Extra caution is needed in corners because of the extra weight.

For a 50cc scooter, the practical response is to ride slower than you would solo, leave more space ahead, brake early with both brakes, and avoid sudden swerves. The MSF also advises practising low-speed control and normal and emergency braking with a passenger in a quiet area before heading into traffic, which is a good use of a car park or a quiet lane.

### Tyre pressure

Honda's Wave RSX FI manual shows a different rear tyre pressure when a passenger is carried: 225 kPa for the rider alone and 280 kPa for rider and passenger, with the front unchanged at 200 kPa. Your 50cc bike will have its own figures, probably on a label near the seat or swing arm. The principle is the point: tyre pressure may need to change for a passenger. Ask the shop to check the pressure and say if the rear needs to be raised. Do not guess, and do not let a roadside stall inflate beyond the label. The same manual says to check tyre pressure when the tyres are cold.

## The passenger's job

The MSF treats a passenger as a second active participant rather than luggage. In practice:

1. **Wear a helmet that fits, with the strap fastened.** The same rule applies to passengers as to riders. Our guide to [child passengers on a motorbike in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/child-passengers-motorbike-vietnam/) covers small passengers, who need particular care.
2. **Sit all the way back, behind the rider,** and not on the rack, with both feet on the footrests.
3. **Hold the grab rail or the rider's waist.** Honda says to hold the rail or the hips, and that feet go on the footrests.
4. **Keep still and lean with the bike,** not against it, in turns. Do not look over the rider's shoulder or shift suddenly.
Then the details that are easy to overlook:

5. **Wear sensible clothes.** Honda warns against loose clothing that can catch on the bike, and recommends visible colours, closed shoes and long trousers. Flip-flops can slip off.
6. **Carry nothing bulky and no umbrella.** The law forbids both for passengers.
7. **Tell the rider if anything feels wrong,** and agree a signal for "stop" before you start.
8. **Do not mount or dismount until the rider says so,** and do so from the left side, away from traffic.

## The rider's job

- **Brief the passenger before you move.** Two minutes at the kerb prevents most surprises.
- **Practise first** in a quiet place with the passenger, including a gentle start and a gentle stop.
- **Ride smoothly:** progressive throttle, early braking, wide turns.
- **Keep a longer gap** to the vehicle ahead, because stopping takes more distance.
- **Choose the route.** Avoid steep ramps, long climbs and fast roads where a 50cc will be the slowest vehicle on it. Our guide to [Hanoi's ring roads and highways](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-ring-roads-highways-motorbikes-allowed/) explains which roads are closed to motorbikes.
- **Watch the heat:** a laden small engine runs hot in a Hanoi summer, so allow rest stops.
- **Do not overload.** If two adults and luggage are too much, drop the luggage or take a taxi.

## When a 50cc bike is the wrong choice for two

| Situation | Better choice |
|---|---|
| Two adults and bags on hilly or long routes | A larger scooter, if a licence allows it |
| A day trip outside the city | A bigger bike or a different mode of transport |
| A child under 12 as the second passenger | Fine in law, but ride slowly and see the child passenger guide |
| A passenger who cannot reach footrests or hold on | Do not carry them |
| Wet, dark or crowded conditions with an inexperienced rider | Walk, use a taxi or wait |

If your licence covers a bigger bike, a 110cc scooter has far more reserve for two; our guide to [Honda Vision versus Air Blade](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/honda-vision-vs-air-blade-which-scooter-to-rent-in-hanoi/) compares two common choices, and the [motorbikes page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbikes/) shows the fleet. ## FAQ: a 50cc scooter with a passenger

### Can two adults legally ride a 50cc scooter?

Yes, as rider and one passenger, each with a helmet. A second passenger is allowed only in the exceptions listed in Article 33, such as a child under 12.

### How fast can a 50cc scooter go with two people?

The legal cap for mopeds is 40 km/h, and the bike's own acceleration and climbing will usually be well below what it does solo.

### Do I need to change the tyre pressure for a passenger?

Possibly. Honda's manual for a larger model raises the rear pressure when a passenger rides. Ask the shop to check the label for your bike.

### Can a child sit in front of the rider?

Our guide to child passengers covers this. The rule allows a child under 12 as an additional person, but the child needs a proper helmet and a safe position, and a small scooter is a poor place for one.

### Should the passenger sit on the luggage rack?

No. The rack is for luggage. The law forbids a passenger from standing on it or sitting on the handlebars, and the passenger should be on the seat with feet on the rests.

### Is a 50cc safe for two on a bridge or flyover?

It can be done, but the climb is slow. Choose a flatter route if you can.
