For a short, regular commute inside Hanoi, an electric bike is cheap to run and simple to live with if you can charge it overnight, while a petrol scooter wins whenever the day is long, the route is unpredictable or there is nowhere to plug in. The deciding factors are the length of your daily ride, whether you can charge reliably and how much range margin you want. This guide compares the two on energy per kilometre using published figures, on refuelling versus charging, on range, speed and traffic, and on rental terms at Nguyen Ha. It uses "electric bike" for the group of electric bicycles, 50cc-equivalent electric scooters and electric motorbikes, and it works through a sample commute in units, not prices, so you can apply today's local prices yourself.

## Start with the commute, not the vehicle

Before comparing machines, write down your commute.

- How many kilometres each way, and how many days a week?
- Is the route flat city streets, a ring road or a mix?
- Do you ever add errands, a lunch across town or a trip at the weekend?
- Where will the bike sleep: a hotel, an apartment with a socket or a street?
- How long will you rent? Weekly and monthly rates are the ones that matter for commuters.

A commuter who rides 8 km each way on city streets and parks at a flat with a socket is a good electric candidate. A commuter who rides 25 km each way on a mix of streets and a ring road, with unplanned stops, usually is not.

## Energy per kilometre: the numbers behind the choice

Published figures let you compare energy use without guessing.

**Petrol.** Honda Vietnam's product page for the Air Blade 125 lists fuel consumption of 2.12 litres per 100 km and a 4.4-litre tank. Dealer pages citing Vietnam Register data give about 1.85 litres per 100 km for the Honda Vision. Honda's UK page for the Vision 110 lists 1.9 litres per 100 km. So a commuter petrol scooter uses about 2 litres per 100 km on a good day.

**Electric.** VinFast's claimed ranges for its electric motorbikes work out at about 17 to 18 Wh per km under test conditions (a steady 30 km/h, 65 kg rider). Real city use will be higher. Charging is not perfectly efficient either, and one source puts losses at 10 to 15 per cent.

Apply them to a sample commute of 20 km a day for 22 working days, which is 440 km a month.

| Item | Petrol scooter | Electric bike |
|---|---|---|
| Energy per 100 km | about 2.0 litres | about 1.75 kWh at test conditions |
| Monthly energy, 440 km | about 8.8 to 9.3 litres | about 7.7 kWh at test conditions, about 8.8 kWh with charging losses |
| Typical full-tank or full-battery range | about 200 km on a 4.4-litre tank at 2.12 litres per 100 km | claimed 117 to 205 km, much less in real use |

To find money, multiply the litres by the pump price and the kilowatt-hours by your electricity price. This guide does not quote either, because both change. What the table shows is that the electric bike uses a small amount of energy and the petrol scooter a small amount of fuel. Neither is expensive to run on a 20 km commute, so the rental price and the convenience matter more than the energy bill.

The figures for electric come from VinFast models and are not a measurement of any rental bike.

## Refuelling versus charging

This is where daily life differs most.

**Petrol:** pull into any station, fill in a couple of minutes, pay and go. The shop hands petrol bikes over with about 0.5 to 2 litres and expects about 0.5 to 1 litre back, and an empty tank at return costs VND 20,000. Our guide on [fuel in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/fuel-in-hanoi-petrol-stations-rental-scooter/) explains stations and filling.

**Electric:** charge at night. A published figure for a 2.4 kWh VinFast pack on a 400 W charger is 6 hours 30 minutes, and a 2.0 kWh pack takes 5 hours 20 minutes. Overnight charging fits a commute well, as long as you have a safe socket. If you do not, an electric bike turns into a chore. Our guide on [charging an electric scooter in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/charging-an-electric-scooter-in-hanoi/) covers hotels and apartments.

The electric bike also forces a small habit change. A petrol rider refuels when the tank is low. An electric rider plugs in whenever the bike is home and parked, so it starts every day full.

## Range margin and weekend plans

A petrol scooter on a 4.4-litre tank gives about 200 km on the published figure. That is ten days of a 20 km commute between refills, and it covers a surprise trip.

An electric bike's headline range is 117 to 205 km on VinFast models, but real range is lower, especially with two people, hills or hot weather. For a 20 km commute that is still plenty. The question is the occasional long day. If you want to ride to a craft village or the Red River on Saturday, a single charge covers it. If you want a 100 km trip to Ninh Binh, a petrol scooter is the safer choice. Our [Hanoi to Ninh Binh guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-to-ninh-binh-by-motorbike/) describes that ride.

A good rule is to keep one third of the range in hand on the commute, whichever type you pick.

## Speed, roads and traffic

Hanoi's streets reward small, quick and nimble machines, and both types qualify. Differences appear at the extremes.

- **Top speed.** Electric options include models limited to about 48 km/h and others up to about 70 km/h. A petrol scooter like the Vision 110 lists a top speed of 89 km/h on Honda UK's page. For city streets that difference is irrelevant, but on a fast arterial road a 48 km/h bike is a slow vehicle among faster traffic. Our guide to [Hanoi's ring roads and highways](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-ring-roads-highways-motorbikes-allowed/) explains where motorbikes are allowed.
- **Acceleration.** An electric motor delivers its pull instantly, which is pleasant from a traffic light but needs a gentle hand at first.
- **Noise.** Electric bikes are nearly silent. Pedestrians may not hear you, so use the horn lightly.
- **Gears.** Both are twist-and-go. There is no clutch or gearbox to manage on an electric bike or an automatic scooter.

## Weather, heat and rain

Rain affects riders far more than vehicles. VinFast lists IP67 ratings for its batteries and motors, which means the models mentioned can withstand water, according to the maker, but it is not an invitation to ride through deep flood water. Both types need a raincoat and a sensible approach to slippery roads. The shop does not include a raincoat for short rentals, so bring your own.

In very hot weather, a petrol engine and a battery both work harder, but a commuter on short trips will see little difference. Our guide to [riding in Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) goes through the season and the streets that flood.

## Licence and registration questions

The vehicle's class decides whether you need a motorcycle licence. A petrol automatic scooter of about 110 cc generally needs one, while some electric models limited to about 48 km/h and a lower power are built for riders without one. Which category a specific bike falls into is for the shop to confirm, because models differ. Our overview of [licence rules for foreigners](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbike-licence-rules-for-foreigners-in-vietnam/) has the detail. Helmets are required on both, and one basic helmet per rider is included.

## FAQ: electric bike or petrol scooter for commuting

### Which is cheaper to run on a daily commute?

Both use little energy over 20 km a day. By published figures, a petrol scooter uses about 2 litres per 100 km and an electric bike about 1.75 kWh per 100 km at test conditions. Multiply by local prices to compare.

### Which has the longer range?

A petrol scooter with a 4.4-litre tank gives about 200 km per fill on the published figure. Electric headline ranges are 117 to 205 km on some VinFast models, and real range is lower.

### Can I charge an electric bike at a hotel?

Often, but ask first. Staff can point out a safe socket, and a full charge takes several hours.

### Is an electric bike easier to ride?

Both are automatic and twist-and-go. The electric motor's instant pull needs a gentle start, and the lack of engine noise means you should be careful around pedestrians.

### What if I need to go further than planned?

A petrol scooter can be refilled in minutes. An electric bike needs a charge, so plan longer days around a socket or rent petrol.
