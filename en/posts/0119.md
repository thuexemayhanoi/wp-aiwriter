Before you ride off on a rented automatic scooter, check five things on each tyre: whether it looks and feels properly inflated, whether the tread wear marks are still hidden, whether the sidewalls are free of cracks and bulges, whether anything is stuck in the rubber and whether the valve cap is in place. It takes about five minutes with the bike on its stand and staff beside you, and it needs no tools beyond your eyes, your fingers and, ideally, the shop's gauge. Honda's manuals say tyre pressure should be measured with a gauge at least once a month and always when the tyres are cold, that tyres whose wear indicators have appeared should be replaced immediately and that improperly inflated or excessively worn tyres can cause a crash. This guide turns that into a daily-rental routine. It is general information, not an inspection, and the shop has the final say on your bike.

## Why tyres deserve five minutes

On a scooter the tyres are small, the contact patch is small and everything you do, from braking to leaning to pulling away, passes through them. A tyre that is soft, worn or damaged affects braking and steering before it ever shows as a warning light. Honda's owner's manuals carry the same warning in several models: riding on tyres that are excessively worn or improperly inflated can cause a crash in which you can be seriously hurt, and you should follow the instructions about inflation and maintenance.

For a daily renter the question is not "how do I maintain a tyre" but "is this tyre fit to ride today, and what do I tell the shop if it is not". Maintenance, replacement and repair belong to the shop. Your job is to notice, photograph and report.

## The five-point tyre look

### 1. Pressure: look, press and ask

A scooter tyre that is low often shows it as a flat-looking bulge at the bottom, a squashed look from behind or a heavy, vague feel when the bike is pushed. Press the tread with your thumb: a properly inflated tyre feels firm, not squashy. These are only clues. The honest way is a gauge, so ask the shop to check the pressure, or to inflate it, and note the figure it reads.

Honda says to use an air pressure gauge at least once a month, or any time the tyres look low, and to check when the tyres are cold, because a warm tyre reads higher. At handover the tyres are usually cold, which is why the check belongs before the first ride rather than after it.

### 2. Tread and the wear indicator

Honda's manuals tell riders to inspect the tread wear indicators, which are small raised marks in the grooves, usually pointed to by a TWI mark or a location mark on the tyre shoulder. If the indicators become visible, Honda says to replace the tyre immediately. On a rental you can check by eye: if the grooves look shallow or the tread looks smooth and shiny, ask whether the tyre is new or well worn, and photograph the tread.

Look also for uneven wear. Honda's guidance mentions inspecting the contact surface for abnormal wear, since badly worn areas shorten tyre life and bring vibration and noise. A flat strip down the centre, one shoulder worn away or patches that look scalloped are all worth a question.

### 3. Sidewalls: cracks, cuts and bulges

Turn the front wheel and look at the sidewalls on both sides, then walk round to the rear. You are looking for:

- Fine cracks in the rubber, especially around the rim, from age or sun.
- Cuts or scrapes that show cord.
- A bulge or a blister, where the tyre looks swollen.
- A tyre that has marks of rubbing against the mudguard.

Do not accept a tyre with a bulge or an exposed cord. Ask for another bike.

### 4. Things stuck in the tyre

Honda's global guidance says to inspect the whole circumference of the tyre for cracks, damage and any nails, stones or other foreign objects stuck in it. To do that, rotate the wheel slowly, with the bike on its stand, and look at the full tread. If you see a nail or a screw, do not pull it out. Photograph it and tell the shop, because it can be the only thing sealing the hole.

### 5. Valve and rim

Look at the valve stem and cap on each wheel. A missing cap lets dirt into the valve, which can cause a slow leak. Check that the stem stands straight and does not look torn at the base. Glance at the rim for dents or scrapes, especially on the edge, where a kerb strike leaves its mark. Honda also tells riders to inspect the tyres after any contact with the side of a kerb.

## Pressure figures from Honda's manuals

Pressure differs by model, and the figure that counts is the one on the bike. The tyre information and cargo limit label on a Honda scooter carries the cold pressure, and Honda's global page says that this label is often on the frame or chain guard. The figures below come from Honda manuals and are examples, not instructions for your bike:

| Model (manual) | Front, rider only | Rear, rider only | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Honda Lead 125 (Vietnam) | 175 kPa (25 psi) | 225 kPa (33 psi) | Cold pressure on the label |
| Honda Air Blade 160 (Vietnam) | 200 kPa (29 psi) | 225 kPa (33 psi) | Same with passenger for the 160 |
| Honda Air Blade 125 (Vietnam) | 200 kPa (29 psi) | 225 kPa (33 psi) | 280 kPa (41 psi) rear with a passenger |
| Honda Click125 | 200 kPa (29 psi) | 225 kPa (33 psi) | Same with a passenger |

Because the numbers differ between a Lead and an Air Blade, asking "what is the cold pressure for this bike" is better than assuming. Many shop gauges and roadside pumps show psi or bar rather than kPa, so ask the shop to convert, or use the label. Our guide on [reading a Honda Wave tyre size and pressure label](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-read-a-honda-wave-tyre-size-and-pressure-label/) explains the label on a manual bike, which works the same way.

### What the passenger changes

A heavier load changes the right pressure on some models, as the Air Blade 125 figure shows. If you will carry a passenger or heavy luggage, ask whether the rear pressure should be different, and tell the shop before you leave.

## A daily-rental tyre routine

| When | What to do | Time |
|---|---|---|
| At handover | Full five-point look, photograph both tyres and valves, ask for a gauge reading | 5 minutes |
| After the first ten minutes | Notice whether the bike feels heavy, vague or noisy | Passive |
| Mid-day stop | Glance at both tyres and valves | 1 minute |
| After a kerb strike, pothole or debris | Stop and look at the tyre and rim | 2 minutes |
| After heavy rain | Look for new cuts or objects | 1 minute |
| At return | Show the shop any new marks | 2 minutes |

The shop's day is 24 hours from handover, and the shop is open from 09:00 to 21:00 daily, so plan the handover check in daylight. The [10-minute rental scooter inspection](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-scooter-inspection-checklist/) puts the tyre check in the full routine.

## Hanoi conditions that wear on tyres

- **Heat.** Hot asphalt in summer raises tyre temperature and pressure, so a reading taken after a long ride is higher than one taken cold.
- **Rain.** Wet roads make worn tread dangerous, and our guide to [riding in Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) explains the season. Check before heading out on a wet day.
- **Debris.** Roadworks, construction sites and market streets often have nails, glass and wire on the surface.
- **Kerbs and potholes.** Hit one slowly and at right angles, and check the tyre afterwards.
- **Loading.** A passenger and bags push more weight onto the rear tyre.

## Signs of trouble while riding

Stop in a safe place and look at the tyres if:

- The steering feels heavy or the scooter pulls to one side.
- The rear feels loose or the scooter wobbles in a straight line.
- You feel a rhythmic thump with each wheel turn.
- You hear a hiss, a flapping noise or a scraping sound.
- The scooter feels unusually soft over bumps.

Do not ride on a flat tyre, because it can damage the rim and make the scooter hard to control. Call the shop on 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969) and share your location. ## What not to do yourself

- **Do not change the tyre or the tube.** Honda says to have tyres replaced by a dealer, and the right size and type matter, since the wrong tyre can affect handling and stability.
- **Do not remove nails** or other objects.
- **Do not overinflate.** Higher is not safer.
- **Do not repair or patch** a tyre by the road unless the shop tells you to.
- **Do not ride on a visibly damaged tyre** to reach the nearest stall.

## What to tell the shop

If something looks off, speak up before you ride away, and be specific:

- "The front tyre looks soft. Can you check the pressure and tell me the figure?"
- "I can see a nail in the rear tyre."
- "The tread looks shiny. Is this tyre near replacement?"
- "The valve cap is missing on the front wheel."
- "Can you tell me the correct pressure for me alone and for two people?"

A good shop will check, fix or swap the bike without fuss. Prices and rental terms are on the [prices page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/prices/) and in the [rental guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-guide/).

## FAQ: scooter tyre checks

### How often should tyre pressure be checked?

Honda says at least once a month, and any time the tyres look low. For a daily rental, check at handover.

### Should the tyres be cold or warm when I check?

Cold. Honda notes that a warm tyre reads higher.

### What are the small marks on the tyre shoulder?

They point to the tread wear indicators. If the indicators become visible, Honda says to replace the tyre immediately.

### Is the pressure the same on every scooter?

No. The figure differs by model and load, so read the label on the bike.

### Can I top up the air myself?

Ask the shop first. If you do use a pump, use the cold pressure from the label.

### Who pays if I get a puncture?

It depends on the cause and the contract, so ask before you sign.
