The ten minutes between signing a rental contract and riding away are the most useful ten minutes of the whole rental. A quick, methodical look at the scooter tells you whether it is safe, gives you the evidence you need if there is a disagreement about damage when you bring it back, and catches the small faults, such as a dead brake light or a missing mirror, that can earn a fine at the first police stop. You do not need to be a mechanic. This guide gives a minute-by-minute routine for an automatic scooter of the kind most visitors rent in Hanoi, such as a Honda Vision, Air Blade or Lead, with notes for electric scooters. It reflects Vietnamese rules as of October 2026 and the way Nguyen Ha hands over its bikes; if you are renting a geared bike like a Honda Wave, add the extra checks in our guide to [checking a semi-automatic motorbike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-check-a-semi-automatic-motorbike-before-renting-in-hanoi/).

## Before you start: agree the time and switch on your camera

Do the inspection with the shop owner or staff standing next to you, in daylight or under a good light, before you sign or immediately after. Note the handover time on the contract, because rental days run in 24-hour blocks and the return time is counted from it. Then start a video on your phone and keep it running while you walk around the bike. A continuous video with the date stamp is far more persuasive than a handful of photos, because it shows the whole bike and cannot be accused of hiding a panel.

Speak while you film: "front left, scratch on the panel", "rear tyre, good tread". It feels awkward for a minute, but it creates a record that both you and the shop can refer to later. A good rental shop welcomes this, because a clear record protects them from disputes too.

## Minute 1–2: walk around the bodywork

Start at the front and walk slowly round the scooter. Look at every plastic panel, the front mudguard, the mirrors, the seat and the exhaust guard, and film any scratch, crack, dent or missing piece up close. Pay attention to the lower edges and the ends of the handlebars, which are where a dropped scooter usually marks first. Show the number plate clearly, front and back, so the video identifies the exact bike.

Small cosmetic scratches are normal on a city rental and are not a reason to refuse it. What matters is that they are recorded before you leave, so that nobody has to argue later about whether a mark is old or new. If the shop writes damage onto the contract or a diagram, check that it matches what you filmed.

## Minute 3–4: tyres and wheels

Crouch down and look at both tyres. The tread should be clearly visible across the width of the tyre; a smooth, shiny centre strip means a tyre near the end of its life, which is dangerous on wet Hanoi streets. Look for cuts, bulges on the sidewall and nails or screws in the tread. Press down hard on the seat and then on the handlebars: each tyre should give a little but not squash flat. A soft tyre can be pumped at the shop or at any roadside repair stand, but it needs to be done before you ride.

Turn the front wheel by hand if you can, or roll the scooter forward a metre: it should run smoothly without scraping or wobbling. A bent wheel rim shows as a visible wobble at the edge of the tyre.

## Minute 5–6: brakes and steering

On an automatic scooter the right-hand lever works the front brake and the left-hand lever the rear brake, often linked to the front on newer models. Squeeze each lever and push the scooter forward: the wheel should lock firmly before the lever touches the handlebar grip. A lever that comes back all the way, feels spongy or squeals loudly needs attention before you ride. Then sit on the scooter with both brakes on and rock it forwards and back; there should be no clunk from the front.

Turn the handlebars from one side to the other. They should move freely with no tight spots or grinding, and the steering lock should engage when you turn them fully left and turn the key, which matters because Nguyen Ha does not provide an extra lock and expects you to lock the steering whenever you park.

## Minute 7–8: lights, horn, mirrors and indicators

Switch on the ignition. Check the headlight on dipped and full beam, the tail light, both brake-light switches (squeeze each lever while someone looks at the back of the bike, or film it), all four indicators and the number-plate light. Press the horn. Look in both mirrors and adjust them so that you can see behind you without moving your head.

These are not just safety items. Vietnamese law requires a motorbike on the road to have a working horn, number-plate light, brake light and a left-hand mirror, and under point a of clause 1, Article 14 of Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, effective since 1 January 2025, the rider of a bike missing any of them can be fined VND 400,000–600,000. The fine falls on whoever is riding, not on the shop, so a dead bulb found now saves you money later. Riding at night needs every light working; our guide to [riding at night in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-at-night-in-hanoi/) explains why.

## Minute 9: fuel, battery and the dashboard

Start the engine. It should start within a few seconds and settle to a steady idle without dying when you open and close the throttle gently. Look at the dashboard for warning lights, and look at the fuel gauge. Nguyen Ha hands petrol bikes over with roughly 0.5 to 2 litres in the tank and expects about 0.5 to 1 litre back; returning the bike completely empty costs VND 20,000. Film the fuel gauge so that the starting level is on record, and plan to fill up at the first petrol station, because a small amount will not take you far.

On an electric scooter, check the battery percentage on the display instead and confirm with the shop how and where it can be charged, which is agreed at handover. Our guide to [renting an electric scooter in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/renting-an-electric-scooter-in-hanoi/) covers range and charging.

## Minute 10: papers, helmet and keys

Finally, check what travels with the bike. Police at a roadside stop will ask for your licence, the vehicle registration certificate and the compulsory third-party insurance certificate. Ask the shop to show you where these documents are kept and make sure they are with the bike before you leave; take a photo of them as well. Your own licence and International Driving Permit stay with you; read our [licence rules for foreigners](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbike-licence-rules-for-foreigners-in-vietnam/) if you are not sure yours is valid.

Nguyen Ha provides one basic helmet per rider free of charge. Put it on, check that it fits without rocking and fasten the strap, which the law requires. Raincoats are not included for short rentals, so buy one if the forecast looks wet. Check that the key works in the ignition and in the seat lock, open the storage compartment under the seat, and close it again. If there is a spare key, note it on the contract.

## What should stop the handover

Most inspections end with a few notes on scratches and a confident ride away. A few findings should stop you until they are fixed or you are offered another bike:

- A brake lever that reaches the grip, or brakes that do not stop the wheel firmly.
- A tyre with exposed cords, a bulge or almost no tread.
- No working headlight, brake light or horn.
- Steering that is tight, notchy or clunks over bumps.
- An engine that will not idle or a warning light that stays on.
- Missing registration or insurance papers.

Ask calmly. A reputable shop will rather fix a fault or swap the bike than have a customer ride away on something unsafe. The final price is confirmed on the actual bike before the contract is signed, so you are not obliged to accept a bike you are unhappy with.

## Keep the record until the deposit is back

Keep the video and photos until the rental is finished and your deposit has been returned in full. At Nguyen Ha the cash deposit is VND 2–5 million depending on the bike and the contract; your identity documents and licence are photographed for the contract, and the originals are not kept. When you bring the bike back, repeat the same walk-round with the staff, ideally filming again, so that the comparison is easy. Our guide to [how a rental deposit works](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbike-rental-deposit-hanoi/) explains what can and cannot be deducted, and the [rental guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-guide/) covers the rest of the handover routine.

If something goes wrong during the rental, such as a puncture or a light that fails, phone the shop before getting it repaired. Many small repairs are routine and inexpensive, but the shop needs to know, and a photo of the fault and the receipt keeps everything clear when you return.

## FAQ

### How long should a rental scooter inspection take?

About ten minutes for an automatic scooter, slightly longer for a geared bike. It is quicker with the shop staff beside you, so that you can point out anything you find and agree it on the spot.

### Should I take photos or a video of a rental scooter?

Both work, but a single continuous video with the date stamp, walking all the way round the bike and showing the plate, the fuel gauge and any damage, is the strongest record.

### What equipment must a rental scooter have by law?

A working horn, number-plate light, brake light and a left-hand mirror, among other items. Riding without them can be fined VND 400,000–600,000 under Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, and the rider pays.

### How much fuel will the scooter have at handover?

At Nguyen Ha, petrol bikes are handed over with roughly 0.5 to 2 litres and should come back with about 0.5 to 1 litre. Returning it empty costs VND 20,000.

### Can I refuse a scooter I am not happy with?

Yes. If the brakes, tyres, lights or papers are not right, ask for the fault to be fixed or for another bike before you sign. The price is confirmed on the actual bike you take.
