Check the brake lights and indicators every day before your first ride, again after rain or a long stop and once more before you return the bike. It takes about a minute: switch on the ignition, press each brake lever or pedal in turn and look at the tail light, flick each indicator left and right and watch all four lamps, try the hazard switch if the bike has one and wipe the lenses clean. A brake light that does not work means the rider behind has no warning that you are stopping, and an indicator that does not work leaves everyone guessing where you are going. Law 36/2024/QH15 requires operators to signal before changing direction and to keep the signal on during the turn (Article 15), and it bans putting vehicles that do not meet technical safety requirements on the road (Article 9).

This guide gives a daily routine, explains how to check alone, what a failing lamp looks like, how brake switches work in plain terms, what the law and the fines say about signalling, what to do if the lights fail while you ride, how to keep lenses clean and what to tell the shop. Our guide to [how to check a scooter's horn and turn signals](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-check-a-scooters-horn-and-turn-signals/) covers the handover check in detail. This guide is general information based on the law and published maintenance guidance as of October 2026, not an engineering opinion.

## Why a daily check

The bike you collect in the morning is not necessarily the bike you will have at noon. Bulbs fail, connectors loosen on rough roads, rain gets into switches and a tipped bike can crack a lens. A lamp that worked at handover can fail the next day, and the first time you find out should not be when a bus brakes behind you. Honda publishes maintenance guidance for the headlight and turn signals of its motorcycles, and the safety rule is the same for every rider: if a light is faulty, do not ride until it is fixed, especially at night.

A daily check also protects you in a dispute. A bike that you reported as faulty at 09:00 is a different matter from one that you say failed at 15:00. Our guide to [how to report damage that appears during your rental](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-report-damage-that-appears-during-your-rental/) explains how to record a fault.

## The one-minute routine

Do these in order, with the ignition on and the bike on its stand on level ground.

1. **Clean the lenses.** Wipe the tail light, the indicator lenses and the headlight with a cloth. Mud and dust can hide a lamp that is working.

2. **Rear brake light, lever.** Squeeze the right brake lever, which on most scooters operates the front brake, and look at the tail light. It should light up brighter than the running light. Release it and check that it goes back.

3. **Rear brake light, pedal or left lever.** Press the rear brake, which is the left lever on most automatic scooters and the foot pedal on most manual bikes, and check again. Each brake has its own switch, so check both.

4. **Left indicators.** Switch on the left indicator and check the front-left and rear-left lamps, and the dashboard indicator. They should flash at a steady rate.

5. **Right indicators.** Do the same for the right.

6. **Cancel.** Make sure the switch cancels each indicator and that none stays on.

7. **Hazard lights,** if the bike has a switch. All four lamps should flash together.

8. **Headlight and tail light.** Switch on the lights, check the low beam and the high beam, and the running tail light.

9. **Number plate light,** if fitted, and the plate itself, which should be clean and clearly visible.

10. **Horn.** Press it briefly. Our guide to how to check a scooter's horn and turn signals explains how to judge a weak horn.

To check the brake light alone, park with the rear of the bike facing a shop window, a glass door or a wall in the dark, and watch the reflection while you press each brake. If you cannot see it, ask a friend, the shop staff or a passer-by to look.

## What the signs of trouble look like

| What you see | What it may mean | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| One indicator lamp does not flash | A blown bulb or a loose connection | Ask the shop to repair it or swap the bike |
| The indicators flash much faster than usual | A lamp has failed, on many bikes | Check all four lamps and tell the shop |
| No flashing at all | The switch, the flasher unit or a fuse | Do not ride; ask for another bike |
| An indicator will not switch off | A switch fault | Report it before riding |
| The brake light never lights | Bulb, switch or wiring | Do not ride until fixed |
| The brake light works on one brake but not the other | A faulty switch on that brake | Report it; ask for repair |
| The brake light stays on | A switch stuck or misadjusted | Report it; it confuses riders behind and can drain the battery |
| The lights flicker or dim | A loose connection or a weak battery | Report it |
| A cracked or fogged lens | Water may enter and the lamp may fail | Report it, and photograph it |
| The dashboard warning does not match | A wiring or bulb fault | Report it |

You are not expected to repair a rental yourself, and wiring faults are a job for the shop. ## How brake lights work, in plain terms

A brake light is not driven by the brake itself but by a small switch connected to each brake. When you pull the lever or press the pedal, the switch closes and sends power to the tail light. The front lever has its own switch, and the rear lever or pedal has another. If either switch is dirty, wet, loose or misadjusted, that brake may not light the lamp even though it slows the bike. On a bike with combined brakes, one lever may operate both wheels, but the switch layout can differ, so test them all.

Rain and washing can make switches stick, and a bike that has been dropped may have a bent lever or a shifted switch. That is why the check after rain or a fall matters.

## The law on signalling

Article 15 of Law 36/2024/QH15 sets out the signalling duty for a change of direction.

- **Signal before you turn.** The operator must observe the surroundings to ensure a safe distance with the vehicle behind, slow down and send out a signal showing the direction of change, or give a hand signal for vehicles that have no direction indicators, and gradually move to the lane closest to the direction of the turn.

- **Keep the signal on.** Direction change signals or hand signals shall be used continuously during the direction change.

- **Only when safe.** The change may be made only when safety conditions are satisfied and it is not likely to cause obstruction.

Article 14 requires an overtaking vehicle to signal the direction it is changing, and Article 20 requires front lights to be on between 18:00 and 06:00 or when fog, smoke, dust, rain or bad weather limits visibility. Article 9 bans putting motor vehicles that do not meet the technical safety requirements into traffic.

Under Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, as read on the Government portal, a rider who changes lane without a signal or at a place where it is not allowed faces a fine of VND 200,000 to 400,000, a rider who turns without observing, slowing down or signalling, or who does not keep the signal on during the turn, faces a fine of VND 600,000 to 800,000, and riding without lights at night or in poor visibility faces a fine of VND 200,000 to 400,000. Check the current text of the decree before relying on a figure. ## If a light fails while you ride

1. **Do not panic,** and do not stop suddenly in the lane. The riders behind may not know you are slowing.

2. **If the brake light fails,** increase your distance from the vehicle ahead, brake earlier and more gently, and use your hand to signal slowing if it is safe to take a hand off the bar. Our guide to [how to follow a safe distance in Hanoi traffic](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-follow-a-safe-distance-in-hanoi-traffic/) explains how.

3. **If an indicator fails,** use a clear arm signal for the turn as a stopgap to the next safe stop, and check your mirrors more often. Do not take a hand off the bar in a way that reduces your control.

4. **Head for the nearest safe place** and stop lawfully, as our guide to [where to stop safely after a motorbike breaks down](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/where-to-stop-safely-after-a-motorbike-breaks-down/) explains. Article 18 requires that a vehicle stopped because of a technical fault on part of the carriageway shows an emergency light signal or a warning sign behind it.

5. **Switch on the hazard lights,** if the bike has them and they work.

6. **Check the cause if you can,** for example a loose connector, a tilted lens or an obvious bulb failure, but do not tamper with the wiring.

7. **Call the shop** on 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969), open 09:00 to 21:00 daily. Our guide to [rental bike breakdown in Hanoi: what to do](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-bike-breakdown-hanoi-what-to-do/) explains the steps.

8. **Do not ride on at night** with a failed brake light or headlight. Honda's riding guidance, as summarised in our guide to checking a scooter's horn and turn signals, says never to ride at night with faulty lights.

## Keeping lights visible

- **Clean the lenses** each day. Dirt can cut the brightness.

- **Do not cover them.** A poncho, a bag or a cover can hide the lamps. Our guide to [using a rain poncho without obscuring lights or wheels](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/using-a-rain-poncho-without-obscuring-lights-or-wheels/) explains.

- **Check after a fall,** a tip-over or a knock in a car park.

- **Avoid fitting extra lights or stickers** that change the lamps. Article 9 bans mounting or using inappropriate audio and lighting devices that affect traffic order and safety.

- **Report a cracked lens straight away.** Water can get in, and the repair is easier before it fails.

## Before you return the bike

Repeat the check the day you return the bike, and tell the shop about anything that changed. The shop's published terms say that both sides inspect brakes, tyres, lights, mirrors, bodywork, accessories and the fuel level at handover. ## A quick checklist

1. **Clean the lenses.**

2. **Press each brake and look at the tail light.**

3. **Flick left and right indicators; check all four lamps and the dashboard.**

4. **Check the hazards, the headlight and the tail light.**

5. **Check after rain, a fall or a long stop.**

6. **If a light fails, do not ride at night; call the shop.**

7. **Use early, clear signals, and keep them on during the turn.**

## FAQ: brake lights and indicators

### How often should I check the brake lights and indicators?

Before your first ride each day, after rain or a fall, and before you return the bike. It takes about a minute.

### Why are my indicators flashing fast?

On many bikes it means a lamp has failed. Check all four lamps and tell the shop.

### Is it illegal to ride with a broken brake light?

Article 9 of Law 36/2024/QH15 bans putting vehicles that do not meet technical safety requirements into traffic. Beyond the legal risk, a brake light that does not work is dangerous. Do not ride until it is fixed.

### What is the fine for not signalling?

Vietnamese legal publications report a fine of VND 200,000 to 400,000 for changing lane without a signal, and VND 600,000 to 800,000 for turning without observing, slowing down or signalling. Check the current decree.

### Can I use hand signals if my indicators fail?

As a stopgap to the next safe stop, with care. Stop riding at night and call the shop.

### Who fixes a faulty light on a rental?

Report it to the shop and ask for a repair or another bike. Do not repair the wiring yourself.
