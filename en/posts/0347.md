Hung Yen is the easiest provincial day trip south-east of Hanoi, but it needs more planning than the short distance suggests. Hung Yen city, home of the old trading port of Pho Hien, is about 55 km from central Hanoi according to VnExpress, which estimates around 1 hour 15 minutes by vehicle, and other sources measure up to 64 km to Pho Hien itself. The roads that connect Hanoi with Hung Yen include several expressways that motorbikes cannot use, the province was enlarged in 2025 by a merger with Thai Binh and the places worth visiting are spread out. This guide helps you choose a target, pick a motorbike-legal road, build a timetable around a 09:00 pickup and prepare the bike and yourself for a ride of roughly 110 to 130 km in total.

## What "Hung Yen" means after the 2025 merger

On 12 June 2025 the National Assembly passed Resolution 202/2025/QH15, which took effect the same day and combined the whole of Thai Binh province and Hung Yen province into a new province named Hung Yen. According to the legal portal ThuVienPhapLuat, the new province covers about 2,515 square kilometres and borders Bac Ninh, Ninh Binh, Hanoi, Hai Phong and the sea. Another ThuVienPhapLuat explainer says Hung Yen city was reorganised, with the area around the old port becoming a ward called Pho Hien.

For a day trip from Hanoi, this changes less than it might seem. The sights that visitors know, such as the Pho Hien relics, Chu Dong Tu's temples and the Van Giang area near Ecopark, are all on the old Hung Yen side, closest to Hanoi. The former Thai Binh area lies much further away and is not a day-trip target from the capital. What does change is naming: older guides, signs and apps may use old district and commune names, and new ones may appear. If a place does not show up in your map app, search by the landmark rather than by the old administrative name.

## Choosing a target

Hung Yen has no single headline attraction, so the trip needs a purpose. These are the realistic options for a rental motorbike.

| Target | What it is | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Pho Hien relic complex | A former international trading port | Furthest of the main options, about 55 to 64 km, depending on the source |
| Chu Dong Tu temples (Da Hoa and Da Trach) | Temples tied to a famous folk legend, in the Khoai Chau area, beside the Red River | Check the distance and the final road in a map app |
| Chrysanthemum fields at Van Lam | A seasonal flower area | VnExpress says about 30 km from Hanoi, blooming from November |
| Local food | Longan, Dong Tao chicken and eel noodle soup | Named in the culture ministry's destination page and in VnExpress |

Pho Hien is the best-known stop, and the rest of this guide is built around it, though the roads are similar for the others.

## Pho Hien in a paragraph

The old port of Pho Hien was a major trading centre in the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries. Vietnamese tourism and heritage sources describe it as a special national relic complex, recognised in 2014, made up of 16 monuments. The list includes the Xich Dang Temple of Literature, Mau Temple, Chuong Pagoda, Tran Temple, Hien Pagoda and Pho Pagoda, among others. Mau Temple, beside Ban Nguyet Lake, is often the first stop. A tourism authority page says Mau Temple worships a Song dynasty concubine, a sign of the port's Chinese trading community. The monuments are spread across several wards, so expect to ride between them, not to walk a single compact site.

## Roads that motorbikes can use

VnExpress lists the main links between Hanoi and Hung Yen as the north-south expressway, the Hanoi to Hai Phong expressway, National Highway 5 and the Hung Yen to Thai Binh expressway, plus smaller roads. Only some of these are open to you. Motorbikes and mopeds may not use expressways. This is set out in Article 25 of the Road Traffic Order and Safety Law, Law 36/2024/QH15, in force since 1 January 2025. The practical consequence is that a navigation app set to the quickest car route can send you up an expressway ramp, so check the route type and the signs before you leave. Our guides on [checking whether a navigation app suggests an expressway](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-check-if-a-navigation-app-suggests-an-expressway/) and on [which ring roads and highways allow motorbikes](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-ring-roads-highways-motorbikes-allowed/) explain how.

Older travel guides describe two ordinary-road approaches:

- **National Highway 5 and Highway 39.** A Vietnam tourism guide says to take National Highway 5 to Pho Noi, then road 39A through rice fields and longan orchards to Pho Hien. A 2022 Traveloka article gives a similar picture, crossing by Long Bien or Vinh Tuy Bridge and then joining National Highway 5.
- **The Hanoi to Hung Yen provincial road.** A route page for Hung Yen describes crossing Chuong Duong Bridge, then provincial road 195, then provincial road 379, then National Highway 39 into Hung Yen city.

I could not confirm which is faster today, and road works change from year to year, so compare both in a map app set to motorbike. National Highway 5 is a main freight route towards Hai Phong, so expect trucks. The provincial road passes several industrial zones, according to a 2024 news report on its upgrade, so expect lorries and shift-change traffic there too. Whichever you choose, stay out of the expressway lanes and follow the signs to the next town, not the line on the screen.

## How long it really takes

VnExpress's 1 hour 15 minute estimate is for a vehicle in ordinary conditions from the centre of Hanoi. A motorbike from the east bank of the Red River, in daytime traffic, with a stop for water and fuel, is a longer proposition. As a planning allowance rather than a quoted figure, allow around two hours each way, and expect more if you start in rush hour or have to cross the city first.

| Time | Plan |
|---|---|
| 09:00 | Collect the bike, inspect it and check the fuel |
| 09:30 | Depart, cross the Red River and head south-east |
| About 10:45 | Water and fuel stop, check how the bike feels |
| About 11:45 | Arrive near Pho Hien |
| 12:00 to 13:30 | Lunch, local specialities |
| 13:30 to 15:00 | One or two monuments, not all sixteen |
| 15:00 | Start the return |
| By 21:00 | Return the bike to the shop |

This is a full day. If it looks too long, a shorter ride such as Ecopark or Bat Trang may be better, and our guide to [Gia Lam, the east bank and Bat Trang](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/gia-lam-east-bank-bat-trang-by-scooter/) is a quieter way to see the east side of the river. For a comparison with another long day, read [Hanoi to Ninh Binh and back in one day](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-ninh-binh-one-day-motorbike-itinerary/).

## Visiting the monuments

Pho Hien's monuments are active places of worship and heritage sites. A few habits help:

- **Dress modestly** when you go inside a temple or pagoda, and remove your helmet before the gate.
- **Park outside, not at the gate.** Follow any directions from staff and do not block the entrance or the lane.
- **Keep your voice low** and do not photograph worshippers without asking.
- **Check opening hours on the day,** because I could not find a single authoritative timetable for all the monuments, and festival days may change them.

Do not plan to see everything. The relics are spread across several wards, and two or three, such as the Temple of Literature, Mau Temple and Chuong Pagoda, are plenty for an afternoon.

## Season, flowers and weather

The chrysanthemum fields at Van Lam are a seasonal option. VnExpress says the flowers are planted from June and bloom from November, with the fields about 30 km from Hanoi. If you are travelling in late autumn or early winter, they could make a pleasant add-on, but check the bloom before you go, as the timing varies with weather. In summer the plain can be hot and storms can arrive quickly; our guide to [riding in Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) explains the gear and the decision to wait.

In winter, daylight is shorter. Plan to start the return in the early afternoon, and read [riding at night in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-at-night-in-hanoi/) if you think you might be late. If you are, call the shop on 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969).

## FAQ: Hanoi to Hung Yen by motorbike

### How far is Hung Yen from Hanoi?

VnExpress gives 55 km from the centre to Hung Yen city, and another transport portal gives about 64 km to Pho Hien. The figure depends on the start point and the road.

### Can I use the expressways to get there faster?

No. Motorcycles and mopeds are not allowed on expressways under Law 36/2024/QH15, in force since 1 January 2025.

### Did Hung Yen really merge with Thai Binh?

Yes. Resolution 202/2025/QH15, effective 12 June 2025, combined the two provinces into a new province called Hung Yen.

### What is the best stop for a first visit?

Pho Hien, the old trading port, is the best known, with a complex of 16 monuments. Choose two or three for the day.

### Is it too far for one day?

It is a full day for a confident rider. Start at the shop's opening time, keep to two or three stops and turn back by mid-afternoon.
