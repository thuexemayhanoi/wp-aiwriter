The safest overtaking decision on a scooter is often not to overtake. When you do, Vietnamese law lets you overtake only on the left, only when there is no obstacle ahead and no oncoming traffic in the stretch you will use, only when the vehicle in front is not itself overtaking and has signalled and moved right, and never at junctions, on pedestrian crossings, on narrow one-lane bridges, on bends or crests where you cannot see, or in tunnels. Article 14 of Law 36/2024/QH15 sets these rules, and it also tells the driver in front to slow down, signal right and move close to the right when it is safe to let you pass. The decision therefore has two parts: whether the law allows it, and whether it is truly safe.

This guide explains the rules, a step-by-step way to decide, how to signal, what to do if you are being overtaken, special cases such as buses, trucks, parked cars and crowded streets, and the fines for getting it wrong. It is general information based on the law as of October 2026, not legal advice.

## What counts as overtaking

Article 14 defines overtaking narrowly. It is a traffic situation on a road with only one motor-vehicle lane for each direction, where the vehicle behind moves to the left to get ahead of the vehicle in front. On a road with two or more motor-vehicle lanes in the same direction, separated by lane markings, getting ahead of another vehicle is governed by the lane rules in Article 13: you stay in a lane, change only where it is allowed, move one lane at a time, signal in advance and check the vehicles in front, behind and on both sides. Our guide to [checking mirrors and blind spots before changing lanes](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/checking-mirrors-and-blind-spots-before-changing-lanes/) explains the lane-change routine.

## The rules in Article 14

Law 36/2024/QH15 sets out the following.

1. **Overtake on the left.** The exception is where the vehicle in front is signalling a left turn or is turning left, or where a special-purpose vehicle is working on the road and a left pass is impossible. Then you may pass on the right.

2. **Overtake only if the way is clear.** There must be no obstacle ahead and no oncoming traffic in the stretch where you intend to overtake, and the vehicle in front must not be overtaking another one and must have signalled right and moved to the right.

3. **Signal your intention.** Flash the front lights or use the horn, except for vehicles with neither. Also signal the direction you will move, and keep a safe distance from the vehicles in front and behind throughout. In urban and populous areas between 22:00 and 05:00, you may signal with lights only.

4. **Do not overtake in these cases:** where the conditions above are not met, on a narrow bridge with one lane, on bends with limited visibility, on two-way roads at crests with limited visibility, at road intersections and level crossings, when weather or road conditions are unsafe, when you meet a priority vehicle, on pedestrian crossings, when pedestrians or wheelchair users are crossing and in road tunnels.

Article 21 allows horns to warn of circumstances affecting safety and to announce an intention to overtake, and bans continuous use. A short tap is enough.

## A decision routine: should I overtake?

Use this routine before every pass.

1. **Why?** Is the vehicle in front slow enough to justify the risk? A few seconds saved are rarely worth a collision.

2. **Is it legal here?** Look for the places on the list above: junction, crossing, bend, crest, narrow bridge, tunnel, pedestrians. If any applies, stay behind.

3. **Is the road clear ahead?** Look well beyond the vehicle you want to pass. Can you see far enough to finish the pass before anything comes? Look for oncoming traffic, vehicles pulling out and people crossing.

4. **What is the vehicle in front doing?** Is it signalling, slowing, drifting, about to turn? Is it itself overtaking?

5. **Do I have room to get back?** You need space ahead of the vehicle you pass, not only space beside it.

6. **Do I have the speed and the power?** A 50cc bike or a heavy load may not accelerate quickly. Our guide to [when a 50cc bike is too slow for a planned route](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/when-a-50cc-bike-is-too-slow-for-a-planned-route/) explains the limits.

7. **Is anyone behind me about to overtake me?** Check the mirror and over your shoulder.

8. **If in doubt, don't.**

If you cannot answer yes to the first six, stay where you are.

## How to overtake safely

When the decision is yes:

1. **Drop back slightly** to see past the vehicle and to build speed for the pass.

2. **Check the mirror and over the shoulder** on the side you will use.

3. **Signal with the indicator and flash the headlight, or tap the horn,** as Article 14 requires. At night in town, use lights only.

4. **Move out smoothly** and accelerate steadily.

5. **Pass quickly,** keeping a margin beside the vehicle. Do not linger alongside.

6. **Return to your lane only when you can see the vehicle in your mirror,** with a safe gap ahead of it.

7. **Cancel the signal.**

Do not weave, do not accelerate hard just before the pass and do not cut back in sharply. Our guide to [signalling turns while keeping control of a scooter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/signalling-turns-while-keeping-control-of-a-scooter/) explains the signal habits.

## When someone wants to overtake you

Article 14 puts a duty on the vehicle in front too. When you see a vehicle behind that intends to overtake, you must observe the road ahead, and if it is safe, slow down, signal right to tell the other operator they can overtake and move close to the right side of the carriageway until they have passed completely. You may not obstruct the overtaking vehicle. If you see an obstacle or the road is unsafe, you must signal left to warn the vehicle behind that overtaking is not yet permitted.

In practice:

- **Keep to the right** and hold your line.

- **Do not speed up** when someone tries to pass.

- **Use the right signal to invite, and the left signal to warn.** Do not rely on this alone, because the other rider has to judge for themselves.

- **Do not assume a signal from the vehicle in front means it is safe.** Check the road yourself.

## Special cases

### Large vehicles: buses and trucks

Large vehicles have blind spots, long stopping distances and wide turns. Pass only where it is clearly safe, do it promptly and do not cut in close in front. The US Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration advises making sure you can see the driver in the mirror before passing and not lingering in the blind spot. Our guides to [how to ride beside heavy trucks in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-ride-beside-heavy-trucks-in-hanoi/) and [sharing the road with buses while riding a scooter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/sharing-the-road-with-buses-while-riding-a-scooter/) go into detail.

### Parked cars and obstructions

Moving round a parked car is not overtaking in the legal sense, but the same care applies. The vehicle that faces the obstruction gives way to the oncoming vehicle that does not. Our guide to [passing parked cars without entering oncoming traffic](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/passing-parked-cars-without-entering-oncoming-traffic/) covers the technique, and our guide to [opening car doors as a hazard for scooter riders](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/opening-car-doors-a-hazard-for-scooter-riders/) explains why to leave room.

### Crowded streets and filtering

In dense Hanoi traffic, scooters move through gaps. That is not the same as a planned pass, and it is easy to drift into the right-hand side of other vehicles, which the law does not allow except in the cases above. Keep to your lane, move one lane at a time, and do not weave between vehicles at speed.

### Roads with a single lane each way

This is the road type that Article 14 describes. The danger is oncoming traffic, because you will use the opposite lane. Only pass where you can see clear road for the whole manoeuvre, and abandon the pass if anything appears.

### Wet roads and poor visibility

In rain, stopping distances are longer, spray cuts your view and surfaces are slippery. Article 14 forbids overtaking when weather or road conditions are not safe for it. Our guides to [riding in Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) and [safe scooter riding when visibility drops in rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/safe-scooter-riding-when-visibility-drops-in-rain/) explain why to wait.

### At night

You see less, and you judge distance and speed worse. Do not overtake unless you can see the road ahead clearly and you know the stretch. ## Fines and consequences

Overtaking offences are priced separately, and they rise sharply when they cause an accident. Under Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, as read in Vietnamese legal publications citing Article 7, for riders of motorcycles and mopeds:

- **No signal before overtaking, or not keeping the signal on throughout:** a fine of VND 200,000 to 400,000.

- **Overtaking on the right where it is not allowed:** a fine of VND 600,000 to 800,000.

- **Overtaking in cases where it is not allowed, or where a sign prohibits overtaking for your vehicle type:** a fine of VND 800,000 to 1,000,000.

- **Overtaking in breach of the rules and causing an accident:** a fine of VND 10,000,000 to 14,000,000, with 10 licence points deducted.

Check the current text of the decree and its amendments, including Decree 238/2026/ND-CP, if a figure matters to you. A no-overtaking sign may apply to some vehicle types and not others. ## Common mistakes

1. **Overtaking at a junction or crossing.**

2. **Overtaking on the right.**

3. **Not signalling, or not keeping the signal on.**

4. **Starting a pass without seeing far enough ahead.**

5. **Cutting in too soon.**

6. **Overtaking a vehicle that is itself overtaking, or is about to turn.**

7. **Overtaking in rain or at night when you cannot see.**

8. **Speeding up when someone tries to pass you.**

## A quick checklist

1. **Ask whether you need to overtake at all.**

2. **Check that the place is not on the prohibited list.**

3. **Check the road ahead, the vehicle in front and your mirror.**

4. **Signal, and flash lights or tap the horn.**

5. **Pass on the left, promptly, with room.**

6. **Return only when you can see the vehicle in your mirror.**

7. **If in doubt, stay behind.**

## FAQ: overtaking on a scooter in Vietnam

### On which side must I overtake?

On the left, except where the vehicle in front is signalling or turning left, or a special-purpose vehicle is working on the road (Article 14).

### Where is overtaking banned?

On narrow single-lane bridges, on bends and crests with limited visibility, at intersections and level crossings, on pedestrian crossings, when pedestrians are crossing, in tunnels, when conditions are unsafe and when a priority vehicle approaches.

### Do I need to signal?

Yes. Flash your front lights or use the horn, signal the direction you will move and keep the signal on throughout. In towns between 22:00 and 05:00, signal with lights only.

### What should I do if someone is overtaking me?

Slow down if safe, signal right and move close to the right. If it is unsafe, signal left to warn them not to pass.

### What is the fine for overtaking on the right?

VND 600,000 to 800,000 for riders of motorcycles and mopeds under Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, as reported. Check the current text.

### Is it better not to overtake?

Often, yes. If you cannot clearly see that the whole manoeuvre is safe and legal, stay behind.
