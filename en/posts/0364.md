You cannot ride a motorbike all the way to the Perfume Pagoda, and that is the whole shape of the trip. A rented scooter takes you from Hanoi to the wharf at Ben Duc in Huong Son, about 60 km south-west of the city, according to travel guides, and from there the journey changes mode: you park, board a shared wooden rowing boat up the Yen Stream, then either climb or take a cable car to Huong Tich Cave. Published guides give 1.5 to 2 hours for the road leg, 30 to 60 minutes for each boat leg and several hours at the site, which makes this a full day with a firm turnaround time. This guide explains each transfer in the order you will meet it, which parts are still unconfirmed, and when a scooter is a good idea and when a bus or tour is smarter.

## Why the transfers matter more than the pagoda

The Perfume Pagoda (Chua Huong) is not one building. It is a spread-out complex of shrines and cave temples in the Huong Tich mountains, and the route between them is part of the experience. A pilgrimage-style visit usually runs: road to the wharf, boat to the Thien Tru landing, Thien Tru Pagoda, then the ascent to Huong Tich Cave, which a travel guide calls the spiritual heart of the complex.

That means your motorbike is only one link in a chain of four. Plan the day backwards from the last boat and the last safe riding hour, not forwards from the pagoda. A common mistake is to treat it as a scenic ride with a stop at the end; in practice it is a stop with a ride at each end.

## Leg one: the road to Ben Duc wharf

Two travel guides describe the same general approach for motorbike or car: from central Hanoi through Thanh Xuan, Ha Dong, Ba La and Van Dinh, then on towards the wharf. One of them quotes about 1 hour 50 minutes for a motorbike, and the airline guide gives 1.5 to 2 hours for either vehicle. These are planning figures, and a scooter in morning traffic through Ha Dong can easily take longer, so use a map app at departure and check it against signs.

Some points for the ride itself:

- **Start from the right place.** From Nguyen Ha's shop in Long Bien you must first cross the Red River to the west bank, which adds time. Allow for it.
- **Avoid fast roads.** Car routes to this area may use expressway-grade roads that motorbikes cannot enter. Our [navigation-app check](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-check-if-a-navigation-app-suggests-an-expressway/) and the [ring roads guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-ring-roads-highways-motorbikes-allowed/) explain how to keep to permitted roads.
- **Fill up before the countryside.** Our [fuel guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/fuel-in-hanoi-petrol-stations-rental-scooter/) explains how stations work; a half-day of normal riding does not need a large reserve, but do not start near empty.
- **Expect village roads near the end.** Traffic includes buses, trucks and pedestrians heading for the wharf, especially at weekends.

As of October 2026, the Road Traffic Order and Safety Law (in force since 1 January 2025) keeps ordinary motorbikes off expressways, requires helmets for rider and passenger, and limits passengers; our general guides on [helmet rules](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/helmet-rules-in-vietnam/) and [documents to carry](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/documents-to-carry-riding-in-vietnam/) cover the practical side.

## Leg two: parking at the wharf

One travel information site states that even drivers with their own car must park at Ben Duc and take the boat, because there is no road access to the temples. That matches tour descriptions of leaving the vehicle in a car park and, in some itineraries, using a short electric shuttle, described as about 3 km, to reach the stream side.

What nobody can promise from afar is the exact parking arrangement for a rented scooter: which lot, what it costs and how far the walk is. Choose a guarded lot, take any ticket you are given and keep it with the key. Our guide to [parking a motorbike in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-motorbike-in-hanoi/) explains the ticket routine, and [how to pay for fuel, parking and tolls](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/paying-fuel-parking-tolls-hanoi-visitor/) covers the cash habit. Lock the steering, as the shop does not provide an extra lock. Do not leave valuables in the seat.

## Leg three: the Yen Stream boat

The boat ride is the part most visitors remember. Travel guides describe wooden boats rowed by local women, sometimes using their feet, drifting between rice fields and limestone cliffs. The numbers differ by source:

| Item | What guides report |
|---|---|
| Boat length each way | about 30 minutes, about 45 minutes, or about an hour |
| Stream distance | about 3 to 4 km one way |
| Capacity | shared boats seating around 8 to 12 |
| Basics | wooden benches and a canopy; one guide mentions no life jackets |

Treat those as a range, not a promise. The boat fare is described as included in the entrance ticket by one source and as a separate ticket by another, so confirm at the ticket counter. The same goes for the amounts. This guide does not quote ticket prices because they differ between sources and seasons.

Safety is the traveller's responsibility: ask at the wharf what life jackets are available, sit low and keep bags on your lap. Carry cash in small notes, because one guide says vendors do not accept cards.

## Leg four: Thien Tru, then climb or cable car

The boat lands at the Thien Tru landing, next to Thien Tru Pagoda. From there, sources describe two ways to Huong Tich Cave:

1. **Walk.** Guides give times from about 45 minutes to about an hour and a half, and one counts more than 1,200 stone steps with a steep section near the cave. Sturdy shoes matter.
2. **Cable car.** A ride of roughly 10 to 15 minutes is described, which is the easier choice for children or older relatives. Many visitors ride up and walk down, or the reverse.

A smaller quieter stop, Tuyet Son Pagoda, is described as about a 15-minute walk from Thien Tru. Opening hours at the site are given as roughly 06:00 to 18:00 by one guide, so confirm them and aim to leave the cave well before the last boats.

## Festival season versus a quiet day

The Perfume Pagoda Festival runs from the sixth day of the first lunar month through the third lunar month, which is roughly February to April in most years. One travel information site claims 30,000 to 50,000 visitors a day on peak weekends and boat queues of an hour or more by mid-morning. Whether those figures are exact or not, the pattern is clear: festival weekends are crowded and slow.

Outside the festival the picture changes. Two guides say the boats and cable car still run and the site is much quieter, and October to December is described as cooler and drier. That is the window you are in now. A weekday visit in the autumn or early winter is the easiest way to see the stream without a queue, though you should check on the day that the boat and cable car are operating.

On a festival weekend, consider leaving the riding to someone else: a bus or organised tour removes the parking and traffic problem entirely.

## A day plan with a turnaround time

Use this as a template and shorten it if the weather is poor:

- **Leave Hanoi early.** Guides suggest around 07:00. With 1.5 to 2 hours on the road you reach the wharf mid-morning.
- **Board the boat and visit Thien Tru.** Allow time for a meal in the site's food stalls, bringing cash.
- **Ascend to Huong Tich Cave by cable car or on foot.** Walking up and riding down is common.
- **Fix a turnaround.** Decide before you start that you will be back on the scooter by mid-afternoon, giving daylight for the ride home. In the cooler months dusk can arrive before 18:00, and our [night riding guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-at-night-in-hanoi/) explains why to avoid it.
- **Take breaks.** The ride back is the tiring end of the day; see [how to plan breaks on a long ride](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-plan-breaks-on-a-long-semi-automatic-bike-ride/).

If the weather turns, the stone steps and the boat both become less pleasant; read [riding in Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) before you decide whether to go. A basic raincoat is not included in short rentals at Nguyen Ha, so bring a poncho between spring and autumn.

## What to wear

The Perfume Pagoda is an active religious site. Travel guides ask for covered shoulders and knees inside the temples and caves, comfortable walking shoes and sun protection. Light cotton clothes work well on the boat. Carry water; stalls near Thien Tru sell food but sources say it is basic.

## FAQ

### Can I ride my rental scooter to the pagoda itself?

No. Sources describe the boat as the main route to the temples, with the road ending at the wharf area. You park the scooter and continue by boat.

### How long does a day trip take from Hanoi?

Guides give 1.5 to 2 hours each way by road, 30 to 60 minutes each way by boat and about three to four hours at the site. Together that is a full day, so start early.

### Is it better to go during the festival?

Only if you want the festival atmosphere and accept crowds. For an easier ride and shorter queues, a weekday outside February to April is calmer.

### Do I need to take the cable car?

No. You can climb, and many do, but it is a long staircase. The cable car is the easier option for children and older travellers.
