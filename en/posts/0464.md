Before you change lanes on a scooter, run the same four steps every time: check your mirror, signal, look over your shoulder on the side you will move to and move only if the space is clear, one lane at a time. Mirrors never show the whole picture, so the quick shoulder look is what covers the blind spot. Law 36/2024/QH15 sets the legal frame in Article 13: on a road with several lanes in the same direction you keep within one lane, change only where it is allowed, move only to the adjacent lane, signal your intention in advance and observe the surroundings to ensure safe distances with the vehicles in front, behind and on both sides before the change.

This guide explains where a scooter's blind spots are, how to set the mirrors, how to do the shoulder check without losing control, what the law says, how large vehicles and passing scooters change the picture, and what to check on a rental bike before you ride. It is general information based on the law as of October 2026, not legal advice.

## What the law requires

Two articles of Law 36/2024/QH15 apply directly.

**Article 13, use of lanes.** Vehicles moving at a lower speed must keep to the right in their direction. On a road with many lanes in the same direction, distinguished by lane markings, operators must keep within one lane and change lanes only where it is allowed. They may only change from a lane to the adjacent one at a time, they must send out a signal indicating their intention in advance, and they must observe the surroundings to ensure safe distances with the vehicles in front, behind and on both sides before changing lane.

**Article 15, change of direction.** Before changing direction, the operator must observe the surroundings to ensure a safe distance with the vehicle behind, slow down and signal, and may change direction only when safety conditions are met and the change is not likely to obstruct people or other vehicles.

Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, as read on the Government portal, sets a fine of VND 200,000 to 400,000 for changing lanes without a signal or where it is not allowed. ## Why you need both mirrors and a shoulder look

A mirror shows you the space behind and to the side, but only part of it.

- **Mirrors cover a narrow cone.** Anything beside and slightly behind your shoulder falls outside it. That area is the blind spot.

- **Scooter mirrors are small, and they vibrate.** The image can blur and shake, especially on rough roads.

- **Many mirrors are slightly curved,** which widens the view but can make vehicles look farther away than they are. Judge speed and distance with care.

- **Scooters are fast in gaps.** In dense Hanoi traffic, other scooters pass on both sides, sometimes quickly, and can arrive in your blind spot between one look and the next.

So the mirror tells you what is coming from behind. The shoulder look tells you what is already beside you. You need both.

## Step 1: set the mirrors properly

Mirrors are only useful if they are set for you.

1. **Adjust them from the riding position,** sitting on the bike on its wheels, not on the stand, with your hands on the grips and your helmet on.

2. **Set each mirror to show the lane behind you** and a small part of your own arm or the side of the bike, so that you can place what you see.

3. **Check that both are secure.** A loose mirror shifts when you ride.

4. **Wipe them clean.** Rain and dirt blur the image.

5. **Re-check after any knock,** a stop in a crowded area or a change of rider.

## Step 2: the sequence before a lane change

Use the same order each time, so that it becomes a habit.

1. **Look ahead first.** Check the space you are moving into, and the vehicle in front of you, because Article 13 requires a safe distance in front as well.

2. **Check the mirror on the side you will move to,** and the one on the other side. Look for approaching vehicles, especially scooters.

3. **Signal.** Use the indicator on the side you are moving to, early enough for others to see it.

4. **Look over your shoulder on that side,** quickly, to check the blind spot.

5. **Move gradually,** one lane at a time, if the space is clear.

6. **Cancel the signal** once you are in the new lane. Many scooters do not cancel it automatically.

If the shoulder look shows a vehicle, stay where you are, keep the signal on and wait for the space.

## Doing the shoulder check without losing control

A shoulder check on a scooter is a quick glance, not a long turn of the head.

- **Keep your hands steady** on the grips and your eyes returning to the road ahead. Do not turn the handlebar as you turn your head.

- **Turn your head and let your eyes lead,** so that you see the side and slightly behind, then look forward again.

- **Make it short.** A glance of about a second is enough. A long look away from the road is a hazard.

- **Do it when the road ahead is clear.** If the vehicle in front is slowing, check it first.

- **Slow down slightly** if traffic is dense, so that you have time to look.

Practise on a quiet street until it feels natural, and then use it in traffic.

## One lane at a time

Article 13 says you may only change from a lane to the adjacent one at a time. A move across two or three lanes in one sweep is both illegal and dangerous, because the vehicles in the lanes you cross cannot predict you. If you need to cross several lanes, for example to turn left, do it in steps, with a signal, a mirror check and a shoulder look for each one. ## Lane markings and where a change is allowed

You may change lanes only where it is allowed. A dashed white line allows a change across it when it is safe, and a solid white line does not. A solid yellow line separates opposing traffic and is not to be crossed. A lane that is reserved for a certain type of vehicle, such as a bus lane, is closed to other vehicles. Look at the road before you check the mirror, so that you do not move toward a line you may not cross. ## Large vehicles and their blind spots

Buses and trucks have much larger blind spots than a scooter.

- **If you cannot see the driver's mirror, the driver cannot see you.** Do not ride beside a large vehicle, especially on the side near the kerb.

- **Do not move into the space between a large vehicle and the kerb.**

- **Do not cut in just ahead** of a large vehicle. It needs more room to stop.

- **Do not change lanes beside a turning vehicle.** A long vehicle swings out and then in.

## Scooters passing on both sides

In dense traffic, other riders pass on both sides, and your lane change can put you in their way. So:

- **Look for scooters before you look for cars.** They are faster in gaps, and they appear suddenly.

- **Check both sides,** not only the one you are moving to, because someone may be filtering on the other side and may swerve.

- **Do not assume a rider has seen your signal.** Wait for them to pass.

- **Keep your move smooth and gradual,** so that others can adjust.

## Wet, dark and crowded conditions

- **In rain,** mirrors blur, and visors fog. Slow down, wipe the mirrors when you stop and increase your gap. Our guide to [safe scooter riding when visibility drops in rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/safe-scooter-riding-when-visibility-drops-in-rain/) explains how.

- **At night,** the lights of vehicles behind you can dazzle you in the mirror. Look at the road beside you rather than the lights, and use your shoulder look.

- **In heavy traffic,** do not change lanes for a small gain. The saving is a few seconds, and the risk is a collision.

## Common mistakes

1. **Relying on the mirror alone.**

2. **Signalling and moving at the same moment.**

3. **Moving across several lanes in one go.**

4. **Not checking the side you are not moving to.**

5. **Looking too long over the shoulder.**

6. **Mirrors set for someone else.**

7. **Leaving the signal on afterwards.**

8. **Changing lanes where the line does not allow it.**

## A quick checklist

1. **Set the mirrors from the riding position.**

2. **Look ahead, then the mirrors.**

3. **Signal early.**

4. **Quick shoulder look on the side you will move to.**

5. **One lane at a time, smoothly.**

6. **Cancel the signal.**

7. **Stay clear of large vehicles' blind spots.**

## FAQ: mirrors, blind spots and lane changes

### What does the law say about lane changes?

Article 13 of Law 36/2024/QH15 requires you to keep within one lane, change lanes only where allowed, move one lane at a time, signal in advance and check safe distances in front, behind and on both sides.

### Why do I need to look over my shoulder?

Mirrors do not show the blind spot beside and slightly behind you. The shoulder look covers it.

### How should I set my mirrors?

From the riding position, to show the lane behind you and a small part of your own arm or the bike's side.

### Can I change two lanes at once?

No. The law allows only the adjacent lane at a time.

### What is the fine for changing lanes without a signal?

VND 200,000 to 400,000 under Decree 168/2024/ND-CP. Check the current text.

### Where is the blind spot of a bus or truck?

Close to its sides, directly in front and directly behind. If you cannot see the driver's mirror, the driver may not see you.
