To estimate travel time conservatively on a 50cc moped, do not take the time your map app shows for a car or a bigger scooter. Instead, divide the distance by a deliberately low average speed, add a fixed allowance for each stop, then add a buffer of about a quarter on top. In central Hanoi a planning speed of roughly 15 to 18 km/h is realistic, on open roads outside the city around 25 to 30 km/h is a cautious figure, and a moped may never exceed 40 km/h on roads other than expressways. This guide shows the arithmetic with worked examples, explains where the numbers come from, and shows how to check that you will be back before the shop closes at 21:00.

## Why map apps give the wrong answer for a moped

A navigation app calculates from the road's speed and live traffic for a typical vehicle, usually a car. A 50cc moped differs in three ways. Its legal maximum is 40 km/h, set by Circular 38/2024/TT-BGTVT for mopeds on roads other than expressways, so it cannot take advantage of a clear road the way a faster bike can. Its engine is small, so it accelerates slowly from every junction and slows on climbs. And it is the slowest vehicle in the flow, so it spends time being passed and giving way.

None of this makes a moped a poor choice for short city trips. It means that the app's estimate is a floor, not a forecast, and a trip planned to the minute will run late. ## Start from real data: how slow Hanoi traffic is

The TomTom Traffic Index, which draws on anonymous driver data, gives a picture of the whole city. For 2025 it reports an average of 33 minutes 20 seconds to drive 10 km in Hanoi, which is an average speed of about 18 km/h over the year. In the morning rush hour the 10 km took about 39 minutes, and in the evening rush about 44 minutes, at 15.3 and 13.6 km/h. The whole-day figure for distance covered in 15 minutes was 4.5 km.

Those figures describe all traffic, not mopeds specifically, and they cover the larger metropolitan area, but they set a useful baseline for city planning: even without a speed cap, the average vehicle in Hanoi moves at roughly 14 to 18 km/h. A moped in the same flow is unlikely to do better, so 15 to 18 km/h is a fair planning figure in the city, and 12 to 14 km/h is safer for the evening rush.

## Choose a planning speed by road type

The figures below are editorial planning assumptions, not measured or official speeds. They are deliberately cautious, so that you finish early instead of late.

| Type of route | Planning speed | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Central streets and the Old Quarter | 12 to 15 km/h | Junctions, pedestrians, parking and narrow lanes |
| City streets outside the rush hour | 15 to 18 km/h | In line with the all-day TomTom average |
| Rush hours (morning and evening) | 12 to 14 km/h | TomTom reports about 14 km/h in 2025 |
| Open roads outside the city | 25 to 30 km/h | Below the 40 km/h cap, allowing for hills, wind and lorries |
| Anything with long climbs | 20 to 25 km/h | Small engines slow on gradients |

A moped cannot legally exceed 40 km/h, so treat 40 as an impossible average, since you would need to hold the cap constantly with no stops. If you are in doubt, choose the lower end of the range. Hills, headwinds and lorries all pull the open-road figure down further, so lean to the slow end of each range.

## The method in four steps

1. **Measure the distance.** Use a map app to find the distance of your route, not the time. If you plan several stops, add them up.
2. **Divide by a planning speed.** Time in hours equals distance divided by speed. Multiply by 60 to get minutes.
3. **Add stop allowances.** Allow 10 minutes to park and walk at each destination, and 5 minutes for each extra stop such as a traffic jam, a red light queue or a navigation check. For rides over an hour, add a rest break.
4. **Add a buffer of 25 per cent.** Multiply the total by 1.25. If the trip has a deadline, such as a train, a booking or the 21:00 return, add a further 15 to 20 minutes of slack.

The buffer is a rule of thumb, not a standard. Its purpose is to make a late arrival rare without making the plan absurd.

## Worked examples

These distances are illustrative round numbers, not specific routes.

**Example 1: a 6 km trip across the city in the middle of the day.** At 18 km/h, 6 km takes 20 minutes. At 14.3 km/h, the rush-hour average, it takes about 25 minutes. Add 10 minutes to park and walk, giving 30 to 35 minutes, and a quarter on top gives about 38 to 44 minutes. A planner who read "15 minutes" from a map app would be well out.

**Example 2: a 15 km city and east-bank loop.** At 18 km/h that is about 50 minutes of riding, and at 14.3 km/h about 63 minutes. Add 15 minutes for two stops, giving 65 to 78 minutes, and with the buffer about 81 to 98 minutes. Call it an hour and a half.

**Example 3: a 40 km ride out of the city and back later.** On open roads at 28 km/h, 40 km takes about 86 minutes. At 25 km/h it takes 96 minutes. With a 15-minute rest and a 10-minute stop, that is between 111 and 121 minutes, and with the buffer roughly 2 hours 20 minutes to 2 hours 30 minutes each way, before any time inside the city to reach the edge of town.

**Example 4: a 90 km one-way trip, for illustration only.** At 28 km/h this is over 3 hours of pure riding, at 25 km/h more than 3.5 hours, and the planning speed assumes no serious climbing. With half an hour of breaks and the buffer, the one-way time approaches 5 hours, so a return trip fills the day. This is why a 50cc bike is rarely sensible for a long destination; our [Hanoi to Ninh Binh guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-to-ninh-binh-by-motorbike/) describes that route for comparison.

## Build the return into the plan

The rental day is 24 hours, and the shop is open from 09:00 to 21:00. The practical way to plan is to work backwards from the time you intend to return, not forwards from the time you set out.

1. Pick the time you must be back, for example 20:00 for a 21:00 close.
2. Subtract the estimated time of the return leg, with the buffer.
3. Subtract the time you want at the destination.
4. The result is the latest time to leave, and your outbound leg must fit before that.

Late returns are charged. The shop charges VND 30,000 per hour for the first one to five hours late, and from six hours late it counts as one extra rental day. A conservative estimate protects you against that. ## Daylight, rain and fuel

Time is not the only limit. Plan to be off unfamiliar roads before dark, because a small bike and unlit road sections are a poor mix. Check the sunset time for your date and subtract an hour for safety. Our [night riding guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-at-night-in-hanoi/) explains the lighting rules and visibility.

Rain slows everyone. If a shower is forecast, add a further 20 to 30 per cent to the estimate and consider cancelling the longer part of the route. Our [rain riding guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) lists the streets that flood.

Fuel stops add time too. Petrol bikes at Nguyen Ha are handed over with about 0.5 to 2 litres and returned with about 0.5 to 1 litre, so plan one stop for a longer ride. Our [guide to fuel in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/fuel-in-hanoi-petrol-stations-rental-scooter/) covers stations and paying.

## Update your estimate as you ride

An estimate is only a starting point. After the first 15 to 20 minutes, compare your real distance with your plan. If you have covered less than expected, extend the estimate straight away and drop a stop rather than rush. Rushing on a light bike with small wheels is the way mistakes happen. If you are tired, stop; our [guide to planning breaks](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-plan-breaks-on-a-long-semi-automatic-bike-ride/) shows how often to rest.

## A one-minute checklist

- Get the distance from a map, not the time.
- Choose a planning speed from the table, leaning slow.
- Add 10 minutes per destination and 5 minutes per extra stop.
- Multiply by 1.25 for a buffer.
- Work backwards from the return time and add slack.
- Check daylight, rain and fuel.
- Re-check the estimate after the first 20 minutes.

## FAQ: estimating moped travel time

### What average speed should I assume for a 50cc moped in Hanoi?

For central streets, 12 to 18 km/h depending on the hour. On open roads, 25 to 30 km/h at most. These are cautious planning figures, not official speeds.

### Can a 50cc moped actually reach 40 km/h?

It may, on a clear flat road, but 40 km/h is also the legal cap for mopeds, so it cannot be an average.

### Why add 25 per cent?

Because stops, junctions and small delays pile up. The figure is a rule of thumb for this guide, not a standard.

### Should I trust the time shown by a map app?

Use it for distance and for spotting bad traffic, but not as your estimate.

### What happens if I return late?

The shop charges VND 30,000 per hour for the first one to five hours, and from six hours one extra day.

### Is a longer day trip realistic on a 50cc bike?

Rarely. Slow speeds make long routes tiring and push the return into the evening.
