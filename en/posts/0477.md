In Vietnam a motorbike or scooter may not be ridden along a footpath, and it may not enter a street or section reserved for pedestrians. Law 36/2024/QH15 says in Article 33 that drivers of motorcycles and mopeds may not drive on road sections reserved for pedestrians and other vehicles, and Article 77 states that roadways are for traffic and pavements are for pedestrians. Vietnamese legal publications report a fine of VND 4,000,000 to 6,000,000 under Decree 168/2024/ND-CP for riding on a pavement, with 2 licence points deducted. The one widely reported exception is crossing a pavement to enter a house or an office.

This guide explains where the rules come from, what counts as a footpath or a pedestrian street in Hanoi, the difference between riding, walking the bike and parking, how to get in and out of a driveway or a parking place lawfully, how weekend walking streets work, what to do when traffic is jammed and what to tell the shop. It is general information based on the law and published guidance as of October 2026, not legal advice.

## What the law says

**Pavements are for pedestrians (Article 77).** Article 77 begins from the principle that roadways are used for traffic and pavements are used for pedestrians. Using a pavement for another purpose needs approved plans, so a rider cannot simply decide that a pavement is a road.

**Motorcycles and mopeds may not drive on pedestrian sections (Article 33).** Among the acts banned for drivers of two-wheeled motorcycles, three-wheeled motorcycles and mopeds is driving on road sections reserved for pedestrians and other vehicles.

**Pedestrians have their own space (Article 30).** The Law tells pedestrians to walk on sidewalks, roadsides or paths reserved for pedestrians, and it asks all road users to help children under 7, pregnant women, old and weak people and people with disabilities cross the road.

**Stopping and parking (Article 18).** Operators may stop and park close to the right roadside or sidewalk, with the nearest wheel no more than 0.25 metre from it, and without obstructing people or vehicles. They may not stop or park on the roadway or sidewalks in contravention of law, on pedestrian crossings, or within 5 metres of the edge of an intersection.

## The fine

Vietnamese legal publications, the Government's guidance and news coverage agree on the main points under Decree 168/2024/ND-CP.

| Act | Fine reported | Licence points |
|---|---|---|
| Riding a motorbike along a pavement, unless crossing it to enter a house or office | A fine of VND 4,000,000 to 6,000,000 | 2 deducted |
| Parking or leaving a motorbike on a pavement unlawfully | A fine of VND 400,000 to 600,000 | None |
| Riding on a pavement and causing an accident | A fine of VND 10,000,000 to 14,000,000 | 10 deducted |

These figures come from secondary summaries of the decree, including a provincial government portal, so read the current text of Article 7 before relying on them. Riding on a pavement is also a camera offence in many cities, and a notice can reach the registered owner of a rental. ## What counts as a footpath or pedestrian street

The easiest mistakes come from places that look like roads.

- **Raised pavements beside a street,** with a kerb. These are footpaths even if they are wide and empty.

- **Tiled or paved areas around lakes and plazas,** which are meant for walking.

- **Streets reserved for pedestrians by a sign,** including the weekend walking streets, which are closed to vehicles during set hours.

- **Footbridges, underpasses and lakeside paths.**

- **Shopfront areas used by vendors and cafes,** where the pavement is full of people and stalls.

- **Alleys that are too narrow for two-wheelers to pass people safely.** A narrow alley may be lawful to ride in but not pleasant, and our guide to [riding an automatic scooter through a narrow market lane](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-an-automatic-scooter-through-a-narrow-market-lane/) explains how.

## Riding, walking the bike and parking are different

| What you are doing | Is it allowed on a footpath? |
|---|---|
| Riding along it | No |
| Riding straight across it to enter a house, an office or a lawful parking place | The exception reported for the fine; take it slowly and give way to pedestrians |
| Walking beside the bike with the engine off | A Vietnamese driving-school guide says this is not the prohibited act of riding; follow any local instruction |
| Parking on it | Only where the authorities have designated and signed pavement parking |
| Stopping briefly to let a passenger off | Not on a crossing or within 5 metres of a junction; do it at the kerb on the roadway |

The exception is narrow. Crossing a pavement to enter a driveway is not the same as using it to pass a queue. If you use a driveway, go straight across at walking pace, give way to anyone on the pavement and do not stop on it.

## How to get in and out of a parking place

Many parking places in Hanoi sit behind a pavement, with a ramp over the kerb.

1. **Check that the place is a real parking place,** with an attendant, a ticket and posted hours, not a pavement that has simply filled up with bikes. Our guide to [how to find legitimate guarded parking near tourist sights](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-find-legitimate-guarded-parking-near-tourist-sights/) explains what to look for.

2. **Stop at the kerb,** look both ways along the pavement and wait for people to pass.

3. **Cross at walking pace,** straight, with your feet ready to touch the ground.

4. **Do not ride along the pavement to reach a space further down.** Park where you enter.

5. **If the entrance is blocked by bikes,** walk the bike in with the engine off, or go to another place.

6. **When you leave,** look for pedestrians before you ride off the pavement and into the street.

Our guide to [parking a motorbike in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-motorbike-in-hanoi/) explains tickets and claim procedures.

## Weekend walking streets

Hanoi's walking streets around Hoan Kiem Lake and the Old Quarter are closed to motor vehicles at set times. According to published reports, motorbikes and cars are barred from the pilot core zone from 19:00 to midnight on Fridays, Saturdays and Sundays. Times and boundaries can change for holidays and events, so check before you ride. A scooter inside the perimeter at the start of the closure is in a zone that has been closed to it, and a barrier or a police officer's instruction is binding. Do not weave around a barrier, even if other bikes do. Our guide to [Hanoi weekend walking streets: where to leave your scooter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-weekend-walking-streets-where-to-leave-your-scooter/) explains the schedules and the outer parking points, and our guide to [one-way street patterns around Hoan Kiem](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/one-way-street-patterns-around-hoan-kiem/) helps you plan an approach.

## When traffic is jammed

The temptation to hop onto a footpath is strongest in a jam. Resist it.

- **Staying in your lane is legal,** and a short wait costs far less than a fine and a risk to pedestrians.

- **Pedestrians are the most vulnerable road users.** The WHO notes that more than half of all road traffic deaths are among pedestrians, cyclists and motorcyclists, and that a pedestrian hit at 65 km/h is 4.5 times more likely to die than one hit at 50 km/h.

- **Look for a legal alternative.** Turn at the next junction, or park in a lawful place and walk.

- **Use a map app,** but check what it suggests. Our guide to [how to handle stop-and-go traffic on a semi-automatic bike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-handle-stop-and-go-traffic-on-a-semi-automatic-bike/) shows how to stay calm in a queue.

## If you realise you are on a footpath

1. **Slow down and stop,** if you can do so without a sudden move.

2. **Switch off the engine and walk the bike** to the nearest lawful place, giving way to everyone.

3. **Do not ride off the kerb into traffic** without looking.

4. **If an officer stops you,** be polite and follow the steps in our guide to [what to do if a traffic officer stops your motorbike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-to-do-if-a-traffic-officer-stops-your-motorbike/).

## A quick checklist

1. **Stay on the roadway, in your lane.**

2. **Do not use a pavement to pass a queue.**

3. **Cross a pavement only to enter a house, an office or a lawful parking place, slowly and straight.**

4. **Park only in signed or guarded places.**

5. **Walk the bike, engine off, where riding is not allowed.**

6. **Respect barriers and walking-street hours.**

7. **Call the shop if you get a notice.**

## FAQ: footpaths and pedestrian streets

### Can I ride my scooter on the footpath in Vietnam?

No. Article 33 of Law 36/2024/QH15 bans motorcycles and mopeds from road sections reserved for pedestrians, and Article 77 says pavements are for pedestrians.

### What is the fine for riding on a pavement?

Legal publications report VND 4,000,000 to 6,000,000 and 2 licence points under Decree 168/2024/ND-CP. Check the current text.

### Can I cross a footpath to enter a car park?

The decree's exception, as reported, covers crossing a pavement to enter a house or an office. Cross slowly and straight, give way to pedestrians and do not stop on the pavement.

### Can I walk my scooter along a pedestrian street?

A Vietnamese driving-school guide says pushing a bike with the engine off is not the prohibited act of riding, but follow any instruction on site, since some streets restrict vehicles of any kind at set hours.

### Can I park on the pavement?

Only where the authorities have designated and signed it. Unlawful pavement parking is reported to carry a fine of VND 400,000 to 600,000.

### What if the road is blocked?

Wait, or take another route. Do not move barriers.
