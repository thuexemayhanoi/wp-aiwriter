For a quick, light errand, a traditional market can be the more interesting stop, but a supermarket or mall is usually the easier place to leave a rented scooter. Markets such as Dong Xuan, Hom and Hang Be sit on tight streets where kerb space is scarce and parking is improvised or run by small attended yards. Supermarkets normally have their own yards and fixed hours, which makes the end of the shopping trip more predictable. Neither is wrong; the right choice depends on what you are buying, the time of day and how much you plan to carry. Facts here were checked as of October 2026.

## Match the stop to the shopping

Before comparing car parks, decide what you are actually after. A traditional market is a place to browse, bargain and eat; a supermarket is a place to get a list done quickly in an air-conditioned building.

If you want fruit, flowers, textiles, souvenirs or a snack in a market's alleys, go to the market and accept a slightly messier parking situation. If you want bottled water, toiletries, packaged food or anything bulky, the supermarket's yard and trolleys make the journey home simpler. If you want both, do the market first on an early outing, and the supermarket on the way back, so perishable goods spend less time on the bike.

## How traditional-market parking usually works

Hanoi's best-known covered markets were built for pedestrians and handcarts, not for hundreds of scooters. The result is a mixture of small attended yards, kerbside rows and informal arrangements.

**Dong Xuan Market.** Vietnam Airlines' visitor guide gives the address as 15 Cau Dong Street in the Old Quarter and the hours as 6 am to 6 pm daily, open until 10:30 pm on Friday to Sunday nights. A VinWonders guide says visitors can park at a yard near the market's east gate. A commercial travel blog from 2019 warned that the market's parking area is not large, and that anyone unfamiliar with the Old Quarter should consider taking public transport or walking.

**Hom Market.** A travel guide dated August 2025 gives 79 Pho Hue as the address, hours of 6 am to 6 pm and says the market has a parking lot, with fees reported as slightly above the city average. Its advice is the useful part: pay the attendant on arrival, and keep the ticket safe.

**Hang Be Market.** Vinpearl's guide says it is best visited at 7 to 9 am or 5 to 8 pm and suggests parking on nearby streets such as Luong Van Can and Hang Vai. Those streets are also busy, so arrive with patience.

These are commercial travel sources rather than official notices, and arrangements change. Treat them as a way to know what to look for.

## How supermarket parking usually works

Supermarkets come in two main forms. Some sit inside shopping centres and share the mall's motorbike yard; for example, the Lotte Mart supermarket is on basement level 1 of Lotte Mall West Lake, and Royal City has a WinMart supermarket among its underground shops. Others are stand-alone stores with their own front yard or basement.

Opening hours are the most useful difference. The Royal City WinMart is listed as open from 8:00 to 22:00, so you can shop well after traditional markets have closed for the day, and Lotte Mart announced a change to its opening hours in June 2024, so check the current times before you go. A yard is usually signed from the road, has an entrance ramp and is staffed. That makes it easier to find, but it is also easier to lose a bike among many rows, so note your bay.

For the mechanics of tickets, lost-ticket problems and mall layouts, see our parking guide in the [Hanoi parking article](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-motorbike-in-hanoi/) rather than repeating them here.

## When each place is easiest

Traditional markets have strong peaks. Hang Be's guide names 7 to 9 am and 5 to 8 pm as its busiest periods, and a guide to Dong Xuan describes wholesale activity peaking in the early morning, particularly at weekends. If you want to park easily, aim for the quieter slots between the peaks, such as late morning or mid-afternoon.

Supermarkets and malls fill up in the evenings and at weekends. If you are shopping for a family outing, a weekday mid-morning visit usually means a lighter yard.

Be careful in the Old Quarter at weekends. The Friday-to-Sunday night market around Dong Xuan and Hang Dao is described as closing streets to vehicles in the evening. Separately, since 1 July 2026, Hanoi's low-emission pilot in the Hoan Kiem core has banned motorbikes, mopeds and cars from 7 pm to midnight on Fridays, Saturdays and Sundays in its first zone, which includes the Old Quarter and the night market. Phase one runs until 31 December 2026, and later phases are planned, so check current notices. For a weekend evening at Dong Xuan, walking from a car park outside the zone or using a taxi is the sensible plan.

## Carrying what you buy

A scooter is not a delivery van, so what you carry matters more than where you park.

Law 36/2024/QH15, in force since 1 January 2025, says motorbikes and mopeds must not carry goods that extend more than 0.3 m beyond either side of the cargo rack or more than 0.5 m behind it, and the stack must not exceed 2 m in height from the road surface. A few shopping bags fit comfortably inside those limits. A folding table or large box does not.

A few practical habits make a difference:

- Put raw meat, fish and wet produce in sealed bags so liquid does not reach the seat.
- Keep heavy items low and in front, not hanging from the handlebars, where they affect steering.
- Do not hang a bag where it can touch the wheel or exhaust.
- Buy fragile things last, and wrap them well.
- If you are buying a lot, ask the shop to deliver it or take a taxi for the return.

## Parking manners at a crowded market

Markets are working places. Pavement space is used by sellers, by shoppers, by porters and by wheeled carts. The same Law that sets the load limits says in Article 18 that vehicles on city streets should stop or park along the kerb or pavement on the right, no more than 0.25 m from it, without obstructing pedestrians or other vehicles, and that stopping at pedestrian crossings is not allowed. A bike left across a market entrance breaks that rule and also the day of whoever needs to get past.

In practice:

- Use a yard or an attendant-run row rather than the lane itself.
- Leave pavements walkable and do not block alley openings.
- Do not park behind a loading vehicle.
- Ask if you are not sure; attendants usually point to the right spot.
- If a seller asks you to move, move without debate.

The [Long Bien Market guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-visit-long-bien-market-without-blocking-deliveries/) goes into the same etiquette for a wholesale market.

## A simple decision guide

- **You want to browse and eat for an hour in the Old Quarter:** park outside the busiest streets and walk.
- **You want a few souvenirs and have no deadline:** a daytime market visit on a weekday.
- **You are buying a week of groceries:** a supermarket yard, early or mid-morning.
- **It is raining or after dark:** a mall or supermarket with covered or underground parking.
- **You are unsure where to leave the bike:** ask the shop before you set off, via the [contact page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/contact/).

## FAQ

### Is it easier to park at a market or at a supermarket?

A supermarket, as a rule. It has a marked yard and longer hours. Markets can be more fun but have tighter space.

### Can I park on the pavement outside a market?

Only where it is clearly allowed. Law 36/2024/QH15 restricts parking on pavements contrary to the rules, and obstructive stops can lead to relocation of the vehicle.

### Do markets charge for parking?

Often yes. Hom Market's guide mentions fees above the city average. Look for a posted rate and keep your ticket.

### What time is best for Dong Xuan?

Between the early-morning rush and the evening crowds. Weekend evenings are the busiest and fall under the Hoan Kiem pilot restrictions.

### Can I carry a whole market haul on a scooter?

Only a modest load. The Law's limits and ordinary balance both apply. For bulky items use a taxi or delivery.
