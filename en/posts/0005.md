The deposit is the part of a motorbike rental that worries visitors most, and with reason: it is the largest sum of money that changes hands, it is tied up for the whole rental, and in Hanoi it is still sometimes replaced by something far more valuable than cash, your passport. This guide explains what a deposit is for, how Nguyen Ha sets its cash deposit and returns it, why you should never leave an original document with a shop, and the small habits at handover and return that make getting the deposit back a formality. The business terms are those published by Nguyen Ha in October 2026; the legal points come from Vietnam's road traffic law in force since 1 January 2025.

## Why rental shops ask for a deposit at all

A scooter worth tens of millions of dong is handed to a stranger for the price of a lunch per day. The deposit is the shop's security that the bike comes back on time, undamaged and with its papers, and that charges agreed in the contract, such as a late return or an empty tank, can be settled at the end. Two models exist in Hanoi. Some shops ask for a cash deposit and nothing else; others ask for no money but keep the renter's passport or identity card until the bike returns. The second model looks convenient and is a bad idea for the renter, as the next section explains.

Nguyen Ha uses the first model only. The deposit is cash, between VND 2 million and 5 million depending on the bike and the length of the rental, and it is paid together with the rent when the contract is signed. Your passport or identity card and your driving licence are checked and photographed for the contract and handed straight back; no original is kept. Because the cash deposit is what secures the bike, the shop never describes the service as deposit-free, and a visitor who is offered a "no deposit, just leave your passport" deal elsewhere should read on before accepting.

## Why you should keep your passport

Your passport is the one document you cannot replace in an afternoon. Hotels and guesthouses in Vietnam register foreign guests and ask to see it at check-in; a bank, a SIM card shop or a domestic flight asks for it; and if you are stopped by the police on the bike, your passport is the document that proves who you are while your licence proves you may ride. A passport sitting in a drawer across town does none of this, and a shop that holds it holds a lever over you in any dispute about damage or charges at the return.

Vietnam's Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (Law 36/2024/QH15, effective 1 January 2025) requires a rider to carry the documents of the bike and the driving licence while riding, and Decree 168/2024/ND-CP sets a fine of VND 200,000–300,000 for a motorbike rider who holds a licence but does not have it on them. Carry your licence and its International Driving Permit, the copy of the bike's registration the shop gives you, and your passport or a good copy of it. Our guide to [licence rules for foreigners in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbike-licence-rules-for-foreigners-in-vietnam/) explains which permits are recognised.

## How much the deposit is, and how it is set

At Nguyen Ha the deposit is VND 2–5 million. The exact figure depends on the bike and on the contract: a longer rental or a larger, newer scooter sits towards the top of the range, a short rental of a small bike towards the bottom. The amount is confirmed, together with the rent, before you sign, so there is no gap between what you were told and what you pay. Weekly and monthly renters pay the same kind of deposit as daily renters; the rent itself is the part that is packaged differently, as the [price guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/prices/) shows.

Bring the deposit in cash. Card payments and bank transfers are spreading in Hanoi, but a cash deposit is counted, recorded in the contract and returned in the same form at the end, which keeps the transaction simple on both sides. Ask for the amount to be written into the contract, and keep your copy.

## What the deposit can be used for

The deposit is not a fee; it is held and returned. It is drawn on only for charges the contract allows, and those are the ones you can read in advance: a late return, which Nguyen Ha charges at VND 30,000 per hour for the first one to five hours and as a full extra rental day from six hours; an empty fuel tank at return, charged at VND 20,000; a lost helmet or missing accessory; and damage to the bike beyond the condition recorded at handover. Returning the bike early does not touch the deposit, but the unused rent is not refunded either, so the deposit is not a way to recover a shortened rental.

Damage is where disputes happen, so settle the rules before you sign: how a scratch, a broken mirror or a cracked panel is priced, whether the shop uses dealer parts, and what happens with a breakdown or a puncture on the road. Nguyen Ha asks you to call the shop first when something goes wrong, and to confirm how a roadside repair will be handled before you agree to it; keep the receipt.

## Getting the deposit back

The return is a mirror of the handover. You bring the bike back at the agreed time to the shop in Long Bien, or hand it to the collection rider on a weekly or monthly rental with delivery, with the fuel it should have, the helmet, the key and the papers. The shop compares the bike with the handover record, settles any charge, and returns the deposit under the contract. If nothing is late, missing or broken, that takes a few minutes.

Four habits make it that simple. Photograph the whole bike at handover, including the mirrors, the lights, the seat and the underside of the floorboard, with the time visible on your phone. Note the fuel level and the odometer together with the shop. Keep your copy of the contract and the shop's phone number on you for the whole rental. And return on time: the late-return charges exist because the next renter is waiting for the bike, not to catch you out, and a phone call ahead keeps everyone calm even though it does not waive the charge.

## Protecting yourself beyond the deposit

A deposit protects the shop; three things protect you. First, insurance: Vietnamese motorbikes carry compulsory third-party liability insurance in the owner's name, but your own medical cover comes from your travel insurance, and almost every travel policy requires you to hold a licence valid for the bike you ride. Rent a bike your licence covers, and read the motorbike clause of your policy before you leave home. Second, the contract: read it, including the Vietnamese version if you can get it translated, and make sure the deposit, the rent, the return time and the fuel level are written down. Third, your own checks: the ten-minute inspection described in our [rental guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-guide/) is the best protection against paying for someone else's scratch.

## FAQ

### How much is the deposit at Nguyen Ha?

VND 2–5 million in cash, set by the bike and the length of the rental and confirmed before you sign. It is paid with the rent and returned at the end of the rental under the contract.

### Can I leave my passport instead of a cash deposit?

Not at Nguyen Ha, and you should not want to anywhere. You need your passport for hotels, banks, flights and police checks, and a shop that holds it holds a lever in any dispute. The shop photographs your documents for the contract and gives them back.

### Is the deposit refundable if I return the bike early?

The deposit is returned as normal, but the rent for the unused days is not refunded. Choose a rental period that matches your plans, and extend rather than overbuy.

### What can be deducted from the deposit?

Only charges the contract allows: a late return, an empty tank, a lost helmet or accessory, and damage beyond the handover condition. Settle how damage is priced before you sign.

### Do I pay the deposit by card?

Bring cash. The deposit is counted and written into the contract and returned in cash at the end of the rental.

### What if I lose the contract?

The shop keeps its copy, and your handover photos and messages fill the gaps; tell the shop as soon as you notice, before the return.
