Before you put a child on a rented scooter in Hanoi, verify six things: the child is old enough and steady enough to sit and hold on; a helmet that fits is ready for them; they will sit behind the rider, on the seat, with their feet on the footrests; the bike and the route suit a trip with a child; the rider holds the right licence and has the right cover; and the shop has confirmed what it provides. Law 36/2024/QH15 allows a motorcycle or moped to carry one person, or two where one is a child under 12, and requires every person carried to wear a helmet with the strap fastened (Article 33).

A helmet matters: the World Health Organization says correct helmet use can reduce the risk of death in a crash by more than six times and the risk of brain injury by up to 74 per cent.

This guide gives a checklist to complete before you leave, and explains each point: the legal limits, how to judge whether a child is ready, how to check a helmet, how a child should sit, how to choose the bike and route, what insurance to look at and what to ask the shop. Our [child passengers guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/child-passengers-motorbike-vietnam/) covers the legal detail and the fines, so this guide does not repeat it. It is general information based on the law and published guidance as of October 2026, not legal or medical advice.

## The six-point check

| Point | What to verify |
|---|---|
| 1. **Readiness** | The child can sit upright, reach the footrests, hold on and stay awake |
| 2. **Helmet** | A helmet that fits the child, in good condition, with a fastened strap |
| 3. **Seating** | Behind the rider, on the seat, feet on the footrests; never standing or in front |
| 4. **Bike and route** | A suitable scooter, a short and calm route, good weather |
| 5. **Rider and cover** | A licence valid for the bike, and insurance that covers the passenger |
| 6. **Shop confirmation** | What the shop provides, what it asks and what it allows |

## 1. Is the child ready?

The law sets an age threshold for the number of passengers, not a minimum age for sitting on a scooter. Article 33 lets a rider carry two people when one is a child under 12. The law does not say how old a child must be to ride at all, so the decision is yours, and the sensible tests are practical.

- **Can the child sit upright without help?** A child who slumps or cannot hold the position is not ready.

- **Can their feet reach the footrests?** Dangling feet can touch the wheel or the ground. Do not use a bike where the child's feet cannot rest.

- **Can they hold the rider's waist or the grab rail,** and do they understand that they must keep holding?

- **Can they stay awake?** A tired child may fall asleep and slump sideways. Plan rides when they are alert and keep them short.

- **Will they follow simple instructions,** such as "feet on the pegs, hold on, do not move"?

- **Do they have a health problem** that matters, such as fainting or travel sickness? Ask a doctor if unsure.

If the answer to any of these is no, use a taxi or a ride-hail car instead. Our guide to [when to choose walking or public transport over a 50cc rental](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/when-to-choose-walking-or-public-transport-over-a-50cc-rental/) helps you decide when a scooter is not the right choice.

## 2. The helmet

Article 33 applies the helmet rule to everyone carried, with no age exception. How the penalty decree treats a very young child differs from the law's duty, as our child passengers guide explains, but a rider who wants the child protected should not rely on that.

- **Ask the shop what it provides.** The published terms say one basic helmet per rider is included at no charge. They do not say whether a child-sized helmet is available, so ask.

- **Do not use an adult helmet on a small head.** It will move, slip down over the eyes or come off.

- **Check the fit.** A helmet should sit level and low on the forehead, snug on the cheeks, and should not shift when the child shakes their head. Published fitting guidance describes a 2-2-2 check: about two fingers above the eyebrows, two fingers' width between the strap and the chin, and the side straps forming a V just below the ears.

- **Try the lift test.** With the strap fastened, hold the back edge and try to roll the helmet forward off the head. It should not come off.

- **Fasten the strap properly,** every time. Our guide to [how to fit and fasten a motorbike helmet properly](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-fit-and-fasten-a-motorbike-helmet-properly/) walks through the steps.

- **Inspect the helmet.** Cracks, a loose lining or a broken strap are reasons to ask for another. Our guide to [what to do if a helmet does not fit at pickup](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-to-do-if-a-helmet-does-not-fit-at-pickup/) explains how.

If you bring your own child's helmet, check that it meets the Vietnamese standard described in our child passengers guide, and keep it with you when you park.

## 3. Where the child sits

- **Behind the rider, astride the seat, feet on the footrests.** This is the standard position, and the only one suitable for a child of six or older.

- **Not in front of the rider.** Our child passengers guide explains the law on a child sitting in front, and the risk is real: the rider's arms and view are restricted.

- **Not standing on the footboard,** on the seat or on the cargo rack. Article 33 bans carrying people standing on a motorbike, and bans passengers from standing on the saddle or cargo rack or sitting on the steering wheel.

- **Not held in an adult's arm.** A child held in one arm is not protected, and the adult cannot use both hands.

- **Not between two adults without a plan.** With two adults and a small child, the child may sit between them, as many families do, but keep the total to two passengers and make sure the child cannot slide.

- **Not with a heavy bag on their back.** Keep luggage light and secure. Our guide to [why loose scarves and bags are dangerous on motorbikes](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/why-loose-scarves-and-bags-are-dangerous-on-motorbikes/) explains how.

Teach the child the basics before you start. Our guide to [riding with a passenger: balance and communication](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-with-a-passenger-balance-and-communication/) lists the signals and the rules for feet and hands.

## 4. The bike and the route

**The bike.** A scooter with a low seat, a grab rail and footrests suits a child better than a high manual bike. Ask the shop which bike has these features and what its maximum load is. Our guides to [choosing a scooter with space for a passenger helmet](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/choosing-a-scooter-with-space-for-a-passenger-helmet/) and [riding a 50cc scooter with a passenger: safety factors](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-a-50cc-scooter-with-a-passenger-safety-factors/) explain what to look at.

**The route.** Choose a short, quiet route with few junctions, and avoid:

- **Peak traffic,** school times and the Old Quarter's narrow streets. Our guide to [school-zone riding: children and sudden pedestrian movements](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/school-zone-riding-children-and-sudden-pedestrian-movements/) explains why those times are busy.

- **Rain, strong wind and darkness.** Our guide to [safe scooter riding when visibility drops in rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/safe-scooter-riding-when-visibility-drops-in-rain/) explains how to judge it.

- **Rough roads,** potholes and works. Our guide to [how to ride near construction sites and temporary barriers](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-ride-near-construction-sites-and-temporary-barriers/) lists the hazards.

- **Long trips.** A child tires faster, so plan stops, shade and water, and be ready to turn back.

- **Roads closed to motorbikes,** such as expressways. Our guide to [how to check which roads restrict motorbikes](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-check-which-roads-restrict-motorbikes/) explains how.

**Your riding.** Ride more slowly than you would alone, brake early and keep more distance. A child on the back is a reason to ride below your normal pace.

## Do not

- **Do not carry more than the legal number.** Article 33 allows two people at most, and only in the listed cases.

- **Do not let a child hold anything that could fall,** such as a phone or a balloon.

- **Do not ride after drinking.** Article 9 of the Law bans riding with any alcohol in the blood or breath. Our guide to [drink-driving rules for motorbikes in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/drink-driving-rules-motorbike-vietnam/) explains.

- **Do not leave a child on a parked scooter.** It can fall, and the bike can be hot.

- **Do not ignore the child's signals.** If they say they are scared or uncomfortable, stop.

## A quick checklist

1. **The child can sit upright, reach the footrests, hold on and stay awake.**

2. **A helmet that fits, with the strap fastened and the lift test passed.**

3. **Behind the rider, feet on the footrests, never in front or standing.**

4. **A suitable scooter, a short and calm route, good weather, no peak hours.**

5. **A valid licence and cover that includes the passenger.**

6. **Shop answers noted: children allowed, helmet, load, tyres.**

7. **Signals agreed, and a plan to stop if the child is unhappy.**

## FAQ: children on scooters

### Is there a minimum age for a child passenger in Vietnam?

Law 36/2024/QH15 does not set a minimum age for sitting on a scooter. It allows two passengers when one is a child under 12. Use the practical tests above, and see our child passengers guide.

### Does a child need a helmet?

Article 33 requires every person carried to wear a helmet that meets the standard, with the strap fastened. Our child passengers guide explains how the penalty decree treats very young children.

### Can a child sit in front of me?

Our child passengers guide explains the legal position, and it is a risk to the child and to your control of the bike. Seat the child behind you.

### Can my child ride the rental scooter themselves?

No. A person must meet the age and licence rules, and a child cannot. Our child passengers guide covers the rule.

### Does the shop provide a child helmet?

The published terms say one basic helmet per rider. They do not mention child sizes, so ask.

### What if the child is frightened?

Stop at the next safe place, let them get off, and decide whether to continue or take a taxi.
