Set the mirrors before you move, sitting on the bike in your normal riding position, so that about a quarter of each mirror shows your own arm and the other three quarters show the traffic behind you. That is the rule in Honda Vietnam's safe-riding training document, and it takes under a minute. A mirror that shows too much of your shoulder or too much of the sky is a mirror you will not trust, and you will end up not using it. This guide explains how to adjust the mirrors on a rented Honda Wave step by step, why mirrors alone are not enough, how to check the blind spots with a shoulder check, what to do if a mirror will not hold its position and what the law says about the left-hand mirror. It is general information. Always follow the demonstration for the exact bike you rent.

## Why mirrors matter on a small bike

On a light bike in dense traffic, you cannot afford to turn your head for every decision, and the mirrors give a constant, quick look at what is behind. In Hanoi that means other motorbikes filtering up on either side, buses and trucks and delivery riders moving fast. A well-set mirror shows you the lane behind and the edge of the lanes beside, which is what you need before a lane change, a stop or a turn.

A badly set mirror shows the wrong thing. If it is aimed too far out, it shows only the road beside you. If it is aimed too far in, it shows your own arm and shoulder. If it vibrates, it shows a blur. Rental bikes are ridden by many people of different heights, so the mirrors are rarely set for you when you pick the bike up.

## Honda's rule for setting them

Honda's training document, dated August 2026, describes how to set the mirrors. Horizontally, one quarter of the mirror should show your arm and the remaining three quarters should show the traffic behind you. The document also gives a vertical guide, but the wording in the copy we could read is unclear, so ask the shop to show you or use a simple version: the mirror should show the road behind you and the horizon, with a little of the road surface near your own bike, and not mostly the sky or mostly the ground.

The horizontal rule is the one that matters most. Seeing a sliver of your arm gives you a reference, so you know what the mirror is looking at and can tell how far back and how far across the view is.

## Step by step

Do this with the bike on its stand on level ground, the engine off and the helmet on, so that your eye position matches riding.

1. **Sit on the bike in your riding position.** Hands on the grips, elbows relaxed, back straight and head up, as Honda's posture advice describes. Do not sit forward or slouch.
2. **Look into the left mirror.** If a lot of your arm and shoulder is visible, rotate the mirror outwards until about a quarter of the view is your arm.
3. **Tilt the mirror vertically** so you see the road behind you and a little of the horizon, not just the sky or the road immediately behind your wheel.
4. **Repeat with the right mirror.**
5. **Check you can see a vehicle behind** at a distance. Ask someone to stand a few metres behind the bike. You should see them in both mirrors.
6. **Check each mirror is firm.** Press it lightly with a finger. It should stay in place and not vibrate, flop or turn too easily.
7. **Adjust again when someone else rides.** If a passenger or another rider uses the bike, the position will change.

Do not adjust a mirror while moving. Pull over, or ask the shop to do it.

## Blind spots and the shoulder check

No mirror shows everything. Honda's training document names the blind spots of the mirrors and gives the cure: look at both sides and behind not only through the mirrors but by turning your head over your shoulder and looking directly. It warns against turning your whole body or your shoulders, because the handlebars can turn with you and make the bike swerve suddenly.

In practice, before a lane change, a turn or a pull-away from the kerb:

1. **Glance in the mirror** to see what is behind.
2. **Signal.**
3. **Look over your shoulder** on the side you are moving to, turning only your head.
4. **Look forward again** and move only if the space is clear.

The shoulder check takes about a second, and it is the habit that stops riders being clipped by a bike in the blind spot. ## What the law says about mirrors

Under Article 14 of Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, which we read on the Government portal, a rider who rides a motorbike without a horn, a number-plate light, a brake light or a left-hand mirror, or with one that does not work, is fined VND 400,000–600,000 as of October 2026. Decree 238/2026/ND-CP, which amends the decree from 15 August 2026, changes other wording in Article 14 (it concerns number plates) and does not touch this fine for the left mirror. So a working left mirror is a legal requirement, and a broken or missing one is a reason to refuse the bike.

Check both mirrors at handover. If the left mirror is cracked, loose or missing, tell the shop and ask for it to be fixed or the bike swapped before you leave. ## If a mirror will not stay put

A mirror that vibrates or slips is useless and annoying. Common causes are a loose stem nut, a worn joint or a bent arm. Do not force it or bend the arm. Ask the shop to tighten it or swap it. Some mirrors have a lock nut at the base. A mechanic can tighten it in seconds. If the mirror glass is cracked, a clear crack can be distracting, and a cracked mirror may break further, so ask for a replacement.

If you drop the bike or it falls over while parked, check the mirrors again, because they are often knocked out of position. ## Tall and short riders, and passengers

A tall rider tends to see more of the road than the sky and may need the mirror tilted upwards. A short rider may need the opposite. The horizontal rule still works: a quarter of the view should show your arm. If you wear a bulky jacket or a backpack, your shoulders may block more of the view, so adjust with the gear on.

A passenger changes the view. A pillion who sits tall or leans can block part of the mirror. Tell them to sit upright and keep their head out of your line of sight, and adjust the mirrors with them on board if possible.

## Mirrors and speed

At speed, a mirror vibrates more and shows less detail. If the mirror blurs at a certain speed, the mount may be loose. Slow down and ask the shop to check. In stop-and-go traffic, the mirror is most useful for judging the gap behind you before you stop and deciding whether to brake gently or firmly. Honda's training document sets a following-distance rule for the vehicle ahead, the two-second rule, and a four-second rule in poor light, bad weather or when a vehicle behind is very close. A mirror lets you see that vehicle behind and decide how to brake. Our guide to [handling stop-and-go traffic on a semi-automatic bike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-handle-stop-and-go-traffic-on-a-semi-automatic-bike/) explains the technique.

## A mirror routine for every ride

- **Before you ride:** sit in your riding position and set both mirrors to the quarter and three-quarter rule.
- **At the first stop:** check again, because road vibration can shift them.
- **Before every move:** mirror, signal, shoulder check, move.
- **After a fall or a bump:** reset them.
- **If someone else rides:** reset them.

## FAQ: adjusting mirrors on a Honda Wave

### How should the mirrors be set?

Sitting in your riding position, about a quarter of each mirror should show your arm and three quarters the traffic behind, as Honda's training document says.

### Are mirrors enough to see behind me?

No. Honda says to look over your shoulder as well, turning only your head, to cover the blind spots.

### Is a left mirror legally required?

Yes. As of October 2026, riding without a working left-hand mirror is fined VND 400,000–600,000 under Decree 168/2024/ND-CP.

### What if the mirror keeps moving?

Ask the shop to tighten or replace it. Do not ride with a loose mirror.

### Should I adjust the mirror while riding?

No. Stop in a safe place first.

### Do I need to adjust the mirrors if someone else rode the bike?

Yes. A different rider changes the view, so always reset them.
