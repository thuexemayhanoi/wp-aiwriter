Plan fuel stops on a scooter day trip around the tank, not around the gauge: fill up before you leave the city because rental scooters are handed over with only a small amount of petrol, expect to refuel at about half a tank rather than at the warning light, and make sure each leg of the route ends at a place where you know a petrol station exists. Automatic scooters have small tanks. Honda Vietnam lists the Vision at 4.8 litres and the Air Blade at 4.4 litres, so a long day can easily need two stops. This guide explains what the shop's handover fuel means for your first stop, a simple way to estimate range, a half-tank rule, how the new E10 petrol fits Honda scooters, how to refuel safely, what to do if the low-fuel light comes on far from a station and how to return the scooter without paying for an empty tank.

## Start with the handover fuel

The shop's published practice is to hand over petrol bikes with about 0.5 to 2 litres in the tank and to expect them back with about 0.5 to 1 litre. That is deliberately small, so the fuel you buy on the day is the fuel you ride on.

For a day trip, the practical lesson is that your first petrol stop should be early. Do not leave the city thinking the scooter has enough fuel because the gauge shows something. Fill the tank at a station close to the shop before you head out, then check the dashboard reading against the tank. Our guide to [fuel in Hanoi and filling up a rental scooter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/fuel-in-hanoi-petrol-stations-rental-scooter/) explains how stations work, and the shop's [rental guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-guide/) describes the return expectations.

When you return, an empty tank costs VND 20,000, so aim to come back with a little in the tank, within the 0.5 to 1 litre expectation. You do not need to fill up at the end. You only need to avoid running it dry.

## Work out a realistic range

Honda publishes fuel-consumption figures for its scooters: 1.82 litres per 100 km for the Vision and 2.23 litres per 100 km for the Air Blade 160. These are test-condition figures. Real riding in traffic, with two people, luggage, hills, wind or heat, uses more.

A rough worked example shows why planning matters. A 4.8-litre tank at 1.82 litres per 100 km works out to roughly 260 km on paper. A 4.4-litre tank at 2.23 litres per 100 km works out to roughly 200 km. Those numbers are a ceiling. A prudent traveller takes a margin of a third or more off them, and treats the gauge as an approximation. So, on a 200 km day, the tank-capacity arithmetic says you could make it on one fill, but a sensible plan still adds a stop.

Three things make the real number worse than the test number:

- **Weight.** A passenger and a bag raise consumption.
- **Hills and headwinds.** Tam Dao or Ba Vi routes ask more of a small engine. See [Hanoi to Tam Dao](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-to-tam-dao-by-motorbike/) and [Hanoi to Ba Vi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-to-ba-vi-by-motorbike/).
- **Stop-and-go traffic,** especially leaving and re-entering the city at busy times.

## A half-tank rule

A simple rule works better than precise arithmetic: **refuel when the gauge reaches about half, or at the next station after that, whichever comes first.** On a small scooter tank, half is only two litres or so, but it leaves a margin for a closed station, a wrong turn or a detour.

Add two refinements.

1. **Refuel before, not after, a long empty stretch.** If the next section is rural, mountainous or on a smaller road, top up in the last town, even if you are above half.
2. **Fill up at the turnaround, not on the way back.** If you are at your destination and have used about half a tank, fill up before the return so you do not start the homeward leg with doubts.

The rule is general guidance, not a manufacturer instruction, but it fits what Honda says about the gauge itself.

## What the fuel gauge and warning light tell you

Honda's Vision page notes an LCD display showing fuel level, and its Plus One article on indicators describes the low-fuel indicator: it illuminates when petrol is low, and if it flashes you should refuel as soon as possible. The same article notes that the amount left depends on the vehicle and is given in the owner's manual. So the light is a last warning, not a plan.

Some practical habits:

- **Check the gauge at every stop,** not just when you remember.
- **Do not trust a needle on a slope.** A reading can look lower or higher on an incline, as the fuel moves in the tank.
- **Treat the light as "next station, now."** If it comes on, switch to a gentle throttle and head for the closest station.
- **Keep your phone charged and a map open.** Our guide on [paying for fuel, parking and tolls as a visitor](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/paying-fuel-parking-tolls-hanoi-visitor/) covers the payment side. Carry some cash in case a card terminal is not working.

## Which petrol to buy

Honda's manuals for its scooters specify unleaded petrol with an octane rating of RON 91 or higher, and Honda Vietnam has said that its motorcycles can use ethanol petrol with up to 10% ethanol.

The market has also changed this year. Under Circular 50/2025/TT-BCT of the Ministry of Industry and Trade, from 1 June 2026 unleaded petrol sold for petrol engines nationwide must be blended as E10, containing 10% ethanol, while E5 RON92 remains on sale until the end of 2030. The Vietnam Association of Motorcycle Manufacturers' preliminary assessment, as reported by Nhan Dan, was that current Honda and Yamaha motorcycle models need no modification to run on E10.

For a renter, this means:

- You are likely to see pumps labelled E10 RON 95 and E5 RON 92. Either grade is within what Honda describes for its scooters, but ask the shop which it prefers for your bike.
- Fill with unleaded petrol at a reputable station. Honda's manual says leaded petrol damages the catalytic converter and recommends the stated octane number.
- If in doubt about your specific bike, ask at handover, and write the answer in your notes.

## Choose stops before you leave

Planning the stops is a ten-minute job at the hotel table.

1. **Mark the route** and its major towns on your map app.
2. **Search for petrol stations along it** and save two or three as pins, spaced so that no gap between them is more than about half the tank's range.
3. **Note where the road is empty.** Look at the satellite view for long stretches without settlements.
4. **Find a backup.** If your main stop is closed or out of petrol, where is the next one?
5. **Agree a rule with your group** about who watches the gauge.

## Roadside petrol sellers

In rural Vietnam you will see small stalls selling petrol from bottles. They are a fallback for an emergency, not a plan. Quality and quantity are not guaranteed, and with a scooter's electronic fuel injection and, on some models, a catalytic converter, poor fuel is more than an inconvenience. If you must use one, buy only enough to reach a station and ride gently. A station with a pump is better, because the fuel is dispensed and metered.

## Refuelling safely

Honda's Vision manual describes the procedure, and the sequence is the same on most scooters:

1. **Stop and switch the engine off.** Honda says petrol is highly flammable and explosive. Keep sparks, flames and smoking away.
2. **Open the seat if the filler is under it,** as it is on the Vision, and then the fuel cap.
3. **Do not fill above the lower edge of the filler neck.** Honda gives this limit, because overfilling can spill fuel.
4. **Close the cap firmly,** lining up the arrow marks, then close the seat.
5. **Wipe up any spill** and let the area dry before you start.

The scooter is easiest to fill on its centre stand on level ground; ask the shop at handover how to use the stand. At the station you can ask the attendant for a set amount instead of a full tank, which suits a short day trip.

## If the low-fuel light comes on far from a station

1. **Reduce speed and throttle.** Smooth, moderate riding saves fuel.
2. **Do not coast with the engine off** to save fuel. It makes the scooter harder to control.
3. **Look at the map** for the nearest station and a person who can help.
4. **If the scooter stops,** pull off, switch off the ignition and ring the shop on 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969), open 09:00 to 21:00 daily, with your location. ## FAQ: fuel stops on a scooter day trip

### How often should I refuel on a scooter day trip?

A good rule is at about half a tank, and before any long empty stretch. Small tanks mean that two stops in a long day are normal.

### How far can an automatic scooter go on one tank?

Honda's test figures suggest roughly 200 to 260 km for the Air Blade 160 and the Vision. Real use is lower. Plan stops well before the theoretical limit.

### Do I have to return the scooter with a full tank?

No. The shop expects the bike back with about 0.5 to 1 litre, and an empty tank at return costs VND 20,000.

### Can I use E10 petrol?

Honda Vietnam says its motorcycles can use fuel with up to 10% ethanol. Ask the shop if you are unsure about your particular bike.

### Is bottled roadside petrol safe?

It is a last resort. Use a proper station whenever you can.
