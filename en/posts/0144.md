Choose a scooter with space for a passenger helmet by testing it, not by reading a specification: take both helmets to the shop and see whether the passenger's helmet drops into the under-seat box of the exact scooter, front facing forward, with the seat closing and latching without force. Honda's manuals for the Vision, Lead and Air Blade all say a helmet can be stored in that box, but they also warn that some helmets are too large or the wrong shape to fit. An open-face or half-shell helmet is much more likely to fit than a full-face one. Whatever you choose, the helmet hook is for parked bikes only, never for riding. This guide explains the three storage places, what Honda says about each, how to run the fitting test, how to park safely with two helmets and what to do if they do not fit.

## Three places a scooter can keep a helmet

Most automatic scooters give you three options, and each has a rule.

**The under-seat box.** This is the main storage space and the only one big enough for most helmets. Honda's manuals for the Vision, Lead and Air Blade say the helmet can be stored inside with its front toward the front of the scooter, and that some helmets will not fit because of their size or design. The same box carries a load limit of 10 kg.

**The front inner compartment.** Honda describes this compartment, on the Vision and Lead, as a place for small items and limits what hangs on the hook or sits in it to 1.5 kg. It is meant for small items such as a phone, a charging cable or sunglasses, not for helmets. The current Vision also has a USB charging port there, according to Honda Vietnam's product page.

**The helmet hook.** Honda places the helmet hook under the seat on these models, which means you lift the seat to use it. The manuals say to use it only when the scooter is parked and give a firm warning: hanging a helmet from the hook while riding can interfere with safe operation and lead to a crash. If the helmet is on the hook, it comes off before you start the engine.

## Why the fitting test beats the spec sheet

A box can have a large nominal volume and still fail the helmet test, because what matters is its shape. Helmets are round and rigid, and a box with a narrow section or an intrusive frame member may hold fewer helmets than its volume suggests. Honda's own warning that some helmets will not fit says as much. Other things also compete for room:

- **The tool kit.** The Vision manual says the tool kit lives in the box.
- **Documents.** The Lead manual suggests keeping the manual and registration papers in a plastic pouch inside the box, and warns that water can enter the box and damage them.
- **Your own bag, a rain layer or a bottle of water.** All of these reduce the room.
- **The smart key.** Honda tells riders not to keep the key fob in the box, because anyone could ride away if the ignition is left on, and also not to put a phone or radio device there, because it can disturb the smart key system.

So ask for a bike where the box is nearly empty at handover, or have the shop remove the tool kit and keep it somewhere you can see.

## The helmet itself

A helmet that fits the box is no use if it fits the passenger badly. Honda's riding guide describes three types, half-shell, three-quarter and full-face, and suggests checking the shell, the foam lining, the strap and buckle, and the quality sticker. It also describes a three-step fit test: put the helmet on and turn the head to be sure it fits, fasten the buckle and check that two fingers fit between the chin and the strap. It adds that a helmet that has been cracked or hit hard should not be used.

On our side, the shop's published terms include one basic helmet per rider, free. Ask how that applies to your passenger, what sizes are available and whether it is the same type of helmet as the rider's. If you want a better helmet, bring your own. A practical point on size: a full-face helmet is the hardest to store. If either of you owns one, test it in the box first. A basic half-shell helmet from the shop is usually the easiest.

## A five-minute fitting test at the shop

Bring both helmets, or ask for the ones the shop will supply.

- **Open the seat** with the shop's help and look at the box with the tool kit and any contents removed.
- **Place the passenger's helmet** front forward, as Honda describes. Try it in both orientations in case it fits better one way.
- **Add the rider's helmet,** if you want to stow both. Many scooters take one helmet comfortably and two only if they are small.
- **Close the seat** without pushing. Honda says to press the rear of the seat down until it latches and to check that it is locked by lifting it gently. A seat that will not latch is not an option.
- **Check what else you need to store,** such as a rain layer or a small bag, and see whether the helmet still fits.
- **Remove everything** and check that the helmet did not mark, jam or catch on anything.
- **Compare two scooters** if the shop has them. The comparison tells you more than any specification.

## Parking with two helmets

The shop does not provide an extra lock, so the safest habit is to keep helmets in the box, lock the steering, and park in guarded places. Our guide to [parking a motorbike in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-motorbike-in-hanoi/) explains how guarded lots work.

Honda adds a few cautions for the box. Honda says not to keep valuables or fragile items in the box, and not to store flammable things or anything that heat can damage. A helmet is fine, but a lighter or a phone is not. And do not leave the key in the box when you close the seat.

If the helmets will not fit, you have several options:

- **Carry them.** A passenger can hold a helmet while you walk. It is awkward but secure.
- **Use the hook while parked only,** and take it off before riding.
- **Switch to a half-shell helmet** if the shop offers one.
- **Ask for a different scooter** with a better box.
- **Leave them at your hotel** if you are walking around a market or a museum.

## While you ride

Both of you wear the helmets, fastened. Never carry a helmet on the handlebar, on a mirror or on the hook while riding; Honda warns that it can interfere with control. Keep the load within the 10 kg limit for the box, and remember that Honda's manuals allow a rider and one passenger. A passenger should keep both feet on the footrests and lean with the rider in turns. ## FAQ: passenger helmets on a scooter

### Does a helmet fit under the seat of every scooter?

No. Honda's manuals say some helmets will not fit because of their size or design, so test the exact scooter.

### Can I hang the passenger's helmet from the hook while riding?

No. Honda says the hook is for parked scooters only and warns that a hanging helmet can interfere with safe operation.

### Which helmet type is easiest to store?

A half-shell helmet is usually the easiest and a full-face the hardest, but the box shape decides. Try it.

### Is the passenger's helmet included in the rental?

The shop's published terms say one basic helmet per rider is free. Ask how that applies to a passenger and what sizes are available.

### Can I keep my phone or key in the box?

Honda advises against keeping the smart key fob or a phone in the box, and against valuables generally.

### What if both helmets will not fit?

Stow one, carry the other, use the hook only while parked, or ask for a scooter with a roomier box.
