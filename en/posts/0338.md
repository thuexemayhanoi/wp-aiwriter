On a large cultural day in Hanoi, do not plan to ride into the middle of the action. The safest approach is to treat the centre as a place you reach on foot: park the scooter at a guarded spot on the edge of the crowd, walk the last stretch and leave before the crowd does. Closures in central Hanoi are announced by the traffic police shortly before big state events, the weekend walking street around Hoan Kiem Lake shuts out motor vehicles every week, and an officer's hand signal outranks any sign or traffic light. This guide explains which events change the roads, what the published closures looked like, what is still uncertain about petrol-bike limits in the Old Quarter, and a simple plan for the day.

## Which events actually change the roads

Not every festival matters to a rider. Three kinds do.

**Weekly events.** The pedestrian zone around Hoan Kiem Lake and the night market that runs into the Old Quarter operate every weekend, so they are the closure a visitor is most likely to meet.

**Holiday peaks.** Lunar New Year, the National Day holiday around 2 September and the days before Mid-Autumn Festival put far more people on the same streets. Hang Ma and Luong Van Can, the streets known for Mid-Autumn lanterns and toys, are described by a travel operator as always crowded with locals and visitors at that time.

**State ceremonies and anniversaries.** For these, the city police close or restrict whole blocks, sometimes for a full day, and the restrictions can cover private motorbikes.

Temple and village festivals are different. They rarely close city roads, but they crowd narrow lanes in the countryside. Co Loa, for instance, holds its festival on the sixth day of the first lunar month, according to VietnamPlus, so the roads near the site fill up that day.

## The weekend walking street

The pedestrian zone around Hoan Kiem Lake opened in September 2016 after a two-year trial, and Vietnam's government news site reported that it runs from 7 p.m. on Friday to midnight on Sunday. It covers streets such as Trang Tien and Dinh Le and the lake itself, and the night market extends through Hang Duong, Hang Ngang, Hang Dao, Hang Buom, Ma May and Hang Giay toward Dong Xuan Market.

Published hours differ slightly between sources. One 2026 guide lists Friday from 7 p.m. to midnight, Saturday from 6 p.m. to midnight and Sunday from 6 p.m. to 11 p.m., so treat any single timetable as a guide and look for signs and barriers on the day.

Two facts help a rider plan:

- VnExpress reported in 2023 that the walking street draws an average of 25,000 to 35,000 people each weekend, rising to as many as 50,000 around Lunar New Year.
- In 2023 the Hoan Kiem authorities extended the pedestrian zones to four days for the National Day holiday, according to the government news site. Holiday extensions can happen again, so do not assume that the normal weekend timetable applies in a holiday week.

Parking is the real problem, not the closure. Our guide to [where to park a scooter near Hoan Kiem Lake](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/where-to-park-a-scooter-near-hoan-kiem-lake/) goes through it, and the wider rules for [guarded lots, tickets and towing](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-motorbike-in-hanoi/) apply anywhere in the city. Ask the attendant for the price before you leave the bike, because we cannot confirm a current rate for any particular lot.

## What a state-event closure looks like

For the 80th National Day in 2025, VnExpress reported that Hanoi police closed or restricted a long list of streets around Ba Dinh Square and the West Lake and in the Hoan Kiem area. Closures ran for rehearsals as well as for the day itself: for the celebration on 2 September, streets were closed from 6 p.m. on 1 September until 3 p.m. on 2 September. Inside Ring Road 1, which takes in the Old Quarter, Sword Lake and West Lake, traffic was limited to authorised vehicles during the closure windows, and in outer areas some roads restricted private vehicles and motorcycles. People were advised to walk or use public transport around Ba Dinh Square and Sword Lake.

A smaller example came in July 2026. VietnamNet reported that Hanoi traffic police announced restrictions on central streets from 9 to 12 July for events marking the 80th anniversary of the People's Public Security Force. Dinh Tien Hoang Street was fully closed on the evening of 9 July, and private cars and motorcycles faced restrictions on a long list of central streets, including Hang Gai, Hang Dao, Hang Ngang and Hang Khay. The traffic police asked road users to follow the officers' directions.

The pattern is worth remembering:

1. The city announces closures days or even weeks ahead, often with a street list and times.
2. Rehearsal days can close the same streets as the main day.
3. Restrictions can extend beyond cars and buses to private motorbikes.
4. The closure window starts well before the event and ends after it.

These are examples, not a calendar. Each year's schedule is different, so look up the current notice when a national event falls during your stay.

## Lunar New Year: crowds before, quiet during

Tet works differently from the other peaks. In the days before it, the city is at its most congested. VnExpress reported in February 2026 that, one week before Lunar New Year, key routes such as Ring Road 3 were gridlocked, and traffic police were stationed at main junctions all day rather than only at peak hours. Motorbikes laden with offerings and plants for the holiday added to the crush.

On Tet itself the centre can feel nearly empty, but a quiet street is not a safe one. A travel guide on Vietnam Tourism describes emptier roads encouraging faster riding and warns about drinking on Tet eve and the first day of the year, but it does not give a source for its accident figures, so treat that as general caution rather than a statistic. Our guide to [drink-driving rules for riders](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/drink-driving-rules-motorbike-vietnam/) explains why the sensible rule is not to ride after drinking at all.

Shops, museums and some attractions close for several days at Tet. If you plan to rent over the holiday, ask the shop about opening days and returns well in advance. See our [prices page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/prices/) and [rental guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-guide/) for the standard terms.

## The petrol-bike question in the Old Quarter

Rental scooters in Hanoi are petrol bikes, so a proposed restriction on petrol motorbikes in the Old Quarter matters to renters. As of October 2026, here is what we could verify, and what we could not.

In November 2025 the Hanoi People's Council approved a resolution on low-emission zones inside Ring Road 1, with pilots from 1 July 2026, VnExpress reported. In April 2026, reports described a draft for a first phase in Hoan Kiem Ward on 11 streets, including Trang Tien, Hang Khay, Le Thai To, Hang Dao, Hang Ngang, Hang Buom, Ma May, Hang Bac, Hang Mam, Nguyen Huu Huan and Ly Thai To, with petrol motorbikes barred from 6 p.m. on Fridays and from 6 a.m. on Saturdays and Sundays.

The draft then changed. In May 2026 a city official told Tuoi Tre that personal petrol motorbikes would not yet be banned from the zone during the first phase, and VietnamNet reported on 31 May that the outright ban had been dropped in favour of a phased approach, with a proposed evening restriction on all road vehicles in the core streets on weekends. We could not find a final notice confirming exactly which rule is in force today.

The practical advice is simple. If your plan includes the streets named above on a Friday evening or at the weekend, read the signs at the entrance to the street, ask your hotel or the shop, and be ready to park outside and walk in. Never push into a street that has a barrier or a sign you do not understand.

## The rule that overrides everything else

Vietnam's Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (Law 36/2024/QH15, in force since 1 January 2025) puts road signals in an order of priority. Article 11 lists them from top to bottom: first the signals of the person directing traffic, then traffic lights, then road signs, then road markings and the other devices. In practice, a police officer or a traffic marshal waving you off a street outranks the green light above you.

On event days that has three consequences:

- Do not follow a navigation app through a barrier. The app does not know about a closure that was announced yesterday.
- Stop when directed, and turn around when told to.
- Do not try to slip past a closure by using the pavement. Our guide to [crossing Hanoi intersections](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/crossing-hanoi-intersections/) describes how the city's traffic flows normally; event days break that flow.

This is general information, not legal advice, and we are not listing penalties, because none can be tied to a specific closure here.

## A simple plan for event days

| When | What to do |
|---|---|
| The day before | Search for the traffic police notice for your date. Note the street list and the start and end times. |
| Morning | Fill the tank, check the lights and brakes, and photograph the bike. Our [10-minute inspection checklist](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-scooter-inspection-checklist/) covers it. |
| Before the closure window | Park at a guarded spot well outside the barriers, not the closest one. |
| During the event | Walk. Keep the key and parking ticket on you and photograph where you left the bike. |
| Leaving | Wait for the crowd to thin, or walk away from the exit streets before collecting the bike. |
| Riding home | Take the long way around closed blocks, and avoid riding in the dark if you are tired. See our guide to [riding at night](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-at-night-in-hanoi/). |

If it rains, vendors and crowds change, and streets that flood become problems of their own. Our guide to [riding in Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) lists them.

## Better choices on the biggest days

Some days are simply not worth riding in the centre. On the evening of a national celebration or in the hours before Tet eve, consider these instead:

- Do a different ride that day. A quiet route out of the centre, such as the [Long Bien and Red River loop](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/long-bien-red-river-first-ride/), keeps you away from the closures.
- Visit the centre in the morning, when the closures have not started, and leave by lunchtime.
- Walk or take a taxi into the closure area, and keep the scooter parked.
- Use our [Old Quarter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/old-quarter/) and [Hoan Kiem](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hoan-kiem/) area pages to plan a walking day.

The Old Quarter is hard enough to ride on an ordinary day. Our guide to [riding in the Old Quarter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-old-quarter/) explains when to walk instead, and an event day is nearly always one of them.

## FAQ: riding during Hanoi cultural events

### Is the walking street around Hoan Kiem Lake open every day?

No. It runs on weekends, from Friday evening until Sunday night, and on weekdays traffic around the lake runs as normal. Holidays can extend it.

### Can I ride a scooter on the walking street?

Motor vehicles are kept out of the pedestrian zone while it is open. Park outside it and walk in.

### Will the roads be closed for National Day?

In recent years, yes, for the central streets around Ba Dinh Square and parts of the Old Quarter. Check the police notice for the current year.

### Does a police officer's signal really beat a green light?

Under Article 11 of Law 36/2024/QH15, the signals of the person directing traffic come before traffic lights and signs.

### Are petrol scooters banned in the Old Quarter at weekends?

Drafts proposed time-based limits for 11 streets from July 2026, but they were revised. we could not confirm the rule in force, so read the signs and check on the day.

### Should I rent a scooter for Tet?

Ask the shop about opening days and returns first. Roads before Tet are congested, and the shop's hours are on the [contact page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/contact/).
