A week on a rental electric scooter is manageable if you treat charging as a daily routine rather than a daily emergency. Plan to charge once a day, in the afternoon or early evening when you are present, top up rather than fill when your rides are short, keep one lighter day for a longer charge and agree the arrangements with the hotel and the shop before day one. Seven days means seven chances for the battery to run low at the wrong moment, and seven evenings when you will want to leave it plugged in and go out. This guide turns that into a plan: what to settle first, a day-by-day rhythm, how to handle a hotel change, bad weather and a missing charger, and what to check at return.

## Why a week is different from a day

A single-day rental asks one thing of the battery: get through the day. A week asks something harder of you, because the charging happens repeatedly and the habits you form on day one either hold or fray by day five.

Three things change over a week.

- **Charging becomes routine, and routine breeds shortcuts.** On the first night you are careful. By the fifth, leaving it charging while you sleep seems harmless. Fire services say it is not. London Fire Brigade advises never leaving a battery charging unattended or while you are asleep, and the Gia Lai provincial fire-prevention department says to stop charging before going to bed or leaving, and not to charge overnight.
- **Charging time stacks against your schedule.** If a full charge takes many hours, you cannot fit one around every evening out.
- **The battery's reserve matters more.** Daily use, wet weather and hills all draw on it, and there is more chance one day will go badly.

The weekly pattern also differs from a short rental in how the shop treats it. Weekly and monthly packages at Nguyen Ha are priced on their own, not as a day rate multiplied by seven. ## Settle these before day one

Most charging problems in a week come from things nobody asked on the first day. Settle them while the shop is open and you are standing next to the bike.

**With the shop (open 09:00 to 21:00 daily, 0334 699 969 / +84 334 699 969):**

1. Which model is it, and is the battery removable?
2. Which charger belongs to it, and is it in the bike or with you?
3. How long does a full charge take, and what is a normal top-up?
4. What is the battery level now? Photograph the indicator.
5. What does the shop expect at return?
6. What do I do if the battery runs low or the bike will not charge?
7. Can I bring it back mid-week for a check, and when?

Nguyen Ha confirms the battery level and the charging arrangement for electric bikes at handover, so these are normal questions.

**With the hotel:** where would the charging happen, who is present, and does the building allow it? If you stay in more than one place during the week, ask each. Note that the Gia Lai notice also advises charging in a dry, airy place and not in a closed room.

If either answer is "no", change the plan before you leave the shop, not after.

## A rhythm that fits the fire-safety advice

The tension in a week is simple. A slow charge can take many hours, and you are advised not to charge overnight. A 2023 ICCT study of VinFast scooters reported full-charge times of about 4.8 to 12 hours on a household supply. That study covers VinFast's own models at that time, not your rental, but the scale is clear: a full charge from low may need most of an evening.

The Gia Lai notice also recommends against charging continuously for more than about eight hours and suggests waiting about 20 minutes after use before charging. That is advice for electric motorbikes and e-bikes in general, not for your specific bike, so ask the shop how it applies.

A workable pattern is:

1. **Come back mid-afternoon or early evening.** Park, switch off.
2. **Let the battery cool for about 20 minutes.** Have a drink, check your photos, sort your bag.
3. **Plug in at a safe, dry, agreed place,** with someone present.
4. **Check on it** every so often while you are around.
5. **Unplug before you go out for dinner or to bed.** Whatever level it reached is your day's charge.
6. **Do the next top-up the following afternoon.**

This means you rarely reach 100 per cent in one session, which is fine if your daily distance is modest. If you ride a long way, you will need a different plan, such as a rest day.

## A seven-day sketch

This is a template, not a timetable. It assumes short-to-moderate daily rides and a hotel that allows attended charging.

| Day | Typical plan | Charging note |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Collect the bike, photograph the indicator, ride a short loop | Charge in the evening if the level is not high, attended |
| 2 | A moderate city day | Top-up in the afternoon |
| 3 | A longer ride | Check the level before setting out and turn back at about half |
| 4 | Light day, short hops | A longer attended charge in the afternoon |
| 5 | Moderate day | Top-up |
| 6 | A longer ride | Plan the charge before leaving, not after |
| 7 | Short ride and return | Do not leave charging to the last night. Check the level against the handover photo |

Adjust it to your trip. The point is that the longer days are followed by an attended charge, and a lighter day sits in the middle so that the battery is not constantly running down.

## Plan for the longer rides

Your range is not a fixed number. It falls with a passenger, luggage, hills and hard acceleration. Our guide to [renting an electric scooter in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/renting-an-electric-scooter-in-hanoi/) discusses range, and our guide to [taking a rental motorbike out of Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/take-rental-motorbike-out-of-hanoi/) covers what to ask before you leave the city. For an electric bike, the second is especially relevant, because charging away from the shop is harder.

For a longer day:

- **Start with the highest charge you can get,** within the fire-safety guidance, from the previous afternoon.
- **Plan a turnaround point** at about half the battery.
- **Know where you can stop** if the level drops, and have the shop's number ready.
- **Do not count on a public charger or a swap cabinet.** Public charging exists, and VinFast describes the use of its charging posts through an app, but a renter on a shop's bike cannot assume access. Swap cabinets are usually built around subscribers. Ask the shop.
- **Check the weather.** Rain affects both the ride and the evening charge.

Our guide to [riding at night in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-at-night-in-hanoi/) explains why you want to be home before dark with charge to spare.

## Wet days in the week

Hanoi's rainy season runs roughly from May to October, according to Vietnam Tourism, and October rainfall averages about 128 mm. A week in autumn will often include at least one wet day. Plan for it.

- **Dry the bike, the port and the plug** after a wet ride before you plug in.
- **Wait for the battery to cool** as usual.
- **Delay the charge** rather than charge in a damp place. A lower battery tomorrow morning is better than a wet port tonight.
- **Do not ride through deep water.** London Fire Brigade advises avoiding flooded roads and says a submerged battery should not be used.

If a storm is forecast, put the bike in a guarded place that will not flood and do not leave a battery on a basement floor. Our guide to [riding in Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) lists the streets that flood.

## Changing hotel mid-week

If you move, the charging arrangement moves with you. Before you check out:

1. **Ask the next hotel** where and when you can charge, as soon as you know you are going.
2. **Charge the day before the move** if you can, so that you are not hunting for a socket in the middle of a transfer.
3. **Do not carry a removable battery on a crowded bus or in a taxi** without asking the shop. Packs are heavy, and Honda says a pack of its own Mobile Power Pack e: weighs about 10 kg. That is Honda's battery and not the rental bike's, but it shows what to expect.
4. **Tell the shop** if the bike's location or your plan has changed.

## If something goes wrong in the week

- **The charger stops working.** Do not substitute another. Stop, unplug and call the shop.
- **The charger or plug is damaged.** Stop charging. Gia Lai's advice is to replace a swollen or cracked battery, and fire services advise replacing damaged chargers with proper ones.
- **The battery is much lower than expected after a night.** Do not assume it was a bad charge. Note the level, check the plug and the socket, and tell the shop.
- **The bike will not power on.** Do not force it. Photograph the display and call.
- **The charger is lost.** Tell the shop at once. Do not buy a substitute.
- **The bike is damaged in a fall.** Do not charge a battery that has been dropped or damaged. London Fire Brigade advises not to use one that has been dropped or damaged.

## Storage and security through the week

Where the bike sleeps matters as much as where it charges. Park it in a guarded place, lock the steering and do not leave the charger on view. Nguyen Ha does not provide an extra lock, so lock the steering and park where someone is on duty. If the battery is removable and the shop and hotel agree, you may bring it inside, but only if it will not be charged overnight and not in a corridor or stairwell.

## At return

On the last day, do not leave the charge to chance.

1. **Compare the indicator with your handover photo.** The figure agreed at handover is the reference.
2. **Charge the day before** if the shop expects a particular level. Ask what it expects.
3. **Bring the charger** and any parts you were given.
4. **Report anything odd** such as a flaky display, a loose plug or a slow charge.
5. **Return in opening hours,** 09:00 to 21:00.

## A one-page checklist

**Day one:** charger, battery level photo, shop's questions answered, hotel's answer saved.
**Each afternoon:** park, cool for about 20 minutes, plug in at the agreed place, stay nearby.
**Each night:** unplug before sleeping or going out, park in a guarded place.
**Wet days:** dry first, delay if damp.
**Long days:** full charge the evening before, turn back at about half.
**Last day:** compare level, bring the charger, return in hours.

## FAQ: a week with a rental electric scooter

### How often should I charge during a week?

Usually once a day, in the afternoon or early evening, while you are present. How much depends on your daily distance. Ask the shop for the normal top-up for your model.

### Can I leave it charging overnight to save time?

Fire-safety advice from both London Fire Brigade and the Gia Lai provincial fire-prevention department says not to. Charge while you are awake and nearby, and unplug before you sleep.

### Will one charge last a week?

No, not unless you barely ride. Plan on daily or near-daily charging. Range depends on the bike, load and conditions.

### What if my hotel will not let me charge?

Choose another place before you collect the bike, or ask the shop about a petrol bike. Do not charge in a spot you have been told not to use.

### What does a week cost?

Weekly electric rental is priced on its own, and the range is on the prices page. The final price is confirmed on the actual bike. Ask whether electricity or any other charging cost applies.

### Should I take the battery in each night?

Only if the shop says it is removable and the hotel agrees. Do not carry a heavy pack through corridors, and do not charge it overnight.

### What if I need to extend the rental?

Ask the shop as early as you can, before the week ends. Availability is not promised, so do not assume the same bike will be free.
