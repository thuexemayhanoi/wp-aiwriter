To use offline maps on a countryside ride near Hanoi, download the whole area you might ride through while you are on Wi-Fi, check that the route sits entirely inside it, then test it in airplane mode before you leave. Google Maps can do this: its help page says offline maps can guide you to your destination as long as the entire route is within the downloaded area, but offline you get driving directions only, with no traffic information and no alternative routes. A free app such as Organic Maps works without any connection, including search and navigation. The catch for a rider is that these apps route mostly for cars, walkers or cyclists, so you must still check the road type for a motorbike and use common sense where a lane looks too narrow. This guide shows how to choose the tool, size the download, set it up, test it and mount the phone safely.

## Why bother with offline maps on a short ride

Hanoi's edges are close to the centre but they do not behave like the city. On a ride along a river dyke, through craft villages or across rice fields, a phone signal can weaken, a data plan can run out and a map that depends on a live connection can freeze at the junction where you most need it. Offline maps remove that dependency. They also save mobile data and, in some apps, battery, because the phone is not constantly fetching map tiles.

There is a second reason that has nothing to do with signal. A map that is already stored on the phone loads instantly, so you spend less time looking at the screen and more time looking at the road.

Sources for the table are Google's help page, the Organic Maps site and Google Play listing, and the OsmAnd documentation, listed below. The free-tier cap for OsmAnd comes from a third-party review of offline motorcycle apps and may change, so check the app. Google also warns that you cannot download offline maps in some countries or regions for contractual or other reasons, so confirm in the app that the Hanoi area downloads before the day.

None of the three lists a dedicated motorbike profile in the offline help text we read. Google Maps does have a two-wheeler travel mode in Vietnam according to travel sites, but Google's help page says offline directions cover driving only, so I could not confirm that the two-wheeler mode works offline. Test it yourself, as described below.

For most riders the sensible set-up is two apps: Google Maps as the familiar everyday map, plus Organic Maps as a free offline backup that needs no signal at all.

## How to download an area in Google Maps

The steps below follow Google's help page and its 2023 product post.

1. **Connect to Wi-Fi** and sign in to Google Maps.

2. **Search for your destination** or the area, for example a village name, then open its place sheet and choose **More** and **Download offline map**. Or tap your profile picture, choose **Offline maps** and then **Select your own map**.

3. **Move and resize the rectangle** so it covers the entire route, the roads around it and some margin, then tap **Download**. The app shows the file size before you commit.

4. **Turn on auto-update** in the offline map settings. Google says offline maps must be updated before they expire, and that it tries to refresh them over Wi-Fi when they have 15 days or less left.

5. **Check the list** under Offline maps to confirm the area is saved.

When you ride, the app behaves as it normally does, with one important condition: Google says the offline map can guide you only if the entire route is within the downloaded area. A route that leaves the rectangle will fail halfway.

## How to set up Organic Maps

Organic Maps describes itself as an offline maps and GPS app with no ads, tracking or registration, powered by OpenStreetMap data. Its site lists turn-by-turn walking, cycling and car navigation with voice guidance, and fast offline search on downloaded maps. The Google Play listing says it offers unlimited map downloads and updates its data about every two weeks.

1. Install it from your phone's app store while on Wi-Fi.
2. Open the map download list and download the map for Vietnam, or the region that covers your route. Maps are downloaded by country or region, so one download covers every road inside it.
3. Switch to airplane mode and search for a village, a temple or a petrol station to check that offline search works.
4. Plan a route to a test destination to see how it handles the roads you intend to ride.

If you prefer OsmAnd, its documentation says you must download vector maps for each region first and that there is no single option to download every region at once.

## Size the download so it never runs out

Think of the download as the fence around your day.

- **Cover the whole loop,** not only the destination. A common mistake is to download the village but not the road home.
- **Add margin on every side.** Detours, closed roads and wrong turns take you off the planned line, and the margin keeps the map under you.
- **Include the shop and the main roads home.** If you get confused, the easiest fix is to navigate to a familiar landmark.
- **Keep an eye on storage.** A wide rectangle is a large file. Do it on Wi-Fi and check free space.
- **For a day trip, one rectangle is enough.** For an overnight ride, download each area you will cross.

## The motorbike problem: check the road, not just the line

An offline app draws a line, but it does not know that you are on a small motorbike. Check four things before you trust it.

1. **Expressways and ring roads.** Some fast roads around Hanoi do not allow motorbikes. Read [which ring roads and highways allow motorbikes](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-ring-roads-highways-motorbikes-allowed/) and [how to check if a navigation app suggests an expressway](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-check-if-a-navigation-app-suggests-an-expressway/) before you start, because an app built for cars can route you onto one.

2. **Lane width and surface.** A thin line on a map can be a narrow lane, a farm track or a dyke top. If the surface turns rough and you are on a small bike, turn back to the main road.

3. **Closures.** An offline map does not know about repairs. In spring 2026 Long Bien Bridge was closed to motorbikes and pedestrians from 28 March to 27 May for repairs, and any app working from stored data would not have known. Check the news before a ride that depends on a bridge.

4. **Signs beat the screen.** Weight-limit, no-entry and flood signs on dyke roads and village routes win over any map.

## The airplane-mode test

Do this at home the evening before.

1. Turn on airplane mode, and turn Wi-Fi off too.
2. Open the app and search for your first destination.
3. Start navigation from the shop or your hotel, and watch whether the route appears and updates.
4. Zoom into the village stretches and check the road names and landmarks.
5. Open the saved places and confirm they are all there.

If anything fails, you have time to download again. Do not rely on a map you have not tested.

## Phone, battery and mount

- **Use a mount, not your hand.** Article 9 of Law No. 36/2024/QH15, in force since 1 January 2025, bans holding and using a phone or other electronic device while driving a moving vehicle. As of October 2026 the full text is on the Government policy portal. A handlebar mount or a pocket with audio directions keeps your hands on the grips.

- **Charge fully and carry a power bank.** Screens and navigation draw power, and a dead phone is a lost map.

- **Lower the brightness** and let the screen sleep when you are on a long straight stretch, if the app allows it.

- **Protect against rain.** A water-resistant pouch helps, but do not cover the ventilation or the front sensors, and do not look at the screen at junctions.

- **Save pins in advance:** the shop, your destination, one or two petrol stations and a lit cafe you could use as a safe stop. ## What offline maps cannot do

- **Show live traffic.** Google says offline you cannot get traffic information, so arrival estimates ignore congestion.
- **Offer alternative routes.** If a road is blocked, you decide the detour from the map.
- **Warn about new roads or closures.** The data is only as fresh as your last update.
- **Replace local judgement.** Ask a shopkeeper if a lane is passable. In rural areas locals are the best source.
- **Work for every route type.** In Google Maps offline you will not get cycling or walking directions, so use your own eyes for footpaths.

## FAQ: offline maps near Hanoi

### Does Google Maps work offline in Vietnam?

Google's help page says you cannot download offline maps in some countries or regions, so check in the app that the Hanoi area downloads. Where it works, offline directions are driving only.

### Is there a free offline map app that needs no signal?

Organic Maps is free, with no ads or registration, and says it supports its features without an internet connection. It uses OpenStreetMap data.

### How big an area should I download?

Cover the whole loop plus a generous margin on every side, including the road back to the shop.

### Will an offline map avoid roads closed to motorbikes?

Not reliably. Check the route type yourself and read signs on the road.

### Can I use my phone in my hand while riding?

No. The 2024 law bans holding and using a phone while driving a moving vehicle, so use a mount and audio directions.
