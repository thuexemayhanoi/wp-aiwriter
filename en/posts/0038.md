Charging a rental electric scooter in Hanoi is mostly a planning job, not a technical one. Most rental electric scooters charge from an ordinary wall socket and need many hours to fill, so the useful questions are where you will plug in, who will be around while it charges and how to fit the charge around your day. Battery-swap cabinets are spreading across the city, but the operator's own rules limit who may use them, and a renter should not assume they are available. This guide explains the realistic options, the fire-safety advice issued by Hanoi's fire-prevention unit in September 2026, and a simple routine that keeps a rental scooter ready without surprises. For range, riding feel and rental prices, see our companion article on [renting an electric scooter in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/renting-an-electric-scooter-in-hanoi/).

## The short answer

- **Plug-in charging is the default.** Ask the shop which charger comes with the bike and whether the battery can be taken off the scooter.
- **Swap cabinets are for the bike's owner.** V-Green's published conditions require the person swapping to be the owner with a valid battery-rental contract, which is not a renter's position.
- **Public chargers exist, but treat them as a backup.** Do not plan a day around finding one.
- **Charge when someone can watch.** Hanoi's fire-prevention unit advises against charging overnight or with nobody at home to monitor it.
- **Confirm the battery level at handover and return.** Nguyen Ha confirms the battery level and the charging arrangement for every electric model when you pick it up.

## Two ways to power an electric scooter

Electric two-wheelers reach a full battery in one of two ways. The first is a plug-in charger, which you connect to a wall socket, either by charging the whole scooter or by removing the battery and charging it indoors. The second is a battery swap, in which you take a depleted battery to a cabinet and exchange it for a charged one. Which system your rental uses depends on the model.

Nguyen Ha's electric range includes electric bicycles, 50cc-equivalent electric scooters and some VinFast electric motorbikes, and the bike you receive is confirmed when you book. Ask the shop which system it uses before you plan anything else, because it decides what the rest of this article means for you.

## Plug-in charging at your hotel

For a plug-in scooter, the hotel is the most practical charging point. Ask these questions when you book the room, not when you arrive with a flat battery:

- **Is there a socket near where the scooter will be parked?** A lobby, courtyard or parking-area socket is common. A room socket may be allowed if the battery is removable.
- **Is the area dry and ventilated?** Fire-safety advice names dry, airy places away from heat and combustible materials.
- **Who is there during charging?** A reception desk or a staffed lobby is better than an empty corridor.
- **Can you charge in the afternoon or early evening?** The timing question below explains why.
- **Does the hotel have its own rule?** Some buildings restrict charging indoors. Follow the building's rule, not a general tip.

Use only the charger supplied with the scooter. Keep the cable off beds, curtains and soft furnishings, do not run it across a walkway and do not use an extension reel or a multi-plug adapter you do not trust. If the hotel does not permit charging, tell the shop before you leave with the bike, and agree an alternative.

## What Hanoi's fire-prevention unit advises

In early September 2026, a regional newspaper reported advice on electric-vehicle charging from the fire-prevention and rescue unit of the Hanoi city police, published on 5 September. The practices it advises against include:

- charging overnight, or when nobody is at home to monitor it;
- charging straight after a ride, while the battery and parts may still be hot;
- using a charger of the wrong type, of unknown origin, or one that has been modified;
- modifying the vehicle's electrical system or fitting incompatible devices;
- charging in a cramped area or with flammable goods around the vehicle.

Its recommendations include stopping the charge before leaving the home or going to sleep, checking regularly while charging, letting a warm battery cool before charging, using the charger and power supply specified by the manufacturer, and keeping the charging area dry, ventilated and clear of heat and flammable material. It also says to stop charging and check the vehicle if the battery, charger or cable shows anything abnormal, such as excessive heat, swelling, cracks, deformation or an odd smell.

Two practical rules follow for a renter. Do not leave the scooter charging while you sleep or go out, and do not improvise with another charger if the supplied one fails. Call the shop instead.

## Timing: building a charging window around your day

Slow plug-in charging takes many hours. VinFast's April 2022 launch coverage gave about ten hours for a full charge of the Evo200 on its bundled 400 W charger, and about six hours for four other 2022 models on a 1,000 W charger. Treat those as the scale of the problem, since your rental may differ. A scooter with a smaller battery charges faster, and an electric bicycle usually takes a few hours.

That raises an obvious tension with the fire-safety advice. If a full charge takes eight to ten hours and you should not leave it running overnight, you need to plan. Some options:

1. **Charge in the daytime while you are in the hotel.** If you return in the early afternoon for a break, start the charge then, check it, and finish before you go out.
2. **Charge in top-ups.** If your daily distance is short, a few hours of attended charging is often enough to keep the battery above half.
3. **Schedule rest days.** On a trip of several days, plan a day with a short ride so that the scooter can charge while you are around.
4. **Ask the shop for a bike with more range** if your itinerary needs it. Our guide to [electric bikes in Hanoi: rules, speed limits and where you can ride](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/electric-bikes-in-hanoi-rules/) covers what the different types can do.

Do not decide this at 10 p.m. with a flat battery. Work out the day's distance in the morning, compare it with the shop's guidance on range and decide when the scooter will charge.

## Battery-swap cabinets: what they are and who can use them

A battery-swap cabinet is a roadside or building-mounted unit in which you insert a depleted battery and receive a charged one. V-Green, the charging company in VinFast's group, runs the network for VinFast's swappable electric motorbikes. Its website advertises a swap in about twenty seconds, and a guide on the same site says a practised user takes under a minute.

V-Green's published conditions say that:

- the bike must be one of the swappable models;
- the person swapping must be the bike's owner;
- the bike must have a valid battery-rental contract at the time;
- there must be no overdue charging or rental fees;
- the battery must be in good health, undamaged and able to accept a charge.

A swap also carries a small per-swap fee that V-Green publishes. The practical point for a visitor is the second condition: the process is designed around the owner of the bike and its contract. A person who rents a scooter from a shop is not the owner. If the shop's scooter is a swappable model, ask whether staff will do the swap, whether the shop has its own arrangement or whether you should return to the shop to exchange the battery. Do not scan a cabinet's code and expect it to work.

In June 2026, reports described an agreement between V-Green and the Hanoi electricity company EVNHANOI to roll out up to 10,000 battery-swap cabinets across the city's wards and communes, using the electricity company's existing sites. That is a plan, not a count of working units, and V-Green's own targets have moved over time. For a visitor, the sensible reading is that swap cabinets may be common soon, but your rental will tell you whether you can use one.

## Public charging stations

VinFast also operates public charging stations in places such as car parks, bus stations and along main roads, and publishes a per-kilowatt-hour price on its charging page. These are useful as a backup when your scooter can use them, but a visitor cannot count on finding one near a particular stop. Ask the shop whether your scooter can use public stations before you plan to. If it can, use the operator's app to check availability before you ride.

Do not rely on a public charger to rescue a nearly empty battery. A rental scooter with little charge left is a call to the shop, not a puzzle for you to solve with an unfamiliar socket.

## Range and the charge you leave with

Before you leave the shop, ask what range the scooter realistically gives with your weight, a passenger and Hanoi's stop-and-go traffic, and compare it with the day's route. Manufacturers' range figures come from standard test conditions and are higher than what riders usually see. Leave a margin of at least a third. Our guide to [riding at night in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-at-night-in-hanoi/) is a reminder that you do not want to run out of charge in the dark.

At Nguyen Ha, the battery level and charging arrangement are confirmed at handover for every electric model, in the same way that fuel levels are noted for petrol bikes. Take a photo of the battery indicator at pickup and at return, so both sides see the same figure.

## If the battery runs flat

If the scooter slows or the indicator drops faster than you expected, stop at a safe place and call the shop on 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969), open 09:00 to 21:00 daily. Describe where you are and what the indicator shows. Staff can advise whether to wait, push to a nearby café with a socket or arrange a pickup. Do not use another person's charger, and do not open the battery or the charging port. ## A simple routine

1. **At booking:** ask which charging system the scooter uses and whether your hotel allows charging.
2. **At pickup:** check the battery level, the charger and where it should be used, and photograph the indicator.
3. **Each afternoon:** decide whether tonight's plan needs a charge, and start it while you are present.
4. **Before you sleep or leave:** stop the charge, check the charger and cable, and park the scooter in a guarded place.
5. **At return:** leave the battery at the level agreed at pickup and photograph the indicator again.

## FAQ: charging an electric scooter in Hanoi

### Can I charge a rental electric scooter at my hotel?

Often yes, but ask first. Many hotels have a socket in the lobby, courtyard or parking area, and some allow a removable battery to be charged indoors. Follow the building's rule.

### How long does a rental electric scooter take to charge?

It depends on the model. VinFast's 2022 launch coverage gave about ten hours for a full charge of the Evo200 on its bundled charger and about six hours for four other models on a faster one. Smaller bikes charge faster.

### Can I use a battery-swap cabinet with a rental scooter?

V-Green's published conditions require the person swapping to be the bike's owner with a valid battery-rental contract, so a renter normally cannot. Ask the shop how swaps are handled.

### Is it safe to charge overnight?

Hanoi's fire-prevention unit advises against charging overnight or when nobody is there to monitor it. Plan your charging for times when you are present.

### Can I use a different charger if mine stops working?

No. The fire-prevention advice is to use the charger specified by the manufacturer. Call the shop if the supplied charger fails.

### What should I do if the battery is nearly empty and I am far from the shop?

Stop in a safe place and call the shop. Do not look for an unfamiliar socket or open the battery.
