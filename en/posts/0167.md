Pick the 50cc bike if you hold no motorcycle licence and your trip is short, slow and local; pick the 125cc-class scooter if you hold a licence that covers it and you want to keep up with traffic, carry a passenger or ride beyond the inner city. The choice is driven first by paperwork and then by speed. Under Vietnam's Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (Law 36/2024/QH15, effective 1 January 2025), a 50cc moped needs no motorcycle licence and may be ridden from age 16, but it is capped at 40 km/h on ordinary roads, while a scooter up to 125 cc needs a class A1 licence and rides at the normal limits. This guide compares the two on the points that decide a rental in Hanoi: who can ride them, how fast and how far they go, what they carry, what they cost at Nguyen Ha, and which one suits which trip, with the rules as of October 2026.

## Start with the licence, because it settles the choice for many riders

The law draws a hard line at 50 cm3. Article 34 defines a moped (xe gan may) as a two- or three-wheeler with a design speed of no more than 50 km/h and, for a petrol engine, a displacement of no more than 50 cm3, or for an electric motor, no more than 4 kW. Anything larger or faster is a motorcycle. Article 57 sets class A1 for two-wheeled motorcycles up to 125 cm3 or an electric motor up to 11 kW, and class A above those figures. Article 59 sets the minimum age at 16 for a moped and 18 for classes A1 and A.

So a rider with no motorcycle licence has only one legal motorbike option in Vietnam, the moped. A rider whose home licence and International Driving Permit do not cover motorcycles is in the same position. Our guide to [licence rules for foreigners](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbike-licence-rules-for-foreigners-in-vietnam/) explains which permits count, and the [50cc rental guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/renting-a-50cc-scooter-in-hanoi/) covers the moped rules in more detail.

If you do hold a licence that covers a 125cc scooter, the licence question no longer decides anything, and the comparison turns to speed, comfort and cost.

## What "125cc" really means for the licence

The number on a scooter's name is rounded. Honda Vietnam lists the Lead's displacement as 124.8 cm3, comfortably inside the class A1 ceiling of 125 cm3, while its Air Blade 160 is listed at 156.9 cm3, which falls into class A. That difference matters when you choose a scooter: a model named 160 is not a 125, and a rider with only class A1 cannot legally ride it. Our [Air Blade 160 versus 125 guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/honda-air-blade-160-vs-125/) goes through the two engines. Whichever scooter you are offered, ask the shop which engine size you are being given before you sign.

## Speed: the 40 km/h cap and what it means in Hanoi

Circular 38/2024/TT-BGTVT, effective 1 January 2025, sets the maximum operating speed for mopeds at 40 km/h on roads other than expressways. Other motor vehicles in built-up areas are held to 60 km/h on dual carriageways and roads with two or more lanes in one direction, and 50 km/h on two-way roads or single-lane one-way roads.

In central Hanoi that gap is smaller than it looks, because traffic on the Old Quarter streets and around the lake is usually slow anyway. It matters on the longer, faster roads such as the Red River dykes, the bridges and the approaches to the ring roads, where traffic can move well above 40 km/h and a moped is the slowest vehicle in the lane. Our guide to [Hanoi's ring roads and highways](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-ring-roads-highways-motorbikes-allowed/) explains where motorbikes are allowed at all.

## Range, fuel and the small tank

Honda's current figures show how scooters differ in fuel capacity: the Vision, a 110cc scooter, has a 4.8-litre tank and a stated consumption of 1.82 litres per 100 km, and the Lead 125 has a 6.0-litre tank and a stated consumption of 2.1 litres per 100 km. Honda's figures come from test conditions, so real use in Hanoi traffic will differ. A 50cc bike will generally have a small tank as well, and Nguyen Ha hands petrol bikes over with only about 0.5 to 2 litres, so for either bike the first task is a fill-up.

The more important point is time rather than distance. At 40 km/h or less, the same journey takes noticeably longer on a moped, which cuts the number of places you can reach in a day. Plan the day by hours, not kilometres.

## Carrying a passenger and luggage

A 125cc scooter is the better choice for two. It has more power, a longer seat, a larger box and a rear grab rail with a stated load limit. Honda Vietnam states that the Lead's under-seat space exceeds 37 litres and holds two large full-face helmets, a capacity the 50cc bikes do not match. A Cub-style 50cc moped has little built-in storage, so a backpack will ride on the rider's back or the rack.

Two adults can ride a 50cc legally with a helmet each, but the moped will be slow and the small engine will feel strained on a gradient or a bridge. Our guide to [two riders and luggage on a Lead or Vision](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/scooter-for-two-riders-and-luggage-hanoi/) lists the load limits for the larger scooters.

## What each costs at Nguyen Ha

Nguyen Ha's published figures for a 50cc bike are VND 200,000 per day, VND 1,000,000 per week and VND 2,000,000 per month. Automatic scooters are VND 150,000 to 200,000 per day, VND 600,000 to 1,000,000 per week and VND 1,200,000 to 2,000,000 per month. In other words, the 50cc bike is not the budget option. Its advantage is legal rather than financial, so do not choose it to save money.

## Common mistakes in choosing

- **Choosing a 50cc bike to save money.** It is not cheaper, and the speed cap limits what you can do with it.
- **Renting a 125cc without a licence that covers it.** Riding without the right licence is an offence, and most travel insurance policies will not pay for an accident in that case.
- **Assuming a bike named 125 is a moped.** Only a petrol engine of no more than 50 cm3 and a design speed of no more than 50 km/h qualifies.
- **Planning a day trip on a moped.** The 40 km/h cap and national-road traffic make it a long and tiring day.

## FAQ about choosing between a 50cc and a 125cc rental

### Can I ride a 50cc bike with no licence in Vietnam?

Yes, from age 16, under Law 36/2024/QH15 in force since 1 January 2025. Every other rule of the road still applies, including the helmet.

### Is a 125cc scooter much faster than a 50cc?

In practice yes. The moped is capped at 40 km/h, while a 125cc scooter can ride at the motorbike limits that apply to the road you are on.

### Can a 50cc carry two adults?

It is allowed with a helmet each, but it will be slow. A 125cc scooter is more comfortable for two.

### Which is better for a first-time rider?

If you have a licence, a small automatic scooter is usually the easier bike to ride in traffic. If you have none, the 50cc is the only legal choice.

### Do I need a licence for a 125cc scooter?

Yes. Class A1 covers motorcycles up to 125 cm3. A foreign licence is used with an International Driving Permit that Vietnam recognises, as explained in our [licence guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbike-licence-rules-for-foreigners-in-vietnam/).
