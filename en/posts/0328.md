Yes, you can visit Hanoi's flower markets on a rented scooter, but "flower market" covers three very different experiences. Quang Ba in Tay Ho is a wholesale market that trades through the night and peaks after midnight. The Old Quarter's Hang Luoc market is a seasonal Tet event that runs for a couple of weeks in daylight and evening. The flower villages on the edge of the city sell mainly to traders and to the markets themselves. Choose the one that matches your riding comfort, park legally away from the busiest lanes, and carry home only what fits safely on the bike. Details below were checked as of October 2026.

## Three kinds of flower market, three kinds of trip

Most visitors picture one place with buckets of roses on a pavement. In practice Hanoi's flower trade is layered. Growers in villages such as Tay Tuu and Nhat Tan cut or pot their produce, wholesalers gather it in a night market, and retailers carry it into shops, street stalls and the temporary Tet markets. Each layer suits a different visitor.

If you want colour and photographs in normal waking hours, the seasonal Tet markets are the easy choice, because they are built for crowds on foot. If you want to see how the trade works, the night wholesale market is the real thing, but it is a workplace first and an attraction second. The villages are best treated as a drive-through, since most of the activity happens in fields and sheds rather than on a visitor-friendly street.

Decide first whether you want atmosphere, photographs, a bouquet to take home, or an understanding of the trade. That single choice settles the time of day, the route and whether a scooter is the right tool at all.

## Quang Ba: the night wholesale market in Tay Ho

Quang Ba is usually described as Hanoi's largest wholesale flower market and sits on Au Co Street in Tay Ho, north of the centre and close to West Lake. Travel and hotel guides agree on its rhythm even where they disagree on exact times: trading starts late in the evening, builds through the small hours and fades towards dawn. One local seller quoted by a travel site puts the real peak at around three in the morning, while another guide gives roughly one to two. Before Tet, trading starts earlier, because demand is higher.

The flowers come from nearby villages and farther provinces, and some are imported or brought from Da Lat. Stalls work under bare bulbs, buyers bargain in cash, and trucks and minibuses load and unload at the kerb. It is vivid and slightly chaotic.

Treat it the way you would treat any working market. Do not stand in loading lanes, ask before photographing a seller up close, and buy a bunch if you are enjoying the view. Our guide to [visiting Long Bien Market without blocking deliveries](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-visit-long-bien-market-without-blocking-deliveries/) covers the same etiquette for another night trade market, and most of it applies here.

## The Tet-season markets in the Old Quarter and along Au Co

The most accessible flower markets appear in the weeks before Lunar New Year. Vietnam's national tourism administration describes the Hang Luoc Flower Market as centred on Hang Luoc, Hang Khoai, Hang Ruoi, Hang Ma and Phung Hung streets, open every day between 8 am and 10 pm during its run. Traditionally it gathered once a year, from the fifteenth to the last day of the twelfth lunar month, with farmers from villages such as Ngoc Ha, Nghi Tam, Nhat Tan and Quang Ba bringing bonsai kumquat, peach blossom and flowers.

In 2026, state radio VOV reported that the market had been expanded beyond Hang Luoc to Hang Khoai, Hang Ruoi and Hang Ma, plus the Phung Hung mural street, with stalls kept to the pavements so traffic could keep moving. The same report mentioned craft booths, calligraphy and folk-art activities along the railway viaduct.

Further out, the same tourism report mentions a Tet flower market on Au Co Street in the Quang An area, stocked with peach blossom, ochna and kumquat bonsai from the nearby Nhat Tan flower village. Exact dates move with the lunar calendar and are announced by the district each year, so check a current notice rather than relying on last year's schedule.

## Getting there on a rented scooter

The ride itself is straightforward by Hanoi standards, but the timing changes what kind of ride it is.

For Quang Ba, you will probably be travelling between about midnight and dawn. That is the hardest riding time in the city: low light, tired drivers, delivery trucks and few other tourists. If you have not ridden in Hanoi after dark before, take a taxi or ride-hailing car for this trip and keep the scooter for daylight. Our guide to [riding at night in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-at-night-in-hanoi/) explains lights, visibility and the streets to avoid. If you do ride, check the headlight and brake light before leaving and keep your speed modest near the market, where people step out between stalls.

The shop's published hours are 09:00 to 21:00 daily, so a night run means collecting the bike during the day and planning the rental period around it. The [Tay Ho by scooter guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/tay-ho-west-lake-by-scooter/) describes the lakeside roads leading towards Au Co and is a good daytime warm-up.

For the Old Quarter market, a scooter is rarely the best way to arrive. The streets around Hang Luoc are narrow and, during the event, very crowded. Read our guide to [riding in the Old Quarter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-old-quarter/), then consider parking outside the centre and walking the last stretch.

### A note on the 2026 low-emission pilot

Hanoi began a low-emission zone pilot on 1 July 2026 in the core of Hoan Kiem ward. According to VOV and the Lao Dong newspaper, in the first zone, which covers the Hoan Kiem lake pedestrian area, the Old Quarter and the night market, motorbikes, mopeds and cars are banned from 7 pm to midnight every Friday, Saturday and Sunday, with exceptions for priority vehicles. The phase described runs until 31 December 2026 and later phases are planned, so a Tet-season visit in early 2027 may face different rules. Check current notices before you plan an evening there.

## Parking and stopping without blocking anyone

Flower markets generate a lot of short, hurried stops: a van unloading, a customer grabbing a bouquet, a rider pulling in "just for a minute". Do not copy that habit with a rental.

Under Law 36/2024/QH15, in force since 1 January 2025, vehicles on city roads may stop or park only along the kerb or pavement on the right in the direction of travel, with the nearest wheel no more than 0.25 m from it, and not in places that obstruct pedestrians or other vehicles. The Law also bars stopping or parking at pedestrian crossings and bridges unless traffic arrangements allow it. Where a vehicle obstructs traffic and the owner is absent, police may move it. Our [guide to parking a motorbike in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-motorbike-in-hanoi/) explains guarded lots, tickets and towing in more detail.

In practice:

- Choose a marked attended parking area, even if it adds a few minutes of walking.
- Never leave the bike in a loading lane, across a market entrance or on a crossing.
- Lock the steering, take the key, and keep the parking ticket somewhere safe.
- Do not leave a bag or camera in a basket.
- Note where you parked; flower streets look identical in the dark.

## Carrying flowers and plants home

Bouquets travel better than you might fear, and potted trees travel worse. A wrapped bunch of roses or lilies fits behind the rider when held by a passenger or tucked into a basket, but flowers are fragile and wind speed matters. Ask the seller to wrap the stems tightly in paper and keep the bunch vertical, head up, held against your body rather than flapping beside the bike.

A kumquat or peach tree for Tet is a different matter. Law 36/2024/QH15 says motorbikes and mopeds must not carry goods that extend more than 0.3 m beyond either side of the cargo rack or more than 0.5 m behind it, and stacked height must not exceed 2 m from the road surface. A branching tree on a Honda Vision does not respect those limits. Ask the stall about delivery, or use a taxi or delivery vehicle. For weekly or monthly renters, the [prices page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/prices/) and [rental guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-guide/) explain what a rental covers, including that one basic helmet per rider is provided.

## FAQ about visiting flower markets

### What is the best time to visit Quang Ba?

Sources agree it trades through the night. If you want a quieter look, one guide suggests going between roughly 5 am and 7 am, when trading eases. For the busiest scene, go after midnight, preferably by taxi.

### Is the Old Quarter flower market open all year?

No. The Hang Luoc flower market is a seasonal event tied to Lunar New Year, and the district announces dates each year.

### Can I ride to the Old Quarter on a Friday evening?

Since 1 July 2026 the core Hoan Kiem zone has restricted motor vehicles from 7 pm to midnight on Fridays, Saturdays and Sundays. Check current notices and plan to walk or use a taxi.

### Can I carry a big plant on a scooter?

Not safely, and not within the loading limits. Ask the seller to deliver it or use a larger vehicle.

### Do I need to bargain?

Prices at wholesale stalls are negotiable, and a polite attitude helps. Pay in cash with small notes and agree the price before the seller wraps your flowers.
