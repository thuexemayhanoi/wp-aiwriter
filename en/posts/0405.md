Before you book a motorbike that is brought to your hotel, settle eight things: whether delivery is offered for your rental length, whether your hotel address is inside the delivery area, what it costs each way, the exact handover spot and time, what checks and paperwork happen on the pavement, how the deposit is taken, how the bike is collected at the end and what happens if the bike is not right. At Nguyen Ha Motorbike Rental, delivery is offered only for weekly and monthly agreements, within 10 km of Hoan Kiem Lake, at VND 50,000 per trip or VND 100,000 both ways, with distances beyond 10 km agreed in advance and no airport service. Shorter rentals are collected from the shop.

## First question: does your rental qualify?

Delivery is a feature of longer rentals, not an add-on you can request for a single day. Nguyen Ha offers it only when the booking is for a week or a month, and only within 10 km of Hoan Kiem Lake. A visitor who wants a two-night rental should plan to collect the bike at the shop in Long Bien, which is open 09:00 to 21:00 daily. Start with the shop's own [services page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/services/), then ask the shop to confirm your case. Our guide to [weekly and monthly rentals](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/weekly-monthly-motorbike-rental-hanoi/) lists what long-term renters should ask.

Ask these three questions in your first message:

- "My rental will be [number] days or weeks. Is delivery available for that length?"
- "My hotel is at [full address]. Is it within the delivery area?"
- "Is the delivery fee for one trip or both ways, and is it paid in cash at handover?"

Write down the answers with the date. A hotel near the Old Quarter or around Hoan Kiem Lake is likely to be inside the 10 km limit, but the shop decides, so do not assume.

## Second question: what does the fee cover?

The published fee is VND 50,000 for one trip and VND 100,000 for both ways, which suggests a delivery to you and a collection from you at the end. Check which you are buying. A one-way fee means you may be asked to bring the bike back to the shop yourself. A both-ways fee means the shop comes for it, so ask for the collection time as well.

Also confirm these points:

1. Whether the fee is separate from the rental price and the deposit.
2. Whether any waiting time at the hotel is charged if you are late to meet the rider.
3. Whether a change of hotel mid-rental changes the arrangement.

The [prices page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/prices/) shows the shop's rental figures, and the final price is confirmed on the actual bike before the contract is signed.

## Third question: where exactly will the handover happen?

Hotels in Hanoi often sit on narrow streets with no room to stop. Vietnam's Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (Law 36/2024/QH15) sets rules on where a vehicle may stop or park. Among the places it lists as off limits are bridges unless permitted, pedestrian crossings, intersections and the first five metres from their edge, pick-up and drop-off points, and the road in front of a gate that serves vehicles. On a city street, a stopped vehicle must keep close to the right-hand kerb, with the nearest wheel no more than 0.25 metre from it, and must not obstruct people or traffic. These rules are why a handover cannot simply happen wherever the rider stops.

Ask the hotel and the shop to agree on the spot:

- Is there a bay, a side alley or the hotel's own bike parking where the handover can happen?
- Will a hotel staff member be at the door to receive the rider?
- If the street is one-way or closed at certain hours, which side should you wait on?
- What is the backup spot, such as a nearby cafe or petrol station, if the first one is blocked?

Our guide to [parking a motorbike in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-motorbike-in-hanoi/) explains guarded lots and how to find them, which matters just as much for the days after the handover as for the handover itself.

## Fourth question: when, in clock terms?

Give the shop a window, not a single minute. A rider crossing Hanoi at rush hour may arrive early or late. Questions to settle:

- Between which hours will the rider arrive, and will they call you when they are close?
- Does the rental day start when the bike reaches you or when it leaves the shop? At Nguyen Ha a rental day is 24 hours, so this decides when your return falls.
- Is the handover possible in the evening, and until what time? The shop's published hours are 09:00 to 21:00, so ask whether deliveries follow the same hours.
- What happens if your flight or train is delayed?

Bring the phone number into the conversation. The shop's number is 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969), and the hotel front desk should also have it so that the rider and the staff can find each other.

## Fifth question: what happens on the pavement?

A handover at a hotel is the same handover as at the counter, only outdoors. Ask the shop to confirm that all of the following still happen before you ride away:

1. **The inspection.** You walk around the bike, test the brakes, lights, horn, mirrors and tyres and photograph existing marks. Our [10-minute rental scooter inspection checklist](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-scooter-inspection-checklist/) gives the steps.
2. **The contract.** You read and sign it before the keys change hands. Our explanation of [what the clauses in a Hanoi rental contract mean](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-motorbike-rental-contract-explained/) helps.
3. **The identity and licence check.** Staff check your documents and photograph them for the contract, and they do not keep the originals.
4. **The deposit.** At Nguyen Ha it is cash, between VND 2,000,000 and 5,000,000 depending on the bike and contract, so have the right amount ready.
5. **The helmet.** One basic helmet per rider is included.
6. **The fuel level.** Petrol bikes come with a small amount, roughly half a litre to two litres, so ask where the nearest petrol station is.

The law expects a rider to carry a licence suited to the bike along with the vehicle papers, and the shop checks the licence for that reason. Our guide on [what documents to carry](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/documents-to-carry-riding-in-vietnam/) explains the list.

If a rider says the inspection can be skipped because it is raining or late, that is a reason to move the handover under cover or to the next morning, not to skip the check.

## Sixth question: what if the bike is not the one you expected?

Models on the price page are those the shop may have; the bike actually available is confirmed when you book. Ask for the model, the type of gearbox and the plate number before the rider sets out. If a different bike arrives, you need to be able to say no without losing money. Ask before booking:

- Can I decline the bike at the door if it is not what we agreed, and is the delivery fee refunded or credited?
- What happens if the bike has a fault I find during the inspection?
- Can a replacement be brought, and how long would that take?

Get the answers in writing. Our comparison of the [Honda Vision and Air Blade](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/honda-vision-vs-air-blade-which-scooter-to-rent-in-hanoi/) shows how different two common automatics can feel.

## Seventh question: who tells the hotel?

Some hotels are glad to receive a rider and hold the keys; others have no space for a visiting bike and no wish to take responsibility for it. Before you rely on the hotel, ask:

- Will reception accept a visitor arriving with a bike, and will it allow the rider to wait?
- Is there secure parking for the bike overnight, and is it free or charged?
- Does the hotel mind the bike being left in a lobby or alley while you sort the paperwork?

Do not leave the deposit, the keys or your documents with the hotel desk to give to a third party unless the shop and the hotel have both agreed this clearly. Handing cash only to the person who is verified as the shop's rider, with the contract in front of you, is safer.

## Eighth question: how does the end of the rental work?

Plan the return before you accept the delivery. Ask:

1. Will the shop collect the bike from the hotel, and at what time?
2. Where should you leave the bike, and in what fuel condition? Petrol bikes at Nguyen Ha come back with roughly half a litre to a litre.
3. Who checks for damage, and when? Our guide to [returning a rental motorbike in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/returning-your-rental-motorbike-hanoi/) explains the usual checks.
4. When is the deposit returned, and in what form?
5. What is charged for a late return? The shop charges VND 30,000 per hour for one to five hours and an extra rental day from six hours.

## FAQ: hotel pickup and delivery

### Can the shop bring a bike to my hotel for a one-day rental?

At Nguyen Ha, delivery is only for weekly and monthly agreements. Shorter rentals are collected from the shop.

### How far does delivery go?

Within 10 km of Hoan Kiem Lake the fee is VND 50,000 per trip or VND 100,000 both ways. Beyond 10 km, the arrangement is agreed in advance.

### Is there an airport delivery?

No. Nguyen Ha does not offer an airport service.

### Do I still need to inspect the bike if it is delivered?

Yes. The inspection, contract, document check and deposit happen at the handover, wherever it takes place.

### Can I pay the deposit by card?

At Nguyen Ha the deposit is cash. Ask the shop before you arrive if you want to confirm the arrangement.
