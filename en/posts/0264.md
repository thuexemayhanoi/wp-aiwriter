Usually yes, for a scooter designed to plug into the mains: the VinFast Evo 200 manual says its charger takes ordinary 220-volt alternating current at 50 hertz and draws at most about 400 watts. But "ordinary socket" comes with conditions. Use the charger that came with the bike, make sure the socket and any lead are undamaged and rated for the load, charge indoors in a dry, ventilated place away from anything flammable, and never plug in if the charging port or plug is wet. Some electric vehicles, especially those with swappable batteries, are not meant to be charged this way at all. This guide explains what the manual says, what it means for a renter in a Hanoi hotel or apartment, and what to ask the shop.

## The short answer, and what it depends on

Whether a rental EV can charge from a wall socket depends on three things:

- **The bike.** Plug-in scooters and electric bicycles use a charger that connects to the mains. Swap-battery systems use cabinets or special chargers instead.
- **The charger.** Each bike has a charger matched to its battery. A charger from another vehicle is not a substitute.
- **The socket and place.** The outlet has to be sound, dry and able to carry the load, in a place where charging is safe.

The shop's own fleet can include electric bicycles, 50cc-equivalent electric scooters and some VinFast electric motorbikes, and the shop confirms the actual bike and the charging arrangement at handover. So the first step is always to ask which bike you have and how it is meant to be charged.

## What the manufacturer's manual says

The VinFast Evo 200 is a different model from whatever you may be given, but its manual shows what a mains-charged scooter expects. The main points are these:

- **The supply.** The charger plugs into a 220-volt alternating-current supply at 50 hertz. The manual says the supplied 72-volt, 5.5-amp charger consumes at most about 400 watts. At 220 volts that works out at roughly 1.8 amps drawn from the wall, which is my own arithmetic and not a figure from the manual.
- **The place.** The charger is designed for indoor use. Charge in a ventilated place, protect the charger and socket from water, moisture and other liquids, and keep away from flammable materials.
- **The charger itself.** Use only the charger supplied with the bike or an equivalent supplied by VinFast. The manual warns that the wrong charger can damage the electrical system and cause fire or explosion.
- **The socket and lead.** Make sure the socket and cable meet the voltage and current stated on the charger, and that the plug is firmly seated. Do not use a socket, lead or charger that is damaged, faulty or modified.
- **The port.** Connecting when there is water or dust in the charging port or plug can cause a fire or an electric shock.

The rest of the manual's advice concerns what to do while and before charging:

- **Smell and smoke.** Stop charging at once if you notice an unusual smell or smoke.
- **The battery switch.** Turn the battery switch under the seat on before plugging in, or the bike will not charge.

Two points concern how to connect:

- **The order.** The manual advises connecting the charger to the bike first and then the plug to the wall, and plugging in firmly so the connection does not flicker.
- **The time.** Standard charging takes about 10 hours, depending on the battery's condition.

That is a long list, but most of it is common sense once you picture the setup: a small power supply, a short lead and a socket you do not own.

## What counts as a good socket

The manual does not define the perfect hotel outlet, so I will stay with what it says and with ordinary caution. A socket that suits a scooter charger is:

- **Sound.** The plug fits snugly, there is no cracking, scorching, discolouration or wobble, and the cover plate is intact.
- **Dry.** It is indoors or under a roof, away from spray, washing water and damp walls. Our guide to [riding in Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) explains why wet-season humidity makes this more than a formality.
- **Not overloaded.** The charger is the main thing plugged into it. A socket that already powers an air conditioner, kettle or heater is a poor choice.
- **Reachable without a trailing lead.** If the lead crosses a doorway or footpath, someone can trip over it, and it can be crushed.

If the hotel or building offers an extension lead, the manual's wording suggests checking that it is undamaged and rated for the charger's current. When in doubt, ask the staff for a better outlet instead of improvising.

## What "ordinary socket" does not mean

Some people hear "household socket" and assume any adapter, reel or cheap multi-plug will do. The manual does not say that. It says the socket and lead must meet the charger's rating and be free of damage. It also says the charger is for indoor use, so a bike left charging in the open air or in a wet corridor is outside what it describes.

Two other things are not covered by an ordinary socket:

- **Public fast charging and swap stations.** VinFast's charging-post instructions, for instance, have you plug the bike's own charger into a socket on the post and then start the session in an app. That is a different system from a wall socket, and it only works for the models and accounts it supports. Our guide to [charging an electric scooter in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/charging-an-electric-scooter-in-hanoi/) covers hotels, swap points and timing.
- **Chargers for cars.** VinFast's portable chargers for its cars are a separate product with their own plug types and earthing requirements. They are not for scooters, and a car charger and a scooter charger should not be swapped.

## A safe charging routine for a rental

1. **Ask where and when.** Agree with the hotel or host where you will charge and whether someone can see the bike.
2. **Park on a flat, firm, dry spot** near the socket, with the bike locked and the side stand down.
3. **Inspect the charger, lead and plug.** Look for cuts, kinks, burn marks or a loose pin. Do not use anything damaged.
4. **Check the port is dry and clean.** If it is wet, wait.
5. **Switch the bike off and, if the manual says so, turn on the battery switch.**

Now connect and monitor:

6. **Connect the charger to the bike first, then plug into the wall,** firmly.
7. **Look for the charging symbol.** The manual says a charging symbol appears on the display while charging is active and that the bike cannot be ridden then.
8. **Stay within earshot for the first half hour.** If you notice a smell, noise or heat, unplug and call the shop.
9. **Unplug when done.** The manual describes the charger's indicator, and the shop will explain how it looks on yours.
10. **Coil the lead loosely** and keep the plug out of the rain.

For overnight charging questions and the shop's own rules, ask directly. Our guide to [renting an electric scooter in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/renting-an-electric-scooter-in-hanoi/) puts charging in the context of a whole rental.

## Why you should not use the wrong charger

It can be tempting to borrow a similar-looking charger or buy a cheap one if yours goes missing. The manual's warning is direct: the wrong charger can damage the vehicle's electrical system and cause fire or explosion. If the charger is lost or damaged, tell the shop, and do not improvise. Our guide to [what to do about breakdowns with a rental bike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-bike-breakdown-hanoi-what-to-do/) covers the practical steps for problems on a rental.

## Temperature and timing

Charging heats the battery a little. The manual lists 55 degrees Celsius as the maximum internal temperature during charging and describes protections for charging when too hot or too cold. VinFast's FAQ advises against charging a battery while it is hot. After a ride in the Hanoi sun, let the bike cool in the shade before plugging in.

On the question of how full to charge, VinFast's advice is that keeping the battery between 20 and 80 per cent is better for its life, and the manual adds that you should not hesitate to charge when you can. For a short rental you will simply want enough charge for the next day, so follow the shop's guidance on what to leave and what to return.

## FAQ: charging a rental EV from an ordinary socket

### Can I use any wall socket?

Use a sound, dry, indoor socket that can carry the charger's load. Avoid damaged, loose or wet outlets.

### Can I use an extension lead?

The manual says the socket and cable must meet the charger's rated current and be undamaged. If the hotel offers one, check its condition, or ask for a better outlet.

### Can I use a different charger?

No. The manual says to use only the supplied charger or an equivalent from VinFast.

### Can I charge outdoors?

The manual says the charger is for indoor use, so charge indoors in a ventilated, dry place.

### How long will it take?

About 10 hours on the Evo 200's standard charger, depending on the battery. Ask the shop for your bike's time.

### Is it safe to leave it charging overnight?

That depends on the place, the equipment and the shop's rules. Ask before you leave, and use sound equipment in a dry, ventilated, supervised spot.
