Before you leave the shop on a rented electric scooter, test each brake on its own. With the scooter on level ground, squeeze one lever firmly, try to push the scooter forward and back, and check that the wheel stays held; then repeat with the other lever. Make sure the levers feel firm and return fully, that the brake light works, that the scooter's power responds correctly when a lever is squeezed and that a slow test stop in the shop's street feels straight and controlled. Electric scooters are heavy, quiet and quick off the line, so a weak brake is more serious than it first seems. This guide explains what to test, what the manufacturer's manual says about it and which findings mean "ask for another scooter". It is general guidance, not a mechanical inspection.

## Know what you are testing

The first question for any scooter is which lever works which wheel. On the VinFast Evo 200, the manual says the right-hand lever controls the front wheel and the left-hand lever controls the rear. Other models may link both brakes to one lever or use a different layout, so ask staff to show you before you touch anything. Our article on [front brake versus rear brake on small manual motorbikes](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/front-brake-versus-rear-brake-on-small-manual-motorbikes/) explains why the difference matters, although it describes petrol bikes.

Two more points from the Evo 200 manual:

- It says that for the best braking you should squeeze both brakes together, and that you should avoid squeezing too suddenly.
- It says to release the throttle when you brake.

On electric models the front brake is often a disc and the rear may be a drum or another disc. Ask what your scooter has, because the checks differ slightly, as the sections below show. A manual for another VinFast scooter, the Motio, also mentions regenerative braking, where braking energy is converted into electricity and returned to the battery, which means an electric scooter can feel different from a petrol one even before you touch the levers. The figures and wording in this article come from VinFast manuals for other models, not necessarily from the scooter you will be given.

## Test 1: the push-and-squeeze check, with the scooter off the stand

The Evo 200 manual's own pre-ride brake check is simple: squeeze the brake lever hard and then try to push the scooter forwards and backwards to see how well the brake holds. It tells you to make sure the brakes work well.

To do it safely at the shop:

1. Ask staff to hold the scooter upright, or put it on the centre stand if it has one and the wheel is not on the ground for the test.
2. If the scooter is on its wheels, make sure the side stand is up and the key is off, so that the scooter cannot move under its own power.
3. Squeeze the front lever firmly and try to push the scooter forward. The wheel should lock against your push.
4. Release, squeeze the rear lever and try to push the scooter back. The rear wheel should resist.
5. Repeat and compare. A weaker or later bite on one brake is a note for the shop.

A scooter that rolls freely with a lever squeezed hard has a brake problem, not a feel problem. Do not ride it.

## Test 2: lever feel and free travel

Levers should feel consistent: firm, smooth and returning on their own.

- **Squeeze slowly.** The lever should meet resistance before it gets close to the grip. A lever that goes almost to the bar, or feels spongy, is a warning.
- **Compare left and right.** Different feel is normal on a front disc and rear drum, but neither should be loose or rough.
- **Check the return.** Release the lever fully and watch whether it springs back. A sticking lever can keep a brake dragging or cut power unexpectedly.
- **Free play at the end.** The Evo 200 manual says that when squeezing the right lever there should be no free play at the end of the lever travel, and that if there is, the brake system should be checked by the manufacturer's service network.
- **Look at the pivots and cables,** for dry, rusty or damaged parts.

The manual also says the left and right levers should be checked before each ride and the pivot lubricated when needed. That is a mechanic's task, not a renter's task, so the useful thing is to report a stiff lever, not to oil it yourself.

## Test 3: what you can see of the pads and shoes

You cannot inspect everything at a counter, but a few signs are visible.

### Disc brakes

On the front disc, the Evo 200 manual describes a wear groove in each pad, so a mechanic can judge wear without removing the pad; when the groove has nearly disappeared the pads should be replaced. A renter cannot always see the groove, but can look for:

- A very thin pad or visible metal backing.
- A disc with deep grooves, a visible lip or a blue or dark colour.
- Oil, grease or a shiny, glazed look on the pad or disc. The manual warns that lubricant on the brake disc or pad significantly reduces braking and can cause an accident.
- A squealing or grinding sound when you roll the scooter and squeeze.
- Brake fluid on or around the calliper or the lever.

### Drum brakes

The manual says wear of the rear brake shoes is judged by how far the lever travels before the brake acts, and that if braking performance falls, the shoes should be replaced by the service network. In practical terms, if the left lever has to travel much further than it should or the rear wheel barely slows, mention it.

### Brake fluid

For hydraulic discs, the Evo 200 manual says not to let the fluid fall below the minimum mark, recommends DOT 4 fluid and says a low level can indicate worn pads or a leak, and that if the level drops suddenly the scooter should be checked before riding. A renter should not open the reservoir. If you see a visibly low level or fluid leaks, say so.

VinFast's warranty terms also list brake pads and brake discs among items that wear naturally, which is a reminder that worn brakes are normal maintenance for a hard-working rental scooter. The question for you is only whether they are safe today.

## Test 4: brake light, throttle cut-out and parking mode

Electric scooters connect their brakes to other systems.

- **Brake light:** switch the scooter on, squeeze each lever in turn and have someone look at the rear light. It should light for each lever, with no delay.
- **Throttle cut-out:** some models cut power when a lever is squeezed, so the scooter should not drive against the brake. Ask staff whether yours does. If the throttle seems to keep pulling while you squeeze the lever, report it.
- **Parking mode:** on the Evo 200, entering parking mode needs you to hold the brake while pressing the button, so a weak or non-working lever can make the scooter hard to put into and out of that mode. Our guide to [what to do if an electric scooter stops powering on](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-to-do-if-an-electric-scooter-stops-powering-on/) explains why a scooter can light up and still refuse to move.
- **Warning lights:** a brake-related warning icon on the display should be photographed and shown to staff.

If the throttle is involved in any way with a lever fault, the only safe place to find out is a stationary test with the shop beside you.

## Test 5: a slow, straight test stop

When the stationary checks pass, ask permission and do a short test.

1. Choose a quiet stretch of the street, with no cars close behind.
2. Roll at walking pace, then squeeze both levers evenly and stop.
3. Repeat at a brisk walking or jogging pace.
4. Try each lever alone at a very low speed, so you know how each feels.
5. Notice any pulling to one side, grabbing, juddering, squealing or a long stopping distance.
6. If everything is smooth, ride a little faster and repeat the test, never braking hard on a bend or on a damp surface.

The US Consumer Product Safety Commission's guidance for e-scooters says to know how to use the brakes and how long the scooter takes to stop, and notes that stopping distance varies significantly from one scooter to another. A test stop is how you learn that figure for the scooter in front of you, before you have traffic to worry about. Allow more distance than you expect on a wet road. ## Reject or ask for a change if

- A lever does not hold the wheel when you push the scooter.
- A lever goes close to the grip, feels spongy or does not return.
- There are loud grinding or squealing noises, or the wheel will not spin freely with the lever released.
- You can see oil, brake fluid or contamination on pads or discs.
- The brake light does not work.
- The scooter pulls to one side when stopping.
- Staff cannot tell you which lever works which wheel.
- A warning icon appears on the display that nobody can explain.

If you are uneasy, say you would like another scooter or a mechanic's check. A shop that is sure of its bikes will not mind. ## FAQ: electric scooter brake checks

### Which brake should I use first?

On the Evo 200, the manual advises squeezing both together and not too suddenly. Ask the shop what applies to your scooter.

### Is regenerative braking enough to stop?

No. Regeneration is an extra effect of braking, and the mechanical brakes are what you rely on. The Motio manual describes the recovery of braking energy as a feature of braking.

### Can I adjust the levers myself?

No. Ask the shop. Wrong adjustment can leave a brake weak or dragging.

### Do I need to test the brakes every day?

A quick push-and-squeeze check before each ride is a good habit, and the Evo 200 manual says the levers should be checked before each ride.

### What if the brakes feel fine in the shop but weak on the road?

Stop in a safe place and call the shop. Do not keep riding a weak brake.
