Most dashboard lights on a Honda-style automatic scooter fall into three groups. Some are simple information, such as the turn signals, high beam and Idling Stop. Some are reminders or minor faults, such as the oil change reminder and the smart-key battery warning. A few mean you should slow down or stop: the engine fault lamp, the coolant overheating lamp, the ABS lamp and the battery charge lamp. Every light should come on briefly when you switch the ignition on, as a bulb check, and then go out. If one stays on or comes on while you ride, slow down, pull over somewhere safe and call the shop rather than ride on. Which lights your scooter has depends on its model and version, so this guide draws on Honda Vietnam's owner's manuals for the Vision, Lead and Air Blade and on Honda's global explanation of indicators.

## How scooter dashboards work

A scooter dashboard has two jobs: to show you the speed, fuel and trip figures, and to warn you about faults. Honda's global guide to indicators makes two points worth remembering. Every indicator means something, and if a light comes on while you are riding it could mean a fault. And the indicators differ from one model to another, so the owner's manual tells you which ones your scooter has.

The bulb check is the first thing to learn. The Vision manual says the engine fault lamp lights briefly when the ignition is turned on. The Air Blade manual says the ABS lamp lights when the ignition is on and goes out once the scooter reaches about 10 km/h. If a light that should come on does not, or a light that should go out stays on, Honda says to have the scooter checked by an authorised dealer. For a renter, the equivalent is to tell the shop before you ride away.

Symbols and colours vary between models, so this guide names lights by their function. As a general rule, a light that stays on after the engine starts deserves attention.

## Lights that mean stop or call the shop now

**Coolant overheating lamp (liquid-cooled models).** Some scooters, including the Lead and the Air Blade in the manuals we read, have a coolant overheating indicator. The Air Blade manual describes overheating as the lamp coming on or the scooter accelerating slowly. It says to pull over, stop the engine with the ignition, let the engine cool with the ignition off, check for leaks from the radiator hoses once it has cooled and, if there is a leak, not to start the engine and to take the scooter to a dealer. It also says to check the coolant level in the reserve tank. If everything looks normal, you may continue but should watch the indicator closely. Honda warns that driving on while overheated can seriously damage the engine. A renter should simply stop, switch off and call the shop.

**Engine fault lamp (PGM-FI).** This lamp relates to the fuel-injection and emissions system. Honda's manuals and its global guide explain two cases:

- **If it comes on and stays on:** reduce speed and have the scooter inspected as soon as possible. Honda says driving with the lamp on can damage the emissions system and the engine.
- **If it flashes:** park in a safe place away from flammable material, stop the engine and wait at least 10 minutes until it cools. If it flashes again after restarting, Honda says to ride to the nearest dealer at no more than 50 km/h.

For a rental, the practical version is to stop, wait, and call the shop before deciding whether to continue.

**Low engine oil pressure (where fitted).** Honda's global guide says that if a low oil pressure indicator comes on while riding or idling, the pressure may be low, and you should stop the engine immediately. It is not shown on every scooter, but if you see an oil-can symbol you should stop.

## Lights that mean ride gently and report it

**ABS lamp (models with ABS).** The Air Blade manual says that if the ABS lamp comes on or flashes while riding, does not light when the ignition is turned on, or does not go out above 10 km/h, the system may have a serious problem. The brakes still work as a conventional system without anti-lock. The manual adds that the lamp can flash if you spin the rear wheel with the scooter lifted off the ground, in which case turning the ignition off and on clears it. Report any ABS fault to the shop. The manual also explains that the system works on the front wheel and does not shorten braking distance.

**Battery charge lamp (where fitted).** The Air Blade manual explains that this lamp coming on while riding may mean low battery voltage or reduced battery performance, and the scooter should be taken to a dealer. If the battery is too weak, the lamp may not light at all, so a scooter that is hard to start or has dim lights deserves a mention to the shop.

**Fuel gauge and low-fuel warning.** The fuel gauge is a dashboard light in effect, and Honda's global guide says that if the low-fuel indicator flashes you should refuel as soon as possible, because running out of petrol stops the engine from firing and may damage the catalytic converter. The amount left depends on the model. The Vision manual says that when the first bar (the E mark) flashes there is about 0.9 L left. The Lead manual gives about 1.2 L at the same point, and the Air Blade manual gives about 1.2 L when the gauge is down to the E bar, with flashing following as the level falls further. The Vision manual advises refuelling when the gauge reaches E. The Vision tank holds 4.8 to 4.9 L by version. Because petrol scooters are handed over with only about 0.5 to 2 litres, the low-fuel warning can appear early in a rental, so see our guide to [fuel in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/fuel-in-hanoi-petrol-stations-rental-scooter/).

**Fuel gauge fault display.** The Vision manual says that if the fuel system has a fault, the fuel indicators show a fault pattern on the display, and the scooter should be taken to a dealer as soon as possible.

**Smart-key lamp.** On scooters with a smart key, the smart-key indicator lights when the scooter and the key fob have recognised each other. The Vision manual says that if it flashes five times, the fob battery needs replacing, and that if it flashes while the ignition is on, the connection with the fob has been interrupted, perhaps by strong radio noise or a dropped fob. The manual says this does not affect the scooter until the engine is stopped, so avoid switching off until you are somewhere safe, and call the shop.

## Reminders and information lights

**Oil change reminder.** On the Lead, the manual says the oil change indicator lights first at about 1,000 km, then every 6,000 km once reset, and stays on until it is reset. On the Air Blade the interval can be set by the owner. This is a service reminder, not an emergency, and a renter should mention it to the shop rather than reset it.

**Turn signals and high beam.** The indicators flash with each turn signal and glow when the high beam is on. A signal that flashes unusually fast usually points to a failed bulb, which you can report.

**Idling Stop indicator.** On scooters with Idling Stop, the indicator lights when the system is ready to stop the engine and flashes when the engine has been stopped by the system. The Vision manual advises not leaving the scooter while the indicator flashes, because turning the throttle can restart the engine.

**Smart-key lit steadily.** This simply means the key fob has been recognised, and the lamp goes off when the ignition is turned on.

## A sixty-second dashboard check at handover

At pick-up, with the shop staff beside you:

1. Switch the ignition on and watch the dashboard. Note which lights come on and which go out.
2. Start the engine and check that the engine fault lamp, ABS lamp and battery lamp are off after a moment or at about 10 km/h.
3. Ask which lights your model has, and whether it has Idling Stop or a smart key.
4. Look at the fuel gauge and ask how much petrol is in the tank.
5. Take a photograph of the dashboard with the ignition on, including the odometer.
6. Operate the turn signals and the high beam and check the dashboard indicators.

## When a light comes on while you ride

1. **Do not ignore it.** Slow down and look for a safe place to stop.
2. **Pull over safely** away from traffic and flammable material.
3. **Read the symbol and note what the scooter is doing.** Is it running roughly, slow to accelerate, or fine?
4. **Switch off if it is an overheating or oil light.** Let the engine cool.
5. **Photograph the dashboard.**
6. **Call the shop on 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969),** open 09:00 to 21:00 daily, and describe what you saw.
7. **Do not try repairs or reset indicators.**

## FAQ: scooter warning lights

### Should every light come on when I turn the key?

Several do, briefly, as a bulb check. The Vision manual says the engine fault lamp lights briefly when the ignition is turned on. If a light that should come on does not, tell the shop.

### What does a flashing engine fault lamp mean?

Honda says it flashes when engine misfiring is detected. Stop in a safe place, switch off and wait at least 10 minutes. If it flashes again after restarting, the manual's advice is to ride to a dealer at no more than 50 km/h.

### Does my scooter have a coolant temperature light?

Some do, such as the Lead and Air Blade in the manuals we read. Others, such as the Vision, may not. Ask the shop which lights your model has.

### Can I ride with the ABS lamp on?

Honda says the brakes still work as a conventional system, but without anti-lock. Ride gently, avoid wet roads and tell the shop.

### Is the oil change light urgent?

No, it is a service reminder, but the shop should know about it.

### What if the smart key light flashes five times?

The Vision manual says the key fob's battery needs replacing. Tell the shop rather than opening the key yourself.
