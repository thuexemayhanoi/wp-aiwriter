Whether you need a licence for an electric vehicle in Vietnam depends on which legal class the machine falls into, not on what the shop calls it. An electric bicycle that cuts out at 25 km/h needs no licence. An electric moped, with a design speed of no more than 50 km/h and a motor of no more than 4 kW, needs no licence but the rider must be at least 16. Anything faster or stronger is a motorcycle: it needs a class A1 licence up to 11 kW and a class A licence above that, and the rider must be 18. This guide gives you a three-step test using the numbers on the spec sheet, worked examples from real electric scooter models, and a checklist of what to ask the shop before you pay. It reflects the rules as of October 2026.

## Start with the class, not the label

Hanoi shops and sellers use "electric bike", "e-scooter", "xe dien" and "xe may dien" loosely, often for all three classes at once. The Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (Law 36/2024/QH15), in force since 1 January 2025, does not use those labels. It sorts two-wheelers by what the machine is built to do.

- **Motor-assisted bicycle, including the electric bicycle.** A bicycle whose motor helps the rider, where the motor drive is cut when the rider stops pedalling or when the bicycle reaches 25 km/h.
- **Moped (xe gan may).** A two- or three-wheeler with a design speed of no more than 50 km/h and, for an electric motor, a motor power of no more than 4 kW. The moped class does not include motor-assisted bicycles.
- **Motorcycle (xe mo to).** Any motorised two- or three-wheeler that is not a moped.

The Ho Chi Minh City traffic police publish the same three-way split on their official site, and they stress that parents and riders often mistake one class for another. If you take only one idea from this page, take this: you cannot answer the licence question until you know which of these three you are riding.

## Step 1: Does it have pedals and an assist cut-out at 25 km/h?

Look for working pedals and a motor that only assists. That is a motor-assisted bicycle, which Vietnam treats with bicycles. There is no driving licence for it, no number plate, and no minimum age in the law, according to the Ho Chi Minh City traffic police page. The rider must still obey the road rules and wear a helmet; our guide on [electric bikes in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/electric-bikes-in-hanoi-rules/) covers those rules and where such a bike may travel.

A flat floor, a seat box and no pedals is not an electric bicycle, however slow the scooter looks. Some sellers fit token pedals to a machine that is really a moped. Do not rely on the pedals alone: ask whether the motor stops assisting at 25 km/h and what the paperwork says about its class.

## Step 2: Check design speed and motor power

If the machine has no pedals, you are in moped or motorcycle territory, and two numbers decide it: the design speed and the motor power. Under the law, a moped must satisfy the 50 km/h limit and, for an electric motor, the 4 kW limit. A motorcycle is everything else. That means the test works in one direction only: a machine that is faster than 50 km/h is a motorcycle even if its motor is under 4 kW, and one with a motor above 4 kW is a motorcycle even if it is speed-limited.

Two details catch people out:

- **Design speed is not the speed you reach in practice.** It is the maximum the manufacturer built the vehicle for. A moped-class scooter might reach 49 km/h on a flat road and no more, while its twin without a speed limiter reaches 70 km/h and is a motorcycle.
- **Motor power on a spec sheet can mean several things.** Some brands quote a nominal figure and a higher peak figure. Ask for both figures, and for the vehicle class shown on the registration, rather than relying on a single headline number.

## Step 3: Match the class to a licence

Once you know it is a motorcycle, the licence class follows the motor. Article 57 of Law 36/2024/QH15 says:

- **Class A1** is for two-wheel motorcycles up to 125 cm3, or with an electric motor of up to 11 kW.
- **Class A** is for two-wheel motorcycles above 125 cm3, or with an electric motor above 11 kW, and it also covers everything A1 covers.

The minimum age for a class A1 or A licence is 18, under Article 59 of the same law. A moped, by contrast, may be ridden from age 16. One more transition detail: licences issued before 1 January 2025 stay valid for the period shown on the card, and an old class A1 licence keeps its old scope, so a resident with an older card should read the wording on it.

## Age and papers on the day

Age is the other half of the answer. The law lets a 16-year-old ride a moped, and requires 18 for a motorcycle. In 2026 a government decree on traffic penalties, Decree 238/2026/ND-CP, took effect on 15 August 2026 and reworded the offence for riders aged 16 to under 18 on motorcycles. The Thu Vien Phap Luat legal portal explains that this does not ban 16- and 17-year-olds from mopeds or electric mopeds. A visitor in that age group should still ask the shop what it allows, because a rental shop can set stricter conditions than the law.

Mopeds and motorcycles, electric included, are registered vehicles with a plate. A motor-assisted bicycle is not registered. If the machine has a plate, the registration certificate should match the bike you are given.

## If you are a visitor

If the vehicle is a motorcycle, a foreign visitor needs a home licence covering motorcycles together with an International Driving Permit issued under the 1968 Vienna Convention, as our guide on [motorbike licence rules for foreigners](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbike-licence-rules-for-foreigners-in-vietnam/) explains. If you do not have both, the lawful electric choices are an electric moped or an electric bicycle. Those are the vehicles to ask about, and there is no need to disguise the question: say plainly which licence you hold and ask which models are lawful for you. ## Where to find the numbers on a rental bike

Do not rely on the word "Lite" or a sticker. Ask to see:

1. The **registration certificate**, which shows the vehicle type and the plate.
2. The **manufacturer's specification**, for design speed and motor power.
3. The **model name on the vehicle**, so you can check it against the maker's page.
4. A **written note** of which class the shop says it is, if you rely on the no-licence option.

If the shop cannot tell you the design speed and power of the bike on offer, choose another bike or another shop. A short question now costs less than a police stop later.

## Mistakes that cost riders their lawful choice

- **Treating "electric" as a class.** There are three classes, and only the bicycle and the moped avoid a licence.
- **Judging by size.** A scooter that looks small can be a motorcycle if it is capable of more than 50 km/h.
- **Assuming a speed mode changes the class.** An Eco mode that holds you to a lower speed does not turn a motorcycle into a moped, because the law's moped definition is written around the design speed. Our guide to [renting an electric scooter in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/renting-an-electric-scooter-in-hanoi/) explains what else to expect from the bike.
- **Forgetting the age.** A licence-free class still has an age floor for mopeds.
- **Relying on an old table.** Class names and scopes changed on 1 January 2025.

## FAQ: electric vehicle licences in Vietnam

### Do I need a licence for an electric bicycle?

No. A motor-assisted bicycle that cuts out at 25 km/h is treated with bicycles. You still need a helmet and must follow the road rules.

### Do I need a licence for an electric scooter?

It depends on its class. A moped-class scooter, with a design speed of no more than 50 km/h and a motor of no more than 4 kW, needs no licence for riders aged 16 or over. A faster or stronger one is a motorcycle and needs class A1 or A.

### Is a 49 km/h speed limit enough to avoid a licence?

Only if the vehicle's design speed and motor power both fit the moped definition. A machine that can exceed 50 km/h is a motorcycle even if its speed is later limited, so check the specification and the registration, not the setting.

### Which licence do I need for an electric motorcycle up to 11 kW?

Class A1, under Article 57 of Law 36/2024/QH15. Above 11 kW you need class A.

### Can a visitor use a foreign licence on an electric motorcycle?

Yes, if the home licence covers motorcycles and is accompanied by a 1968-convention International Driving Permit. Otherwise choose an electric moped or an electric bicycle.

### Who decides which class my rental is?

The vehicle's registration and manufacturer specification. Ask the shop to show you both.
