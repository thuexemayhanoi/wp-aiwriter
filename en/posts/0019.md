Many couples and friends travelling together rent one scooter instead of two. It halves the rental, keeps you together in traffic and means only one of you needs to be a confident rider. It also changes what you should rent. A scooter that feels light and lively with one person on board can feel heavy, slow to stop and short of space once a second adult and two bags are added. In Hanoi the two automatic scooters most often chosen for this are the Honda Lead and the Honda Vision, and they suit quite different kinds of trip. This guide compares them specifically for two riders, explains the rules for carrying a passenger in Vietnam as of October 2026, and gives practical advice on packing and riding two-up. If you are choosing a scooter mainly for yourself, our comparison of the [Honda Vision and Air Blade](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/honda-vision-vs-air-blade-which-scooter-to-rent-in-hanoi/) is the better starting point.

## What a second person changes

A passenger adds somewhere between 50 and 90 kg to a scooter that may itself weigh less than 100 kg, so the machine underneath you behaves differently. Acceleration from the lights is slower, and the gap you used to squeeze into may close before you get there. Braking distances grow, because there is more weight to stop with the same small brakes and tyres. The steering feels lighter at the front, since more of the load sits over the rear wheel, and slow U-turns in a narrow lane become wobbly if the passenger moves.

None of this makes riding two-up dangerous, and the sight of two people on a scooter is utterly normal in Hanoi. It does mean that the rider should be comfortable on the bike alone before adding a passenger, that the bike needs enough power and space for the job, and that both people should know what to expect. A first-time rider should spend at least a day riding solo before carrying anyone.

## The Honda Lead: built for carrying

The Lead is the scooter Vietnamese families use for school runs and market trips, and it shows. Its standout feature is the under-seat compartment: Honda describes it as a 37-litre space able to hold two full-face helmets, which is far more than any other scooter commonly rented in Hanoi. For two travellers that means both helmets can be locked away when you park, or a daypack and rain gear can travel out of sight while you ride.

The Lead has a 125cc liquid-cooled engine, a long, broad seat and a flat floor, and at around 114 kg it is noticeably heavier than the Vision. The extra weight and the larger engine work in its favour with two people on board: it pulls away from junctions without strain, holds a steady speed on the ring roads and feels planted rather than nervous over bumps. The fuel tank of about six litres also gives it a comfortable range for day trips. The downsides are size and weight when manoeuvring: a smaller rider may find it tiring to push backwards into a tight parking row, and its bulk takes more care in the narrowest lanes of the Old Quarter.

## The Honda Vision: light and nimble, with limits

The Vision is the most popular scooter in Vietnam and the easiest one to ride: a 110cc air-cooled engine, a seat height of about 761 mm on the standard versions and a weight a little under 100 kg. Solo riders love it in city traffic, and two adults on a Vision is a common sight at Hanoi speeds. For a couple staying in the city and riding short distances between neighbourhoods, it does the job.

Its limits appear with luggage and distance. The under-seat compartment is roughly half the size of the Lead's, enough for one half-face helmet and a raincoat but not two helmets or a full daypack. The smaller engine is fine in city traffic but works hard with two adults on a long incline, on a bridge ramp or on the open road, and the shorter seat leaves less room for a tall passenger. The Vision's lighter weight does make it easier for a less experienced rider to hold upright at a standstill, which counts for something when a passenger shifts their weight at a red light.

## The rules for carrying a passenger

Vietnam's Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (Law 36/2024/QH15), in force since 1 January 2025, sets the limits in Article 33. A motorbike may carry the rider and one passenger. A second passenger is allowed only in listed cases, such as carrying a child under 12, taking a sick person for emergency treatment, carrying an elderly or disabled person, or escorting someone who has broken the law. Two adults and a friend squeezed on behind is not one of them, and under Decree 168/2024/ND-CP the rider can be fined VND 400,000–600,000 for it.

Everyone on the bike must wear a helmet that meets the national standard, with the strap fastened; our [helmet rules guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/helmet-rules-in-vietnam/) covers the details and the separate fines for rider and passenger. Nguyen Ha provides one basic helmet per rider free of charge, so tell the shop when you book that two people will ride and check both helmets at handover. The same article of the law forbids the rider from carrying bulky objects. A suitcase balanced on the floor or a large box held by the passenger is both illegal and a good way to fall off.

## What fits, and how to pack for two

The best luggage plan for two people on a scooter is a small one. Here is what works in practice:

- Keep the heavy things low and central. A bag on the flat floor between the rider's feet is acceptable if it is small, sits flat and leaves room for both feet; the hook under the handlebars takes a light shopping bag, not a heavy one.
- The passenger can wear a small, snug backpack. A large rucksack pulls the passenger backwards and makes the scooter light at the front, so carry it only for a short distance.
- Use the under-seat compartment for what must stay dry and secure: documents, a phone power bank, rain gear. On the Lead that leaves room for helmets too.
- Never hang bags from the mirrors, and do not let straps dangle near the rear wheel.
- Leave suitcases at your hotel. Moving between hotels or to the airport is a job for a taxi; Nguyen Ha does not offer an airport service, and a scooter is the wrong tool for it.

If you are riding out of the city for a night or two, pack a single soft bag per person and leave the rest of your luggage at the hotel. ## Riding two-up safely

A few habits make a big difference with a passenger on board. For the rider: brake earlier and more gently than usual, using both brakes together, and leave a longer gap to the bike in front. Pull away smoothly so the passenger is not thrown backwards. Slow right down for speed humps, potholes and the edges of bridge ramps, where the extra weight bottoms out the rear suspension. Ask the shop to check the rear tyre pressure for two people before you leave.

For the passenger: sit close to the rider, keep both feet on the footrests at all times, and hold either the rider's waist or the grab rail rather than nothing at all. Lean with the scooter in bends instead of sitting upright against it, and keep still at low speeds and when the bike is stopping, which is when sudden movements upset the balance most. Hands should stay off the rider's arms and shoulders. Phones and cameras are best kept in a zipped pocket; a passenger filming while holding on with one hand is the classic way to drop a phone into traffic.

## Which one to rent: three typical pairs

**A couple spending a week in central Hanoi.** Short rides between the Old Quarter, West Lake and the cafes of Ba Dinh, with a daypack at most. The Vision is cheaper to run, easy to park and perfectly adequate. Choose the Lead only if you want both helmets locked away while you sightsee.

**Two friends planning day trips.** Rides of 100 km or more, perhaps to [Ninh Binh](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-to-ninh-binh-by-motorbike/), with water, rain gear and a camera. Rent the Lead: the extra power, larger tank and storage space are what make a long day comfortable for both of you.

**A tall passenger or a nervous one.** The Lead's longer seat gives more room, but the Vision is easier for a less experienced rider to hold upright. If the rider is new and the passenger is heavy or uneasy, consider two scooters instead, or a day of solo practice before riding together.

## FAQ

### Can two adults ride a Honda Vision in Hanoi?

Yes. Two adults on a Vision is common at city speeds. It feels strained on long climbs, on the open road and with heavy bags, which is where the Lead is the better choice.

### How much luggage fits under the seat of a Honda Lead?

Honda describes the Lead's under-seat compartment as a 37-litre space that holds two full-face helmets. In practice that is two helmets, or one helmet plus a daypack and rain gear.

### Is it legal to carry two passengers on a scooter in Vietnam?

Only in the cases listed in Article 33 of Law 36/2024/QH15, such as carrying a child under 12 or a person needing emergency care. Otherwise the limit is the rider plus one passenger, and the rider can be fined VND 400,000–600,000.

### Can we carry a suitcase on a rental scooter?

No. Carrying bulky objects is prohibited for the rider, and a suitcase makes the scooter unstable. Use a taxi for hotel changes and airport runs.

### Do we each need a helmet?

Yes. The rider and the passenger must both wear a standard helmet with the strap fastened. Tell the rental shop that two people will ride so that two helmets are ready at handover.
