A rented 50cc bike should come with a registration certificate that matches the bike in front of you: the plate number on the card is the plate on the bike, the engine and frame numbers on the card match the numbers stamped on the machine, and the card is legible and not altered. Beyond that single card, Vietnam's road traffic law of 2024 lists three other papers that a rider of a registered motor vehicle must carry, and as of October 2026 a rider of a moped is exempt only from the driving licence requirement, not from these. This guide explains what each paper is, what to look at on the registration card, what to ask about originals, copies and digital versions, which red flags should make you ask for another bike, and what the rental shop may reasonably keep. It is general information, not legal advice.

## The four papers the law names

Law 36/2024/QH15 on Road Traffic Order and Safety, in force since 1 January 2025, sets out in Article 56 what a driver must carry when taking part in road traffic. The list for ordinary motor vehicles has four items:

1. The **vehicle registration certificate**, in Vietnamese giay chung nhan dang ky xe and often called the cavet. The article also allows a certified copy together with the original confirmation of the lender, but only where the vehicle is mortgaged to a credit institution.
2. The **driving licence** appropriate to the vehicle, with a specific exception for moped riders, whom the article treats separately in its fourth clause.
3. The **technical safety and environmental inspection certificate**, as required by law for the vehicle in question.
4. The **certificate of compulsory civil liability insurance** for the vehicle owner.

For a 50cc bike, the second item does not apply: a moped is defined in Article 34 as a vehicle with a design speed up to 50 km/h and, if petrol-driven, an engine of no more than 50 cubic centimetres, and Article 56 says moped riders need to understand the rules, have riding skill and meet the age and health conditions rather than hold a licence. The other three remain. Whether and when a given moped needs an inspection certificate is a detail of other regulations that this guide has not checked, so ask the shop what applies to the bike you are given. Our wider guide to [documents to carry when riding in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/documents-to-carry-riding-in-vietnam/) puts the list alongside the papers you carry yourself.

## What a rider can ask the shop to show

You are borrowing the bike, so the registration card normally belongs to the shop or to the person the shop rents it from. Before you leave, it is reasonable to ask the staff to show you:

- **The registration card itself,** so that you can read it.
- **The insurance certificate** and its end date. The separate guide on [insurance questions for 50cc riders](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/travel-insurance-motorbike-rental-vietnam/) explains what that paper does and does not cover.
- **What you will carry on the road:** the original, a copy, or a photograph.
- **What the contract says** about who is responsible if the papers are checked and something is missing.

You are not being difficult by asking. A shop that works properly will have answers ready, and our explanation of [what the clauses in a Hanoi rental contract mean](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-motorbike-rental-contract-explained/) shows where such points usually appear.

## Reading the registration card

A Vietnamese registration card is a small printed card with security features and a series of fields. The exact design has changed over the years and different cards are in circulation, so treat the following as the usual content and not a template.

| What to look for | What it should be | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Plate number (bien so) | The same as the plate on the bike, character for character | A mismatch suggests the wrong card or a swapped plate |
| Owner name and address | A person or business | Tells you whom the bike belongs to; the shop should be able to explain the link |
| Make and model | Consistent with the bike in front of you | A card for a different make is a warning |
| Colour | Close to the bike's actual colour | Repainted bikes are not always a problem, but ask |
| Engine number (so may) | Matches the number stamped on the engine casing | Confirms the card belongs to this machine |
| Frame number (so khung) | Matches the number stamped on the frame | Same reason |
| Engine capacity (dung tich) | About 50 cc or below for a moped | A 110cc bike with a 50cc card is a problem |
| Condition | Legible, not torn, not peeled or altered | A damaged card is hard to present when asked |

Our separate guide to checking the engine capacity covers the capacity line in more detail, so this page does not repeat it. The point to remember is that all the fields should tell one consistent story about one bike.

### How to compare card and bike

Take your time. Ask the staff to point out where the engine and frame numbers are stamped, usually on the engine casing and on the frame near the steering head or under the seat on the frame rail. Read the numbers aloud and compare them with the card. Rust, paint or dirt can hide a stamp, in which case ask the shop to wipe it. If staff cannot find the numbers, or the numbers on the bike are scratched, filed or re-stamped, ask for another bike.

## The plate

Article 35 of the law requires a motor vehicle to be registered and to carry a plate, and Article 36 says plates are issued by the competent state authority. In practice that means every rented bike should have a plate, fixed in the right place, which is readable and matches the card. Because a plate is a legal requirement in its own right, check that it is not missing, bent over or covered with tape. If a plate has been replaced, the shop should be able to explain why. A plate that has been painted over, or that is a different size from usual, is a reason to ask.

## Original, copy or phone?

The text of Article 56 names the registration certificate itself, or a certified copy together with a lender's confirmation where the bike is mortgaged. It does not mention a plain photocopy, so ask the shop what they consider sufficient and what they give to renters. Do not assume the answer.

Digital documents are changing the picture. Clause 3 of Article 56 says that if the listed papers have been integrated into an electronic identity account, they can be presented through that account. The Ministry of Public Security's Circular 37/2026/TT-BCA, which took effect on 8 June 2026, amends the vehicle registration rules so that electronic registration data is integrated into the VNeID national identity application and the VNeTraffic application. But an electronic identity account is tied to a resident's own data, and a law firm's note on electronic identification states that a foreigner needs a Vietnamese permanent or temporary residence card to obtain an e-ID account at all. For a tourist, the realistic position is that the registration card on a rental bike will be paper, a copy or a photograph supplied by the shop, and the shop is the one who must explain which version is sufficient.

## What the shop may keep, and what you should keep

A shop's practices differ and this guide cannot say what any particular shop does. Nguyen Ha's published terms state that identity documents and any licence are checked and photographed for the contract and that originals are not kept. That statement concerns your documents; ask separately what happens with the bike's papers. Whatever the arrangement, make sure you have a clear answer to three questions:

1. Who holds the original registration card while you ride?
2. What do I show if I am stopped and asked?
3. Who deals with a missed or lost paper, and who pays?

Photograph the card, the plate and the insurance certificate before you leave, in good light and with the whole page in frame. Keep those photographs until the bike is returned and the deposit settled; our guide to [how a deposit works](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbike-rental-deposit-hanoi/) explains why a clear record helps. If you are asked to produce papers by an officer, stay calm, show what you have, and call the shop on 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969) if anything is missing.

## What can go wrong without papers

The rules on carrying papers are enforced through administrative penalties. Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, in force since 1 January 2025, sets penalties for failing to carry or hold a valid insurance certificate and, in various circumstances, for failing to carry or hold a registration certificate. The amounts quoted by secondary sources differ depending on the vehicle and the exact offence, so look at the decree's text or ask the shop what it understands to apply, and do not rely on a single number. Beyond penalties, a missing card can mean a delay at a check, a dispute with the shop or an insurance question if there is an accident. None of that is likely if you have asked for the papers and seen them before you ride.

## Red flags and what to do

| Red flag | What to do |
|---|---|
| Plate on the bike differs from the plate on the card | Ask for the right card or another bike |
| Engine or frame number does not match the card | Ask for another bike |
| Card is cracked, peeled, laminated over or altered | Ask for a clear replacement |
| Owner name is unrelated and the shop cannot explain | Ask for an explanation in writing or choose another bike |
| Card describes a larger bike than the one in front of you | Ask for another bike, and do not ride a larger machine you may not be allowed to ride |
| No plate fixed to the bike | Do not ride it |
| Insurance certificate is out of date | Ask for a valid one before you ride |
| Shop will not show any paper | Walk away |

None of these is an accusation. Many mismatches are clerical, such as an old card or a plate changed after repair, but the shop can resolve them in minutes if it wants to.

## FAQ: 50cc registration papers

### Do I need a driving licence to ride a 50cc bike?

Under Law 36/2024/QH15, moped riders are treated separately from licence-holding drivers and need to meet the age, health and rules-knowledge conditions. Other papers on the bike still apply.

### Which papers must be on the bike?

Article 56 lists the registration certificate, the inspection certificate where required and the compulsory insurance certificate, plus a licence for vehicles that need one.

### Can I show a photo of the card on my phone?

The law allows electronic presentation where the papers are integrated into an electronic identity account, but tourists generally do not have such an account. Ask the shop what it accepts.

### What if the owner's name on the card is not the shop?

It can happen, for example where a shop rents bikes owned by someone else. Ask the shop to explain the arrangement and put it in the contract.

### Does the card prove the bike is really 50cc?

It is the best evidence, but only if the engine and frame numbers match the bike. A badge or a word on a price list is weaker proof.

### Who is responsible if the papers are missing at a check?

It depends on the contract and on what the shop has told you. Ask before you sign.
