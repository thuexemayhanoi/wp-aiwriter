On a small manual bike such as a Honda Wave or Yamaha Sirius, the safest place for a folded raincoat is the box under the seat, and the safest place for a helmet is on your head. A helmet that is not being worn needs a different plan: either carry it in your hand or on a pillion's lap when parked, or take it with you, because the small under-seat box is not designed to swallow a full helmet and the hook beneath the seat is only for use while the bike stands still. Honda's own manual for the Wave RSX FI limits that box to 5 kg and warns against riding with a helmet hung from the hook. This guide explains what fits where, how to keep a wet raincoat from damaging anything else, what never to hang from the handlebars, and a simple loading routine you can repeat every time the sky darkens.

## Know what you start with

Nguyen Ha Motorbike Rental gives one basic helmet per rider free with a rental. A raincoat is not included for short rentals, and it may be considered for monthly rentals, so on a day or weekly hire you should plan to bring or buy your own. The shop also does not provide an extra lock, so any helmet you leave on the bike is your own risk. Ask staff at handover what is in the box already: some bikes carry a tool kit or documents there.

The practical question is therefore small: you have one raincoat, one or two helmets and a bike with little storage. Where does each item go?

## What the manuals say about storage

Honda's Wave RSX FI owner's manual describes a storage box under the seat. It says never to load it beyond the stated maximum of 5 kg, not to keep flammable items or items that heat can damage in it, and not to leave valuables or fragile objects in it. The same manual notes that the tool kit also lives in this box, which uses some of the space.

Yamaha Motor Vietnam describes the Sirius FI's under-seat compartment as wide enough for a half-head helmet and a raincoat, and opened with the key at the main ignition. A 2017 Yamaha announcement quotes about 9.2 litres for the compartment. These are manufacturer descriptions for the Sirius FI; an older or different version may be smaller, so check the actual bike.

For the Wave, Honda's manual gives a weight limit but no volume or helmet fit, so do not assume that a helmet will go in. Test it on the bike you are given, with staff watching, before you rely on it.

## The raincoat: fold it, bag it, keep it dry

A raincoat is light and compressible, which is why it suits the under-seat box. Put it in the box before you ride off, not when the rain starts, so that you can reach it at a safe stopping place without unpacking a bag.

Useful habits:

- **Choose a coat that folds small.** A two-piece set or a compact poncho takes less room than a heavy rain jacket.
- **Fold it into its own pouch or a plastic bag.** This keeps it clean and lets you stuff it away wet later.
- **Keep it away from papers and phones.** Honda's washing advice notes that water getting into the under-seat box can damage documents or other items inside, so a wet coat should go in a plastic bag and the bag should not sit on top of paperwork.
- **Do not leave it in the box for days wet.** It will smell, and damp plastic can mark other things.
- **Check the box is not overfull.** Honda warns against exceeding the weight limit, and a lid that will not close properly can open in traffic.

If the box is already full, a rolled raincoat can go inside a small backpack worn on your body. Our guide to [solo travellers with a backpack](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/manual-motorbike-rental-for-solo-travellers-with-a-backpack/) explains why a worn bag is safer than a bag strapped to a small bike.

## Wearing it safely: loose cloth and moving parts

Honda's manual advises avoiding loose clothing because it can catch on a part of the bike. That applies most to a poncho. A flapping hem can brush a wheel, the chain or the rear brake area on a small manual bike, which has exposed moving parts. General practice, not a Honda instruction, is to pull the poncho down over your knees rather than leaving it loose, and to keep the hem clear of your feet and the chain.

Put the raincoat on at a safe stopping place, off the road, rather than while riding. Check that the hood or collar does not push your helmet forward over your eyes and that nothing covers the lights, mirrors or indicators on the bike. ## The helmet: on your head, not on the bike

Vietnam requires motorbike riders and passengers to wear helmets; our guide to [helmet rules in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/helmet-rules-in-vietnam/) sets out the details. Beyond the rules, Honda's manual says to use a helmet that is properly fitted, with the strap fastened under the chin.

That leaves the question of the helmet you are not wearing. Honda's manuals for both the Wave and the Vision are clear on one point: the helmet hook under the seat is to be used only when the bike is parked, and you must not ride with a helmet hanging from it, because it can interfere with safe riding and lead to serious injury.

So the spare helmet options are:

1. **Carry it with you.** If you are on foot, a helmet is easy to hold or hook on a backpack strap.
2. **Wear it.** A rider who is changing between riding and walking should keep it on until arriving.
3. **Use the hook only while parked.** Take it off the hook before you start the engine.
4. **Check whether it fits in the box.** The Sirius FI's compartment is described as holding a half-head helmet. A larger or full-face helmet may not fit; try it.
5. **Do not hang it from a mirror or the handlebars while riding.** It swings, can strike your hand or knee and can catch on the controls.

Our guide to [choosing a helmet that fits for a manual motorbike journey](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/choosing-a-helmet-that-fits-for-a-manual-motorbike-journey/) covers fit and condition.

## What never to hang on the bike

Honda's Wave RSX FI manual does not describe a carrier hook for shopping or other loads, and it says to tie all luggage tightly, balanced and close to the centre of the bike, and to keep it away from the lights and exhaust. Applying that to raincoats and helmets:

| Item | Where | Why not |
|---|---|---|
| Wet raincoat | Plastic bag in the box or your backpack | Dripping onto the seat, the lock or documents |
| Spare helmet | In your hand, on your head or in the box if it fits | A swinging helmet can catch controls |
| Raincoat over the handlebars | Nowhere | Covers lights and can catch in the steering |
| Raincoat over the exhaust | Nowhere | Heat and fire risk, and Honda says to keep luggage away from the exhaust |
| Helmet on the seat | Avoid | It can fall, and passers-by can move it |
| Helmet on a mirror | Avoid | The mirror's setting changes and it may snap |

## A loading routine in under a minute

Each time you park and rejoin the bike:

1. Put the bike on its stand on firm ground.
2. Open the seat with the key. Do not leave the key inside the box when you close the lid.
3. Place the folded raincoat in its bag at one side of the box, away from any documents or the tool kit.
4. If the helmet fits, place it in the box with the front facing forward, as Honda shows for scooters, and close the lid firmly. If it does not fit, keep it with you.
5. Lift the lid slightly to check it is latched.
6. Lock the steering, as Honda advises, and close the safety shutter.

That routine takes less time than it reads. Our guide to [parking a motorbike in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-motorbike-in-hanoi/) explains guarded lots, which are a safer place to leave a bike with a helmet nearby than a pavement edge.

## When it starts raining

If rain starts while you are riding, do not reach behind you or lift the seat on a busy road. Signal, move to a safe place to stop, put the stand down, open the box, take out the raincoat and put it on. Then put the helmet back on and check that the visor is clear, and ride on slowly. It is a good habit to leave extra space for braking in the wet.

## FAQ: carrying a raincoat and helmet

### Is a raincoat included with a rental?

Not for short rentals, according to the shop's terms. It may be considered for monthly rentals, so ask.

### Does a helmet fit under the seat of a Honda Wave?

Honda's manual gives only a weight limit for the box. Test the bike you are given; do not assume a helmet fits.

### Can I hang my helmet on the hook while riding?

No. Honda says the hook is for use only while the bike is parked.

### Is it safe to carry a wet raincoat under the seat?

Yes, if it is in a plastic bag away from papers and phones, and the box is not overloaded.

### Where can a second helmet go if I am carrying a passenger?

The passenger wears it. A spare that is not being worn should be carried by hand or on a bag, not hung from the bike while riding.

### Should I lock my helmet to the bike?

The shop does not provide an extra lock. Take the helmet with you if you are leaving the bike somewhere unguarded.
