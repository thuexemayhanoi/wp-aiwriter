Neutral is the position in which the engine is not connected to the rear wheel through any gear, so the bike can idle without moving and can be pushed freely. On a Honda Wave it matters more than it looks, because Honda's procedure for starting the engine begins with neutral, the procedure for ending a ride finishes in neutral and the rotary gearbox has a rule about reaching neutral from fourth gear that catches many new riders. This guide explains what neutral is, how to confirm that you are in it, why the start and end of a ride use it, when you can and cannot select it, what to do at a traffic light and how to avoid the common mistakes. It uses Honda's Vietnamese owner's manual and safe-riding training document. It is general information. Always follow the manual and the demonstration for the bike you rent.

## What neutral does

In neutral, the gearbox's output is disconnected from the engine. The engine can run at idle, the rear wheel can turn freely and the bike can be pushed forward or backward without resistance. In a gear, the engine drives the wheel through the gearbox and the automatic clutch, so when the engine speed rises, the bike moves.

For a rider, neutral has three jobs:

- **Safe starting.** Starting in neutral means the bike will not lurch forward when the engine catches.
- **Resting.** At a long stop or when parked with the engine running, neutral frees you from holding the bike with the brake.
- **Finishing.** At the end of a ride, neutral is where the bike is left before the engine is switched off.

## How to know you are in neutral

A semi-automatic Honda has a neutral indicator, a light marked N on the dashboard. Honda's Wave RSX FI manual says it lights when the gearbox is in neutral, and the manual's starting procedure tells you to shift to neutral until the N indicator is lit. On that model the display also shows gear positions 1 to 4, so you can tell at a glance whether you are in a gear or in neutral.

If the bike has no neutral light, or the light is not working, you can check by feel. With the engine running and the throttle lightly opened, the engine will rev freely without the bike moving. Do this only in a safe place with the brake held, and never rely on it at speed. If the bike is in gear when you think it is in neutral, it will move as soon as you add throttle. For that reason, always keep a brake applied when you are not certain.

## Why Honda starts and ends every ride in neutral

Honda's safe-riding training document sets out the operating procedure for gear bikes in numbered steps. The start includes getting on the bike, turning the key on and shifting to neutral. The end of the ride has the rider reduce the throttle and use the brakes to slow down, shift to neutral, turn the key off and then get off, turning the handlebars fully to the left. Honda's manual's start procedure says the same: key on, shift to neutral until the N indicator lights, release the throttle, then press the start switch or use the kick starter.

The reasons are practical. Starting in neutral prevents a surge. Ending in neutral means the bike is not left in gear on its stand, where a bump could move it. Neutral also makes the bike easy to push.

## The rotary gearbox rule

Honda describes the Wave's gearbox as a four-speed rotary type. The manual gives a rule that matters to every rider: when the bike is stopped, you can shift directly from fourth gear to neutral on the rotary system, but while the bike is moving it uses a return system and you cannot shift directly from fourth gear to neutral. It also says to always reduce the throttle before changing gear and to press the shift lever gently with the toe until it moves.

In practice, that means:

- **If you are in fourth gear and need neutral,** slow down and stop first, then select neutral.
- **If you try to find neutral at speed,** the lever will not give it to you. That is not a fault.
- **If you are at a standstill in any gear,** use the demonstration for your bike to reach neutral, since the exact pedal movement depends on the model and where you are in the sequence.

Ask the shop to show you how to get from first gear to neutral at a standstill on your bike, and then try it yourself a few times in a quiet place. ## Neutral at traffic lights

At a short stop many riders stay in first gear with a brake held, so that the bike is ready to move. At a long stop, such as a long red light, you may shift to neutral to rest. Either is fine as long as you know which one you are in. If you choose neutral, keep the brake on until you select first, and look at the indicator before you set off.

Do not rest your left foot on the shift lever while waiting. Honda warns that a foot on the lever can damage the shifting and clutch mechanisms, and the training document says the left foot should not press the lever. ## Never coast in neutral

Riding in neutral with the engine running or off is dangerous. In neutral the engine provides no braking, so on a descent you rely entirely on the brakes, which Honda warns can overheat if used continuously on a long, steep hill. The bike also cannot respond to the throttle if you need to accelerate away from a hazard. Always keep the bike in gear while moving. ## Pushing and parking

When you push the bike, neutral lets it roll. Honda's training document describes moving a bike with the engine off: stand it upright, straighten the handlebars, retract the side stand and push forward with a hand on the front brake lever to control the speed. For parking, Honda's manual says to switch the engine off, put the bike on a stand, turn the handlebars fully to the left, turn the key to lock, remove the key and close the safety cover. If you must park on a slope or soft ground, park so the bike cannot move or fall. ## A short practice routine for neutral

Neutral becomes automatic with a few minutes of practice in a quiet, flat, legal place. With the engine off and the bike on its stand, ask the shop to show you the pedal movements that take the gearbox from first to neutral and back. Then start the engine in neutral and notice that the N light comes on and the engine revs freely when you open the throttle slightly. Hold the front brake, select first gear and feel the light go out. Release the brake and roll a few metres, stop with both brakes and shift back to neutral at the standstill. Repeat five times. The aim is to know, without looking, how it feels to select neutral at a stop and what the pedal does. Do not practise changing to neutral while moving. Honda's rotary gearbox does not allow fourth gear to neutral at speed.

## Common mistakes

- **Starting in gear.** The bike lurches. Always check for neutral first.
- **Trusting the feel without checking the light.** If the light disagrees, hold the brakes.
- **Trying to find neutral from fourth gear while moving.** The gearbox does not allow it.
- **Stamping on the lever.** Use a gentle toe tap and ease the throttle first.
- **Resting a foot on the lever** at lights.
- **Coasting in neutral.** It removes engine braking and control.
- **Forgetting the neutral light is a warning.** A neutral light that comes on while you are riding in gear means something is wrong.

## FAQ: neutral on a Honda Wave

### How do I know the bike is in neutral?

The N indicator on the dashboard lights. If your bike has no working light, hold a brake and test with a light throttle in a safe place.

### Why does Honda say to start in neutral?

So the bike does not move when the engine starts. Honda's starting procedure calls for neutral with the N indicator lit.

### Why can't I shift to neutral from fourth gear while riding?

Honda's manual says the rotary gearbox allows fourth to neutral only when the bike is stopped. While moving, it uses a return system.

### Should I use neutral at red lights?

Either first gear with the brake held or neutral is fine, as long as you know which you are in.

### Can I coast in neutral downhill?

No. There is no engine braking in neutral, and the brakes alone can overheat.

### What if the bike will not go into neutral?

Stop, do not force the lever and call the shop.
