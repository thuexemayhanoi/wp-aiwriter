Rain in Hanoi rarely arrives politely. On a summer afternoon the sky darkens, the wind picks up dust and leaves, and within minutes the streets are running with water and every scooter rider has pulled over to wrestle on a poncho. An hour later the sun may be out again, but low-lying junctions can stay under water well into the evening. Riding a rental scooter through this is perfectly possible, and millions of Hanoians do it every day, but it rewards a little preparation: the right rain gear, an understanding of how wet roads change the way a scooter stops, and knowledge of which streets flood and when to simply wait. This guide covers the season, the gear, the rules that apply in rain and the flood spots reported in 2026, as of October 2026.

## When it rains in Hanoi

Hanoi has a monsoon climate with a clear wet season that runs roughly from May to October. The heaviest rain usually falls in July and August, much of it in short, intense afternoon or evening storms, and the later months of the season can bring days of steady rain when a typhoon or tropical depression crosses the north. From November to January the weather is mostly dry. In late winter and early spring, usually February and March, a fine, persistent drizzle and very high humidity leave roads greasy for days at a time without ever producing a proper downpour.

For a visitor, that means two different kinds of wet riding. Summer storms are dramatic but predictable: they build quickly and pass, and the sensible move is often to stop under cover for half an hour. Spring drizzle never really stops, so you ride in it, and the hazard is the slippery surface rather than standing water. Check the forecast from the National Centre for Hydro-Meteorological Forecasting, or any weather app, before a longer ride, and take official storm warnings seriously.

## Rain gear that works on a scooter

The cheapest and most common rain gear in Hanoi is the single-piece poncho, sold at every convenience store, market and petrol station in the season. The wide "bat-wing" style that also covers the handlebars keeps your hands and legs dry, but it catches crosswinds and, worn carelessly, its edges can flap into the rear wheel or the chain. If you buy one, choose a size that fits, tuck the back edge under you on the seat and make sure nothing hangs near the wheels.

A two-piece rain suit, jacket and trousers, is far better if you will ride in rain more than once. It does not billow, keeps you drier and is safer on the open road. Add a helmet visor or clear glasses, because rain at riding speed stings and blurs your vision, and keep a waterproof pouch or a plastic bag for your phone, wallet and documents. Sandals cope with puddles better than trainers that stay wet all day.

Raincoats are not included with short rentals at Nguyen Ha, though one may be considered for monthly rentals, so buy one on your first day if the forecast looks unsettled. One thing not to use is an umbrella. Holding an umbrella while riding is prohibited, and under clause 4 of Article 7 of Decree 168/2024/ND-CP the rider is fined VND 800,000–1,000,000; our guide to [traffic fines that catch foreign riders out](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/traffic-fines-vietnam-foreign-riders/) covers the others.

## Lights and visibility

Article 20 of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (Law 36/2024/QH15), in force since 1 January 2025, requires riders to use their headlight not only from 18:00 to 06:00 but also whenever rain, fog, smoke, dust or bad weather limits visibility. Under point g of clause 1, Article 7 of Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, riding without lights in those conditions is fined VND 200,000–400,000. Many recent scooters keep the headlight on whenever the engine runs; if yours has a switch, check it is on, and check the brake light and indicators work before you set off, as described in our [rental scooter inspection](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-scooter-inspection-checklist/).

Being seen matters as much as seeing. A dark poncho on a dark evening makes a rider almost invisible to a car driver peering through a streaming windscreen. A light-coloured or reflective rain jacket helps, and so does riding where drivers expect you: in the stream of scooters, not hugging the kerb where the water is deepest. After dark the problems multiply, so read our guide to [riding at night in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-at-night-in-hanoi/) before combining the two.

## How wet roads change riding

The first rain after a dry spell is the most slippery, because water lifts oil and dust off the surface before washing it away. Painted road markings, metal manhole covers, the steel plates of bridge decks and smooth tiled pavements at building entrances all become slick, and so do the fallen leaves that collect in gutters after a storm. Ride across them upright, without braking or accelerating, rather than leaning the scooter over them.

Stopping takes longer on a wet road, so start braking earlier and more gently, using both brakes together, and double the gap you leave to the bike in front. Make turns slowly and smoothly. Puddles hide potholes, so ride around standing water you cannot see the bottom of, and slow down before you reach it rather than in it. Wet brakes are weaker until they dry, so after riding through deep water squeeze the brakes lightly a few times while moving slowly to dry them. On the old [Long Bien bridge](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/long-bien-red-river-first-ride/), with its steel deck and its traffic running on the left, choose a dry day if you can.

## The streets that flood

Hanoi's drainage cannot keep up with the most intense storms, and some streets flood every season. The city treats water at least 30 cm deep that lasts more than 30 minutes as waterlogging, and its 2026 flood-response plan warns that rain of more than 100 mm an hour for several hours could produce up to 220 local flooding points across 54 wards and communes. City officials reported that drainage work had cut the number of regular hotspots, and that many now drain within 15 to 30 minutes after the rain stops, but deep water still appears quickly in a heavy storm.

Places reported under water during the 2026 season include the junction of Duong Dinh Nghe and Pham Hung behind the Keangnam towers, Hoa Bang, Phan Van Truong and Tran Binh streets and Le Duc Tho road in the west of the city, Thuy Khue along West Lake, and the junction of Phan Boi Chau and Ly Thuong Kiet. Newer districts such as My Dinh and the area around Vo Chi Cong road have flooded in past seasons, and parts of Long Bien and Gia Lam on the east bank have been on the city's watch list. Road underpasses are the worst places of all: in one mid-September downpour the city's drainage company reported 0.8 metres of water in a civil underpass in Dong Anh.

The rule for floodwater is simple. If you cannot see the road surface, or the water reaches the bottom of the scooter's body, do not ride into it. Water sucked into the air intake or the exhaust stalls an engine and can damage it, and a short downpour in July 2026 left many riders pushing stalled scooters through flooded streets. Floodwater also hides open drains and missing manhole covers. Wait at a cafe or under cover until the water drops, or take a route on higher ground.

## If your scooter stalls in water

Switch the ignition off, get off and push the scooter out of the water to higher ground. Do not keep trying to restart it: if water has reached the engine, repeated attempts can turn a cheap fix into an expensive one. Phone the rental shop on 0334 699 969 and explain what happened before taking the scooter to a roadside repair stand. Nguyen Ha asks renters to call first when anything goes wrong, so that the repair can be agreed, and to keep any receipt.

Electric scooters deserve extra care. Their batteries and motors are designed to cope with rain, but not with being driven through deep standing water, so treat flooded streets as a no-go on an electric bike; our guide to [renting an electric scooter in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/renting-an-electric-scooter-in-hanoi/) has more on range and care.

## Storms and when not to ride

In the typhoon season, usually from late summer into autumn, the authorities may advise people to stay at home while a storm passes, and strong winds bring down trees and branches across the city; when Typhoon Yagi hit Hanoi in September 2024, fallen trees blocked streets all over the centre. Do not ride in strong winds or while warnings are in force. A scooter is light and easily pushed sideways by a gust, and a passenger or a bat-wing poncho makes it worse.

The same applies to your own limits. Riding through a heavy storm with poor visibility on unfamiliar roads is a poor use of a holiday. If you are caught out, pull over somewhere safe, away from trees and overhead cables, park in a covered spot if you can, and wait; our guide to [parking a motorbike in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-motorbike-in-hanoi/) explains how the guarded lots work. Downpours usually pass within an hour, and a coffee under an awning is part of the Hanoi experience.

## After a wet ride

Wipe the seat before you sit down and keep a small cloth under the seat for the purpose. Under-seat compartments are not designed to be waterproof, so keep documents and electronics in a sealed bag even there. If you rode through deep water, mention it when you return the scooter so the shop can check the brakes and air filter. Ride gently for the first few minutes after a downpour while the roads drain, and remember that the next storm may be only a few hours away.

## FAQ

### When is the rainy season in Hanoi?

Roughly May to October, with the heaviest rain in July and August. Late winter and early spring, around February and March, bring persistent drizzle that leaves roads slippery.

### Is it safe to ride a scooter in Hanoi in the rain?

Yes, with care: wear proper rain gear, use your headlight, brake earlier and more gently, and avoid painted lines and metal covers. Do not ride through floodwater, and wait out storms with strong winds.

### Can I use an umbrella on a motorbike in Vietnam?

No. Holding an umbrella while riding is prohibited, and the rider is fined VND 800,000–1,000,000 under Decree 168/2024/ND-CP.

### Do I need my headlight on in the rain?

Yes. Article 20 of Law 36/2024/QH15 requires the headlight whenever rain or bad weather limits visibility, as well as from 18:00 to 06:00. Riding without it is fined VND 200,000–400,000.

### What should I do if my scooter stalls in floodwater?

Switch it off, push it out of the water and do not keep trying to restart it. Phone the rental shop on 0334 699 969 before any repair.
