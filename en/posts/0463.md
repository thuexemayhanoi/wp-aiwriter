Headlight alignment matters because the same lamp can either light the road ahead of you or blind the rider coming towards you. A beam aimed too high dazzles oncoming riders and drivers, and a beam aimed too low shortens the distance you can see, so you have less time to react. Law 36/2024/QH15 requires operators to switch from high beam to low beam when they meet oncoming traffic, pedestrians crossing, lit populous areas and when changing direction at intersections (Article 20), and a low beam that is aimed too high does not do the job, whichever switch position you choose. Honda lists headlight aim as a routine maintenance item, so it is a normal thing for a shop to check, and a renter can ask.

This guide explains what headlight alignment is, what a good and a bad beam look like, what moves the aim on a scooter, how to do a simple check without tools, what the law says about lighting, what to ask the rental shop and what to do if you are dazzled. It is general information based on the law and manufacturer guidance as of October 2026, not legal advice or a mechanical instruction.

## What alignment means

Alignment, or aim, is the direction the headlight points. A low beam is designed to light the road in front of the bike, with a cut-off that keeps most of the light below the height of an oncoming rider's eyes. The aim sets where that pattern falls. If the lamp tilts up, the pattern rises and spills into other people's eyes. If it tilts down, the pattern lands too close to the front wheel and you see only a short stretch of road. Aiming is separate from brightness and from bulb type. A perfectly good bulb in a lamp that is aimed wrongly is still a problem.

## Why it matters

### For you: seeing in time

At night, your headlight is the main way you see the road. If the beam is aimed too low, a pothole, a pedestrian or an unlit bicycle appears only when it is close, and at speed you may not have time to stop. Published rider-training guidance makes the principle clear: you should be able to stop within the distance you can see. Our guide to [how to ride after dark with limited street lighting](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-ride-after-dark-with-limited-street-lighting/) explains how to match speed to sight distance, and a lamp aimed too low shrinks that distance.

### For others: not dazzling

A beam aimed too high shines into the eyes of oncoming riders and drivers, and into the mirrors of the vehicle ahead. Glare reduces their ability to see for a moment, and a dazzled rider may swerve or slow suddenly. That is why the law asks you to dip your lights. Article 20(2) of Law 36/2024/QH15 requires operators to turn off high beams and use low beams when meeting pedestrians crossing the road, in populous areas with working street lighting, when meeting oncoming traffic and when changing direction at intersections. Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, as read on the Government portal, sets a fine of VND 200,000 to 400,000 for using high beam where low beam is required. Check the current text if a figure matters to you. If your low beam is aimed so high that it works like a high beam, you may dazzle people even when you have done the right thing with the switch.

### For the vehicle's lawful condition

Article 9 of the Law bans putting into traffic motor vehicles that do not meet the legal technical safety requirements, and bans mounting or using inappropriate audio and lighting devices that affect road traffic order and safety. A poorly aimed lamp is a safety defect, and an aftermarket lamp that is too bright or badly fitted can be an inappropriate lighting device. On a rental, you should not alter the bike's lights, and you should tell the shop if something looks wrong.

## What moves the aim on a scooter

The aim is not fixed for ever. Several things change it.

- **A load or a passenger.** Weight at the back lifts the front of the bike and tips the beam upward. Our guide to [checking a manual bike's lights before riding at night](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/checking-a-manual-bikes-lights-before-riding-at-night/) explains why a heavy load can send the beam higher, so ask staff to look at the aim with your load on the bike.

- **A knock or a fall.** A bump to the front, a drop at a stop or a slow-speed tip-over can shift the lamp in its housing.

- **A loose lamp or case.** Vibration can loosen the mounting, and the aim drifts. The housing should not rattle.

- **Tyre pressure and suspension.** Low pressure or worn suspension lets the front sink or rise differently, and the beam follows.

- **A replaced bulb or lamp.** A wrongly seated bulb can change the pattern.

- **Wear and rough roads.** Over a long period, rough roads and wear can move the adjustment.

None of these is unusual on a bike that has been rented many times. It is simply a reason to check.

## A simple check you can do without tools

You cannot measure the aim exactly without proper equipment, but you can spot a bad one.

1. **Choose a level place** facing a wall, a shuttered shop front or a garage door, a few metres away, in shade or after dusk.

2. **Put the bike upright on its stand,** or hold it upright with your weight on it, and make sure the tyres are properly inflated.

3. **Switch on the low beam** and look at the lit patch on the wall.

4. **Look at the shape and height.** The patch should be roughly even and sit lower on the wall than the lamp, with a cut-off line, not a spot that rises to the top of the wall or a patch so low that it lies on the floor.

5. **Compare** with another rental bike of the same type if the shop has one.

6. **Repeat with a passenger or luggage on the seat** if you will carry one.

7. **Test at dusk** in a lit and quiet street, and watch whether oncoming riders flash you or look away.

Our guides to [how to check a 50cc bike's headlight for night riding](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-check-a-50cc-bikes-headlight-for-night-riding/) and [scooter headlight checks for evening rentals](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/scooter-headlight-checks-for-evening-rentals/) describe the wall test in more detail.

This is a visual check, not a measurement. If the patch looks high, low, uneven or split, tell the shop.

## Do not adjust it yourself on a rental

Many bikes have an adjuster for the aim, but the correct setting depends on the model and its load. On a rental, adjusting it yourself can leave it worse and may count as altering the bike. Honda lists headlight aim among its routine maintenance items for scooters and motorcycles, and a mechanic can set it using the manufacturer's procedure. So report it, and ask the shop to correct it or give you another bike.

## What to ask the rental shop

Before you ride at night, ask:

1. **"Can we switch on the lights and look at the beam against a wall?"**

2. **"Is the aim right for a rider and a passenger?"** if you will carry one.

3. **"Can you check it with my luggage on?"**

4. **"If the beam is too high or too low, can you adjust it or swap the bike?"**

5. **"Is the lamp case firmly fixed?"**

## Riding when the aim is not perfect

If you find that the aim is slightly off and cannot be fixed at once:

- **Slow down,** because your sight distance is shorter than normal if the beam is low.

- **Use low beam in all the cases the law lists,** and dip early for oncoming traffic.

- **Avoid unlit roads** until it is corrected.

- **Keep your visor clean,** because scratches scatter light. Our guide to [safe scooter riding when visibility drops in rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/safe-scooter-riding-when-visibility-drops-in-rain/) explains visor care.

- **Do not ride at night at all** if the beam is clearly too high or too low and the shop cannot fix it. Take a different bike.

## If you are dazzled

When an oncoming vehicle's light is too bright:

1. **Look to the right edge of your lane,** not into the lights.

2. **Slow down,** and keep your line.

3. **Do not flash back or brake hard.** Honda's safe-riding guide advises riders to avoid looking straight at oncoming headlights, which dazzle.

4. **Wait for your eyes to recover** before speeding up.

5. **Increase your gap** to the vehicle ahead.

## Common mistakes

1. **Checking only that the light comes on,** not where it points.

2. **Ignoring a beam that dazzles others.**

3. **Forgetting the effect of a passenger or luggage.**

4. **Adjusting the lamp on a rental.**

5. **Fitting a brighter bulb or an extra lamp.**

6. **Using high beam in traffic** because the low beam is weak.

7. **Not telling the shop.**

## A quick checklist

1. **Aim matters as much as brightness.**

2. **Check the low beam on a wall, with your load.**

3. **Look for a patch that is too high, too low, uneven or split.**

4. **Do not adjust it yourself on a rental.**

5. **Ask the shop to check, fix or swap the bike.**

6. **Dip for oncoming traffic, and slow down if the beam is poor.**

7. **If dazzled, look to the right edge and slow down.**

## FAQ: headlight alignment at night

### What is headlight alignment?

It is the direction the headlight points, which decides where the beam falls on the road and whether it dazzles others.

### What happens if the beam is too high?

It dazzles oncoming riders and drivers and the mirrors of vehicles ahead, even on low beam.

### What happens if the beam is too low?

You see a shorter stretch of road, so you have less time to react to hazards.

### Does a passenger change the aim?

Yes. Weight at the back lifts the front and tips the beam upward, so ask for the aim to be checked with your load.

### Can I adjust the aim myself on a rental?

It is better not to. Tell the shop, and ask for it to be corrected or for another bike.

### What does the law say about lights at night?

Article 20 of Law 36/2024/QH15 requires front lights from 18:00 to 06:00 and in poor visibility, and low beam in four cases, including when meeting oncoming traffic.
