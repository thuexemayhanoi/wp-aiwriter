Ninh Binh is the classic first ride out of Hanoi. The limestone karsts of Tam Coc and Trang An, the old capital at Hoa Lu and the quiet dyke roads around Van Long are all within a morning's ride of the city, and the trip is flat the whole way. It is also the ride where visitors most often get the route wrong, because the fastest road on the map, the Hanoi–Ninh Binh expressway, is closed to motorbikes.

This guide covers the road you can actually use, how long it takes on a rental scooter, where to refuel, what the traffic is like, and how to plan the day so you arrive with energy to spare. Distances and times are planning figures as of October 2026; check a map app for closures on the day you ride. Nothing here is a substitute for riding to the conditions in front of you.

## The route in one paragraph

Leave Hanoi southwards on Giai Phong Street, which becomes National Road 1A (QL1A), and stay on QL1A through Thuong Tin, Phu Xuyen and Phu Ly (the capital of Ha Nam province) to Ninh Binh city. From the city, local roads lead a further ten to fifteen minutes to Tam Coc, Trang An and Hoa Lu. Budget about 95 km from central Hanoi to Ninh Binh city and roughly 100–105 km to Tam Coc, which on a 110–125 cc scooter means two and a half to three and a half hours of riding, plus stops. If you start from Nguyen Ha's shop in Long Bien, cross back to the west bank first (Chuong Duong or Vinh Tuy bridge) and join Giai Phong Street; it adds about twenty minutes.

## Why you cannot take the expressway

Vietnam's expressways are for cars, buses and trucks only. Motorbikes, mopeds and bicycles are not allowed on them, and the Pháp Vân–Cầu Giẽ and Cầu Giẽ–Ninh Bình expressways (part of the CT01 corridor) are no exception. Police check the on-ramps, the fines are real, and more importantly the road has no shoulder for a scooter doing a third of the speed of the traffic around it.

The practical consequence is that every motorbike rides the old road, QL1A, which runs parallel to the expressway for most of the way. It is slower, it passes through towns, and it carries trucks that are also avoiding tolls, but it is a legal, reasonably surfaced road with fuel, food and repair shops every few kilometres.

## What QL1A is like

The first 20 km out of Hanoi are the hardest part of the day. Giai Phong Street and the stretch past Thuong Tin are busy, with buses pulling in and out, scooters merging from every side road and a fair number of trucks. Keep to the right-hand lane, ride at the pace of the scooters around you, and expect to stop at a dozen traffic lights before the city thins out.

South of Thuong Tin the road opens up. It is a divided road for much of the way, with a slow lane that scooters share with agricultural vehicles. The surface is mostly good, with occasional patched sections and the ridged concrete shoulders that catch a front wheel if you drift onto them. Phu Ly, roughly two thirds of the way, is the one real town you ride through; its centre is a sensible place for a coffee and a fuel top-up.

From Phu Ly to Ninh Binh the road is quieter again, and the karst hills start to appear on the horizon. Turn off before the city for Trang An and Hoa Lu, or ride into Ninh Binh and out the other side for Tam Coc. Signposting is in Vietnamese and English at the main junctions.

## Timing the day

For a day trip, leave by seven. You are through the worst of the city traffic before the morning rush peaks, you reach the karsts before the tour buses, and you can be back on the road north by three, which puts you into Hanoi before the evening rush and before dark. Riding QL1A after dark is not pleasant: lighting is patchy, trucks run fast, and the slow lane fills with unlit bicycles.

If you want to see more than one site, stay the night. Tam Coc and Trang An villages are full of guesthouses, the dyke roads around Van Long are at their best in the early morning, and the ride back is far more enjoyable when you are not watching the clock.

A reasonable one-day plan: Hanoi 07:00, coffee in Phu Ly around 08:45, Trang An boat tour or Mua Cave by 10:00, lunch in Tam Coc, Bich Dong pagoda in the early afternoon, on the road home by 15:00, Hanoi by 18:00.

## Fuel, money and what to carry

A 110–125 cc scooter burns well under a full tank between Hanoi and Ninh Binh, so a single fill in Hanoi covers the outbound leg with margin. Fill up again in Phu Ly or Ninh Binh before you turn around; stations on QL1A are staffed and accept cash. Many now take QR payments too, but cash in small notes is still the safest choice.

Carry the bike's registration card (the shop gives you a copy with the contract), your licence and permit, and your passport or a photocopy of it, as police checkpoints are common on this road. Wear a proper helmet with the strap fastened, carry a rain poncho from May to October, and put sunscreen on your forearms: a 100 km ride in the sun burns them.

Nguyen Ha's rental contract allows the bike to leave the city for a day trip like this; tell the shop your plan when you book, confirm the return time, and remember that one rental day is 24 hours, so a 07:00 handover gives you until 07:00 the next morning. A return that is one to five hours late is charged at VND 30,000 per hour, and six hours or more counts as another day. ## Which bike to take

Any automatic scooter will do this ride. If you have the choice, a Honda Air Blade or a Yamaha scooter with a bigger engine is more comfortable at the 50–60 km/h pace of QL1A than a small Vision, especially two-up. A semi-automatic Honda Wave is also a good choice for experienced riders: it is light, economical and easy to repair anywhere along the road. We compare the two most common automatics in [Honda Vision vs Air Blade: which scooter to rent in Hanoi?](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/honda-vision-vs-air-blade-which-scooter-to-rent-in-hanoi/), and the [price guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/prices/) lists the reference rates: automatic scooters and manual bikes both start at VND 150,000 per day.

Check tyre pressure and tread before you leave; QL1A's shoulders are hard on soft tyres. The shop checks the bike at handover, but it does not hurt to ask for the pressures to be topped up if you mention the trip.

## Once you are in Ninh Binh

Keep the scooter: it is the best way to see the area. The roads between Trang An, Hoa Lu, Mua Cave and Tam Coc are small, quiet and flat, with rice paddies on both sides and karst towers everywhere you look. Parking at the main sites is in guarded lots for a small fee; take the ticket and keep it with the bike key.

Van Long nature reserve, about 25 km north of Ninh Binh city near the Cuc Phuong turn-off, is the quietest of the boat trips and sits on a lovely loop of dyke roads. Cuc Phuong National Park itself is a further 45 km west along a good road and makes a second day if you are staying over.

## Alternatives if you would rather not ride the national road

There is no shame in taking the bus or the train to Ninh Binh and renting a scooter there for the karst roads; many visitors do exactly that. The Hanoi–Ninh Binh train runs several times a day and takes a little over two hours, and the limousine buses from Hanoi's southern terminals are quicker still. The national road ride is for people who enjoy the riding itself, not only the destination.

## FAQ

### Can I ride a scooter on the Hanoi–Ninh Binh expressway?

No. Motorbikes and mopeds are prohibited on Vietnam's expressways, including the Pháp Vân–Cầu Giẽ–Ninh Bình corridor. Use National Road 1A, which runs parallel to it.

### How far is it from Hanoi to Tam Coc by road?

About 100–105 km from central Hanoi by QL1A, or roughly 95 km to Ninh Binh city. Plan on two and a half to three and a half hours of riding on a scooter.

### Is the road dangerous?

It is a busy national road with trucks, not a mountain pass. The risks are speed differences and trucks overtaking, not the road itself. Ride in the slow lane at the local pace, avoid the hours after dark, and stop every hour.

### Do I need to tell the rental shop I am leaving Hanoi?

Yes. Nguyen Ha allows day trips with its bikes, but the shop wants to know the plan and the return time, and it may suggest a more suitable bike for the distance.

### How much fuel will I use?

A 110–125 cc scooter covers the trip on well under a full tank each way; expect to fill up twice for the round trip, once in Hanoi and once in Ninh Binh or Phu Ly.
