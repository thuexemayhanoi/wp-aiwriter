If you collect a scooter in the afternoon and return it in the evening, the headlight is the part you will rely on most. Before you ride away, switch the ignition on, flick the headlight between low and high beam, look at the light on a wall or in a shaded corner, and check the high-beam indicator, tail light, brake light and both pairs of turn signals. Look at the lens for cracks and fogging, and ask the shop whether the headlight comes on automatically or needs a switch. In Hanoi in October the sun sets at about 17:30, so a ride that starts in daylight can end in full darkness within a few hours. The checks take about two minutes and are described by Honda as part of normal daily inspection. This guide explains how to do them, what each result means and when to ask for a different scooter.

## Why evening changes the rental

The shop is open from 09:00 to 21:00 daily, which means many renters ride at dusk or after dark without planning to. Timeanddate lists sunset in Hanoi at about 17:30 in mid-October, and average monthly figures put it at roughly 17:15 to 17:30 through November and December. A scooter collected at three or four o'clock has little daylight left.

Darkness changes the rental in three ways. A headlight that looked fine in the sun may turn out to be dim, off-centre or pointing at the road surface. Other road users see you mainly by your lights, so a dead tail light matters. And every small fault, such as an indicator that does not flash, is harder to notice when you are already riding.

Vietnam's road traffic law is relevant here. As of October 2026, Article 20 of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (No. 36/2024/QH15, effective 1 January 2025) requires drivers on the road to use their front lights between 18:00 and 06:00, and also when fog, smoke, dust, rain or other bad weather limits visibility. Treat dusk as the real start of the evening, whatever the clock says.

## The two-minute check before you leave the shop

Honda's global maintenance advice for riders sets out a daily lights check that works equally well at a rental counter. It says to switch the headlight from high to low to check that both work, and it notes that you can check this even in daylight by holding a hand in front of the light. Honda's Vietnamese training document adds that you should check the turn signals, brake light, high beam and low beam, and the horn.

Here is a routine that puts those steps in order:

1. **Ignition on, engine as the shop advises.** Some scooters light the instrument panel for a moment as part of a bulb check. Note which indicators come on and go out.
2. **Find out how the headlight works.** Ask whether it comes on with the engine or needs a switch, and where that switch is.
3. **Check the low beam.** Park facing a wall, or in a shaded corner, a few metres away. You should see an even patch of light, not a dark gap or a bright spot.
4. **Switch to high beam.** The patch should rise and widen, and the high-beam indicator on the dashboard should light.

5. **Switch back to low.** Make sure the indicator goes out.
6. **Walk round and check the rear.** Ask a staff member to squeeze the brakes while you watch the brake light, one lever at a time.
7. **Check the turn signals.** Left and right, front and rear, and make sure the dashboard indicator flashes.
8. **Look at the number plate light** if the scooter has one, and the horn.
9. **Look at the lens.** Look for cracks, deep scratches, broken mounting tabs or water inside.

## What each light should do

| Light | What a good result looks like | What is a reason to ask the shop |
|---|---|---|
| Low beam | An even patch of light on the wall or road, on both sides if there are two | Dim, flickering, one side dark or pointing at the ground |
| High beam | Brighter and higher, with the dashboard high-beam indicator lit | No change when the switch is moved, or indicator stays on in low beam |
| Tail light | Steady red glow when the lights are on | Dark, cracked lens or flickering |
| Brake light | Brighter red when either brake lever is squeezed | Only works with one lever, or not at all |
| Turn signals | Flash at a steady rate, front and back, with dashboard indicator | Fast flashing, one side dead or not flashing |
| Number plate light | Lit, if fitted | Dark, loose cover |

Honda's guidance is to check the tail and stop lights too, because they are out of sight when you ride and easy to forget. Honda also notes that LED lights burn out less often than conventional bulbs but should still be checked.

## High beam, low beam and dazzling others

Switching between beams is not only about seeing. The same law that requires headlights also says when to switch high beam off. According to the English text of Article 20, drivers must turn off high beams and turn on low beams when they meet pedestrians crossing the road, when travelling through populated areas with working street lighting, when meeting oncoming traffic (except where the median is anti-glare) and when changing direction at intersections.

For a Hanoi evening, that means low beam for nearly all city riding. Honda's training document gives similar advice: use high beam for better visibility on dark stretches, but switch to low beam when vehicles or pedestrians approach and when riding in an urban area. Keep the beam switch under your thumb and practise it before you set off.

Honda also lists headlight aim as a routine maintenance item on both scooters and motorcycles. If the beam looks too high or too low on the wall, mention it to the shop rather than trying to adjust it yourself.

## Misting inside the lens after rain or a wash

Hanoi evenings are often damp. The Honda Vision manual says that after washing the scooter, or when riding in rain or humid weather, the inside of the headlight lens can mist over temporarily. It says this clears a few minutes after the engine is started and the headlight is on. It also says that if you see a lot of water inside the lens, or ice, you should take the scooter to an authorised dealer.

For a renter, the practical rule is: a light haze that fades is normal, but a visible puddle or droplets that do not clear is a reason to ask for a different scooter. Do not wipe the inside of the lens or try to remove it.

## Dim, flickering or failing lights

A headlight that looks weak is not always a blown bulb. Honda's manuals suggest other causes to consider. The Air Blade manual includes a battery charge indicator and explains that if it lights while riding, the battery voltage may be low or its performance reduced, and the scooter should be checked by an authorised dealer. The Vision manual adds that on scooters with Idling Stop, the headlight stays on while the engine is paused, so the battery can run down and the engine may not restart. When the battery is weak, it says to switch the Idling Stop switch to its off position and have the battery checked by an authorised dealer.

If a light is dim, flickering or dead at the counter, treat it as the shop's job to fix before you leave. Replacing a headlight bulb on a modern scooter usually means removing parts, and the Vision manual's procedure involves removing the mirrors and the headlight assembly, so it is not a roadside repair. The same manual warns not to touch the glass of a bulb and to let it cool before replacing it.

If a light fails after you have left, pull over somewhere safe and well lit, then call the shop on 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969). ## Being seen, not just seeing

Honda's training document devotes a page to night riding. It advises checking that the lights work before you go and not riding at all if a light is broken, wearing bright or reflective clothing, riding slowly, keeping a safe distance and avoiding looking straight at oncoming lights, which can dazzle you. It also recommends the four-second following rule when visibility is poor or at night, which for a scooter at 20 km/h is about 22 m and at 40 km/h about 44 m.

Hanoi's night traffic seldom allows that much space, but the principle holds: ride slower than you would by day and leave the biggest gap you can. Our guide to [riding at night in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-at-night-in-hanoi/) looks at which streets to choose and which to avoid after dark.

## Timing the evening: pick-up and return

Plan the day around the shop's hours. It is open from 09:00 to 21:00, so if you want to return the scooter after dinner, aim to arrive well before 21:00. According to the rental terms, a late return is charged at VND 30,000 per hour for 1 to 5 hours and one extra rental day from 6 hours, and full details are in our [rental guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-guide/). A good habit is to do the lights check twice: once at handover, and again around 17:00 before the light fades, to find a problem while you still have time to ring the shop.

## Evening lights checklist

- Ask whether the headlight is automatic or switched.
- Test low and high beam on a wall; check the dashboard indicator.
- Test both brake levers against the brake light.
- Test left and right turn signals, front and rear.
- Look at the lens for cracks and water.
- Check the horn.
- Take a photo of the lights working at handover.
- Know the shop's closing time and your plan for the return.

## FAQ: headlight checks for evening rentals

### Do I need my headlight on during the day?

That depends on the scooter. Ask the shop whether the headlight is automatic. After 18:00, or when rain, fog or dust limits visibility, Article 20 of the 2024 law expects front lights to be on.

### Which beam should I use in the city?

Low beam in most Hanoi riding. Honda's training advice and the law both point to low beam near pedestrians, oncoming traffic, intersections and lit streets.

### Is mist inside the headlight a problem?

Light misting after rain or a wash is normal, according to the Honda Vision manual, and should clear after the engine and headlight have been running for a few minutes. A lot of water is a reason to ask for another scooter.

### Can I change a bulb myself?

No, not on a rental. It usually involves removing parts, and the shop should do it.

### What if the headlight fails while I am riding?

Pull over somewhere safe and lit, and call the shop. Do not carry on riding in the dark with a dead headlight.

### Can I ask the shop to test the lights with me?

Yes. A staff member can squeeze the brakes and work the switches while you check each light.
