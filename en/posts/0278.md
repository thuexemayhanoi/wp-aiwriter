An electric scooter rental contract should answer seven battery questions: what the battery level and charger were at handover, how and where you may charge, what counts as ordinary wear, what counts as damage from heat, water or a knock, who is responsible if the battery or charger goes missing, how the deposit is used and who helps if the battery runs flat. Nguyen Ha states publicly that battery level and charging arrangements are confirmed at handover for every electric model, but the full wording of an electric contract is something you read and clarify on the day, so this guide is a reading aid and not a copy of the shop's text. It uses VinFast's published warranty and charging terms to show how a manufacturer treats batteries, and it flags clearly where those terms belong to an owner and a maker and not to a renter and a shop.

## What the shop says publicly

Nguyen Ha's published facts are short. Electric models in its range include electric bicycles, 50cc-equivalent electric scooters and some VinFast electric motorbikes, and the bike you receive is confirmed when you book. For every electric model, the battery level and the charging arrangement are confirmed at handover, in the same way that fuel levels are recorded for petrol bikes. The cash deposit is VND 2,000,000 to 5,000,000 depending on the bike and the contract, one rental day is 24 hours, and late returns are charged by the hour for the first five hours and then as an extra day. The [prices page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/prices/) and the [FAQ](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/faq/) hold the current figures.

Everything else in this article is a question to ask or a clause to look for, not a statement about what your contract contains. ## Why batteries get their own clauses

On a petrol bike, the fuel is a consumable you top up. On an electric scooter, the battery is a major component that wears, can be damaged and cannot be topped up in two minutes. VinFast, for example, lists the battery as a separate line in its Evo200 brochure and offers a battery-subscription option alongside it, which shows how distinct the battery is from the rest of the machine. A rental contract that says nothing specific about it leaves both sides guessing.

Three kinds of problem recur: the battery was not as charged as you thought, the battery was harmed during the rental, or the battery or charger could not be accounted for at the end. Good clauses deal with each.

## Clause 1: the starting state

The handover record is the foundation. Look for wording or a handover sheet that records:

- **The battery level** at pickup, as a percentage or number of bars.
- **Which charger** you were given, and whether it is the one supplied with the scooter.
- **The condition of the battery area,** including the charging port, cover and any removable battery.
- **Any warning symbols** on the display at pickup.

Photograph the indicator and the charger yourself, even if the shop writes it down. A photograph with the date is the simplest evidence for a dispute about whether the scooter started with a full battery. VinFast's own guidance links to an article about false battery readings on electric scooters, so the figure is best recorded at a moment when staff can see it too.

## Clause 2: how and where you may charge

A contract may say which charger to use, where charging is allowed and whether charging unattended is prohibited. Even if it does not, public safety advice is clear on the main points: Vietnam's fire-prevention department says not to charge overnight or for more than eight hours at a stretch, to wait about 20 minutes after riding before plugging in, and and not to modify the vehicle's electrical system. VinFast's guidance says to use only the charger provided by the maker and to let a hot battery cool first.

Ask the shop whether your contract treats breaches of charging instructions as a reason to charge you for damage. If it does, you want to know the rules clearly in advance. ## Clause 3: ordinary wear versus damage

A battery loses capacity over time, and that is not the renter's fault. VinFast's warranty policy says as much: natural battery ageing and gradual loss of maximum capacity are excluded from its warranty, because they depend heavily on charging habits, charging current, the number of discharge cycles and ambient temperature. A different VinFast document, a 2023 brochure for the Evo200, describes a battery-subscription option under which the maker maintains or replaces the battery when its charging capacity falls below 70%. That arrangement is between VinFast and its customer and does not pass to a renter automatically, but it shows the idea: a battery that has simply aged is the owner's concern, and a battery that has been abused is something else.

A rental contract should therefore distinguish between the two. Questions to ask:

- **Is ordinary loss of capacity treated as wear** and not charged to you?
- **How would the shop decide** that a problem was caused by you and not by age?
- **Who decides the amount** if you are asked to pay, and is there a written estimate or a repair invoice?

Ask this plainly. A shop that rents electric scooters will have thought about it, and a clear answer is a good sign.

## Clause 4: heat, water and impact

Manufacturers list specific causes of battery damage that void the maker's cover, and renters should treat the same causes as things to avoid. VinFast's electric-motorbike warranty page excludes, among other things:

- **Swelling, cracking, smoke, fire or explosion** of the battery caused by parking in a high-temperature environment or fitting extra power-consuming devices.
- **Damage from use in flooded conditions.**
- **Damage from non-genuine parts or unauthorised repairs and modifications.**
- **Damage from misuse,** such as overloading or hard use on rough ground.
- **Damage from not following the storage instructions** during periods of non-use.

These are warranty terms for an owner, not a rental contract, and they say nothing about what Nguyen Ha will charge. They do show why the shop is likely to care about where you park in summer, whether you take the scooter through floodwater and whether you charge it with the right equipment. Our guide to [riding in Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) explains which streets flood, and Honda's advice on electric scooters adds that the battery must not be splashed with water and that a high-pressure washer can damage the electrical system, so do not wash the scooter yourself.

Ask: "If the battery swells or stops working because of heat or water, how is that treated, and who decides?"

## Clause 5: missing or damaged parts

The charger, cable and any removable battery are small, valuable and easy to lose. A clear clause says what you must return and what you pay if something is missing or damaged. Ask:

- **Is the cost of a missing charger or battery fixed in writing,** or decided afterwards?
- **Does the contract say who pays** if a removable battery is stolen from a parked scooter?
- **What is the process** if the charger fails through no fault of yours?

Do not accept a vague answer such as "we will see". A figure in writing, or a statement that the replacement cost will be shown on an invoice, protects both sides. ## Clause 6: deposit, late return and early return

Battery problems usually end up at the deposit. Nguyen Ha's cash deposit is separate from the rent, and the shop does not keep your original documents. The deposit can only be returned in full if the return check is clean, so the earlier clauses matter. Our guide to [how a rental deposit works in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbike-rental-deposit-hanoi/) explains what is usually deducted and how to ask for an itemised explanation.

Two further details can affect a battery rental:

- **Late return.** The shop's terms charge VND 30,000 per hour for one to five hours and an extra day from six hours. If the battery runs out and delays you, ask whether the same terms apply.
- **Early return.** Unused rent is not refunded, so returning early because the battery or charging is inconvenient does not reduce the cost.

## Clause 7: what happens if the battery runs flat

The contract or the shop's instructions should say who to call and what happens if you stop with an empty battery: whether staff collect the scooter, whether any transport charge applies and whether you may swap the bike. At minimum, save the shop's number, 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969), open 09:00 to 21:00 daily, and ask what to do outside those hours. ## FAQ: electric scooter contracts and batteries

### Does Nguyen Ha record the battery level at handover?

Yes. The shop confirms the battery level and charging arrangement for every electric model when you collect it.

### Will I be charged if the battery loses capacity during my rental?

Ordinary ageing is normally the owner's concern, but how the shop treats it is a contract question. Ask for the answer in writing.

### Is a heat-damaged battery my responsibility?

Manufacturers exclude heat damage from their own cover, so expect a shop to take it seriously. Ask how it is assessed.

### Can I use a different charger if mine goes missing?

Not without asking. Manufacturer and fire-prevention advice both stress the supplied charger.
