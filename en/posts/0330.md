Hanoi's large shopping centres are among the easiest places to leave a rented scooter, because they have dedicated motorbike yards, attendants and a ticket system, so you do not have to hunt for a legal pavement. The basics are simple: enter through the motorbike ramp or gate, take the ticket or have the plate recorded, park where the attendant points, note the zone, and keep the ticket safe until you leave. The details differ by mall, as rooftop, basement and ground-level yards all exist. This guide explains what official mall pages say, what to confirm on arrival and how to avoid the common slips, checked as of October 2026.

## What "ticketed parking" means at a Hanoi mall

On a public street, parking is a roadside arrangement, sometimes with a pavement attendant. At a mall, parking is a managed service. A barrier or attendant at the entrance records your arrival, usually by handing over a paper ticket or card, and the same operator checks it when you leave. Some operators have been adding automated systems: Vincom Retail's 2020 annual report says a smart parking system had been tested at its malls for automated basement parking and easier payment, although that wording concerns car parks and does not tell you how a particular mall handles bikes.

This matters for a rental because the ticket is your proof that the bike is yours and was left there. Treat it like a key. The rules and any fees are set by the mall operator, and what is free at one centre can be paid at the next. Always read the tariff board at the entrance, because it is the only reliable source for that day.

For a general comparison with street lots, see our guide to [parking a motorbike in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-motorbike-in-hanoi/).

## Three layouts you will meet

Hanoi's big malls differ in how they place motorbikes, and knowing the layout before you arrive saves a lot of circling.

**Outdoor and rooftop yards: AEON Mall Long Bien.** AEON's official figures for its mall at 27 Co Linh Road, Long Bien, list parking for about 1,000 cars and about 10,000 motorcycles. The mall's own parking page describes large outdoor parking with a roof, plus rooftop parking, and says the design avoids the accident risks it associates with basement ramps. It also provides slots for people with disabilities. A different AEON page, for the Ha Dong mall, describes free motorbike parking in outdoor areas at the front and back. That is another mall, so do not assume Long Bien has the same terms; check the tariff when you arrive.

**Basement yards: Vincom Mega Mall Royal City.** This large complex at 72A Nguyen Trai sits under residential towers on two basement levels. A 2012 Vincom presentation described seven mall entrances and five driveways to the parking basements, and a 2025 Vinpearl guide advises visitors to photograph or note their parking position because the underground area is vast with several exits. Opening hours given there are 10:00 to 22:00 on weekdays and 9:30 to 22:00 at weekends.

**Mixed levels: Lotte Mall West Lake.** Lotte World Aquarium's visitor page says guests can park either on the ground-level yard, reached through gates 4 or 5 on Lac Long Quan, or in the basements B1 and B2, which connect to the mall at basement level 1. Lotte Mall's own pages give a daily operating window starting around 9:30 and ending at 22:00.

## Step by step from entry to exit

1. **Approach the motorbike entrance.** Follow signs for xe may (motorbikes). Do not drive through a car ramp unless directed.
2. **Take the ticket or let the system record the plate.** Keep your hands on the bars until the barrier is clear; do not rush the gap.
3. **Park where the attendant directs.** Bikes are often packed in tight rows, so follow the attendant's instruction on which way to face and where to stand the bike.
4. **Note the zone and row.** Photograph a pillar sign or a bay number.
5. **Lock the steering and take the key.** Remove anything valuable from the basket and under the seat.
6. **Take the ticket with you**, in a pocket or wallet rather than on the bike.
7. **On exit, show the ticket, pay if a fee applies and wait for the barrier.** Check that the amount matches the tariff.

If you need to put a helmet somewhere, ask the attendant whether helmet storage is available. The shop provides one basic helmet per rider, which is a good reason not to lose it.

## What to do if you lose the ticket

Losing a ticket is the most common problem and the answer depends on the operator. In general, expect the exit staff to ask you to prove that the bike is yours, often with the vehicle's registration document and your own identification, and may charge a lost-ticket fee. That is a difficulty for a rental, where you hold neither the registration nor ownership.

Before you ride off, ask the shop what proof of rental to carry, such as the rental contract or a copy of the vehicle's registration if the shop provides one. Our [rental guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-guide/) explains what the paperwork covers, and the [contact page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/contact/) lists how to reach Nguyen Ha. On the day, treat a ticket as you would a passport photocopy: keep it separate from the bike.

If you lose it anyway, go to the exit office or customer-service desk before queuing, explain calmly and keep your identification ready. Do not argue with the barrier operator or drive off.

## Check the posted price against what you are asked to pay

Parking disputes in Hanoi are not unknown. Vietnam News reported in 2018 that at some street lots near Hoan Kiem Lake the amount collected was two to three times the amount printed on the ticket. That was a report about street lots, not malls, and the city has promoted automated fee collection since, but it is a sound reason to read the number on the ticket and compare it with what is requested. Mall operators generally post a tariff and issue printed receipts, which makes this easier. If the amount differs from the board, ask politely to see the tariff. Our guide to [paying for fuel, parking and tolls](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/paying-fuel-parking-tolls-hanoi-visitor/) lists what to carry for small payments.

## Opening times, evenings and rain

Malls close, and so do their yards. Hours quoted above run to 22:00, but cinemas, restaurants and the supermarket often keep different times, so check the specific venue and the lot's closing hour. Ask the attendant what happens to the yard after the mall shuts, so you are not surprised at the exit.

Rain makes a mall more attractive and a ride home more demanding. Wet ramps are slippery, and an underground ramp is the worst place to test a brake. Our guide to [riding in the Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) covers gear and the streets that flood. If the weather turns while you are inside, a taxi home is a reasonable choice, and you can collect the bike later within your rental period.

## A short checklist

- Look up the mall's opening hours and which entrance admits motorbikes.
- Read the tariff board and any free-parking conditions at the entrance.
- Photograph your zone or bay.
- Keep the ticket in your pocket, not on the bike.
- Leave valuables with you.
- Know how you will prove the bike is yours if the ticket goes missing.
- Allow time before closing to find the bike and queue at the exit.

## FAQ

### Is mall parking free for motorbikes?

It depends on the mall. AEON's Ha Dong page describes free parking, but the Long Bien page does not state fees. Check the tariff when you arrive, because terms change.

### Where do motorbikes park at AEON Mall Long Bien?

AEON describes roofed outdoor parking and rooftop parking, with capacity for about 10,000 motorcycles. Follow the entry signs and the attendants.

### What if the basement is confusing?

Photograph the pillar or bay number as you park and note which entrance you used. Large basements like Royal City's have multiple exits.

### Can I leave a helmet on the bike?

Do not leave it unsecured. Ask the attendant about storage, or carry it with you.

### What if the mall closes before I return?

Check the lot's closing time first. If you are caught after hours, go to the security desk rather than trying to force the barrier.
