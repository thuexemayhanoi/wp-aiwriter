Vietnam's road signs fall into five groups, and knowing the group tells you most of what you need to do before you have read the symbol. Under Article 11 of Law 36/2024/QH15, prohibitory signs indicate prohibitions, warning signs warn of possible dangerous circumstances, mandatory signs indicate commands that must be obeyed, indication signs give directions or necessary information and additional panels add explanations to the other four. For a rider the working rule is simple: a prohibition means do not, a warning means slow down and look, a mandatory sign means you must, an indication sign is information and a panel underneath changes the scope of the sign above it.

This guide explains the groups, the signs that matter most on a motorbike in Hanoi, how additional panels change a sign, what outranks a sign when signals disagree, what the law says about temporary signs and what to do when a sign, your navigation app and the traffic around you all seem to say different things. It is general information based on the law as of October 2026, not legal advice.

## The five groups, in plain terms

The law defines the groups by purpose, and the purpose is the best way to remember them.

| Group | What the law says it is for | What a rider does |
|---|---|---|
| Prohibitory signs | To indicate prohibitions | Do not do what the sign shows |
| Warning signs | To warn of possible dangerous circumstances | Slow down, look ahead and be ready |
| Mandatory signs | To indicate commands to be obeyed | Do what the sign commands |
| Indication signs | To provide traffic directions or necessary information | Use the information, such as a direction or a facility |
| Additional panels | To give supplementary commentary on the other signs | Read them with the sign above, because they limit or extend it |

In the national technical regulation on road signs, each group has its own shape and colour scheme, which is why you can often tell a prohibition from a piece of information at a distance before you can read the picture. The Law (Article 11, clause 13) leaves the regulation itself to the Minister of Transport, and the current national technical regulation is QCVN 41:2024/BGTVT. Older guides, books and phone apps may use earlier editions of the regulation, with different numbering or codes, so learn signs by their picture and meaning and not by a number.

## Where signs sit in the order of priority

A sign is only one of several kinds of signal on a road. Article 11 says road users must obey signals in this order:

1. commands of traffic guards;

2. traffic light signals;

3. road signs;

4. road markings and other signs on the road surface;

5. delineator posts, protective walls, barriers, reflective nails, reflective posts, kilometre markers and H beams;

6. audible signalling devices.

So if a traffic police officer waves you on at a red light, you follow the officer. If a sign says one thing and a road marking another, the sign comes first. When two signals genuinely conflict and you cannot tell which governs, slow down and stop in a safe place rather than guess.

The law adds a rule that matters near roadworks. When a fixed sign and a temporary sign stand in the same place and mean different things, you obey the temporary sign (Article 11, clause 12).

## The prohibitory signs a rider meets most

Prohibitory signs are the ones that lead to most fines, because they forbid something that is easy to do without noticing. The ones that matter most on a scooter in Hanoi are:

- **Entry and direction bans.** A sign closing a street to vehicles from your direction, which is typically what a one-way street looks like when seen from the wrong end. Hanoi's central streets include many one-way systems, and the sign is often the only warning. Our guide to [one-way street patterns around Hoan Kiem](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/one-way-street-patterns-around-hoan-kiem/) shows how common they are.

- **Vehicle-type bans.** Signs that forbid certain kinds of vehicle on a road or lane. A symbol that shows a motorcycle means motorcycles are not allowed.

- **Turn bans.** Signs that forbid a left turn, a right turn or a U-turn. Law 36/2024 also bans U-turns in a long list of places, including pedestrian crossings, bridges and narrow roads, unless a traffic guard or a temporary sign allows it (Article 15, clause 4).

- **Stopping and parking bans.** Signs that forbid stopping, or forbid parking. The two are different. Where parking is banned, a brief stop to drop someone off may be allowed, while a ban on both stopping and parking is stricter. Check the picture, and see our guide to [parking a motorbike in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-motorbike-in-hanoi/).

- **Speed limits.** A sign showing a number sets a maximum speed for the road ahead. Our guide to [speed limits for motorbikes in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/speed-limits-motorbikes-vietnam/) explains how the limits work.

- **Overtaking and passing bans,** where visibility or width is limited.

If you see a prohibition and you are unsure what it forbids, assume it applies to you, slow down and stop in a safe place to think. A sign you do not understand is a better reason to stop than a reason to guess.

## Warning signs: what to do when one appears

Warning signs do not forbid anything. They tell you what is coming, and the right response is almost always to slow down early. Article 12 of the Law lists places where operators must observe the surroundings and slow down or stop, including where warning signs or hurdles are on the road, at intersections and level crossings, on narrow roads, bends and slopes, in areas with schools, hospitals and crowded public works, and in rain, wind, fog or on slippery surfaces. A warning sign is the road's way of telling you that you have entered one of those situations.

Typical warnings for a rider include a bend, a junction, a pedestrian crossing, a railway crossing, a slippery road, a narrowing, roadworks and a school. Treat each as a request to lower your speed and widen your attention, whatever the speed of the traffic around you. Our guide to [riding in Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) is a good companion for the slippery-road warning.

## Mandatory signs: commands to follow

Mandatory signs tell you what you must do, for example to go in a particular direction, keep to a lane or a side, or use a particular route. They matter at junctions, where choosing the wrong lane or direction is a common mistake.

## Signs that decide who gives way

One sign changes who gives way. Under Article 22 of the Law, at an intersection with no "roundabout ahead" sign, a vehicle gives way to the vehicle approaching from the right. At an intersection with "roundabout ahead" signs, a vehicle gives way to the vehicle approaching from the left. So the presence of one sign reverses the rule. Our guide to [crossing Hanoi intersections](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/crossing-hanoi-intersections/) explains how this works in practice.

Where a road is marked as a priority road with the proper signs, a vehicle coming from a non-priority road or a feeder road must give way to vehicles on the priority or main road from any direction (Article 22, clause 1).

## Indication signs: useful but not a command

Indication signs give directions and information: where a road leads, where a facility is, which lane goes where. They are not commands, but they help you choose a lane early, which is worth more than almost any other habit on a busy road. Reading a direction sign in good time lets you move to the right lane without a last-second swerve. Signs at ramps and approaches can also show that a road is closed to motorbikes. ## Additional panels: read the box under the sign

An additional panel under a sign limits or extends it. Common uses include a distance (the sign applies for so many metres), a direction (the sign applies to a particular direction), a lane (the sign applies to one lane) and a time (the sign applies only during certain hours). The law describes the group simply as giving supplementary commentary. For a rider, the lesson is to read the whole assembly.

- **No panel:** the sign applies on its own.

- **A time panel:** the sign applies during the times shown, and not outside them.

- **A lane panel:** the sign applies to the lane shown.

- **A distance panel:** the sign applies from the sign for that distance.

- **A panel in Vietnamese text:** if you cannot read it, treat the sign as applying in full until you can confirm otherwise, or stop and ask.

A common trap is a parking sign with a time panel, because it may allow parking at some hours and ban it at others. When in doubt, do not park there.

## When your navigation app and the sign disagree

The sign wins. A navigation app does not know about a one-way street changed last week, a temporary roadworks sign or a motorbike ban. If the app tells you to turn where a sign forbids it, do not turn. Go on to a place where you can stop safely, and re-plan. Our guides to [how to check if a navigation app suggests an expressway](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-check-if-a-navigation-app-suggests-an-expressway/) and [how to use offline maps for countryside rides near Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-use-offline-maps-for-countryside-rides-near-hanoi/) explain how to plan routes that avoid the problem. Law 36/2024 also bans holding and using a phone or other electronic device in the hand while operating a vehicle (Article 9, clause 6), so re-plan only when stopped.

## What happens if you miss or ignore a sign

Failing to obey a sign is an offence. Under Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, as read on the Government portal, the general offence of not obeying the instructions of signs or road markings carries a fine of VND 200,000 to 400,000 for riders of motorcycles and mopeds.

Specific offences, such as turning where a sign prohibits it, are dealt with in separate, higher items, and the fine for turning where a sign forbids it is VND 600,000 to 800,000. For offences found through technical devices, the decree provides for a notice asking the vehicle owner and related persons to come and settle it, so ask the shop how a fine notice would be handled before you rent. As of October 2026 the amounts above are the figures in the decree, and you should check the current text if a fine matters to you.

## Habits for reading signs while riding

1. **Look ahead, not at the wheel in front.** Signs are placed for traffic that is looking up the road.

2. **Slow before the sign,** not after it. Most mistakes happen because a rider reads a sign too late to act.

3. **Read the panel as well as the sign.**

4. **Do not follow other riders.** What local riders do is not a guide to what the sign allows.

5. **Treat roadworks and temporary signs as the most current signal.**

6. **When unsure, stop in a safe place,** check, and go on.

7. **Plan the route before you start,** so that signs confirm your plan instead of surprising you.

## A quick checklist

1. **Know the five groups: prohibit, warn, command, inform, explain.**

2. **Remember the order: traffic guard, light, sign, marking.**

3. **Read the panel under the sign.**

4. **Learn signs by picture, not by number.**

5. **Obey the temporary sign over the fixed one.**

6. **Let the sign beat the navigation app.**

7. **Stop somewhere safe if you are unsure.**

## FAQ: road signs for motorbike riders in Vietnam

### How many groups of road signs are there?

Five under Law 36/2024/QH15: prohibitory, warning, mandatory, indication and additional panels.

### Which comes first, a sign or a traffic light?

A traffic light. The order of priority is traffic guards, then traffic lights, then road signs, then road markings and other signs on the road surface.

### What if a temporary sign contradicts a fixed one?

Obey the temporary sign. The law says so where both stand in the same place and mean different things.

### What does a "roundabout ahead" sign change?

It changes who gives way. With the sign, you give way to vehicles approaching from the left. Without it, you give way to vehicles approaching from the right.

### Can I rely on my navigation app instead of the signs?

No. Signs and the law come first. Stop in a safe place to re-plan, and do not hold or use a phone while riding.

### What is the fine for ignoring a sign?

The general offence carries a fine of VND 200,000 to 400,000 for motorbike riders under Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, and specific offences are priced separately. Check the current text.
