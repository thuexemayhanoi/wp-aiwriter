Buying petrol is the one errand every scooter renter in Hanoi has to do, usually within the first hour of the rental, and it is easier than it looks from the kerb. Stations are staffed, the price is the same at every pump of a brand, and an attendant does the filling while you stay with the bike. What has changed recently is the fuel itself: since 1 June 2026 the ordinary petrol sold across Vietnam has been E10, a blend containing up to ten per cent ethanol, alongside the familiar E5 RON 92. This guide explains, as of October 2026, which grade to choose for a rental scooter, how pump prices are set, how to pay, how far a tank will take you, and how fuel works under a Nguyen Ha rental contract, from the handover level to the small charge for bringing a bike back empty.

## Start with the fuel you are handed

Rental scooters in Hanoi are not handed over with a full tank, and it is worth knowing why before you ride away. At Nguyen Ha a petrol bike leaves the shop with roughly 0.5 to 2 litres in the tank and is expected to come back with about 0.5 to 1 litre. The level is noted at handover, and returning the bike completely empty costs VND 20,000. There is no fuel surcharge built into the daily rate; you simply buy what you use.

Half a litre will not take you far. A small automatic scooter uses around two litres per 100 km in city traffic, so the lowest handover level is enough for a short ride across town and not much more. Make the nearest petrol station your first stop, and film the fuel gauge at handover along with the rest of the bike, as described in our [10-minute rental scooter inspection](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-scooter-inspection-checklist/). If you rent an electric scooter, none of this applies: the battery level and the charging arrangement are confirmed at handover instead, and our guide to [renting an electric scooter in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/renting-an-electric-scooter-in-hanoi/) explains range and charging.

## What the pumps sell in 2026: E10 and E5

Under Circular 50/2025/TT-BCT of the Ministry of Industry and Trade, in force since 1 January 2026, unleaded petrol for petrol engines has been blended into E10 nationwide from 1 June 2026, while E5 RON 92 may continue to be blended and sold until the end of 2030. In practice the pumps at a Hanoi station now show two petrol grades. Petrolimex, the largest chain, labels them E10 RON 95-III and E5 RON 92-II; other brands use similar names. The number after RON is the octane rating, and E10 or E5 tells you the ethanol share. Pumps marked DO are diesel and must never go into a scooter.

Which should you choose? Honda Vietnam has stated that all the motorbikes it has made, assembled, imported and officially distributed since 1997 can run on E10, provided the fuel has an octane rating of at least 91 and no more than ten per cent ethanol. Both grades on sale meet that, so for the Honda Vision, Air Blade, Lead or Wave that make up most rental fleets, either is fine. E5 RON 92 is usually a little cheaper; E10 RON 95 has the higher octane rating. For other makes, follow the rental shop's advice, and if you are unsure, ask the attendant for the same grade the shop uses.

## How pump prices are set

Petrol prices in Vietnam are not a free-for-all. As of October 2026, retail prices are capped through a state price-management mechanism run by the Ministry of Industry and Trade with the Ministry of Finance, under Decree 83/2014/ND-CP as amended, most recently by Decree 80/2023/ND-CP, with further changes to the rules from Resolution 55/NQ-CP effective 19 March 2026. The caps are reviewed on a regular cycle that normally falls on Thursday afternoons, and the big retailers announce their new prices at the same time. A new decree on petrol trading that would replace this system was still at draft stage in mid-2026, so check for changes if you are reading this later.

Two consequences matter for a rider. First, there is no point shopping around within the city: a brand's stations in Hanoi sell at the same price, and the main brands are within a few hundred dong of each other. Prices are set for two zones, and Hanoi is in zone 1, the cheaper one. Second, the price changes from week to week, which is why this guide does not quote a figure. The current price per litre is shown on every pump, and Petrolimex publishes each adjustment on its website. For a sense of scale, filling the tank of a small scooter from near-empty costs less than a single day's rental.

## Finding a petrol station

Hanoi has petrol stations on almost every main road, run by Petrolimex, PVOIL and several smaller brands. They sit on the arterial roads rather than in the narrow lanes, so fill up on a main street before you head into the Old Quarter or a residential maze. Searching for "petrol station" in a map app works, and locals call them "cay xang". Most city stations open from early morning until late evening, and some on the main roads stay open around the clock, but do not count on finding one open at 2 a.m. in a quiet district.

On a day trip the picture changes. National roads out of Hanoi have stations at regular intervals, but in hill country they thin out and may close early in the evening. On rides like [Hanoi to Ninh Binh](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-to-ninh-binh-by-motorbike/) or the two-day trip to [Mai Chau](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-to-mai-chau-by-motorbike/), fill up whenever the gauge falls to half rather than waiting for the warning light.

## Filling up, step by step

The routine is the same at every staffed station:

1. Ride up to a petrol pump, not one marked DO, and stop beside it. Switch the engine off; signs at the pumps also ask you not to smoke or use your phone, and staff may remind you.

2. Open the filler cap. On some scooters it sits under the seat, so you get off and open the seat with the key; on others it is behind a small lid at the front. Ask at handover where yours is and how it opens, so you are not fumbling at the pump.

3. Tell the attendant what you want. "Full" is understood everywhere, or you can ask for a round sum such as VND 50,000 or VND 100,000, which is easier when paying cash.

4. Check that the pump display starts from zero, and watch the litres and the amount rise. Stop the attendant if the tank is close to the brim; an overfilled tank can spill petrol when the cap is closed or when the scooter stands in the sun.

5. Close the cap firmly, pay, and wait for your change before you start the engine.

Cash is accepted everywhere, and many stations also take bank cards or QR-code bank transfers, but small change and a few low-value notes make the stop quicker. Since 1 June 2025, under Decree 70/2025/ND-CP, retail petrol sales have had to be recorded on an electronic invoice for each sale, so you can ask for a receipt if you want one.

## How far a tank goes

The figures below are the approximate tank sizes and fuel-consumption figures published for the two most common rental scooters. Treat them as a guide, not a promise: stop-start traffic, a passenger, hills and hard acceleration all raise consumption.

| Scooter | Fuel tank (approx.) | Published consumption | Realistic range in city riding |
|---|---|---|---|
| Honda Vision 110 | about 4.9 litres | around 1.8 l/100 km | 150–200 km |
| Honda Lead 125 | about 6 litres | around 2.1 l/100 km | 180–250 km |

The realistic range is deliberately lower than the arithmetic suggests, because nobody rides a city scooter at the test-cycle figure and because it is unwise to run a tank dry. For most visitors riding around Hanoi, one full tank lasts several days. On a longer trip, think in halves: fill up at half a tank and you will never be hunting for a station at dusk.

## Fuel and your rental contract

The fuel rules at Nguyen Ha are short. The handover level is recorded; you bring the bike back with roughly 0.5 to 1 litre; an empty tank at return costs VND 20,000. You do not need to return the tank full, so on the last day buy only what you need to get back. The [rental guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-guide/) and the [FAQ](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/faq/) cover the rest of the return routine, and our article on [how a rental deposit works](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbike-rental-deposit-hanoi/) lists what the deposit can be used for.

If you run out, switch off, get off and push the scooter carefully along the edge of the road to the nearest station, rather than riding on the pavement. In rural areas you may see petrol sold in bottles at small roadside shops. It is outside the licensed station network, and its quality is unknown, so use it only to reach a proper station. If the scooter will not restart after refuelling, phone the shop on 0334 699 969 before anyone starts taking it apart.

## FAQ

### Which petrol should I put in a rental scooter in Vietnam?

Either of the two grades on sale, E10 RON 95 or E5 RON 92, suits the Honda scooters that make up most rental fleets; Honda Vietnam says its motorbikes made since 1997 can use E10. Never use the pump marked DO, which is diesel.

### How much does petrol cost in Hanoi?

Prices are capped by the state and adjusted on a regular cycle, usually on Thursday afternoons, so they change from week to week. The current price per litre is on every pump and on Petrolimex's website. Filling a small scooter from near-empty costs less than a day's rental.

### Can I pay by card at a petrol station?

Often, yes: many stations accept bank cards and QR-code transfers. Cash works everywhere, so keep some small notes for fuel.

### How far can a Honda Vision go on a full tank?

On paper well over 200 km, but in Hanoi traffic plan on 150 to 200 km. Refill at half a tank on day trips.

### What happens if I return the scooter with an empty tank?

At Nguyen Ha you are expected to return a petrol bike with roughly 0.5 to 1 litre in the tank. Returning it completely empty costs VND 20,000.
