To check whether a road restricts motorbikes, use four sources in order: the law for permanent bans, the signs and markings on the ground for what applies right now, official notices for temporary or time-limited changes, and the rental shop for local knowledge. A navigation app is a convenience, not an authority. The law gives one clear permanent rule. Article 25 of Law 36/2024/QH15 says that motorcycles and mopeds, like low-speed four-wheeled motor vehicles, rudimentary vehicles such as bicycles and pedestrians, are not allowed to move on expressways. Everything else depends on signs, lane markings and notices, and the law gives a sign priority over a marking and a temporary sign priority over a fixed one in the same place (Article 11).

This guide gives a checking routine you can use before a ride and on the road, explains each source and how far to trust it, covers Hanoi's pilot low-emission zone as of October 2026, shows how to read a navigation app critically and says what to do if you find yourself on a banned road. It complements our route-planning guide and ring-road guide by focusing on how to verify. It is general information, not legal advice.

## The four sources, in order of authority

| Source | What it tells you | How far to trust it |
|---|---|---|
| The law | Permanent national rules, such as the ban on motorcycles and mopeds on expressways | Highest, but only for what it covers |
| Signs and markings at the road | What applies at that spot, now | Final word on the spot, within the order of priority in Article 11 |
| Official notices | Temporary closures, time limits, pilots and works | Reliable when from the authority; check the date |
| Navigation apps and maps | A suggested route | A guide only, and often wrong about motorbike restrictions |

The rental shop and local riders are a fifth, practical source. Ask them, but confirm with signs.

## Source 1: the law, for permanent bans

Article 25 of the Law, on traffic on expressways, says in clause 3 that special-purpose vehicles with a design speed below the expressway minimum, motorised four-wheeled passenger and cargo vehicles, motorcycles and mopeds and the like, rudimentary vehicles and pedestrians may not move on expressways, except persons and vehicles serving management and maintenance. So:

- **If a road is an expressway,** a motorbike may not use it, whatever size it is, and whatever the app says.

- **Expressways are identified by signs at the ramps** and by name. Our guide to [Hanoi's ring roads and highways](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-ring-roads-highways-motorbikes-allowed/) explains which roads allow motorbikes, and our guide to [how to check if a navigation app suggests an expressway](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-check-if-a-navigation-app-suggests-an-expressway/) explains how to catch a bad suggestion.

- **A road with the same name can have different parts.** Some roads have a ground-level carriageway open to motorbikes and an elevated section that is not. Check the sign at each ramp.

Other permanent rules apply by lane or vehicle type, and they are shown on signs. Article 13(4) says that for a lane exclusively reserved for a certain type of vehicle, operators of other types may not enter it, and Article 18 bans stopping and parking on roads exclusively reserved for buses.

## Source 2: signs and markings on the ground

The sign at the entrance is the most direct evidence. Article 11 sets the order of priority: traffic guards, traffic lights, road signs, road markings and then other devices. So a sign beats a marking, and a guard beats both.

- **Look for prohibitory signs** at the start of a road or section. A sign with a motorcycle or moped symbol under a red bar or border means that type of vehicle may not enter. Our guide to [understanding prohibited vehicle symbols on Vietnamese signs](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/understanding-prohibited-vehicle-symbols-on-vietnamese-signs/) explains how to tell them apart.

- **Look for supplementary panels** that limit a sign by time, distance, direction or lane.

- **Look for one-way signs.** A one-way street is closed to you from the wrong end. Our guide to [one-way street patterns around Hoan Kiem](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/one-way-street-patterns-around-hoan-kiem/) shows how common they are.

- **Look at the markings** for reserved lanes. Our guide to [how to read motorbike lane markings in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-read-motorbike-lane-markings-in-hanoi/) explains the lines.

- **Check for temporary signs.** Article 11(12) says that if a fixed sign and a temporary sign in the same place mean different things, you obey the temporary one.

- **Look for the end of the restriction.** Some signs have an end sign. Others apply until the next junction, so do not assume a restriction has ended just because you cannot see another sign.

Our guide to [understanding common motorbike road signs in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/understanding-common-motorbike-road-signs-in-vietnam/) covers the sign system as a whole.

## Source 3: official notices for temporary and time-limited changes

Some restrictions change, so a sign you saw last week may not be there, and a road open yesterday may be closed today.

- **Roadworks and events** can close or narrow roads.

- **Floods and storms** close underpasses and low roads.

- **Bridge works and traffic organisation** change from time to time. Our guides to [what to check before crossing major Hanoi bridges](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-to-check-before-crossing-major-hanoi-bridges/) and [Long Bien Bridge area: where a scooter can legally travel](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/long-bien-bridge-area-where-a-scooter-can-legally-travel/) describe examples.

- **Pilot schemes** such as the low-emission zone have their own dates, areas and hours.

For these, look for notices from the city authorities and for current reports in the local press, and check the date. A notice that is not dated, or a social-media post of unknown origin, is not reliable.

## Hanoi's low-emission zone pilot, as of October 2026

Hanoi has begun a phased low-emission zone inside Ring Road 1, and it is easy to misread. Vietnamese official and press reports describe the scheme this way.

- **There is no blanket ban on petrol motorbikes inside Ring Road 1.** The city said on 1 July 2026 that it was not banning all petrol motorcycles, mopeds and cars across Ring Road 1.

- **Phase 1, from 1 July to 31 December 2026,** applies to Area 1 and Area 2 in Hoan Kiem ward. In Area 1, all road vehicles, including motorbikes and cars, are barred from 19:00 to 24:00 on Fridays, Saturdays and Sundays, except priority vehicles. This is the area around the lake's pedestrian streets.

- **Peak hours in the scheme** are defined as 06:00 to 09:00 and 16:00 to 19:30 daily, and some trucks and large vehicles face time limits.

- **Later phases** extend the zone to Hoan Kiem and Cua Nam in 2027 and to all of Ring Road 1 from 1 January 2028, with emission requirements for motorcycles and mopeds in the last phase.

- **Boundaries and details** are set out in the scheme and the decision that approved it, and they may change.

So, for a visitor in October 2026, the practical rule is to check the evening and weekend closures near Hoan Kiem Lake, follow the signs and ask the shop. ## Source 4: navigation apps, used carefully

Apps are useful for finding your way, and they can be wrong about restrictions.

- **An app may not know about a motorbike ban,** a one-way change, a temporary closure or a restricted lane.

- **It may route you onto an expressway,** an elevated deck or a ramp that is closed to motorbikes.

- **It may route you through a narrow alley,** which is allowed in law but difficult in practice.

- **It may be out of date.** Maps lag behind signs.

So:

1. **Set the vehicle mode to motorbike or two-wheeler,** if your app has one, and choose "avoid highways".

2. **Look at the route before you start,** and compare it with the road names you know are restricted. Our guide to [motorbike navigation near Hanoi: toll roads and detours](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbike-navigation-near-hanoi-toll-roads-and-detours/) explains what to check.

3. **When the app and a sign disagree, the sign wins.** Stop in a safe place and re-plan. Law 36/2024/QH15 bans holding and using a phone while operating a vehicle (Article 9), so re-plan when stopped.

4. **Download an offline map** for rides outside the city. Our guide to [how to use offline maps for countryside rides near Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-use-offline-maps-for-countryside-rides-near-hanoi/) explains how.

## A checking routine

Before you ride:

1. **List the roads on your route,** and mark any that you know are expressways, elevated decks or bridges.

2. **Check each against the law and the ring-road guide.**

3. **Check for notices** about closures, works, the low-emission pilot or events on your date.

4. **Ask the shop,** on 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969), open 09:00 to 21:00 daily, whether any road on your route is a problem for your bike.

5. **Plan an alternative** for each doubtful stretch. Our guide to [motorbike-friendly alternatives to highway routes near Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbike-friendly-alternatives-to-highway-routes-near-hanoi/) lists options.

On the road:

1. **Read the sign at every junction and ramp** that you are not sure about.

2. **If a sign shows a motorbike or moped symbol with a bar,** do not enter.

3. **If a road looks like an expressway** (barriers, no crossings, fast traffic, ramps), do not enter.

4. **If you are unsure, stop in a safe place** and check, or take a different road.

## If you enter a banned road by mistake

Mistakes happen, especially at ramps.

1. **Do not stop in the lane,** and do not reverse.

2. **Do not make a U-turn.** Article 15 bans U-turns on expressways, on bridges and in several other places, unless a traffic guard or a temporary sign allows them.

3. **Keep to the right, slow down and switch on your hazard lights** if the road is fast.

4. **Leave at the next legal exit,** and then re-plan. If you cannot, stop at the roadside as far from traffic as possible, as Article 25 allows for technical incidents and force majeure, and call the shop and the emergency number 112 if you are in danger.

5. **Be aware of the penalty.** Vietnamese legal publications report that entering a road where a sign prohibits your vehicle type is fined under Decree 168/2024/ND-CP at VND 2,000,000 to 3,000,000, with licence points deducted. Check the current text.

6. **Tell the shop** if you receive a record or a notice.

## A quick checklist

1. **Expressways are closed to motorcycles and mopeds.**

2. **The sign at the road decides, not the app.**

3. **Temporary signs beat fixed ones.**

4. **Check notices for the date of your ride.**

5. **Near Hoan Kiem Lake, check the evening and weekend closures.**

6. **Ask the shop about doubtful stretches.**

7. **If you enter a banned road by mistake, do not U-turn. Leave at the next legal exit.**

## FAQ: checking road restrictions for motorbikes

### Are motorbikes allowed on expressways in Vietnam?

No. Article 25 of Law 36/2024/QH15 says motorcycles and mopeds are not allowed to move on expressways.

### Can I rely on Google Maps or another app?

Not alone. Apps can route motorbikes onto roads that are closed to them. Check the signs, and set the app to avoid highways.

### Is there a ban on petrol motorbikes in central Hanoi?

Not a blanket one. As of October 2026, the low-emission pilot in Hoan Kiem bars all vehicles in one area on Friday to Sunday evenings from 19:00 to 24:00, and later phases are planned. Check the current notices.

### What if a fixed sign and a temporary sign disagree?

Obey the temporary sign (Article 11(12)).

### Where can I ask about a doubtful road?

The rental shop, on 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969), and the local authorities' notices. Confirm with signs on the ground.

### What if I enter a banned road?

Do not stop or U-turn. Keep right, slow down and leave at the next legal exit, then re-plan.
