If a rental electric scooter is caught in heavy rain or a flooded street, the order of actions is: get yourself and the bike out of the water, switch off, put the side stand down on firm ground, do not plug anything in, take photographs and call the shop. Do not keep riding to see whether it still works, do not hose it and do not charge it until the shop has looked at it. Electric scooters are built to resist rain and splashes, and some makers test them for temporary immersion, but a wet bike is still a high-voltage machine whose condition you cannot judge by eye. This guide sets out what to do in the water, in the first minutes afterwards and over the following day, based on VinFast's manual and public guidance, with the limits of those sources made clear.

## First, what "waterproof" does and does not promise

Makers usually quote an ingress protection (IP) code. Under the IEC 60529 standard the second digit describes protection against water, and the IEC's own explanation of IP67 is that it is dust-tight and resistant only to temporary immersion. The standard's minimum for that grade is submersion to 1 metre for 30 minutes.

VinFast's published figures are more conservative. Its FAQ says its e-scooters use an IP57 motor and IP67 batteries and can operate stably when submerged to 0.5 metres for up to 30 minutes, and its Evo200 Lite brochure makes the same claim for the IP67 standard. Those are manufacturer test claims for specific models, not permission to ride through floods, and your rental may be a different model or maker. The Evo 200 manual itself says that riding in storms or floods is dangerous for the rider and can damage the electrical system, and that you should avoid riding in water deeper than the motor axle. That is the line to remember: shallow splashes are one thing, standing water above the hubs is another.

## In the water: what to do right now

1. **Slow down before you reach water you cannot see through.** A flooded Hanoi street can hide potholes, open drains and kerbs. If you cannot see the road surface, you cannot judge the depth.
2. **Do not ride through water that reaches the motor, the footboard or the battery area.** Turn back or wait. Our guide to [riding in Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) covers the streets that flood and the season.
3. **If the bike stops in water, do not keep pressing the throttle.** Switch off, put the stand down if the ground is firm enough, and move yourself first.
4. **Get the bike onto higher, dry ground, pushing slowly if it must be moved.** Do not drag or tow it. The manual warns that dragging the vehicle along the road can seriously damage its electrical system.
5. **Do not touch damaged wiring, connectors or a cracked battery cover.** The manual describes voltages above 60 volts as hazardous.

If you are in danger, your own safety comes before the bike's, and you can call the shop afterwards.

## Ordinary heavy rain, without flooding

Most wet rides are not floods, just a downpour on a busy street. The bike is designed for that, but your riding needs to change. Slow down, because tyres grip less and your stopping distance grows. Brake earlier and more gently, using both levers. Keep clear of painted lines, metal covers and the oily middle of a lane, which are slippery when wet. Switch on the lights so that others can see you, and wipe your visor or glasses when you can. If rain is so heavy that you cannot see, pull in somewhere safe and wait. Moving off the road costs a few minutes, while a skid costs far more. Afterwards, park where the bike will not stand in running water, and tell the shop about any warning symbol that appeared during the ride.

## The first ten minutes afterwards

Once you are somewhere safe and the bike is switched off:

- **Do not try to start it again at once.** Repeated attempts can turn a minor problem into a larger one if water has entered a connector.
- **Look, without poking.** Is there standing water in the footwell, in the under-seat storage box or around the charging port? Are there warning symbols on the display when you switch on briefly? Do not open panels.
- **Smell and listen.** A burning or chemical smell, hissing, crackling, smoke or unusual heat means stop and move away. Follow the advice in our guide to [what to do about breakdowns with a rental bike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-bike-breakdown-hanoi-what-to-do/) and call the shop.
- **Photograph the scene and the bike,** including the water level on a wall or the road, the display and the plate. Note the time and place.
- **Phone the shop** on 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969), open 09:00 to 21:00 daily, and say exactly what happened, how deep the water was and whether the bike has been moving since.

## Why the charging port matters most

Most of an electric scooter's protection against water is in sealed housings. The charging port and plug are the openings you use every day, so they deserve special care. The Evo 200 manual warns that connecting a charger when there is water or dust in the charging port or the plug can cause a fire or an electric shock, and that the charger and the socket must be protected from water, moisture and other liquids. It also says the charger is designed for indoor use, in a ventilated place, away from flammable materials.

So after a soaking ride:

- **Do not plug in the charger until the shop says it is safe.** Treat that as a hard rule, even if the battery is low. Our guide to [charging an electric scooter in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/charging-an-electric-scooter-in-hanoi/) explains the options, and the shop can tell you where to go.
- **Keep the port cover closed and the port dry.** If the cover is open, you can wipe the outside with a dry cloth, but do not push anything into the port.
- **Do not dry the bike with a heat gun or hairdryer.** That is not in the manual and could damage seals. Let the shop decide.

## Leave the cleaning to the shop

Rain-soaked bikes are muddy, and it is tempting to hose one down at once. The VinFast manual warns against using a high-pressure water jet, against spraying water directly under the seat, and against spraying the headlamp, turn signals, tail light, handlebar switches, the side stand sensor and the ignition lock. It also says to dry the rear brake after washing and to keep water out of the storage compartment. VinFast's own guidance on washing says to let the motor, controller and brakes cool first and to cover the charging port and battery area, because those are the parts that matter. A rental renter should not be improvising any of this.

## What not to do

- **Do not ride on to "dry it out."** Moisture in a connector or a brake can fail later, not at once.
- **Do not open the seat storage and tinker with the battery switch** unless the shop tells you to. The manual describes the battery switch, but it is a tool for the shop's instructions, not a rescue measure.
- **Do not leave the bike plugged in to dry.** That adds electricity to the problem.
- **Do not hide it.** A bike returned wet without a word cannot be checked, and a hidden problem is harder to explain than a reported one.
- **Do not move a damaged bike by dragging.** Ask the shop to arrange transport.

## The next 24 hours

A bike that works after a soaking is not proof that nothing is wrong. Water can sit in a connector or around a switch and only cause trouble later. When the shop inspects the bike, it can decide whether to charge it, test it or swap it. In the meantime:

- Keep it somewhere dry, covered and out of the rain, not on a flooded kerb.
- Do not charge it and do not switch it on repeatedly.
- If you are given another bike, run through the [rental scooter inspection checklist](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-scooter-inspection-checklist/) and check the display carefully before you ride it.

## Who pays when a rental gets flooded

This is a contract question and I cannot answer it for you. What I can say is that VinFast's warranty for its scooters excludes damage caused by factors beyond its control, including floods and other natural disasters, so a water-damaged bike may not be covered by the maker. How the shop treats it depends on the rental contract and the circumstances. Read the clauses before you sign, ask how weather damage is handled and report promptly. Our guide to [what the clauses in a Hanoi rental contract mean](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-motorbike-rental-contract-explained/) is a good starting point.

## Prevention in one paragraph

Check the forecast, avoid known flood spots, park where water will not collect and carry your own rain gear, since a raincoat is not included for short rentals. Put your phone and papers in a dry bag. If a street looks flooded, take another route, wait it out or leave the bike somewhere dry and use a taxi.

## FAQ: an electric vehicle that gets wet in heavy rain

### Can I ride an electric scooter in heavy rain?

The manufacturer's manual treats storms and floods as dangerous. Ordinary rain is what the bike is built for, but slow down, brake early and avoid deep water.

### Is it safe to ride through a flooded street?

Not if the water reaches the motor or the battery area. VinFast's manual says to avoid water deeper than the motor axle, and a flooded street can hide hazards.

### My scooter got wet and still works. Is it fine?

Possibly, but report it. Moisture problems can appear later, and the shop should decide whether to inspect it.

### Can I charge it right after it gets wet?

No. Wait for the shop's advice. The manual warns against connecting a charger when there is water in the port or plug.

### Should I wipe it dry?

You can wipe the outside with a dry cloth. Do not use a hose, a heat gun or anything inside the ports.

### What should I tell the shop?

Where you were, when it happened, how deep the water was, what the display showed and whether the bike has moved since.
