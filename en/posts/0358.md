Cuc Phuong National Park can be reached from Hanoi on a rental motorbike, but it is better treated as an overnight ride than a rushed day trip. The park's own tourism site puts it about 120 km from Hanoi, a two to three hour drive by car, and the last leg runs through hills and forest rather than flat delta. Our shop opens at 09:00, so a same-day return leaves little time inside the park once you add the riding. The sensible plan is to ride to the Ninh Binh area, sleep nearby, visit the park early and ride back the next day. This guide covers the route options, the timing problem, the bike to choose, what the park offers and what you should confirm before setting off. Figures are planning information as of October 2026.

## Why this ride is different from other Ninh Binh outings

Most riders heading south of Hanoi are bound for Tam Coc or Trang An, which sit on flat, quiet lanes close to Ninh Binh city. Cuc Phuong is another kind of trip. It is Vietnam's oldest national park, established in 1962 according to the park's official site, and it lies across the borders of Ninh Binh, Hoa Binh and Thanh Hoa provinces. The reason to go is forest, wildlife conservation and cave sites, not a boat ride.

That changes the planning. You are riding further than to the main Ninh Binh sights, you finish on a road with less traffic and fewer shops, and the attractions are spread out inside the park. If you only have one day to spare, the easier plan is our guide to [riding from Hanoi to Ninh Binh](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-to-ninh-binh-by-motorbike/). Choose Cuc Phuong when forest and wildlife matter more to you than limestone scenery.

## Getting there: the two routes the park describes

The park's "getting here" page gives two ways to approach by road, and it also notes that the road from Nho Quan to the park was being upgraded when the page was written. Because road works change, treat the page as a guide and check a map app on the day.

- **Via Nho Quan.** From Ninh Binh the road leads west to Nho Quan, the nearest town, and then on to park headquarters. The same page says that from Ninh Binh the trip to headquarters takes one to one and a half hours by hired motorbike or car.
- **Via Bai Dinh.** The alternative described on the page leaves Highway 1 after the Gian Khau bridge, follows the big road on the dyke along the Boi river to Bai Dinh Pagoda, and continues about 26 km on a concrete road to the park.

Both routes start on the national road from Hanoi. That road is Highway 1A, not the expressway, and the next section explains why.

## Keep off the expressway

Motorbikes are not allowed on expressways. Article 25 of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety No. 36/2024/QH15, in force from 1 January 2025, says motorcycles and mopeds may not travel on an expressway. A navigation app set to "fastest" can still offer an expressway segment, so check the route preview before you leave and again at junctions. We explain how in [how to check whether a navigation app suggests an expressway](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-check-if-a-navigation-app-suggests-an-expressway/).

Our companion guide on [Hanoi's ring roads and highways](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-ring-roads-highways-motorbikes-allowed/) covers the city end of the same question. Riders who plan the day around the national road, not around the fastest line on a map, avoid the most common mistake on this corridor.

## Choosing the bike for forest roads

The first half of the day is on a flat national road, so any reliable bike will do. The last stretch is different: the park sits among limestone ranges, and a bike that feels fine in the city may feel underpowered or loaded with luggage on hills. Our guide to [steep mountain roads and manual bikes](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/manual-bikes-and-steep-mountain-roads-when-to-choose-another-vehicle/) explains when to pick something else.

## What you can do inside the park

The park's official daily-tour page lists the standard day plan, which gives a sense of how the site is laid out:

- **Visitor centre.** Displays, pictures and models that explain the park.
- **Endangered Primate Rescue Center.** Established in 1993 as a project of the Frankfurt Zoological Society, it describes itself as a rescue, rehabilitation and conservation breeding centre. The park describes it as one of the highlights of a visit.
- **Turtle Conservation Center.** The same official page says it keeps about twenty species of turtles and tortoises for rescue and conservation.
- **Cave of Prehistoric Man.** A 0.3 km walk, where human skeletons and stone tools about 7,500 years old have been found.
- **The ancient tree trail.** The official page describes driving 20 km to the park centre and then walking a loop trail of about 7 km to see a 1,000-year-old tree.

Those distances matter on a motorbike. The 20 km from headquarters to the trailhead is a real addition to the day, and a loop walk of several kilometres takes time whichever way you travel.

## Hours and entry: confirm on the day

Published opening hours for the park and its conservation centres differ between sources. The park's reception counter is listed as open from 06:00 to 22:00 daily, but some visitor guides give daytime-only hours for the rescue and turtle centres. Ticket prices also change. For both, rely on the park's reception or the provincial tourism office's published ticket list, and check on the day. Do not assume the conservation centres are open late.

## Season, weather and what to wear

The park's site notes that visitors in April and May may see thousands of butterflies. Another national tourism page says the dry season, roughly December to April, is the most comfortable time because the heavy forest rain is over. Rain is the bigger factor for a bike: wet limestone roads are slippery, and a long ride in a downpour is tiring. Our notes on [riding in Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) apply here too, with a rain poncho carried from May to October.

For the forest, the park itself suggests boots or sports shoes, a headlamp, a camera, a hat and insect repellent for trail days. Sandals are a poor choice for both the ride and the trails.

## FAQ: Hanoi to Cuc Phuong by motorbike

### How far is Cuc Phuong National Park from Hanoi?

The park's tourism site gives about 120 km, a two to three hour drive by car. A scooter will take longer, so plan on the slower side.

### Can I ride there and back in one day?

It is possible only with an early start and a short visit. Because the shop opens at 09:00, an overnight stay near Ninh Binh is a more realistic plan.

### Is there a bus I can use instead?

Yes. The park describes buses from Hanoi's Giap Bat station to Nho Quan, with a taxi or motorbike taxi from there, and a bus between park headquarters and Giap Bat. Timetables change, so confirm them with the park.

### Do I need a special bike?

No, but a reliable semi-automatic or a scooter with enough engine for hills is more comfortable than the smallest option. Ask the shop what is available.

### Is the park open at night?

The reception counter is listed as open from 06:00 to 22:00, but the visitor sites close earlier in the day. Confirm times locally.
