The safest way to ride beside a heavy truck on a scooter is not to ride beside it: stay out of its blind spots, keep well back, give it room to turn, and if you must pass, do it quickly on the left and only when the road allows. The US Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration puts the basic rule simply: if you cannot see the driver in the truck's side mirror, assume the driver cannot see you, and do not stay in a blind spot. Law 36/2024/QH15 backs the caution. Article 12 requires operators to observe the surroundings and slow down when they encounter vehicles carrying oversized or overweight cargo or dangerous goods, and Article 14 limits where and how you may overtake.

This guide explains where a truck's blind spots are, why trucks behave differently from scooters, how to ride behind, beside and around them, what the law says about overtaking, how wind, spray, loads and darkness change the risk, and what to do at junctions and in tight streets. It is general information based on the law and published safety guidance as of October 2026, not legal advice.

## Why heavy trucks are different

A loaded truck differs from a scooter in four ways that matter at close range.

- **Size and blind spots.** The driver sits high and far from the sides and the rear, and large areas around the vehicle are invisible in the mirrors.

- **Weight and stopping distance.** A truck needs far more distance to stop than a scooter, and more again when loaded or on a wet road.

- **Turning.** A long vehicle needs a wide arc, and may swing out before it turns.

- **What it carries and throws.** Loads can shift or spill, spray and dust rise from the wheels, and a burst tyre can send debris across the lane.

None of this means the driver is careless. It means that the geometry of the vehicle limits what the driver can see and do. ## Where the blind spots are

The FMCSA calls these areas "No-Zones". They lie around the front, the back and both sides of a large truck or bus.

- **Directly in front,** close to the cab, where the driver cannot see a low vehicle.

- **Directly behind,** where the driver cannot see anything and the vehicle may be reversing.

- **Along both sides,** from the cab backward along the trailer. The side nearer the kerb is often the harder one for the driver to see.

- **Just beyond the cab on the left and the right,** where the mirror cannot cover.

The rule of thumb is the one the FMCSA gives: if you cannot see the driver in the side mirror, the driver cannot see you. Do not ride in a blind spot. Slow down or move ahead so that you are visible.

## Riding behind a truck

Staying behind is the lowest-risk position, if you do it with care.

1. **Leave a large gap.** Circular 38/2024/TT-BGTVT defines the safe distance as the minimum distance that avoids a collision if the vehicle in front suddenly slows or stops, and requires more in rain and on slippery roads. Our guide to [how to follow a safe distance in Hanoi traffic](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-follow-a-safe-distance-in-hanoi-traffic/) explains the distance.

2. **Stay slightly off-centre,** so that you can see past the truck and the driver can see you in the mirror.

3. **Do not tailgate.** Tailgating puts you in the rear blind spot. The FMCSA also warns that a vehicle that fails to stop in time can slide under a truck, because trucks are high off the ground.

4. **Watch the brake lights and the load,** and the road surface ahead of the truck.

5. **Be ready for dust, spray and debris.** Drop back if your view is blocked.

## Riding beside a truck

If traffic puts you alongside a truck:

- **Do not stay there.** Either slow down and drop back, or move ahead so that you are clearly visible.

- **Never ride in the gap between a truck and the kerb,** especially if the truck has its turn signal on. The FMCSA advises never trying to squeeze by a truck or bus that has its signal on, or getting between it and the kerb.

- **Keep your distance sideways.** A truck's lane position may drift, and a long vehicle's rear can swing toward you on a bend.

- **Watch for the driver's eyes in the mirror.** If you can see the driver, you are more likely to be seen.

- **Stay calm, and avoid sudden moves** beside it.

## Passing a truck

Overtaking a heavy truck is sometimes needed, but it must be done well.

Article 14 of Law 36/2024/QH15 sets out the rules for overtaking.

- **Overtake on the left,** except where the vehicle in front is signalling a left turn or turning left, or a special-purpose vehicle is working, in which case the right may be used.

- **Only when there is no obstacle ahead and no oncoming traffic** in the section where you will overtake, and the vehicle in front is not overtaking and has signalled right and moved to the right.

- **Signal your intention** with a flash of the front lights or the horn, and signal the direction. In urban and populous areas from 22:00 to 05:00, use lights only.

- **Keep a safe distance** from the vehicles in front and behind throughout.

- **Do not overtake** on a narrow single-lane bridge, on bends with limited visibility, at crests with limited visibility, at intersections or railway crossings, on pedestrian crossings, in tunnels, when weather or road conditions are unsafe or when a priority vehicle approaches.

The FMCSA adds practical advice: make sure you can see the driver in the mirror before passing, signal clearly, move into the left lane and accelerate so that you can get past promptly, do not linger in the blind spot, and make sure the truck is visible in your mirror before pulling in front. It also advises avoiding passing trucks on a downgrade, where they tend to pick up speed.

In a Hanoi street, where overtaking space is limited, the better choice is often to stay behind. A slow truck costs you a minute. A misjudged pass costs far more.

## Turning trucks

Wide turns catch scooter riders out.

- **A truck may swing out to the left before turning right,** leaving a gap on its right that looks like space for a scooter. Do not enter it. The FMCSA notes that buses and trucks swing wide, or may even begin a turn from a middle lane.

- **A truck turning left may take several lanes,** and its rear may track inside.

- **If the signal is on,** wait. Give the truck the whole corner.

- **Do not stop just in front of a truck** at a junction where you could be pushed or hidden.

## Wind, spray and debris

- **Wind.** A passing truck can shield you from a crosswind and then release you suddenly, and its air movement can push a light scooter sideways. Stay clear. Our guide to [riding on bridges during strong winds](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-on-bridges-during-strong-winds/) explains how gusts behave.

- **Spray.** On wet roads, a truck throws up a cloud that hides the road and the vehicles around it. Drop back until you can see.

- **Dust and debris.** Construction trucks may shed sand, stones and mud. Article 9 of the Law bans spilling soil, stones, goods or construction materials onto the road, but you should still expect it.

- **Burst tyres.** Pieces of tyre can fly. Another reason to keep a distance.

- **Exhaust.** Black smoke reduces visibility and is unpleasant, so do not follow too closely.

Our guide to [riding in Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) covers wet conditions.

## Loads, oversize and dangerous goods

Article 12 requires operators to observe the surroundings and slow down or stop when they encounter vehicles transporting oversized or overweight cargo or dangerous cargo. In practice:

- **Give wide loads extra room,** because they overhang and have limited visibility and difficulty manoeuvring.

- **Do not ride close behind a load** that is tied loosely, or that is not covered.

- **Stay away from vehicles marked for dangerous goods,** and do not overtake them in a tight space.

- **Keep clear of cranes, concrete mixers and trucks that reverse** into sites.

## Trucks and the clock in central Hanoi

Hanoi manages truck movements in the centre by time of day. Under the pilot low-emission-zone scheme announced for Ring Road 1 in mid-2026, trucks above certain weights may operate only in set hours in the pilot areas of Hoan Kiem ward, with larger trucks limited to night hours. The result is that you may meet more trucks in the evening and at night in some central streets. Check the current notices and signs. ## At night

Trucks are harder to judge at night.

- **Their lights may be dim or missing,** and parked trucks may have none. Slow down and use low beam.

- **Reflective strips** help, but do not rely on them.

- **Oncoming truck headlights can dazzle.** Look to the right edge of your lane.

- **Do not follow a truck's tail lights** as if they were a guide.

## Practical habits

1. **Treat a truck as a moving wall with blind spots.**

2. **Keep a large gap behind, and do not ride alongside.**

3. **Look for the driver in the mirror.**

4. **Wait for a turning truck to finish.**

5. **Pass only where the law and the road allow, and do it quickly.**

6. **Leave extra room in wind, rain and at night.**

7. **Be patient.** A truck's slowness is not a reason to take a risk.

## A quick checklist

1. **If you cannot see the driver's mirror, the driver cannot see you.**

2. **Stay back, and never between a truck and the kerb.**

3. **Give turning trucks the whole corner.**

4. **Overtake only on the left, in a permitted place, and promptly.**

5. **Do not pass on a downgrade.**

6. **Drop back in spray, dust and strong wind.**

7. **At night, slow down and keep your distance.**

## FAQ: riding beside heavy trucks

### Where are a truck's blind spots?

In front of the cab, behind the vehicle and along both sides, especially the side nearer the kerb. If you cannot see the driver in the side mirror, assume the driver cannot see you.

### Is it safe to ride alongside a truck?

It is better not to. Slow down and drop back, or move ahead so that you are clearly visible.

### How do I overtake a truck?

On the left, only where Article 14 of Law 36/2024/QH15 allows it, with a signal and a flash of lights or a short horn, and quickly. Do not overtake at junctions, on bends, on narrow bridges or in tunnels.

### Why do trucks swing wide?

A long vehicle needs a wide arc, and may swing out before turning. Do not enter the gap that opens beside it.

### Are there time limits for trucks in central Hanoi?

Under the pilot scheme from mid-2026, some trucks in the pilot areas of Hoan Kiem ward may operate only at set hours. Follow the signs and check current notices.

### Should I use the horn to warn a truck?

Only briefly, to warn of a hazard or to signal an overtake. Article 21 bans continuous horn use.
