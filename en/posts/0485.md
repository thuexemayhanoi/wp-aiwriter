A helmet that tips off a seat or a table while empty does not automatically need replacing, but it does need a careful look, and a helmet that has hit the ground with a head inside, come off a moving bike or shows any crack, crushed foam, loose hardware or changed fit should be retired. Published guidance based on the Snell Foundation's helmet FAQ says an occasional drop of an empty helmet from hand, seat or handlebar height should not reduce its protection, because the energy-absorbing liner is compressed mainly by the weight of a head. If you are unsure, the conservative answer is to replace it, and a rental shop should be told before you ride on.

This guide explains why the answer depends on how the helmet fell, what to inspect on the shell, the liner, the strap, the buckle and the visor, how to repeat the fit test, when to replace the helmet, what manufacturers say, how to avoid drops and what to do with a rental helmet. It is general information based on published helmet guidance as of October 2026, not an engineering opinion or medical advice.

## Why the way it fell matters

A modern helmet has a hard outer shell and, under it, a liner of crushable foam, usually expanded polystyrene. In a crash, the shell spreads the force and the liner crushes in a controlled way to slow the head. Once crushed, the foam does not recover, so the helmet cannot protect you again in the same place.

An empty helmet is light. The helmet guidance that refers to the Snell Foundation says that a helmet which occasionally falls off a table, bench, handlebar or the back seat of a motorbike should not need replacing, since there is no head inside to crush the foam. Heavier objects inside, or a violent fall, change the picture, and so does a fall from a height or onto a sharp edge.

So, before you inspect it, answer four questions.

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Was it empty or worn?** | A helmet with a head inside takes the impact the liner is designed for, and should be replaced |
| **How far did it fall?** | A short drop from a seat is different from a fall down stairs; one manufacturer advises considering replacement above about four feet (1.2 m) |
| **What did it hit?** | A flat clean floor is milder than a kerb, a step or a sharp edge |
| **How many times?** | Repeated drops add up, and the history of a borrowed helmet may be unknown |

## What to inspect

Do this in good light, with the helmet turned in your hands.

1. **The shell.** Look for cracks, including fine hairline cracks, deep gouges, flat spots, soft spots and loose trim. A small paint scuff is cosmetic. A crack is not.

2. **The inside.** If the pads can be removed, take them out as the maker allows and look at the foam liner. You are looking for cracks, dents, crushed or spongy areas and places where the foam has separated from the shell. Do not dig, pry, sand or heat the foam.

3. **The strap and buckle.** Check the stitching and the webbing for tears, the buckle for cracks or sticking, and the rivets for looseness. Our guide to [how to fit and fasten a motorbike helmet properly](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-fit-and-fasten-a-motorbike-helmet-properly/) explains what a sound strap does.

4. **The visor and hinges.** Open and close the visor, and check it sits evenly and seals. A visor that no longer closes evenly may mean the shell has flexed.

5. **The vents and fittings.** Look for broken vents or loose fittings that could rattle or catch.

6. **The fit.** Put the helmet on and run the shake, lift and open-mouth tests. If it feels looser, rattles or sits differently from before, treat that as more than cosmetic.

7. **The smell and feel.** A chemical smell after a cleaning attempt, or foam that feels uneven, is a warning.

Write down what happened: empty or worn, the approximate height, the surface and the point of impact. That record helps you, the shop or the manufacturer judge it.

## Decision table

| What happened | What it usually means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Empty helmet fell off a seat or table onto a clean floor, no marks | Often cosmetic | Inspect as above; if all is sound, it may be kept |
| Empty helmet fell and has a dent, deep scratch or soft spot | Possible damage to the shell or liner | Inspect closely; ask the maker or replace |
| Helmet hit the ground with your head in it | The liner took an impact | Replace it |
| Helmet came off a moving bike or fell from a height | High and unknown energy | Replace it, or have the maker inspect it |
| Cracked shell, crushed foam or loose hardware | Not cosmetic | Replace it |
| Several drops over time | Accumulated abuse | Inspect strictly and consider replacing |
| Fit has changed after the drop | The liner or the shell may have changed | Stop and investigate before riding |

## What manufacturers say

Published reports of manufacturers' answers differ in detail, but they agree on the main point: you cannot always tell from outside. One maker advises considering replacement after drops above about four feet. Another invites riders to send the helmet in for a free inspection. A third says any impact could crack the interior foam and suggests removing the pads to look for compression or cracks, and says it cannot confirm damage from photographs. The same reports note that the Snell Foundation does not require replacement for an occasional drop of an empty helmet, and that the researcher found no formal study of short drops, so these are professional judgements, not tested rules.

The practical result: if the helmet is yours, inspect it and, if you are not sure, replace it. If it matters to you, ask the maker. Helmet guidance also advises against buying a used helmet, because you cannot know what happened to it.

## How to avoid drops

- **Do not hang the helmet by its strap** or on a mirror or handlebar, where the liner can be damaged or the helmet knocked off.

- **Do not leave it on the seat.** A passing bump or a gust can push it off. Our guide to [how to transport a helmet on a scooter while parked](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-transport-a-helmet-on-a-scooter-while-parked/) explains the options.

- **Set it down on its back,** on a flat surface, away from edges.

- **Carry it by the strap in your hand,** not on your elbow near a doorway.

- **Keep it away from heat and chemicals,** and clean it gently.

## Your own helmet

If you brought a helmet from home, the same rules apply, and you may be able to ask the maker for advice. Keep a note of its age and any impacts. Law 36/2024/QH15 requires helmets that meet the national technical regulation and a properly fastened strap (Article 33), so a helmet you doubt is a poor choice for a trip in Vietnam. ## A quick checklist

1. **Was it empty or worn when it fell?**

2. **Inspect the shell, liner, strap, buckle and visor in good light.**

3. **Repeat the fit and lift tests.**

4. **Replace it after any impact with a head inside, a fall from a moving bike or any crack or crushed foam.**

5. **If unsure, replace it or ask the maker.**

6. **Do not buy a used helmet.**

7. **For a rental, photograph it, tell the shop and ask for another.**

## FAQ: dropped helmets

### Must I replace a helmet every time I drop it?

Not automatically. Guidance based on the Snell Foundation says an occasional drop of an empty helmet from seat or handlebar height should not reduce its protection, but you should inspect it. Replace it after an impact with a head inside, after a fall from a moving bike or if you find damage.

### Can foam damage be hidden?

Yes. Crush damage can sit under the shell, so a sound-looking outside does not prove the inside is fine.

### How far can a helmet fall before it must be replaced?

There is no agreed figure. One manufacturer suggests considering replacement above about four feet. If the fall was high, onto a hard edge or repeated, replace it.

### Can the maker tell from a photograph?

Usually not. At least one maker says it cannot confirm hidden damage from photographs.

### What should I do with a rental helmet that I dropped?

Inspect it, photograph it and tell the shop. Ask for another before you ride.

### Is a scratched visor a reason to replace the helmet?

A scratched visor is a vision issue, not necessarily a helmet one. Replace the visor if it blurs your view, and check the shell as well.
