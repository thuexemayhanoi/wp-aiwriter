On a bridge in strong wind, the safest plan is to decide before you reach it: if the wind is already pushing the scooter around on open streets, wait or take another route, because the law bans stopping on most bridges and a rider cannot simply pull over mid-span. If you do cross, slow down, ride in the part of your lane that gives you the most room, keep a firm but relaxed grip, steer into the wind as much as you need to and expect sudden gusts at the bridge's start, at gaps in the structure and as large trucks pass. Law 36/2024/QH15 tells operators to observe the surroundings and slow down on bridges and in windy or rainy conditions (Article 12), and it bans stopping and parking on bridges except in places where that is allowed (Article 18).

This guide explains why a light scooter is affected by wind, where gusts hit hardest, how to prepare, how to ride across step by step, how rain and traffic change the picture, what the law says about stopping and turning on bridges and when it is better not to ride. It is general information based on the law and on published riding guidance as of October 2026, not legal advice.

## Why wind matters on a scooter

A scooter is light and has a large flat side, so a gust can push it sideways, and the rider's body adds to the surface the wind pushes against. Wind becomes a problem when it is strong, when it is gusty and when it arrives suddenly. A bridge combines all three. It is open on both sides, it often rises above the surrounding streets and it removes the buildings and trees that block wind elsewhere. Published riding guidance from a US motorcycle-insurance safety guide says that strong winds can catch riders off guard and throw them off balance, and lists bridges, gaps between buildings and trees, open roads, coastal areas and gusts as large trucks pass as the places to watch for. Hanoi has the Red River bridges and long approaches that are open to the weather.

## What the law says about bridges

Law 36/2024/QH15 has several provisions that touch bridges.

- **Article 12:** operators must observe the surroundings and slow down or stop for safety in rainy or windy conditions, and on bridges, narrow culverts, spillways, underground roads and road tunnels. They must keep a speed suited to the conditions of the bridge and the road, traffic density, weather and other factors.

- **Article 18:** operators may not stop or park on bridges, except in places where stopping and parking are allowed. If a technical incident or another force majeure event forces a vehicle to stop in a place not designated for it, the operator must signal with emergency lights or place a warning sign behind the vehicle.

- **Article 14:** overtaking is prohibited on a narrow bridge with only one lane.

- **Article 15:** U-turns are banned on bridges and at bridge ends, unless a traffic guard or a temporary sign allows them.

Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, as read on the Government portal, sets a fine for stopping or parking a motorcycle on a bridge, in the range of VND 600,000 to 800,000. Check the current text if a figure matters to you. The practical consequence is simple. If the wind is too strong, you cannot just stop on the bridge to wait it out. You have to decide at the entrance.

## Before you set off: check the wind

- **Check the forecast** from the national weather service before you ride, and look at warnings for strong winds, storms or typhoon conditions. If a storm or typhoon warning covers Hanoi, do not plan a long ride.

- **Look at what the wind is doing** where you are. Trees bending, loose items blowing and a noticeable push on open streets mean that a bridge will be worse.

- **Plan an alternative route.** Our guide to [motorbike-friendly alternatives to highway routes near Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbike-friendly-alternatives-to-highway-routes-near-hanoi/) and our guide to [motorbike route planning around Hanoi's restricted roads](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbike-route-planning-around-hanois-restricted-roads/) explain how to review a route.

- **Check your load.** A loose raincoat, a large bag or a wide object on the bike catches the wind. Our guide to [how to carry a raincoat and helmet on a small manual bike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-carry-a-raincoat-and-helmet-on-a-small-manual-bike/) explains how to stow them.

If it is a day of strong wind, consider not riding at all. A taxi or a bus for the day costs less than a fall.

## Crossing the bridge step by step

1. **Slow down before the bridge,** not on it. Article 12 requires you to slow down on bridges and in windy conditions.

2. **Look at the approach.** Notice whether the wind is steady or gusty, which way it is coming from and whether other riders are being pushed.

3. **Choose your lane position.** Published guidance advises riding on the side of the lane that gives you the most room to manoeuvre, and keeping your distance from other vehicles and from the side and the centre line of the road, so that a gust that moves you does not put you in someone's path.

4. **Keep a good posture.** Keep a firm but flexible grip, your feet planted and your arms and shoulders relaxed. A rigid body passes every gust straight into the steering.

5. **Steer into the wind as much as you need to,** with small, smooth inputs, and ease off as the gust passes.

6. **Hold a steady speed.** Do not accelerate or brake sharply in a gust.

7. **Look ahead,** to where you want to go, and not at the edge of the bridge.

8. **Stay in your lane,** and do not overtake. Article 14 bans overtaking on narrow single-lane bridges, and gusts make it worse.

9. **Be ready for the end of the bridge,** where the wind can change as the structure falls away.

## Gusts: where they come from

Gusts are the main danger, and they come from predictable places.

- **The bridge entrance and exit,** where the surroundings change.

- **Gaps in the parapet or between structures,** where wind funnels through.

- **Large trucks and buses.** A truck passing can shield you from the wind and then release you suddenly when it moves on, so the push can come at the moment the shield disappears. Stay well clear. Our guide to [sharing the road with buses while riding a scooter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/sharing-the-road-with-buses-while-riding-a-scooter/) explains how to give large vehicles room.

- **Open stretches with no buildings,** such as approaches over the river.

- **Changes in direction,** where a bend turns you to face the wind.

Published guidance adds that you should look ahead along your route and try to determine where the wind is going to catch you, so that you can plan for it.

## Rain, wind and wet bridge surfaces

Wind often comes with rain, and the bridge surface changes.

- **Metal expansion joints and gratings** are slippery when wet, and wind can make you cross them at an awkward angle. Cross them upright and straight.

- **Painted markings** on the bridge are slippery. Our guide to [braking safely on wet road markings](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/braking-safely-on-wet-road-markings/) explains how to treat them.

- **Visibility is lower,** so use your lights. Article 20 requires front lights in rain and bad weather. Our guide to [safe scooter riding when visibility drops in rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/safe-scooter-riding-when-visibility-drops-in-rain/) explains how.

- **Stopping distances grow,** so keep a larger gap. Our guide to [how to follow a safe distance in Hanoi traffic](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-follow-a-safe-distance-in-hanoi-traffic/) covers it.

- **Spray from other vehicles** can hide the road.

## What if the wind is too strong when you are already on the bridge?

Because stopping on a bridge is not allowed except in designated places, the decision is best made before you enter. If you are on the bridge and the wind becomes too strong:

1. **Slow down,** and keep your lane.

2. **Do not stop in the lane,** unless you are forced to.

3. **Do not try to turn round** on the bridge. Article 15 bans U-turns on bridges.

4. **Keep going to the far end,** where you can stop safely.

5. **If you are forced to stop** by a technical fault or a force majeure event, put your emergency lights on, move as far to the right as you can, and place a warning sign behind the vehicle if it is safe to do so, as Article 18 requires.

6. **Call for help** if you need it. The national emergency number is 112, and ambulance, police and fire services can be reached through it. ## When not to ride

There is a point beyond which riding is not worth the risk. Do not cross a bridge, or ride on open roads, if:

- **A storm or typhoon warning is in force** for the area.

- **The wind is already pushing you around** on open streets.

- **Objects are blowing across the road.**

- **You are carrying a passenger or a bulky load** and the wind is strong.

- **You are tired, or new to the bike.**

- **Rain and wind together** reduce your visibility and grip.

In these cases, wait somewhere safe, take a taxi or a bus, or postpone the trip. ## A quick checklist

1. **Check the forecast, and the wind where you are.**

2. **Decide before the bridge: cross, wait or reroute.**

3. **Slow down before you enter.**

4. **Ride in the part of the lane with the most room, relaxed and steady.**

5. **Steer into the wind with small inputs.**

6. **Give trucks a wide berth.**

7. **Do not stop, overtake or turn round on the bridge.**

8. **Take extra care on wet joints and markings.**

## FAQ: riding on bridges in strong winds

### Can I stop on a bridge to wait out the wind?

No. Article 18 of Law 36/2024/QH15 bans stopping and parking on bridges except in places where it is allowed. Decide before you enter.

### Where do gusts hit hardest?

At the entrance and exit, in gaps in the structure, on open approaches and as large trucks pass.

### Which side of the lane should I ride on?

The side that gives you the most room to manoeuvre, keeping your distance from other vehicles and from the edge and the centre line.

### Should I speed up to get across?

No. Article 12 requires you to slow down on bridges and in windy conditions. Keep a steady, moderate speed.

### Can I overtake on a bridge?

Not on a narrow bridge with one lane, and gusts make overtaking risky anywhere.

### When should I not ride at all?

When a storm or typhoon warning is in force, when wind is already pushing you around on open streets or when you are tired, carrying a bulky load or new to the bike.
