A rental electric scooter is plugged in unsafely when nobody is watching it, when it is charged for longer than the maker intends, when the charger is not the one supplied, or when it sits in a cramped, wet or cluttered place. The safest habit is simple: use only the charger that came with the bike, plug in somewhere dry, open and in sight, stay nearby while it charges, and unplug before you sleep or go out. Vietnam's fire-prevention authorities and VinFast give the same core advice, and this guide turns it into a routine for a visitor who is borrowing someone else's battery. It covers the habits to avoid, how to choose a socket, the order for plugging in and unplugging, the warning signs to stop for and what to do if something goes wrong. It is general safety information, not a substitute for the shop's instructions for your bike.

## What "unsafe" actually looks like

Public fire-safety advice in Vietnam is consistent about which charging habits cause trouble. The national fire-prevention and rescue department, quoted by a provincial government site, and a September 2026 report on Hanoi's own fire-prevention unit both list the same ones:

- **Charging overnight or with nobody around** to notice a fault.
- **Charging straight after a ride,** while the battery and parts may still be warm.
- **Using the wrong charger,** one of unknown origin, or one that has been modified.
- **Charging in a cramped place** or beside things that burn easily.
- **Changing the vehicle's wiring** or adding devices that draw power.
- **Charging for too long.** The department's advice is not to charge for more than eight hours at a stretch.

As a renter, you cannot control the bike's age or its wiring, but you control nearly all of the rest. That is why the routine below is mostly about where, when and who is present.

## Before you plug in: choose the place

Run through these questions before you connect anything:

1. **Is it a place you can see?** A lobby you pass or a courtyard you overlook is better than a closed storeroom.
2. **Is it dry and ventilated?** Not under a dripping air-conditioner, not in a puddle, not in a sealed cupboard.
3. **Is it clear of things that burn?** Fire-safety advice names bedding, paper, fuel and gas cylinders. Keep cardboard, luggage and fabric away.
4. **Is it away from the exit?** A private fire-safety company's guidance, which is not an official source, makes the point that charging at the bottom of the stairs or at a doorway can block the only way out of a narrow building.
5. **Is the socket in good order?** No cracks, no scorch marks, no loose fit.
6. **Is there shade?** VinFast advises keeping the battery out of rain and direct sun during charging.
7. **Does the building allow it?** Some hotels and guesthouses prohibit charging indoors. Ask before you assume.

If you cannot answer yes to most of them, do not charge there. Call the shop on 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969) and ask about options. ## Use the supplied charger, and only that one

The shop confirms the battery level and charging arrangement at handover, and the charger is part of that arrangement. VinFast's guidance says to use only the charger supplied by the maker, and the national fire-prevention advice says to use the battery and charger that came with the vehicle and not to modify them. In practice:

- **Do not borrow a charger** from a friend, a hotel or a street stall, even if the plug fits.
- **Do not use an extension reel or a cheap multi-way adapter.** The fire-prevention advice also warns against plugging several devices into one socket.
- **Do not coil the cable under load** or cover the charger with a cloth or bag.
- **Do not charge with the key on.** A fire-prevention summary published in 2025 says that charging with the ignition on can stop the charger from switching to maintenance mode.
- **Report a damaged plug or cable** before you use it. Our [rental scooter inspection checklist](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-scooter-inspection-checklist/) shows the quick checks worth doing at pickup.

## The order matters: plugging in and unplugging

VinFast's published sequence for its electric scooters is a useful model for any bike, though your scooter's manual and the shop's instructions come first. It asks the user to:

1. Switch the scooter off and remove the key.
2. Dry the plug and the area around the charging port.
3. Connect the charger to the scooter first, then plug it into the wall socket.
4. Watch the charger's indicator. VinFast describes a red light for charging and a green light for full on common chargers, but check what your charger does.
5. When it is full, switch off, pull the plug from the wall, then remove the connector from the scooter.
6. Wait about a minute before reconnecting, and wait at least 30 seconds after plugging in before switching the key on.

Never touch a plug with wet hands, and never plug in when the scooter or the socket is wet. Keep the cable off the floor where people walk and out of the doorway.

## Time: build a charging window you can attend

The risk is not the charging itself but the unwatched hours. Fire-prevention advice asks users to stop charging before they leave the house or go to sleep. The Hatinh page quoting the national department also says not to charge for more than eight hours continuously and to wait roughly 20 minutes after riding before plugging in; VinFast's own guide suggests waiting somewhere between 30 minutes and an hour for a battery to cool.

That leaves a visitor with a practical planning problem, because a slow charge can take many hours. The answer is to schedule, not to gamble:

- **Charge in the afternoon or early evening,** when you are in the building anyway.
- **Charge in top-ups.** If tomorrow's ride is short, a few attended hours may be enough.
- **Set a phone timer** for the time you must unplug, and treat it as an appointment.
- **Unplug before dinner out,** even if the indicator says it is not full yet.
- **Ask the shop for a bike with a smaller battery or more range** if your itinerary and your hotel do not allow a safe window.

## Warning signs while it is charging

Check the scooter, the charger and the socket every so often, by sight and by touch of the casing. Stop charging, unplug if it is safe to do so and call the shop if you notice:

| What you notice | Why it matters | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Charger or cable much hotter than usual | Possible fault in the charger or connection | Unplug and leave it |
| Burning or sharp chemical smell | Possible overheating | Stop, ventilate, call the shop |
| Battery casing swollen, cracked or deformed | Damaged cells | Do not charge or use |
| Scorched or melted plug or socket | Poor contact or overload | Do not use that socket |
| Charger light behaves oddly, never turns green | Fault in charger or battery | Unplug, report |
| Smoke or crackling | Emergency | Move away, see below |

The fire-prevention advice lists excessive heat, swelling, cracks, deformation and odd smells as reasons to stop. VinFast adds that if a battery overheats, charging should stop and the battery should be taken to an authorised service point. As a renter, that means calling the shop, not attempting a repair.

## Shared buildings, stairwells and guarded parking

Charging in shared spaces deserves extra care. The fire-prevention advice warns specifically against overnight charging in apartment blocks and unsupervised basements and parking areas. Four rules help:

- Ask the host or reception where charging is allowed, and follow the building's rule rather than a general tip.
- Do not plug a bike into a socket inside a stairwell, a corridor or the only way out.
- Do not hand the scooter and charger to a parking attendant to charge for you unless the shop has said that is fine. You cannot see what happens to it.
- If other bikes are charging nearby, treat the arrangement as less safe, not more, because a fault in one can affect the others.

## If something goes wrong

If a charger smokes, sparks or the bike smells strongly of burning, your priority is people, not property. Move away from the vehicle and warn others. If it is safe to reach the wall plug without going near the bike, pull the plug, but do not move a hot or swollen battery. Leave the area and call the national fire and rescue emergency number, 114. Then phone the shop. Lithium batteries are difficult to extinguish with ordinary equipment, so keep a distance and let the fire service handle it.

After any incident, tell the shop honestly what happened and photograph the scene once it is safe. ## A one-minute checklist before you walk away

- Supplied charger only, plugged into the scooter first and the wall second.
- Dry hands, dry plug, dry floor, no covering over the charger.
- Clear of bedding, boxes, fuel and luggage, and out of any exit route.
- Key off and removed.
- Timer set, with a time to unplug that falls before you sleep or leave.
- Phone number of the shop saved.

## FAQ: charging safely

### Is it safe to leave a rental scooter charging overnight?

Public fire-prevention advice says not to. It asks users to stop charging before they leave home or go to sleep and not to charge for more than eight hours in one go.

### Can I use a charger I bought locally if the supplied one is missing?

Not without asking the shop. Fire-prevention and manufacturer advice both stress the supplied, matching charger. Call the shop and ask what to do.

### How long should I wait after riding before I plug in?

The fire-prevention advice quoted above says around 20 minutes. VinFast's guide suggests 30 minutes to an hour. Let the battery cool to the surrounding temperature.

### Can I charge it in my hotel room?

Only if the hotel allows it and the battery or scooter can be charged somewhere dry and clear of bedding. Ask reception first.

### What if the charger gets hot?

Warm is normal for many chargers, but much hotter than usual is a reason to unplug and call the shop.
