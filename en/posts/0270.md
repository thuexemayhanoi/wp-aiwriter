The etiquette of charging an electric scooter where other people live comes down to six habits: ask the host or building manager before you plug in, charge only where the answer is yes, never block a doorway or stairway, stay close while it charges, use only the charger that belongs to the bike and unplug when you are done. In Hanoi the first habit matters most, because many hosts have become cautious about electric two-wheelers since a deadly apartment fire in 2023, even though the cause was not the one rumour suggested. This guide is about the social side of charging in hostels, homestays, serviced flats and shared houses: who to ask, what to say, how to be a considerate neighbour and what to do when the answer is no. Hotel-specific questions are covered separately in our [charging guide for visitors](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/charging-an-electric-scooter-in-hanoi/).

## Why hosts are cautious in Hanoi

Shared buildings in Hanoi have been through a nervous few years over electric vehicles. After a fire at a nine-storey apartment block in September 2023 killed 56 people, rumours blamed an electric scooter battery. Rest of World reported that several building owners quickly banned charging, even after the rumours were shown to be false, and that district authorities first asked for electric two-wheelers to be removed from apartment garages and then clarified that this was a recommendation, not a ban. A Tuoi Tre report in December 2024 said that several apartment buildings restricted charging for a time before police later ruled out battery charging as a cause, and that some residents stayed fearful.

The caution has not gone away. In December 2025, Lao Dong reported that residents of one apartment complex in Hanoi were arguing over a management announcement that electric vehicles would no longer be accepted in the basement from 1 February 2026, while the ward's vice chairman said the fire-prevention rules did not forbid parking or charging electric vehicles and that management should provide safe, separate charging arrangements instead of banning them. In July 2026, Lao Dong also reported that the Ministry of Construction had issued Circular 31/2026/TT-BXD, effective 15 December 2026, adding rules for charging areas in new and existing apartment buildings, including a preference for outdoor or first-basement charging, separate zones, fire alarms and cameras. As reported, existing buildings have a six-month transition.

What this means for a renter is simple. A host who hesitates is not being difficult. They carry the responsibility for everyone under the roof, and your politeness is part of how they decide.

## Ask before you wheel it in

Ask at booking, not at the door with the bike already inside. The question is short, and the answer shapes everything else.

Useful things to find out:

- Is charging allowed at all, and is there a particular socket or place you may use?
- Is it allowed indoors, in a courtyard or only outside?
- Is there a time window, for example not late at night, and does someone need to be present?
- Who pays for the electricity, and is there a fixed arrangement for guests?
- Where may the scooter be parked when it is not charging?

A message you can adapt:

> Hello, I will be staying from [date] and have rented an electric scooter. It charges from an ordinary socket with the charger supplied. Could you tell me whether I may charge it at the property, where, and at what times? I will stay with it while it charges and unplug it afterwards. If charging is not possible, I will charge elsewhere. Thank you.

Keep the tone relaxed. A yes with conditions is a good answer. A no is also a useful answer, because it lets you plan before you arrive instead of discovering it at night with a low battery.

## Choose the spot as a neighbour would

Once you have permission, where you plug in decides whether the arrangement lasts. Hanoi's fire-prevention unit advises keeping the charging area dry, ventilated and clear of heat and flammable material, and warns against charging in cramped or hard-to-reach places or with lots of goods piled around the vehicle. In a shared house those words translate into practical rules:

- **Not in a corridor, stairwell or exit route.** A parked scooter and a cable across the floor can slow everyone down in an emergency and trip someone on an ordinary evening.
- **Not next to shoe racks, stored bags, bedding, cardboard or the kitchen.** Keep an arm's length of clear space around the bike.
- **Not inside a dormitory room or bedroom** unless the host says so. A scooter does not belong in a room where people sleep, and the noise of a click or beep at midnight is a nuisance as well.
- **Somewhere dry.** An open courtyard is fine in dry weather, but a charger and plug should not sit in a puddle or under a leaking roof. See our guide to [riding in Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) for what a wet season does to plans.
- **Somewhere you can see it.** The reason for the previous point is that charging is meant to be supervised, which leads to timing.

## Timing: the courtesy of a short window

Hanoi's fire-prevention unit, in advice reported on 6 September 2026, says not to charge overnight or when nobody is at home to monitor the vehicle, to stop the charge before going out or to sleep and to check regularly during charging. It also advises waiting for a warm battery to cool before charging. VinFast's owner's manual for the Evo 200, one model of electric motorbike that may be offered, gives similar guidance: it advises not charging when the battery is at or above 45 degrees Celsius and suggests waiting about an hour after riding for the battery to cool. The same manual says keeping the battery between 20 and 80 per cent is better for its life and says not to hesitate to top up at every opportunity.

In shared accommodation this combines into a courteous routine:

1. Arrive, park and let the bike rest for about an hour.
2. Plug in at an agreed time while you are around, for a top-up instead of a full fill.
3. Come back to check, unplug and wind up the cable.
4. Leave the socket free for the next person.

A full charge from empty can take many hours. The Evo 200 manual lists a standard charging time of about 10 hours, depending on the battery's condition. That is a long time to occupy a shared socket and a long time to leave a charger running with nobody watching, so aim for a top-up unless you have a private arrangement. ## Cables, plugs and shared sockets

The charger supplied with a bike is matched to it. The fire-prevention unit specifically warns against chargers of the wrong type, unknown origin or modified parts, and advises using the charger and power supply specified by the manufacturer. In a shared house that gives a few simple rules:

- **Use only the charger that came with the bike.** If it fails, do not borrow a flatmate's. Call the shop instead.
- **Plug directly into a wall socket where possible,** and avoid chains of extension leads that someone else may also be using.
- **Do not unplug other people's devices,** such as a fridge, a router or a phone, to make room. Ask first.
- **Keep the cable out of the walking line,** or tape it down if the host agrees, and tuck it away afterwards.
- **Look at the plug and cable each time.** If anything is hot, cracked, swollen or smells odd, stop charging and tell the host and the shop, as the fire-prevention unit advises.
- **Follow the manufacturer's order.** The Evo 200 manual says to plug the charger into the bike first, then into the wall, and to unplug in the reverse order, with the bike switched off.

## Noise, smell and heat

An electric scooter is quiet, and a charger is quieter, but a shared building notices small things. A fan or a click at night, a warm plastic smell or an unfamiliar bag in a hallway can alarm someone who knows about the news. Tell people what you are doing. A line such as "I am charging my scooter in the yard until about nine" turns a mystery into a fact. If you smell anything burning or see smoke, do not wait for etiquette: switch off at the wall if it is safe, move people away and call the emergency number. ## Sharing the cost

The electricity for a scooter charge is small compared with a room rate, but a shared house may split bills, and a host may have a reason to ask. Ask at the start who pays. If the host says it is free, a thank-you, a coffee or an offer to contribute is a courteous gesture. If you are staying for weeks, offer an arrangement before it becomes an issue. ## Situations at a glance

| Where you are staying | A considerate approach |
|---|---|
| Hostel with a shared courtyard | Ask staff which spot is approved, charge in daylight while someone is around, and unplug before you go out |
| Homestay run by a family | Ask the family directly, agree a time, and keep the scooter out of the living space |
| Serviced flat in a large building | Ask the manager; some buildings have a designated charging area or restrictions on basements |
| Shared house with flatmates | Agree between yourselves, and do not use a socket others rely on |
| Host says no | Thank them, then charge elsewhere or change the vehicle (see below) |
| Host says yes but only certain hours | Keep to the hours exactly, and do not stretch the window |

## A short courtesy checklist

- Asked the host before arriving, or on arrival at the latest.
- Agreed a place, a time window and who pays.
- Parked clear of exits, shoes, bags and bedding.
- Using only the supplied charger, plugged in the right order.
- Staying nearby; not charging overnight or when nobody is in.
- Checked cable and plug; stopped if anything was abnormal.
- Unplugged, coiled the cable and left the socket free.
- Thanked the host.

## FAQ: charging etiquette in shared accommodation

### Do I have to ask before charging in a hostel or homestay?

Yes. Ask even if you think it is allowed. Rules differ between properties, and some buildings restrict charging or basement parking.

### Can I charge in my room?

Only if the host says so. A common answer is that charging happens in a designated spot, not in a sleeping room.

### Is it rude to charge at night?

Hanoi's fire-prevention unit advises against charging overnight or when nobody is around to monitor it. Charge during the day when you are present.

### Should I pay for the electricity?

Ask at the start. Even if the host waves it away, a small gesture is polite, and for a longer stay it is worth agreeing something clear.

### What if another guest is using the only socket?

Wait, ask politely when they will finish, or charge another time. Do not unplug their devices.

### What if the host refuses?

Accept it, and charge elsewhere or ask the shop about a different vehicle.
