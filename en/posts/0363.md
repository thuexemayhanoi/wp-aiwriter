Bich Dong Pagoda is a cliff-side Buddhist complex in Ninh Binh province, a few kilometres from the Tam Coc boat landing, and it is reachable from Hanoi on a rented scooter in a long day or an easy overnight. The ride south on the old national road is flat and legal for motorbikes, the pagoda itself is a short climb of roughly 160 steps in total, and most of the effort goes into the distance, not the visit. Published figures put Ninh Binh city at about 93 to 110 km from central Hanoi, so a round trip from the city is roughly 200 km or more once you add the last stretch to Bich Dong. This guide concentrates on the pagoda: what you will see, how the ticket and parking questions are described, when to go and how to fit it into a day without rushing. For the full highway-by-highway ride to Ninh Binh, use our separate [Hanoi to Ninh Binh route guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-to-ninh-binh-by-motorbike/).

## What Bich Dong actually is

Bich Dong means "Green Grotto". According to the Ninh Binh tourism department, Lord Trinh Sam visited in 1774 and gave the temple that name. The complex sits against the Truong Yen limestone range and is built in three levels that climb the hillside:

- **Lower pagoda (Ha).** Five compartments on level ground at the foot of the mountain. This is where most people arrive and where the long approach between ponds ends.
- **Middle pagoda (Trung).** About 120 steps above the lower one. Part of it is carved into the rock, and the tourism department describes three compartments.
- **Upper pagoda (Thuong).** A further 40 steps up, close to the top of the hill, with the best view over the surrounding rice fields and karst.

The same official page notes that all three pagodas are wooden buildings in the style of the late Le and early Nguyen periods. A commercial guide adds a dark cave reached by 21 more steps from the middle pagoda, where a bronze bell is said to date from 1707, and says a temple was first founded on the site in 1428. Treat those dates as the guide's account of the history rather than something you need to verify on site.

The practical meaning of all this: Bich Dong is a place you walk up through, not a place you photograph from the car park. It rewards a slow visit, and it is quieter than the big boat attractions nearby.

## Where it sits relative to Tam Coc

Bich Dong belongs to the Tam Coc - Bich Dong area, part of the Trang An landscape that UNESCO recognised in 2014. The Tam Coc boat trip runs along the Ngo Dong river through three caves, and some descriptions of the boat route end near Bich Dong. One travel writer places the pagoda about 2 km west of Tam Coc, and the Ninh Binh tourism department describes it as a short drive south of Tam Coc. These descriptions differ a little, so on the day follow the road signs and your map app's pin for "Chua Bich Dong" rather than a remembered direction.

Tam Coc itself is described by one tourism operator as roughly 6 km south-west of Ninh Binh city. That is the useful number to carry: once you are in the city, the pagoda is a short local ride, not another long leg.

## The road from Hanoi, in short

The fast Hanoi to Ninh Binh expressway is not for motorbikes, so a scooter uses the older national road, QL1A, through Thuong Tin, Phu Xuyen and Phu Ly. One travel site describes the private-vehicle route as leaving along Giai Phong and Giap Bat, then the Phap Van to Cau Gie link, and continuing through Phu Ly to Ninh Binh. Another says the ride by motorbike follows National Road 1A. Our [navigation-app check](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-check-if-a-navigation-app-suggests-an-expressway/) explains how to stop a map from sending you onto the wrong road, and the [ring roads and highways guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-ring-roads-highways-motorbikes-allowed/) covers the Hanoi end.

## How long to allow at the pagoda

A local tour operator suggests about 30 to 45 minutes at Bich Dong as part of a half-day village-road ride. If you climb all three levels, enter the dark cave and stop to look at the view, allow longer, and more if you are with children or older relatives. The steps are stone and can be slippery after rain.

The honest answer is that the pagoda takes an hour or less, and the ride to reach it takes several. So build the day around the ride.

## A realistic day plan

Treat this as a template, not a timetable:

1. **Leave early.** The urban start of the ride is the slowest part. Our [Ninh Binh route guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-to-ninh-binh-by-motorbike/) describes why a seven o'clock start works.
2. **Break at Phu Ly.** It is the one real town on the road and a sensible fuel and coffee stop.
3. **Visit Bich Dong first.** Morning light is kinder for climbing, and the pagoda is calmer before tour groups arrive.
4. **Lunch near Tam Coc.** Then decide whether to add the boat ride, which can take around two to three hours according to commercial guides.
5. **Start back by mid-afternoon.** Riding national roads after dark is the part to avoid; see [riding at night in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-at-night-in-hanoi/) for why.

## Best time to go

Commercial guides name mid-May to early June as the best window because the rice fields are golden before harvest, and January to March as the mild spring option. Neither is a rule. Outside those windows the area is still open and green. Check the weather on the morning you ride: the stone steps are the main hazard in wet weather, and heavy rain on the national road is a bigger concern than rain at the pagoda. Our [guide to riding in Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) covers gear and flooded streets.

## What to wear and how to behave

Bich Dong is a working religious site. A commercial guide advises covering shoulders and knees, wearing comfortable shoes for the stairs and carrying water and sun protection. Speak quietly in the prayer halls, do not point your feet at altars, and ask before photographing people who are praying. If you cannot cover your knees, carry a light wrap in the scooter's under-seat storage.

## FAQ about Bich Dong by motorbike

### Is Bich Dong Pagoda worth visiting if I only have one day?

Yes, if you accept a long day on the road. The visit is short and the setting is distinctive, but the ride is roughly 200 km round trip. If that is too much, consider staying in the Tam Coc area for one night.

### Do I need to take the Tam Coc boat to see Bich Dong?

Not necessarily. Sources describe the boat trip as a separate activity, and one traveller reports walking up to the pagoda without a boat ticket. Others say the complex ticket is required. Ask at the entrance.

### How many steps are there?

The tourism department gives 120 steps from the lower to the middle pagoda and 40 more to the upper one. A commercial guide adds 21 steps to the dark cave.

### Can I ride to Bich Dong on the expressway?

No. Motorbikes use the old national road. Follow road signs rather than the car-routing default of a map app.
