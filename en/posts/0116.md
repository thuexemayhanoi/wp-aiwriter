On an automatic scooter, brake with both hands together. The right-hand lever works the front wheel and the left-hand lever works the rear wheel, there is no foot pedal, and on a Honda with a combined brake system, which Honda fits to scooters such as the Vision, Lead and Vario, squeezing both levers at once is exactly what the manual asks for. Close the throttle first, squeeze both levers progressively rather than snatching them, keep the handlebar straight, and ease off just before you stop. This guide explains what each control does, what "combined brake" really means, how the technique changes at junctions, in rain, with a passenger and on a downhill, how to test the brakes at the shop before riding off and which signs mean you should hand the bike back. It is general information, not a riding lesson.

## Which control brakes which wheel

On the automatic scooters commonly rented in Hanoi, such as the Honda Vision, Air Blade, Click and Lead or the Yamaha Mio, there is no gear lever, no clutch and no brake pedal. Both brakes are on the handlebar:

- **Right lever:** the front brake.
- **Left lever:** the rear brake.

That layout is typical, but check it on the bike you are given, because the lever on the left side of a manual bike is often the clutch and people carry that habit across. On a scooter the left hand is not free. It has a brake in it.

The rear brake on many Honda scooters is a drum brake and the front is often a disc, though that varies by model. Our guide to [front and rear brakes on small manual motorbikes](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/front-brake-versus-rear-brake-on-small-manual-motorbikes/) describes a different arrangement, with a foot pedal for the rear brake, so riders switching between the two kinds should expect to retrain their feet.

## What "combined brake" means on a Honda scooter

Honda's Vietnamese manuals for the Vision, Lead 125 and Vario 125 each carry the same boxed note. The bike has a combined brake system that distributes braking force between the front and rear brakes. The distribution differs when only the front lever is used and when only the rear lever is used, so for the most effective braking you should use the front and rear brakes together.

Honda's global explanation of combination brakes adds the principle: normally the front and rear brakes work independently, while a combined system is designed so that operating one brake also operates part of the other. On the Honda "combination brake" type, applying the rear brake with the left lever also activates the front brake.

What this means for a rider:

- **Squeezing the left lever alone** brakes the rear and also puts some braking onto the front. It stops the scooter more evenly than a rear-only brake would, but it is still not the strongest option.
- **Squeezing the right lever alone** works the front. How much reaches the rear depends on the system fitted.
- **Squeezing both together** is what the manuals describe as the most effective braking.

Details vary by model and market, so treat this as the principle and the manual as the authority. Honda's Lead manual also states that the cable adjustment on the combined brake is a dealer job, which is one reason a renter should never adjust anything.

## A smooth stop in five steps

Honda's Lead manual reduces braking to one line: close the throttle fully and use the front and rear brake levers at the same time, and the brake light shows that the brakes are applied. Put that into a sequence:

1. **Look and decide early.** Most hard stops are late stops. Cover the levers with two fingers as soon as something ahead might change.
2. **Close the throttle.** Take your hand off the power before you squeeze.
3. **Squeeze both levers progressively.** Start gently, then increase the pressure, so the weight shifts forward without a jolt.
4. **Keep the handlebar straight and your eyes up.** Braking while turning is when small tyres lose grip.
5. **Ease off a moment before you stop,** so the scooter does not nod and your passenger does not slide into your back.

A scooter has small wheels and a short wheelbase, so it reacts quickly to sudden braking. The cure is not to avoid the front brake but to apply it smoothly.

## Braking in Hanoi situations

### Junctions and flowing traffic

Hanoi traffic flows by small adjustments, and our guide to [how traffic crosses Hanoi intersections](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/crossing-hanoi-intersections/) explains the merge. For the braking, the practical point is to slow in advance with light squeezes rather than leaving it to a sudden stop. A light, early squeeze also lights the brake light for the rider behind you.

### Corners and turns

Honda's Lead manual shows a short diagram on cornering: brake before the corner, then open the throttle slowly through it. Do the heavy braking while the scooter is upright and straight, release most of the pressure as you lean in and do not brake hard mid-corner.

### Wet roads

Rain changes everything, and our guide to [riding in Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) describes the season. For brakes, leave a larger gap, begin braking earlier and softer and avoid painted lines, metal covers and shiny patches. In the first minutes of rain, dust and oil on the surface make it especially slippery. The aim is to do no abrupt braking at all.

### Downhill

This is general guidance rather than a Honda rule. Slow before the slope, use both brakes in short squeezes rather than one long drag, and ease off the throttle completely.

### With a passenger or luggage

Extra weight lengthens the stopping distance and moves the weight to the rear. Brake earlier, squeeze more gently and tell your passenger to hold on and keep their feet on the footrests. Our [guide to carrying a passenger on a Lead or Vision](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/scooter-for-two-riders-and-luggage-hanoi/) covers the setup.

## The rear brake lock lever

Many automatic scooters, though not every variant, have a lock lever next to the rear brake lever for parking. Honda's Air Blade manual says to release it by squeezing the rear brake lever strongly, and warns never to use the lock while riding, because the wheel can lock and you can lose control. The manual says to make sure it is fully released before moving off so that the rear wheel does not drag.

If you feel a scooter drag, smell hot brake or hear a dull scrape after you start, check the lock lever first.

## ABS on some variants

Some Honda variants have an anti-lock brake system. Honda's Lead manual says ABS does not work below 10 km/h, that the front lever may pulse slightly when it works and that this is normal. If your rental has ABS, a pulsing lever in a hard stop is the system working, not a fault. Do not assume the scooter has it, however. Most rentals do not, so ask the shop.

## A brake test before you ride off

At the shop, with staff beside you and the engine off, do this:

| Test | What good looks like | What to do if not |
|---|---|---|
| Squeeze right lever | Firm, with a clear point where the brake bites | Soft or touching the grip: report it |
| Squeeze left lever | Same, with the rear wheel held | Spongy or too much travel: report it |
| Push the scooter forward with both levers squeezed | It will not roll | If it rolls, report it |
| Brake light | Comes on with either lever | Report a dead bulb |
| Lever and grip | No cracks, no bent lever | Ask for another bike |
| Spin the wheels | No dragging or rubbing | Check the lock lever, then report it |

Then ride a short, quiet distance and brake gently once in a straight line, once from a faster speed and once with only the left lever. The scooter should slow evenly without pulling to one side or squealing. Honda's own pre-ride checks for its scooters list brake operation, front fluid level and pad wear, and rear shoe wear and freeplay, which is why the adjustment belongs to the shop. ## Signs the brakes are not right

| Sign | Often means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Lever travels almost to the grip | Wear or air in the system | Stop and report it |
| Brake squeals loudly | Glazed or contaminated pads or shoes | Report it |
| Scooter pulls to one side | Uneven braking | Report it |
| Grinding metal sound | Worn out lining | Stop riding |
| Lever feels spongy | Air or fluid problem | Report it |
| Brake light stays on | Switch fault | Tell the shop |

Do not adjust the brakes yourself. If anything seems wrong, ask for another bike. ## Habits that keep braking smooth

- **Wear sensible shoes,** so you can feel the ground when you stop.
- **Keep two fingers on each lever** in traffic. Ready fingers cut reaction time.
- **Do not coast on the levers.** Dragging them heats the brakes and wears them.
- **Do not brake while the scooter is leaning hard.**
- **Go slower in wet weather,** since the tyres, not the brakes, are the limit.
- **Check the tyres,** because brakes cannot work well on worn or soft tyres.

## FAQ: automatic scooter brakes

### Which lever is the front brake?

Usually the right lever. The left lever is usually the rear brake.

### Is it safe to use only the rear brake?

On a combined-brake Honda it works partly on both wheels, but Honda says to use both levers together for the best braking.

### Is there a foot pedal?

Not on a typical automatic scooter. Both brakes are on the handlebar.

### What is the combined brake system?

A design that spreads braking force between the front and rear wheels, as Honda's manuals describe.

### Should I use the rear brake lock lever while riding?

No. Honda warns that it can lock the wheel. It is for parking only.

### How do I know if my rental has ABS?

Ask the shop. Do not assume it.
