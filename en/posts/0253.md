Plan a charging stop on a longer electric ride the way you would plan a fuel stop on a very slow petrol bike: work out the real range first, set a reserve, choose a stop you would visit anyway, and allow far more time than a petrol fill-up takes. On a standard plug-in charger a scooter gains only a modest amount of range per hour, so a stop of an hour is a top-up, not a refill. For most visitors the best plan is a route that fits inside one charge with a margin, with a charging stop as a backup that you have agreed with the shop in advance. This guide sets out a simple method, using published VinFast figures as examples, and covers where to stop, how to charge safely away from the shop and what to do if the numbers go wrong. The figures belong to specific VinFast models, not necessarily to the bike you rent.

## Step 1: find the range you can actually use

Manufacturers publish range under standard test conditions. VinFast's Feliz 2025 page says so plainly in a footnote: the 262 km figure applies with two batteries, tested at 30 km/h, and real distance can be lower depending on speed, temperature, terrain, riding habits, the weight of rider and passengers, the riding mode and traffic.

A brochure for another model, the VinFast Vero X, shows how large the gap can be. It lists about 134 km per charge under standard conditions and about 86 km under mixed conditions for one 2.4 kWh battery, and adds about 128 km and 83 km respectively for a second battery. The mixed-condition figure is about 64 per cent of the standard one, a ratio calculated here from those two numbers. It is a useful mental discount, but it is one model's brochure, not a rule.

So, before you plan, ask the shop for three things:

- the battery size or the stated range of the bike you will ride;
- whether the figure is a standard-condition or a mixed-condition number;
- whether a second battery is fitted.

Then use the lower number. If the shop gives only a standard figure, apply a discount of about a third as a cautious working assumption, and discount further for hills, a passenger, luggage, hot weather and high speed. Our guide to [renting an electric scooter in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/renting-an-electric-scooter-in-hanoi/) explains how range figures are quoted.

## Step 2: choose a reserve and a first-stop point

A battery indicator is not precise at the low end, and an empty bike on a quiet road is a problem the shop has to solve for you. Keep a reserve.

A workable rule for a visitor:

1. **Keep at least a third of the battery in reserve** at the farthest point of your route.
2. **Plan to turn back, or to start charging, when the indicator is at about half**, not at the last bar.
3. **Without a charging stop, keep the whole round trip inside the remaining two-thirds**, so an out-and-back route should go no further than about a third of the range from the shop.

Write the numbers on your phone before you leave. For example, if the shop says the bike's mixed-condition range is 80 km, two-thirds of it is roughly 53 km, so you would plan a round trip of no more than about 50 km, or about 25 km out. These numbers are an illustration of the method, not a statement about any particular rental bike.

## Step 3: understand how little a one-hour stop adds

Slow plug-in charging is the main reason a stop needs planning. VinFast lists a 400 W standard charger for the Vero X and for the Feliz Lite, with about six and a half hours for a full charge from empty. Earlier VinFast models, such as the Feliz S, were described with a 1,000 W charger and about six hours.

You can turn those figures into a rough estimate. A 400 W charger delivers at most 0.4 kWh per hour, and one 2.4 kWh battery is about one-sixth full after an hour. Using the Vero X's mixed-condition range, that hour might add roughly 14 km, as an estimate that ignores charging losses and the slowing of the charge near full. A two-hour stop would add about double.

Three conclusions follow:

- **A quick coffee does not recharge a scooter.** A twenty-minute stop adds very little on a standard charger.
- **A long meal or a long visit is a real charging window.** Plan the stop where you will stay for ninety minutes to two hours anyway.
- **If your route needs more than one charging stop to complete, choose a different route or a different vehicle.** A scooter is the wrong tool for a long-distance ride.

Remember that this is an estimate based on one model's figures. Your bike's charger, battery and mixed-condition range may differ, so ask the shop for the charger's power and the time for a full charge.

## Step 4: pick where you will stop

Public charging infrastructure exists, but it is not something a visitor can rely on. VinFast says that its network had 100,000 charging ports across Vietnam as of October 2025, but that number covers all of its vehicles and does not tell you whether a rental scooter can use a particular port. VinFast's own advice for charging at public stations is to find the nearest station using the VinFast E-Scooter app and to plug in with quick, definite movements to avoid flickering power that interrupts the charge. A renter may not have that app linked to the bike, so ask the shop whether your scooter can use public stations and how to check.

The more reliable plan is a stop with a normal socket and a person who agrees. Good candidates include:

- **A cafe, restaurant or homestay** where staff say yes and the bike can stand near a socket in sight.
- **A hotel on a multi-day trip**, arranged in advance. Our guide to [charging an electric scooter in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/charging-an-electric-scooter-in-hanoi/) explains what to ask a hotel and why swap cabinets are for the bike's owner.
- **A destination you were visiting anyway**, so that the charge costs you no extra time.

Ask permission before you plug in, and offer to pay for the electricity if the host seems reluctant. Avoid a socket in a cramped, cluttered or damp place.

## Step 5: charge safely away from the shop

A fire-prevention and rescue unit's advice, reported in September 2026, is a good guide for any stop. It advises against charging with nobody present to monitor it, and against charging straight after a ride while the battery is still hot. It recommends using the charger and power supply specified by the manufacturer, keeping the charging area dry, airy and clear of heat and flammable material, and stopping the charge and checking the vehicle if the battery, charger or cable becomes unusually hot, swells, cracks or smells odd.

For a charging stop, that means:

1. **Let the bike cool for a few minutes** before you plug in.
2. **Use only the charger supplied with the bike**, and not a borrowed one.
3. **Stay where you can see the bike** and check the charger, the cable and the battery now and then.
4. **Keep the cable off the walkway** and away from anything that burns.
5. **Stop the charge before you leave**, even if the battery is not full.
6. **Do not charge in the rain** or with a wet port. Our guide to [riding in Hanoi's rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) explains how weather changes a day's plan.

If anything about the charger or cable looks wrong, stop and call the shop on 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969), open 09:00 to 21:00 daily. Do not improvise with a different charger.

## Step 6: check what the rental allows

A longer ride is only a longer ride if the contract allows it. Before you plan beyond the city:

- Ask whether the bike may leave Hanoi at all. Our guide to [taking a rental motorbike out of the city](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/take-rental-motorbike-out-of-hanoi/) explains the issues, and the answer for an electric model may differ from a petrol one.
- Ask whether the shop is comfortable with you charging away from the shop, and whether there is any condition on where.
- Ask what the shop would do if the battery ran flat far from Hanoi, and whether that is your cost.
- Check the electric model's top speed. A bike limited to about 49 km/h is not suited to roads where traffic moves much faster. Our guide to [electric bikes in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/electric-bikes-in-hanoi-rules/) explains the main classes.

Photograph the battery indicator at pickup and keep a note of the charge level at the start. The shop confirms the battery level and charging arrangement at handover for every electric model.

## When the plan goes wrong

- **The indicator falls faster than expected.** Slow down, switch to a calmer mode if the bike has one, avoid hills and head for the nearest safe place.
- **You cannot find a socket.** Do not rely on a stranger's improvised connection. Call the shop and describe your location and the indicator.
- **The bike stops.** Move it off the road if you can, put on the hazard lights if it has them and call the shop. Our guide to [what to do with a rental bike after a breakdown](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-bike-breakdown-hanoi-what-to-do/) lists the steps.
- **It is getting dark.** Do not take a chance with a low battery after dusk. Our guide to [riding at night in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-at-night-in-hanoi/) explains why.

## FAQ: planning a charging stop

### How long does a charging stop take?

On a standard 400 W charger, a full charge from empty is listed at about six and a half hours for two VinFast models. A one-hour stop adds only a fraction of that.

### Can I use a public charging station?

Only if the bike can use it. Ask the shop, and check the station's availability with the operator's app before you rely on it.

### Is a rapid charge possible?

Do not assume so. Ask the shop for the charger's power and whether the bike supports anything faster.

### How much reserve should I keep?

At least a third of the battery is a cautious rule for a visitor. Keep more if you are carrying a passenger or riding in heat or hills.

### Can I charge at a cafe?

Ask the staff first, keep the bike in sight, use only the supplied charger and stop the charge before you leave.

### Should I do a longer ride at all?

Only if the route fits comfortably within the range. For long distances, a petrol bike or another transport option is usually simpler.
