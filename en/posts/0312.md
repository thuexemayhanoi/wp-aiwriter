The best way to handle parking at Bat Trang is to leave the scooter at the first attended space you reach near the ceramic market or the village entrance, take a ticket, and explore the narrow lanes on foot or by electric cart. The village is a working craft community, its lanes are lined with workshops and homes, and on busy days tour buses and shoppers fill the main street. Riding deeper looking for a space in front of the shop you want is how scooters end up blocking doors and delivery routes. This guide covers how you arrive from the dyke road, what the streets are like, how to choose a parking place, what the 2024 road law requires of a parked scooter and how to carry fragile pottery home without overloading a small bike.

## Arriving: the dyke road and the turn into the village

Bat Trang is in a commune of the same name on the left bank of the Red River, which older guides place in Gia Lam District and recent guides describe simply as Bat Trang commune, Hanoi. A national newspaper's 2025 travel guide puts it about 15 kilometres south-east of Hoan Kiem Lake. For riders it lists the bridges at Long Bien, Chuong Duong, Vinh Tuy and Thanh Tri and then the route along the Red River dyke. Vietnamese Wikipedia lists the Long Bien-Bat Trang-Xuan Quan dyke route among the commune's key roads, and its article on Bat Trang pottery describes the traditional way in as following the dyke to the Giang Cao slope and turning down into the village. One tourist guide spells out a path from Chuong Duong Bridge via Hong Tien Street and the Long Bien-Xuan Quan dyke road to Giang Cao Road.

The point for parking is that the last part of the journey changes character. You leave a raised, fairly fast road and descend onto village streets that handle pedestrians, trucks collecting ceramics, shop deliveries and cars. Slow down before the turn, signal and give way, and do not look at your phone while you search for the entrance. Our [Long Bien area page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/long-bien/) is a starting point for the local area, and the [Gia Lam and Bat Trang guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/gia-lam-east-bank-bat-trang-by-scooter/) covers the wider outing and the choice of route.

## One commune, two villages, many places to stop

The commune has two traditional villages, Bat Trang and Giang Cao, both ceramic-making, and the national newspaper's guide says the commune covers about 22 square kilometres. That is large enough that visitors with children or older relatives are advised to use an electric cart to get around. The same guide lists six highlights: the ceramic market, the Van Van old house, traditional workshops, the village communal house, the ceramics museum and the old gourd-shaped kiln.

The places do not sit on one doorstep, so a good parking choice depends on your plan:

- **Ceramic market.** The market is described as about 6,000 square metres with more than 100 stalls, and the 47A bus terminates near it. Parking nearby is simplest, but also the busiest.
- **Van Van old house.** A guide puts it next to the market in a small alley. Park outside the alley and walk in.
- **Museum.** Vietnam Airlines' guide gives the address as number 28, village 5, and says it opened for tourism in 2021. Ask on arrival about its own parking before assuming.
- **Communal house and old village.** These are in the narrower part of the village, which is where walking makes most sense.

## Four ways to park, and one to avoid

1. **An attended space near the market or entrance.** Visitor guides say motorbike parking is available at the village, and one lists it at the entrance. Look for an attendant, a ticket and rows of bikes, not a patch of road with a few scooters.
2. **A space offered by a shop where you are buying.** Ask first and accept a no. A workshop's frontage is often needed for deliveries.
3. **A side-street space outside the busy core.** Use it if it is signed and you are not blocking a gate, a lane or a house entrance.
4. **A longer walk.** On a crowded weekend, a few minutes' extra walk beats a long search with a running engine.
5. **Avoid: riding into the old lanes to find a closer space.** The lanes are narrow, hold pedestrians and often work as loading zones.

The village's own planning rules point the same way. A 2001 Hanoi planning charter for the Bat Trang craft village, published on the thuvienphapluat.vn legal database, calls for the facilities it covers to have their own parking with convenient entrances, and says the roadway and pavement should not be used as a car park. It also set aside land for several car parks and gave the riverside road through the old village a width of 7.5 metres, of which 3.5 metres was carriageway. That is old planning material, and the village has changed since, but it explains why informal roadside parking is poorly tolerated.

## What the local streets are like

Descriptions of Bat Trang are consistent: a main street with showrooms, workshops and homes behind them, and narrow lanes in the old part of the village. A tourism site reports that on weekends tour buses arrive from Hanoi and the main street can become congested. A travel guide that tells riders to bring sun protection says the road in can be dusty.

For a scooter that means:

- **Walkers appear without warning.** People step out of shops, stop to photograph kilns and carry trays of ceramics across the road.
- **Delivery and loading are normal.** Motorbike trailers, trolleys and vans move stock in and out, so do not wait in front of an open door.
- **Wet surfaces matter.** Rain on tight corners makes a hard stop risky. See our [guide to riding in Hanoi's rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/).
- **Kilns and workshops can be hot, dusty or fragile.** Keep your distance from doorways and leave room for people with loads.

## What the road law says about the parked bike

Vietnam's Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety No. 36/2024/QH15, in force since 1 January 2025, sets general parking rules that apply in a village as much as in the city:

- You may stop or park on a street only close to the kerb or pavement on your right-hand side, with your nearest wheel no more than 0.25 metres from it, without endangering or obstructing people and vehicles.
- You may not park at a junction or within 5 metres of its edge, on a pedestrian crossing, at a pick-up or drop-off point for passengers, in a place that hides a road sign or where the carriageway is only wide enough for one motor-vehicle lane.
- When you leave the bike, use the parking brake or another safe measure, and signal when you pull in and out.
- Riders may not ride two or more abreast, enter areas reserved for pedestrians, or carry bulky loads.

Fees for parking vary and should be confirmed with the attendant; we do not quote a figure here.

## Carrying pottery home

Ceramics are heavy and breakable, and a scooter has limited carrying space. The 2024 law lets a motorbike carry goods only within set limits: no more than 0.3 metres beyond the width of the luggage rack on each side, no more than 0.5 metres beyond the rear of the rack, and no higher than 2 metres from the road surface. It also bars riders from carrying bulky loads.

In practice:

- **Buy small, or buy light.** A few bowls in a padded bag are fine. A large vase or a box of plates is not a handlebar job.
- **Ask the seller about packing and delivery.** Ask whether the shop can wrap pieces well or arrange delivery.
- **Do not hang bags from the handlebars.** They affect steering and can catch on a lever.
- **Check your seat box.** Keep it lightly loaded so the lid closes and nothing presses on the fuel tank area.
- **Plan for the return ride.** Our guide to [how much luggage a Honda Wave can safely carry](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-much-luggage-can-you-safely-carry-on-a-honda-wave/) explains the limits.

## Before you leave the bike

1. Is the spot attended, ticketed or clearly allowed?
2. Is the bike out of doorways, lanes and delivery routes?
3. Is the steering locked? The rental does not include an extra lock, so a guarded space is your main protection.
4. Are phone, wallet and fragile purchases out of the seat box and with you?
5. Do you have the ticket and a photo of the place?
6. Is there fuel for the return? See the [guide to paying for fuel, parking and tolls](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/paying-fuel-parking-tolls-hanoi-visitor/).

## FAQ about parking in Bat Trang

### Is there an entrance fee for the village?

Guides say there is no general entrance fee to walk the village and the market. Some attractions, such as the museum, charge their own tickets.

### Is there parking at the village entrance?

Visitor guides say motorbike parking is available at the entrance and in the village. Look for an attended space with a ticket, and ask if you are unsure.

### Can I ride into the old lanes?

You may be able to, but it is usually not a good idea. The lanes are narrow, shared with pedestrians and used for deliveries. Park and walk.

### Is it better to take the bus?

Bus 47A runs from Long Bien and terminates near the market, according to guides, which avoids parking altogether. A scooter gives more freedom to combine other places, but you must handle the dyke road and the parking.

### When is it quietest?

Guides recommend weekdays and mornings. Weekends and holidays bring tour buses and a congested main street.
