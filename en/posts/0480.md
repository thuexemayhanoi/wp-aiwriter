A rain poncho is only safe on a scooter if nothing on it covers the number plate, the tail light, the indicators or the mirrors, and nothing can flap into the wheels, the brake levers or the rider's hands. Law 36/2024/QH15 bans concealing or bending a number plate (Article 9), and Vietnamese legal publications report a fine of VND 4,000,000 to 6,000,000 and 6 licence points under Decree 168/2024/ND-CP for a motorbike with a plate that is covered, unreadable or wrongly fitted. A cape can do exactly that if it hangs down behind you. The rule is simple: wear it so that every light, the plate and the wheels stay visible, and secure every loose edge before you move.

This guide explains the hazards a poncho creates, the legal rules that apply, how to choose between a cape and a two-piece suit, how to fit and secure it, a check to do before you ride, how to ride in it and what to do when it fails. It is general information based on the law and published guidance as of October 2026, not legal advice.

## Why a poncho can be dangerous

A poncho is a large piece of loose plastic in a place with a lot of moving parts. On a scooter, four risks matter.

- **It can hide the plate and the lights.** The rear of a cape hangs over the seat and may fall over the plate, the tail light and the indicators. Other riders then cannot see you braking or turning, and cameras and officers cannot read the plate.

- **It can reach the wheels.** A long hem can blow into the rear wheel, the drive or the brake, and a front panel can billow into the front wheel when the wind catches it.

- **It can cover the controls.** Hanging sleeves and front panels can snag on the brake levers, throttle and mirrors, or block your view of the instruments.

- **It can catch the wind.** At speed a loose cape acts like a sail, tugging at your shoulders and distracting you.

## What the law says

**Number plates (Article 9).** The Law bans mounting number plates not in designated positions, bending or concealing number plates, and altering letters, numbers, colours, shapes or sizes. A poncho that covers the plate is concealing it, whether or not you meant to.

**The fine.** Vietnamese legal publications, including a news report on the Government's decree and a law-firm commentary, say that Article 14 of Decree 168/2024/ND-CP sets a fine of VND 4,000,000 to 6,000,000 for a motorbike with a plate fitted in the wrong position, illegible, bent or covered, and deducts 6 licence points. Check the current text before relying on a figure. The point for a rider is that the penalty is high and the cause may be only a flapping cape.

**Lights (Article 20 and Decree 168/2024).** Vehicles must show lights when visibility is limited. The decree's Article 7 penalises riding without lights at night or when fog, smoke, dust, rain or other conditions limit visibility. Our guides to [how to ride after dark with limited street lighting](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-ride-after-dark-with-limited-street-lighting/) and [safe scooter riding when visibility drops in rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/safe-scooter-riding-when-visibility-drops-in-rain/) explain what to do.

**Umbrellas (Article 33).** Riders may not use umbrellas while riding. A poncho or a rain suit is not an umbrella, but it is a reminder that the law expects both hands on the bars.

**Safe riding in general.** Article 12 requires you to slow down in rain and wind, and Article 33 bans acts that affect traffic order and safety.

## Cape or two-piece suit?

| Type | Advantages | Risks on a scooter | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cape or poncho** | Cheap, easy to put on and share | Flaps; hides the plate and lights; blows into the front wheel | Acceptable only if secured, short and fitted with side loops |
| **Cape with sleeves and a rear cut** | Covers arms; some cut away at the back | Some designs still hang over the tail | Check the rear before every ride |
| **Two-piece suit (jacket and trousers)** | Close-fitting; no loose hem | Hot and slower to put on | Best for a long ride or heavy rain |
| **Rain jacket plus separate leg cover** | Covers the upper body; legs protected | A leg cover can slide down | Good if the cover is fastened at the ankle |

## Choosing a poncho

- **Choose a bright colour,** or one with reflective strips. A dark poncho in rain at night makes you hard to see.

- **Choose a shorter length.** A poncho that reaches the knee is easier to secure than one that reaches the ankle.

- **Look for loops or straps** for the thumbs, the wrists and the waist.

- **Check that the hood fits over your helmet,** or choose a rain cover for the helmet. A hood that blocks your side view is dangerous.

- **Make sure there is a clear window or a visor** that does not mist over.

- **Avoid a one-size cape that hangs over the passenger,** unless it is designed for two and you can secure it at the back.

- **Carry the poncho folded under the seat,** so you can put it on before the rain, not during it.

## How to fit and secure it

1. **Put it on at the kerb,** off the road, in a place where you are not blocking anyone. Do not try to pull it on while riding.

2. **Put the helmet on first,** then the hood or the cape, so the helmet strap and the visor stay accessible.

3. **Loop the thumbs or wrists through any loops** so the front cannot ride up or billow.

4. **Gather the rear hem,** and tuck it under your thighs or the seat so that it lies on the seat, not over the plate and tail light. Check that it does not hang below the seat.

5. **Pull the front down over your knees,** and check that it does not touch the handlebar controls, the mirrors or the instruments.

6. **Tie or clip the sides** if the poncho has fasteners, so that it cannot fly open.

7. **Keep your feet and hands free,** with room to reach the brake pedal or the levers and the horn.

8. **Check the passenger,** if any, in the same way. Our guide to [riding with a passenger: balance and communication](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-with-a-passenger-balance-and-communication/) explains what to agree.

## A check before you ride

Do this quickly, every time. Ask a friend or the shop staff to help, or use a shop window as a mirror.

| Check | What you want to see |
|---|---|
| **Plate** | The whole plate visible from behind, with no plastic over it |
| **Tail light and brake light** | Visible when you press the brake lever or pedal |
| **Indicators** | Visible, and nothing flapping in front of them |
| **Mirrors** | Clear view, not blocked by the poncho |
| **Instruments** | You can see the speedometer and warning lights |
| **Wheels** | No part hangs below the seat or the footboard |
| **Hands and levers** | The poncho does not catch on a lever |
| **Visor and eyes** | Clear view to the side as well as ahead |

Test the brake light and the indicators, then look again. Our guide to [how to check a scooter's horn and turn signals](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-check-a-scooters-horn-and-turn-signals/) explains the checks.

## How to ride in a poncho

- **Slow down.** Wet roads, poor visibility and a flapping poncho all call for a lower speed. Our guide to [riding in Hanoi in the rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) gives more detail.

- **Signal earlier.** The poncho may cover your arm signals and hide your lights, and a rain-covered light is dimmer.

- **Look at the road ahead, not at the plastic.** If it flaps in your peripheral vision, stop and fix it.

- **Keep a longer gap.** Brakes work less well when wet, and others see you later.

- **Watch for gusts.** Riding on a bridge or between buildings can lift the front of a cape. Our guide to [riding on bridges during strong winds](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-on-bridges-during-strong-winds/) explains how to handle it.

- **Stop to adjust it,** not while moving. If it comes loose, pull in at a lawful place and refit it.

## If the poncho gets caught

1. **Do not yank it.** Pulling can twist the bars or pull you off balance.

2. **Ease off the throttle and brake gently,** with both brakes, if the poncho has reached a wheel.

3. **Stop in a safe place,** put the stand down and switch off.

4. **Free the plastic,** and check the wheel, the brake and the drive for damage.

## A quick checklist

1. **Put the poncho on at the kerb, before the rain gets heavy.**

2. **Choose bright colours, a short length and loops.**

3. **Keep the plate, tail light, indicators and mirrors visible.**

4. **Tuck the rear hem under your thighs, away from the wheel.**

5. **Test the brake light and indicators.**

6. **Slow down, signal early and keep more distance.**

7. **Stop to adjust, never while riding.**

8. **Call the shop if anything is damaged.**

## FAQ: rain ponchos on scooters

### Can a poncho cover my number plate?

No. Article 9 of Law 36/2024/QH15 bans concealing number plates, and legal publications report a fine of VND 4,000,000 to 6,000,000 and 6 licence points under Decree 168/2024/ND-CP. Keep the plate visible.

### Is a poncho better than a two-piece rain suit?

A two-piece suit fits closer and has no loose hem, so it is usually safer. A poncho is cheaper and quicker. If you use one, secure it.

### Does the shop provide a raincoat?

The shop's published terms say that a raincoat is not part of short rentals and may be provided on a monthly rental. Ask the shop what is available.

### Can my passenger share my poncho?

Only if it is designed for two and you can secure the rear. Otherwise each person should wear a separate suit.

### Should I turn on my lights in rain?

Yes, when rain limits visibility. Decree 168/2024/ND-CP penalises riding without lights when rain or bad weather limits visibility.

### What if the poncho blows over my face?

Slow down, brake gently and stop at the next safe place. Do not pull at it while moving.
