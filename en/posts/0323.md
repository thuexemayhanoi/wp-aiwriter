Arriving at Gia Lam Bus Station on a rental scooter is easy to do badly and easy to do well. The station is at No. 9 Ngo Gia Kham Street in Bo De Ward, on the east bank of the Red River, and it is one of the busiest places in the area for coaches, city buses and passengers with luggage. The good approach is simple: agree your stopping point before you arrive, keep out of the coach bays and pick-up points, never wait on the pavement or in the carriageway, and treat any scooter return as a deadline that must finish well before your coach leaves. This guide covers the roads around the station, four common situations, the stopping rules that matter and the practical limits of a scooter at a transport hub.

## Where Gia Lam Bus Station sits, and why it matters to a scooter rider

Gia Lam Bus Station belongs to the Hanoi Transport Station Joint Stock Company, which also runs My Dinh and Giap Bat. The operator gives the address as No. 9, Ngo Gia Kham Street, Bo De Ward, with a hotline of 1900 1825 (branch 3). Older and unofficial listings sometimes show a different street number or ward, so rely on the operator's address and check your map pin against the actual entrance before you set off.

The station links Hanoi with provinces to the east and north-east. The operator names Bac Ninh, Hai Duong, Hai Phong, Hung Yen, Nam Dinh, Bac Giang, Quang Ninh, Thai Binh and Vinh Phuc, together with Cao Bang, Lang Son, Thai Nguyen and several others. It also lists 13 city and neighbouring bus routes that start or end here. For each coach departure, the operator's timetable gives a ticket-counter number and a boarding position, which is the information a person you are meeting will need.

For a rider the layout of the surrounding roads is the useful part. A 2023 news summary of city bus route 01 describes buses leaving the station along Ngo Gia Kham, Ngoc Lam and Nguyen Van Cu before reaching Chuong Duong Bridge, so that chain of streets is the main way toward the river and the Old Quarter. A travel site puts Hoan Kiem Lake about 5 km away. Nguyen Ha's shop is in the same ward, in Alley 5 Nguyen Van Cu Street, and our [Gia Lam area page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/gia-lam/) explains how the neighbourhood fits together.

## Four ways you might arrive, and what each one needs

"Arriving with a scooter" can mean several different things. Matching your situation to the right plan avoids most of the problems.

| Situation | Main task | What to settle first |
|---|---|---|
| Riding up to drop a passenger | A short, legal stop | The exact spot, and whether the bags fit on the bike |
| Riding up to meet an arriving passenger | Waiting without blocking | A meeting point away from the coach bays, and a way to track the coach |
| Returning your scooter, then boarding a coach | A deadline | Shop opening hours, fuel level, and a safety margin |
| Arriving by coach and wanting a scooter | A handover | Documents, deposit, and whether the shop is open |

The rest of this guide takes these in turn. Whichever applies, remember that a rental scooter is not a luggage vehicle. If a heavy suitcase has to travel, a taxi or ride-hailing car is the better choice, and our guide to [carrying a passenger and luggage on a scooter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/scooter-for-two-riders-and-luggage-hanoi/) explains the limits.

## Approaching the entrance: coaches, city buses and people with luggage

A bus station is a place where large vehicles turn, stop and pull out with limited visibility, and where passengers walk with bags between them. Slow down well before the entrance. Watch for coaches pulling away from their boarding position, city buses signalling to re-enter the road and pedestrians who step off the kerb because they are looking at a ticket or a phone.

Vietnam's Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (Law 36/2024/QH15, effective 1 January 2025) lists places where stopping and parking are not allowed. In Article 18 these include pick-up and drop-off points for passengers, anywhere within 5 metres of a junction's edge, pedestrian crossings and places where the carriageway is only wide enough for a single lane of motor vehicles. The same article asks you to signal before moving into or out of a stopping place and not to affect pedestrians or other traffic. The article also covers the front of a gate and 5 metres either side of it for agencies and organisations with a vehicle entrance. That clause is written for such premises, but treating any busy vehicle entrance the same way is the safer habit.

Do not rely on how other riders behave. Locals may stop in places that visitors cannot safely copy, and your rental contract holds you responsible for the bike whatever the person in front of you did.

## Dropping off or collecting a passenger without blocking anyone

A drop-off should take about as long as it takes to say goodbye. Before leaving, agree where you will stop: a spot beside the station where stopping is permitted, away from the coach bays and the marked pick-up points. Keep the engine running, keep your hands on the bars and ask the passenger to be ready with bags in hand and helmet off after the bike has stopped.

Collecting someone is harder because you have to wait. Coach arrivals slip, and a scooter circling the entrance adds to the congestion. Ask the passenger to message you when the coach has actually pulled in, and to walk to a named meeting point near, not at, the boarding area. If you must wait, find a place that is signed for parking or a guarded lot, and do not stand on the pavement or in the carriageway. The pavement is for pedestrians under Article 77 of the same law.

Bags need honesty. One small backpack is realistic on a scooter. A large wheeled case, two bags or a box is not, because the load alters the bike's balance and blocks your legs. In that case ride back to the shop, return the bike and take a car, or let the passenger take one.

## If you must park and go inside

Sometimes you need to leave the bike while you buy a ticket or find a counter. The operator's pages do not say whether the station has a dedicated motorbike parking area, so ask a member of staff on arrival rather than assuming. If a guarded lot is available, use it. Look for a posted price, take the ticket, check that the attendant records your plate and keep the stub until you collect the bike.

Parking prices around Hanoi have just changed. News reports of 15 October 2026 say that a city decision doubled the per-trip price ceiling for motorbikes at roadside spots on roads and pavements funded by the state budget, with the same new rate on every street. It does not cover parking built with other money, so what the attendant asks at a private lot may be a different figure. Either way, the price should be on a board.

Lock the steering and take your valuables, and photograph the bike where it is parked. Nguyen Ha does not provide an extra lock, so the steering lock and a guarded place matter. Do not leave the scooter parked at the station while you travel by coach for a day or more. A rental day is 24 hours and the bike stays on your contract, so unattended storage in a public place is something to ask the shop about first. Our guide to [parking a motorbike in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-motorbike-in-hanoi/) covers tickets, guarded lots and what to do if the bike is missing.

## Returning the scooter, then catching the coach

This is the situation that causes the most stress, because two time limits collide. The shop is open from 09:00 to 21:00 daily. A late return costs VND 30,000 per hour for hours one to five, and from six hours it is charged as an extra rental day. Early return does not refund unused rent. Petrol bikes come back with about 0.5 to 1 litre, and an empty tank at return costs VND 20,000.

Work backwards from the boarding time printed on your ticket:

1. Find the counter number and boarding position from the operator's timetable or your ticket.
2. Allow time to carry your bags from the shop to the station, including the last stretch on foot if you are not taking a taxi.
3. Allow time for the handover check, which can take a few minutes if you have photographs to compare.
4. Fill the tank at a station on the way so you do not arrive on empty. Our [fuel guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/fuel-in-hanoi-petrol-stations-rental-scooter/) covers this.
5. Add a margin for Hanoi traffic, which is never exactly what the map predicts.

If your coach leaves before the shop opens, do not assume that an early return is possible. Call 0334 699 969 or +84 334 699 969 the day before and agree how the return will work. Our guide to [returning a rental motorbike in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/returning-your-rental-motorbike-hanoi/) shows what the shop will check.

## Arriving by coach after dark or at a quiet hour

If you arrive by coach and want to rent, the shop's opening hours decide everything. A coach that reaches the station after 21:00 will find the counter closed, so plan accommodation for the night and collect the scooter the next morning rather than looking for a bike late in the evening.

Short rentals are normally collected at the shop. Delivery exists only for weekly or monthly agreements, within 10 km of Hoan Kiem Lake, at VND 50,000 per trip or VND 100,000 both ways, and by agreement beyond 10 km. Whether a station handover fits your agreement is the shop's decision, so ask when you book and do not promise it to anyone.

Bring your passport and driving licence, because the shop checks and photographs them for the contract but does not keep the originals. A cash deposit is also required. ## Leaving the station area by scooter

Before you ride away from the station, check the bike. Brakes, lights, tyres and mirrors take only a few minutes, and our [rental scooter inspection checklist](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-scooter-inspection-checklist/) shows the order. Then pull out carefully: after a long coach ride you may be tired, and the roads next to the station carry buses and coaches that need space.

The usual route to the centre follows Nguyen Van Cu to Chuong Duong Bridge, and our guide to [Long Bien and the Red River](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/long-bien-red-river-first-ride/) describes a gentler first ride on the east bank. If you would rather stay across the river, the [Gia Lam and east bank guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/gia-lam-east-bank-bat-trang-by-scooter/) suggests quiet roads.

One rule applies to the Old Quarter. Hanoi's low-emission pilot began on 1 July 2026, and in its first area, around Hoan Kiem Lake and the Old Quarter, motorbikes and cars are barred from 19:00 to midnight every Friday, Saturday and Sunday. Phase one runs to 31 December 2026. Check the latest notices before heading to that area at a weekend evening, and read our [Old Quarter riding guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-old-quarter/) for the narrow streets.

## FAQ: scooters at Gia Lam Bus Station

### Is Nguyen Ha's shop inside the bus station?

No. The shop is in Alley 5 Nguyen Van Cu Street, Bo De Ward, Long Bien District, which is in the same ward as the station and described as near it. It is not part of the station.

### Can I leave a rental scooter at the station while I take a coach?

Ask the shop first. The bike stays on your contract and the rental day is 24 hours, so storage in a public place for days is a risk you should agree with the shop rather than assume.

### Where should I stop to drop a friend?

At a spot where stopping is permitted, away from the coach bays, pick-up points, junctions and pedestrian crossings. Agree the place in advance.

### Does the station have motorbike parking?

The operator's pages do not say. Ask staff at the station, and use a guarded lot with a posted price if one is available.

### Can I collect a scooter after a late coach?

Not after 21:00, because the shop is open from 09:00 to 21:00. Plan to collect the next morning.
