If your rental motorbike is stolen, first make sure it has not simply been moved or towed, then call the shop at once, report the theft to the nearest police station and keep a written record of the report. Do these things in that order, within the first hour if you can. Stay safe, and do not try to chase or confront anyone. The shop needs to know quickly so it can help and so that the report to the police matches its own records. The police need the plate number, the place and the time. Your contract decides who bears the loss, and your insurance may or may not help, but none of that is settled in the first minutes. This guide sets out the steps in order, explains how to report a crime in Vietnam, which numbers to call, what to gather, what to expect afterwards and the mistakes to avoid. It is general information, not legal advice.

## Step 1: stay safe and check it has not just been moved

Before you assume theft, spend two minutes checking. Bikes are sometimes moved by parking attendants to make space, taken away by the authorities for illegal parking or simply parked somewhere other than where you remember. Look at the street around you. Ask the nearest shop, guard or attendant whether they saw the bike being moved. If you used a guarded car park, ask the guard first.

If you find the bike, take photographs of where it is and of any damage, in case something has changed. If you do not, treat it as stolen.

Do not follow or confront anyone you suspect. A motorbike is not worth a physical risk. If you feel unsafe or if anyone is hurt, call the emergency number straight away.

## Step 2: call the shop

Call Nguyen Ha on 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969), open 09:00 to 21:00 daily. If you are outside those hours, send a message with the same details and call as soon as the shop opens. Tell the shop:

- your name and the plate number of the bike;
- where and when you last saw it;
- whether the key is with you;
- what you have done so far;
- that you will report it to the police.

Do not wait until you have reported to the police to call. The shop may have advice about the nearest police station, may know the area and will want to start its own steps, such as a note on its records. It also needs to hear it from you, not from a third party.

## Step 3: report the theft to the police

Under Vietnam's Criminal Procedure Code, a report of a crime may be made orally or in writing. The Ministry of Public Security's guidance on reporting a crime says reports are received by police investigation bodies, including commune or ward police and police stations, and that a person can report in person, in writing or by a duty telephone number. It also says the person who reports has the right to be told the result of the handling, to ask for protection if threatened and to complain about how the report was handled.

In practice, for a stolen motorbike in Hanoi:

- **Go to the nearest ward police station** (Cong an phuong) or police post, or to the station that covers the place where the bike was taken. A map app can find the nearest one. Bring your passport or a copy, if you have it, and your phone with the bike's details.
- **Or call the emergency numbers.** The national emergency number is 112, set up by Decree 200/2025/ND-CP, in force since 23 August 2025. It is free from fixed and mobile phones, answers 24 hours a day and is linked with 113 (police), 114 (fire and rescue) and 115 (medical emergency), so a call about a police matter is passed to the right centre. If someone is in danger, use these numbers.
- **Use the shop's help.** Ask the staff whether someone can explain the situation to the police or recommend a station. If your Vietnamese is limited, a translation app can help, and a staff member can sometimes join the call.

Be prepared for a procedure that takes time. The police will ask what happened, may write down your statement and may ask you to sign it. They may ask for the plate number, the registration details and the contract. Ask, politely, for a written record or a reference number for your report, and for a copy if it is available. Do not sign a document you cannot read without having it explained to you. If you are asked to sign something in Vietnamese, ask for a translation or for the staff to explain it.

## Step 4: gather the information the police and the shop will need

Prepare a short note, on paper or on your phone, with:

- **The bike:** make, model, colour, plate number and any marks. Use the photographs you took at pickup. Our guide to [how to document existing scratches on a rental bike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-document-existing-scratches-on-a-rental-bike/) explains why the pickup record helps.
- **The place:** the address or landmark, whether the parking was guarded and the name of the car park or shop.
- **The time:** when you parked, when you last saw it and when you noticed it was gone.
- **The key:** whether you have it, and whether you left it anywhere.
- **Witnesses:** the guard, a shop owner or anyone who was nearby, with contact details if they will share them.
- **Cameras:** ask nearby shops and the car park whether they have CCTV covering the spot. Tell the police, who can request the footage. Do not take footage yourself without permission.
- **Your documents:** the rental contract, your passport details and a copy of the key handover.

Photograph the spot where the bike was parked, with surrounding landmarks.

## Step 5: look at your contract

Once the immediate steps are done, read the contract. Our guide to [understanding a rental contract's theft clause](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/understanding-a-rental-contracts-theft-clause/) explains how to read it. Vietnam's Civil Code (Law No. 91/2015/QH13), in force since 1 January 2017, says a lessee must preserve leased property and compensate if it is lost, and the contract fills in the details. As of October 2026, the questions that matter are:

- Is my liability the full value of the bike, a capped amount or the deposit?
- What security did the contract require, and did I meet it?
- What do I have to hand over: the key, a copy of the police report?
- What happens to the deposit?
- Does the rent stop at the time of the theft?

The deposit at Nguyen Ha is a cash deposit of VND 2,000,000–5,000,000 depending on the bike and the contract, and it is held against damage, loss and unpaid charges. Your contract determines how it is applied. Do not agree a figure on the phone or on the street. Ask for any amount to be written down and explained.

## Step 6: check insurance

Insurance may or may not help, so check it early, because some policies require a report within a short time.

- **The compulsory insurance on the bike** covers third-party injury and damage, not the theft of the bike. Our guide to [travel insurance and motorbike rentals](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/travel-insurance-motorbike-rental-vietnam/) explains the difference.
- **Your travel insurance** may exclude hired vehicles, but may cover your own belongings if they were taken as well. Keep the police report, because insurers usually ask for it. Call the insurer's assistance line, and ask what documents they need.
- **Rental-shop cover,** if any, is something to ask the shop about.

## Step 7: protect your own belongings and documents

If your passport, phone, wallet or other items were on the bike, deal with them quickly.

- **Cancel cards and block the phone** if they were taken.
- **Report a lost passport** to your embassy or consulate. Your embassy or consulate can advise on a replacement document.
- **Replace the key,** if it was stolen with the bike, only with the shop's agreement. Our guide to [lost rental bike keys: reporting and replacement steps](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/lost-rental-bike-keys-reporting-and-replacement-steps/) covers keys.

## What not to do

- **Do not chase or confront** a suspected thief.
- **Do not pay anyone** who offers to "find" the bike for a fee, or who claims to be able to arrange its return. Treat anyone who contacts you out of the blue about your bike with suspicion, and check with the shop and the police before you pay or share any details.
- **Do not post accusations** about a person, a car park or a shop online. If you name someone without evidence, you may create new problems.
- **Do not ride another rented bike without telling the shop** if it has been replaced for you.
- **Do not make up details.** Say what you know, and say when you are unsure. False reports can be treated seriously, and Decree 200/2025/ND-CP, which sets up the 112 service, says that giving deliberately false information about an incident is dealt with under the law.
- **Do not agree to terms you do not understand** in a rush.

## After the report

- **Keep a copy of the police report or the reference.** Photograph any paper you are given.
- **Keep all messages and calls with the shop.**
- **Ask the shop what happens next,** including the deposit and any further steps. Our explanation of [what the clauses in a Hanoi rental contract mean](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-motorbike-rental-contract-explained/) helps you read the wording.
- **Ask the police how you will be told of progress.** The Ministry's guidance says the person who reports has the right to be informed of the result.
- **Be ready for no quick answer.** Recovery of a stolen bike is not guaranteed.
- **Keep the report in case a later notice arrives,** for example a traffic fine linked to the bike's plate after the theft, so that you can show when it was taken.

If you need transport while it is sorted out, ask the shop whether another bike is available and on what terms. Prices are on the [prices page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/prices/).

## Preventing a theft in the first place

- **Lock the steering every time,** and take the key.
- **Park in a guarded place.** Our guide to [parking a motorbike in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-motorbike-in-hanoi/) explains guarded lots, and our guide to [electric scooter theft prevention in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/electric-scooter-theft-prevention-in-hanoi/) lists habits that apply to any bike.
- **Do not leave valuables on the bike.**
- **Photograph the bike where you park it.**
- **Keep the shop's number in your phone.**

## A short checklist

1. **Check it was not moved or towed.**
2. **Stay safe, do not chase.**
3. **Call the shop on 0334 699 969.**
4. **Report to the nearest ward police station, or call 112 or 113 if someone is in danger.**
5. **Get a written record or reference.**
6. **Gather the bike, place, time, key and witness details.**
7. **Read your contract and check your insurance.**
8. **Deal with your own documents and cards.**
9. **Keep every record.**

## FAQ: a stolen rental motorbike

### Who do I call first?

The shop, then the police. If someone is in danger, call 112 or 113 first.

### Can I report a theft orally?

Yes. Under Vietnam's Criminal Procedure Code a report may be made orally or in writing, and the Ministry of Public Security's guidance says it can be made in person at ward police.

### What is the emergency number in Vietnam?

112 is the national number, free and answered 24 hours a day, linked to 113 police, 114 fire and rescue and 115 medical emergency.

### Will I have to pay for the bike?

It depends on the contract. The Civil Code says a lessee who loses the leased property must compensate, and the contract sets the amount and conditions.

### Does travel insurance cover it?

Often not. Check the policy and ask the insurer.

### Will the bike be found?

There is no guarantee. Keep your record, and ask the police how progress will be reported.
