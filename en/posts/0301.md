The Hanoi Opera House is at 1 Trang Tien Street, about 500 to 600 metres from Hoan Kiem Lake, and it is easy to reach on a rented scooter as long as you plan for two things: the busy junction in front of it and the short supply of parking nearby. One travel guide says parking is available on the surrounding streets but that spaces may be limited at peak hours, so the realistic plan is to ride to the area, park in a guarded space within a few minutes' walk and approach the building on foot. The building was built between 1901 and 1911 and was modelled on the Palais Garnier in Paris, and it stands at the meeting of several main streets, which is exactly why it is harder to stop at than to find. This guide covers the approach, the parking choices, how a performance evening changes the plan and how to get home afterwards.

## What and where it is

The Opera House, also called the Grand Opera House or Nha hat Lon, was built by the French colonial administration, with construction starting in June 1901 and the building opening in December 1911, according to its Wikipedia entry. It was modelled on the Palais Garnier but at a smaller scale, using materials thought suitable for the local climate. The Vietnam National Administration of Tourism describes it as one of the most important architectural landmarks of the capital.

Its address is 1 Trang Tien Street. It sits at the intersection of Trang Tien, Ly Thai To and Le Thanh Tong streets, according to a travel guide, and it faces August Revolution Square, which other guides place at the heart of the French quarter. Other guides name its neighbours: the Hilton Hanoi Opera hotel, the National Museum of History and the Trang Tien Plaza shopping centre. That is a lot of traffic converging on one spot, and a scooter rider is part of it.

## The approach: a junction, not a street

Because the Opera House stands at a junction where several main roads meet, the approach matters more than the distance. One guide describes reaching it by following Le Thai To Street and passing Hang Trong and Hang Khay to the end of Trang Tien Street, then going round the roundabout at 19/8 Square. We have not verified that as a recommended route for a scooter, but it shows the shape of the area: you come in along one of several avenues and meet the others at the square.

What to do:

1. **Study the junction in map view first.** Look at which entry you will use and where you will leave.
2. **Do not stop in the middle of the junction for a photograph.** The facade is pale and photogenic, and the square in front is the place to stand, after you park.
3. **Keep pace with the flow.** Hanoi junctions work by drivers merging steadily. Our guide to [crossing Hanoi intersections](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/crossing-hanoi-intersections/) explains how.
4. **Go round if you miss the turn.** A long loop is better than a sudden manoeuvre.

For the surrounding streets, the picture of lots and tickets in [parking a motorbike in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-motorbike-in-hanoi/) applies here as much as anywhere.

## Where to leave the scooter

We did not find a published parking area belonging to the Opera House itself, and the building's official website is not a source we could read for this guide, so we do not claim one exists. What the guides we read say:

- One guide says parking is available on surrounding streets and that spaces may be limited at peak hours.
- Another guide says motorbike parking is available on Dinh Tien Hoang Street, near the lake, but that spaces can be limited at peak times. That is a secondary source, so confirm on the spot.
- We do not quote any fee, because the guides' figures are approximate and may be out of date.

The principle is simple: park in a guarded place where an attendant gives you a ticket, not in a no-parking zone, a pavement or a doorway. Lock the steering, since the shop does not provide an extra lock, keep the ticket on you and photograph the place and street.

The same area is covered from another angle in our guide to [where to park a scooter near Hoan Kiem Lake](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/where-to-park-a-scooter-near-hoan-kiem-lake/). Many of the lots you would use for the lake are also a short walk from the Opera House, and parking once and walking between the two is often easier than moving the bike.

## Day visit or an evening performance

The Opera House is a working theatre, and what you can do depends on the day. Guides disagree about whether and when visitors can go inside outside performances, with one naming daily morning tours and another naming only some days, so do not count on seeing the interior without checking with the venue. What you can always do is see the facade and square from outside.

Evenings are different. The Vietnam National Administration of Tourism reported in March 2024 that the theatre launched an outdoor "Music Garden" with performances in daytime and evening sessions, and that the theatre planned tours that let visitors hear music and explore the building. That was two years before this guide, so check what is on now. A guide also says evening performances usually begin around 7:30 pm. For any evening plan:

- check the programme and the start time for your date;
- decide before you leave how you will get home;
- read [riding at night in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-at-night-in-hanoi/) for lights, visibility and the streets to avoid;
- tell the shop your plan. It is open 09:00 to 21:00 daily, so a late finish needs an agreed return time.

## After the show: the ride back

At the end of a performance, many people leave at the same moment, and cars, taxis, ride-hailing vehicles and scooters all pull out into the same junction. Some practical advice:

1. **Stay calm and wait a few minutes** if the exit is crowded, instead of squeezing between vehicles.
2. **Walk to the bike with your helmet already in hand,** and check the ticket.
3. **Put the route in the map before you start,** so that you are not looking at the phone at the junction.
4. **Allow for tired riding.** If you are tired or have had a drink, do not ride. Taking a taxi and collecting the bike next day is better than taking a risk. Our guide to [drink-driving rules for motorbike riders in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/drink-driving-rules-motorbike-vietnam/) explains why this matters.

## Combining the Opera House with the area around it

The Opera House is in the middle of a cluster of sights. According to the guides we read, the lake is about 500 metres away, and the National Museum of History and the Trang Tien Plaza are next to the square. The practical use of this is to park once and make a short walking loop, instead of riding between stops:

| Stop | What the guides say | Plan |
|---|---|---|
| Opera House facade | At 1 Trang Tien, in the square | Walk from the lot |
| August Revolution Square | Surrounds the building | Walk |
| Hoan Kiem Lake | About 500 to 600 m | Walk |
| National Museum of History | Beside the square | Check its own hours |

If you want more of the old centre afterwards, read the [Hoan Kiem area page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hoan-kiem/) and [riding in the Old Quarter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-old-quarter/).

## A quick checklist

1. Check whether there is a performance or event for your date, and at what time.
2. Inspect the scooter, including lights, and check the fuel. See [fuel in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/fuel-in-hanoi-petrol-stations-rental-scooter/).
3. Set the map pin to 1 Trang Tien Street and preview the junction.
4. Ride at a calm hour, and merge steadily.
5. Park in a guarded space near the square, lock the steering and keep the ticket.
6. Walk to the facade, and note the street where the bike is.
7. Plan the ride home before the show ends, and do not ride after drinking.

## FAQ: the Hanoi Opera House by scooter

### What is the address?

The Opera House is at 1 Trang Tien Street, in central Hanoi, near August Revolution Square and about 500 to 600 metres from Hoan Kiem Lake.

### Is there parking at the Opera House?

We could not find a published parking area of its own. Guides say parking is available on surrounding streets, with limited spaces at peak hours. Park in a guarded space and keep the ticket.

### Can I go inside?

Guides disagree about visiting hours, and access depends on performances and events. Check with the venue before you plan around it.

### Is it a good place to ride to in the evening?

It can be, but junctions are busy after shows. Plan the way home, and do not ride after drinking.

### Can I ride a manual bike there?

Yes, if you are confident in central traffic. An automatic scooter is often easier in tight spots. Ask the shop what is available.

### Is it close to Hoan Kiem Lake?

Guides put the lake about 500 to 600 metres away, so parking once and walking between the two is an option.
