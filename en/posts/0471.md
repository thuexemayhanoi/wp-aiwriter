To verify the current fine for a traffic offence in Vietnam, identify the exact offence and the class of your vehicle, find the matching article and clause in Decree 168/2024/ND-CP and any decree that has amended it, check the date the amendment took effect and then compare the figure with the written penalty decision you were given. Do not rely on a figure quoted in a blog, a video or a conversation, including this one. Fines are set by government decree, and they differ by vehicle type: the rules for motorcycles and mopeds are in Article 7 of Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, those for cars in Article 6 and those for special-purpose vehicles in Article 8. The decree has been amended by Decree 238/2026/ND-CP, so check the current consolidated text.

This guide explains how to find the right provision, how to read it, how to check amendments and dates, how to look up camera fines and notices through official channels, how to pay, how to spot scam messages and what to do if you think a figure is wrong. It is general information as of October 2026, not legal advice.

## Why a quoted figure may be wrong

Fines change, and the same act can have different amounts in different places.

- **Different vehicle classes.** The same offence can carry a different fine for a car and for a motorcycle or moped, because the decree sets them in different articles.

- **Different clauses.** A general offence, such as not obeying a sign, has one range, and a specific offence, such as turning where a sign prohibits it, has a higher one.

- **Aggravating results.** The same act that causes an accident is penalised far more heavily.

- **Amendments.** A decree can be amended. Decree 238/2026/ND-CP, which amends Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, was reported in Vietnamese legal publications from 30 June 2026, and those reports described the amendments as not raising the fines or the points. You still have to check the text.

- **Older articles.** Many web pages describe the earlier rules, such as Decree 100/2019/ND-CP, whose amounts were lower. Articles written before 1 January 2025 may be out of date.

- **Mistakes and simplification.** Secondary sources sometimes confuse the clause or the vehicle class.

## Step 1: identify the offence and the vehicle

Write down exactly what the fine is for, and what you were riding.

- **What was the act?** For example, running a red light, riding without a helmet, using a phone while riding, entering a street banned to your vehicle type, overtaking where it is banned, no signal.

- **What was the vehicle?** A motorcycle, a moped (up to 50 cm³ or an electric motor up to 4 kW, with a design speed of 50 km/h at most) or something else. Article 34 of Law 36/2024/QH15 defines the classes, and the class affects both the fine and the licence required.

- **Was there a result?** For example, an accident, an injury, or damage.

- **When and where did it happen?** The date matters for which text applies.

## Step 2: find the provision in the decree

Decree 168/2024/ND-CP on penalties for administrative violations of road traffic order and safety took effect on 1 January 2025. The full text is on the Government's policy portal. Its structure is by vehicle type:

| Article | Applies to |
|---|---|
| Article 6 | Cars and similar vehicles |
| Article 7 | Motorcycles, mopeds and similar vehicles |
| Article 8 | Special-purpose vehicles |
| Article 9 | Bicycles, motorised bicycles and rudimentary vehicles |
| Article 32 and others | Vehicle owners and other duties |
| Article 47 | Procedure, including notices for violations found by devices |

Inside Article 7, the penalties are grouped by clause, with the amount range at the top of each clause and a list of acts under points (a, b, c and so on). To find your offence, search the article for the key words, read the clause heading to see the range and read the point carefully. A reference such as "Article 7, clause 3, point a" tells you the clause, then the point.

Penalty decisions and records normally state the article, clause and point. Compare that reference with the text.

## Step 3: check amendments and dates

- **Check whether the decree has been amended.** Decree 238/2026/ND-CP amends Decree 168/2024/ND-CP. Read its text, or a consolidated version, and check the effective date.

- **Check the date of the offence.** The rule in force on the day you committed the offence usually decides the amount, subject to the transitional provisions in the amending decree.

- **Check licence points.** Law 36/2024/QH15 gives each driver's licence 12 points under Article 58, and the decree states the points deducted for particular offences. A moped rider with no licence has no points to lose, but the fine still applies.

- **Check later guidance.** Ministries sometimes issue circulars on procedure, which can affect how the fine is handled, even where the amount is unchanged.

Do not assume that a figure is current because it appears on an official-looking page. Look at the date of the page, and check it against the decree text.

## Step 4: use official sources

For the text of the law and the decrees, use official or officially maintained sources.

- **The Government's policy portal,** which publishes full texts of laws and decrees, including Law 36/2024/QH15 and Decree 168/2024/ND-CP.

- **The National legal database and the Official Gazette,** where laws and decrees are published, and where amendments appear.

- **The websites of the Ministry of Public Security and the Traffic Police Department,** for procedures and notices.

English translations are useful for understanding, but they are not the legal text. If an English translation and the Vietnamese text differ, the Vietnamese text governs.

## Step 5: checking camera fines and notices

Many offences by visitors are recorded by cameras and found later.

- **How notices work.** Under Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, for violations found through technical devices, the authority sends a notice asking the vehicle owner and related persons to come and settle the case, and the owner must cooperate to identify the driver. Under Circular 73/2024/TT-BCA, as amended by Circular 13/2025/TT-BCA, notices may be sent in writing or electronically, including through the VNeTraffic app. If the owner and related persons do not come within 20 days, a warning may be sent to the inspection and registration agencies, and for motorcycles and mopeds, a notice may also be sent to the commune-level police of the owner's place of residence.

- **Lookups.** Vietnamese media and legal publications describe two main official channels: the VNeTraffic app run by the Traffic Police Department, using a VNeID account, and the Traffic Police Department's web portal. The way the web portal verifies a lookup has changed over time, so follow the instructions shown on the portal itself and do not rely on older guides that describe a different procedure.

- **Local police portals.** Some provinces and cities publish their own lists. Check that the site is an official police or government domain.

- **For a rental.** The notice may go to the shop first, as the registered owner. Ask the shop how it handles notices, and give it your contact details. ## Step 6: paying only what is owed, in the right way

- **Check the decision.** Look at the plate number, the name, the date, the place, the offence, the article and the amount.

- **Pay as the decision states.** Fines are normally paid through the State Treasury, by transfer to the account named in the decision or online through the National Public Service Portal, as official Vietnamese media explain. Keep the receipt.

- **Do not pay in cash at the roadside** unless the paperwork is complete and the procedure is clearly official.

- **Do not pay anyone who offers to make a fine go away for a fee.**

## Scam warnings

Vietnamese media have reported fake fine notices that lead people to fraudulent links, and advise people to check a fine through the official portal or the VNeTraffic app instead of clicking links in messages or emails from strangers.

1. **Do not click a link** in a message about a fine, and do not scan a QR code from it.

2. **Do not give card or banking details** to a caller.

3. **Check independently,** through the VNeTraffic app, the official portal or the police office named in a genuine notice.

4. **Ask the shop** if the message mentions a rental bike.

5. **Report suspicious messages** to the police.

## If you think the figure is wrong

1. **Compare it with the decree text** for your vehicle class, clause and date.

2. **Check that the record and the decision describe the same act,** the same plate and the same place.

3. **Ask the issuing authority** for the basis of the figure, and the image or data if it was a camera case.

4. **Write your objection** on the record if you disagree, and keep a copy.

5. **Use the complaint channels** the law provides. Article 72 of Law 36/2024/QH15 gives operators the right to make explanations and to denounce or initiate lawsuits against administrative decisions or acts affecting their lawful interests.

## FAQ: verifying a traffic fine

### Where is the official text of the fines?

Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, published on the Government's policy portal, as amended, including by Decree 238/2026/ND-CP. Check the consolidated version and the dates.

### Why does the same offence have different fines for cars and motorbikes?

The decree sets fines by vehicle class: Article 6 for cars, Article 7 for motorcycles and mopeds, and other articles for other vehicles.

### How do I check whether I have a camera fine?

Use the official VNeTraffic app with a VNeID account or the Traffic Police Department's web portal, and follow the instructions shown there. Do not use links from text messages.

### How do I pay?

As the written decision states, normally through the State Treasury, by transfer to the account in the decision or online through the National Public Service Portal. Keep the receipt.

### Should I act on a message that pressures me to pay a fine quickly?

Treat it as suspicious. Do not click its link. Check through the official portal or the VNeTraffic app, and pay only the amount in a genuine written decision.

### What if the fine went to the rental shop?

Ask the shop how it handled the notice, and keep copies of any paper.
