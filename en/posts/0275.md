Before you return a rented electric scooter, check six things in this order: the battery level against the photo you took at pickup, the charger and cable you were given, the display for warnings, the body and tyres against your pickup photos, whether the scooter is parked somewhere cool and dry, and the time. Do not charge a hot battery just to match the pickup figure, and do not wash the scooter yourself. Nguyen Ha confirms the battery level and charging arrangement for every electric model at handover, so the return is a comparison of two records, and your own photographs are the simplest way to keep your side of that record. This guide walks through the last ride, the counter and the questions worth asking, using manufacturer advice from Honda and VinFast where it applies. It is general information, not the shop's contract.

## The short version

- **Photograph the battery indicator** before you set off for the shop and again at the counter, and compare both with the pickup photo.
- **Do not plug in a battery that is still warm.** VinFast advises waiting until it has cooled to the surrounding temperature.
- **Return everything you were given:** charger, cable, key or keys, any removable battery cover or bag, and the helmet.
- **Read the display.** A warning light that was not on at pickup is worth mentioning before the shop finds it.
- **Park in shade and keep the scooter dry** on your last day.
- **Arrive well inside opening hours.** The shop is open 09:00 to 21:00 daily.

## Plan the last ride, not just the return

Most disputes about a returned scooter start with a rushed final hour. Plan the last ride the evening before.

The rental day is 24 hours, so work out the exact time you collected the scooter and treat that as your return time. Nguyen Ha's late-return terms are VND 30,000 per hour for one to five hours late and one extra rental day from six hours, and returning early does not refund unused rent. Those figures come from the shop's general terms for all bikes, so ask whether anything is different for an electric model. The [prices page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/prices/) lists the current packages, and the guide to [returning a rental motorbike in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/returning-your-rental-motorbike-hanoi/) covers the petrol-bike version of the same routine.

Then look at the battery before the last ride, not after it. If the indicator is already low, choose a route that ends at the shop and does not detour for a final photograph or a last coffee. A battery that falls to a very low level on the way back turns a simple return into a phone call. Our guide to [charging an electric scooter in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/charging-an-electric-scooter-in-hanoi/) explains the charging options if you need one more evening charge, and the shop is open until 21:00 if you want to hand the scooter back after dinner.

## Battery level: compare with the pickup photo

At handover, the shop confirms the battery level and the charging arrangement. You should have a photo of the indicator from that moment. On return day, take a second photo of the indicator at the counter, with the key turned so that the display is lit, and keep both.

Three things are worth knowing about the figure.

**It is a percentage or a bar count, not a promise.** Electric scooters estimate remaining charge from voltage and recent use, so the number can move when the scooter has been standing or has just been ridden. VinFast's own charging guide links to an article about false battery readings on electric scooters, which is a good reason to photograph the figure at the same moment you ask the shop to read it.

**Whether you must return it at the pickup level is a contract question.** Nguyen Ha's published practice for petrol bikes is that fuel at return should be roughly what you received, within a small range, and that an empty tank is charged. The shop says nothing equivalent in its public facts for electric bikes except that battery level and charging are confirmed at handover. Ask directly: "What battery level do you expect when I return it, and what happens if it is lower?" Write the answer in your notes.

**Do not charge a hot battery to hit a number.** VinFast's guidance for its electric scooters says not to charge while the battery is hot, and to wait until a battery that has just been ridden has cooled to the surrounding temperature. Its charging guide suggests a wait of around 30 minutes to an hour after use. Hanoi's fire-prevention advice, summarised in the Sources, says to let the battery cool before charging and not to charge overnight. If the shop has asked you to top up before return, do it earlier in the day and stay with the scooter while it charges.

## Charger, cable and everything else you were given

A scooter that is returned without its charger is the most common avoidable problem. Before you leave your accommodation, lay out what you received at pickup and check it off:

1. **The charger unit and its cable,** including any adapter the shop supplied.
2. **All keys, key fobs or cards,** and any spare.
3. **The helmet.** One basic helmet per rider is included in the shop's terms.
4. **Any removable item** such as a battery cover, a phone holder or a bag hook that the shop attached.
5. **Paperwork,** including your copy of the contract.

If you charged at a hotel, collect the charger from the socket and check that you have not left the cable behind a lobby desk or under a bed. Wind the cable loosely, because a tight bend at the plug is the first place a cable fails. If the charger or its plug looks damaged, say so before handing it over.

The contract also matters here. Our explanation of [what the clauses in a Hanoi rental contract mean](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-motorbike-rental-contract-explained/) shows where charger and accessory responsibilities usually sit, and the shop can confirm how your contract handles them.

## Display warnings and how the scooter behaves

Switch the scooter on and watch the display for a few seconds, then do a short check:

- **Warning symbols.** Compare with what the display showed at pickup. A new warning is better reported than discovered.
- **Power delivery.** Does it pull away normally, or does it hesitate or cut out?
- **Brakes.** Both levers should feel firm, and the brake light should come on.
- **Lights, indicators and horn.** The same quick test you did at pickup.
- **Unusual noise or smell.** A burning or sweet chemical smell around the battery or charger is a reason to stop, switch off and call the shop, not to ride on.

Manufacturers publish their own warning lists, and VinFast offers owner's manuals for its electric scooters through its documentation page, which is linked in the Sources. A display symbol that you do not recognise is a question for the shop rather than something to look up on the roadside.

## The walk-round: body, tyres and the usual damage spots

Take the walk-round slowly, in good light, and compare with your pickup photos. Our [10-minute inspection checklist](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-scooter-inspection-checklist/) gives the full routine; for an electric scooter, give extra attention to the following.

- **Floor and side panels.** Electric scooters often hide the battery under the floor or seat, so scuffs low on the body deserve a photograph.
- **The charging port and its cover,** if there is one. It should be clean, closed and undamaged.
- **Tyres.** Look for cuts, bulges and low pressure. Honda's maintenance guidance says to check pressure when the tyres are cool, since warm tyres can read higher than they really are, so a reading taken straight after a ride can mislead.
- **Mirrors, levers and footrests,** which are the parts most often scraped in a low-speed drop.
- **Seat and storage compartment.** Check that the compartment closes properly, because water getting into a battery area is a problem for any electric scooter.

Photograph any new mark yourself, from two angles, before the shop points it out. If you did drop the scooter, tell the shop at the start of the return, not at the end. The earlier a problem is mentioned, the more likely it is to be treated as an accident and not as concealment.

## Do not wash it yourself

It is tempting to rinse a muddy scooter before handing it back. Do not. Honda's guidance on washing an electric scooter says a high-pressure cleaner can cause electrical failures, the motor must have cooled before washing, and the battery must not be splashed with water, even wiped with a wet cloth. The same page warns against touching the battery terminals. A forecourt jet wash is the worst place to do any of this.

If the scooter is dirty, a dry cloth on the body is enough, and the shop can decide how to clean it. Mention any mud or sand so that the shop does not mistake it for damage.

## Park cool and dry on your last day

VinFast's published warranty exclusions list battery swelling, cracking, smoke or fire caused by parking in a high-temperature environment. That wording is about owner warranties, not rental contracts, but it shows why a scooter left in full sun for hours before return is a poor idea. On the last day, park in shade or an indoor guarded space, keep the scooter out of puddles and standing water, and do not leave a charger plugged in while you are away. Our article on [riding in Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) explains the weather side if your return day falls in the wet season.

## At the counter: a five-step routine

1. **Arrive and park** where the staff direct you, and keep the helmet with you.
2. **Show the battery indicator** and take your photo while the staff read it.
3. **Hand over the charger, cable and keys** together, and say out loud what you are giving back.
4. **Walk round the scooter with a staff member,** pointing out anything new before they do.
5. **Ask what happens next** with the deposit and get the answer in a form you can keep, such as a message or a signed note. Our guide to [how a rental deposit works in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbike-rental-deposit-hanoi/) explains the usual timing and what the shop may deduct.

## FAQ: returning an electric scooter

### Do I need to charge the scooter before I return it?

Ask the shop. Its public terms say the battery level and charging arrangement are confirmed at handover, but they do not say what level is expected at return. If a top-up is requested, wait until the battery has cooled and stay with the scooter while it charges.

### Should I wash the scooter first?

No. Honda advises against high-pressure cleaning on electric scooters and says the battery must not be splashed. Return it as it is and mention any dirt.

### What if the display shows a warning I did not see at pickup?

Tell the shop at the start of the return, show them the display and photograph it. Do not ride on if the scooter smells burnt, feels very hot or loses power.

### Can I return it early or late?

Early return does not refund unused rent, and late return is charged by the hour for the first five hours and then as an extra rental day. Check how this applies to your electric model.

### What should I photograph on return day?

The battery indicator, all four sides of the scooter, the tyres, the charging port and the charger with its cable, plus any new mark. Keep the pickup photos beside them for comparison.
