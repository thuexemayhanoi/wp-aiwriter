To confirm the exact model you will receive, ask the shop in writing for five details before you pay: the model name, the engine size, the gearbox type, the number plate and a recent photo of the actual bike. Then, at handover, match what you are given against that message and against the vehicle's registration paper. Rental shops in Hanoi usually list models they may have, not a guarantee of one specific bike, and Nguyen Ha Motorbike Rental says the same: the models on its price page are possibilities, the bike actually available is confirmed when you book, and the final price is confirmed on the actual bike before the contract is signed. A confirmed model protects you from surprises in size, comfort, licence class and price.

## Why "Honda Vision or similar" is not a confirmation

A listing that says "Honda Vision or similar" tells you the class of bike, not which one you will ride. For a day in the Old Quarter that may be enough. It is not enough when the difference could change your licence requirements, your comfort or the price. Two bikes with the same name can differ in year, colour, condition and version, and two bikes with different names can be near-identical to ride. The goal is to turn a category into one specific machine, and to hold the shop to it.

The models Nguyen Ha lists include, among automatics, the Honda Vision, Air Blade, Click and Lead and the Yamaha Mio and Mio Ultimo; among manual and semi-automatic bikes, the Honda Wave, Yamaha Sirius and Honda Blade; and also 50cc bikes and electric options. Treat that list as a menu of what may be available, then ask which one is yours.

## The five details to ask for

### 1. The model name and version

Ask for the full name, for example "Honda Air Blade", then ask which version. Some models are sold in more than one engine size. Honda Vietnam's Air Blade page, for instance, covers a 160 and a 125 version under one name, and the Vision is a separate 110cc scooter. "Air Blade" alone is not a complete answer.

### 2. The engine size

Ask for the engine capacity in cubic centimetres. Honda Vietnam's specification for the Vision gives a cylinder capacity of 109.5 cm3, and for the Air Blade 160 it gives 156.9 cm3. Those are figures for new models from the manufacturer's site and a rental bike may be a different year, so use them to understand the class, not as a promise about your bike. Engine size matters because Vietnam's Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (Law 36/2024/QH15) sets licence classes by engine size: class A1 covers two-wheeled motorcycles with a capacity of up to 125 cm3, and larger motorcycles fall under class A. The law also has transitional provisions for earlier licences, so whether a particular bike is open to you depends on your licence, which is why this question should be settled before you book, not at the counter.

### 3. The gearbox type

Say whether the bike is an automatic scooter, a semi-automatic with a foot shift and no clutch lever, or a fully manual bike. Our guide to [manual, semi-automatic or automatic bikes](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/manual-semi-automatic-or-automatic-motorbike-hanoi/) explains the differences. If you have never used a foot shift, say so.

### 4. The number plate

The plate is the quickest way to identify a particular machine. Ask for it before you travel, or at least before you pay in full. The law expects a motor vehicle on the road to be registered and to carry a plate, and the registration certificate is among the papers a driver must carry. A plate lets you check that the bike you are given is the bike the shop described.

### 5. A recent photo

Ask for a photo taken in the last few days, showing the whole bike, the plate and the dashboard. It need not be professional. The point is that you now have a reference to compare with the machine at handover, and a record of any existing marks.

## A message that asks for all five

> Hello Nguyen Ha. For my rental from [date], please confirm the exact bike I will receive: model and version, engine size, automatic or manual, number plate, and a recent photo. If it is not available, please tell me what you would offer instead and whether the price changes.

Keep the reply. If the shop cannot name a specific bike yet, ask when it will be able to, and plan to confirm on the day.

## At the counter: matching the bike to the message

When you arrive, spend two minutes on identity before you spend ten on condition.

1. **Compare the plate** with your message and with the registration paper if the shop shows it.
2. **Read the badge and the dashboard.** The model name is usually on the body panels and the display.
3. **Check the type.** Automatic, semi-automatic or manual should match what you asked for.
4. **Ask for the engine size** if it was not in the message, and write the answer next to the plate in your phone notes.
5. **Photograph the plate and the whole bike** with the agreed model in view.

Then go on to the condition checks in our [10-minute rental scooter inspection](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-scooter-inspection-checklist/).

## Checking the bike against its papers

The registration certificate is the document that ties a plate to a specific machine. A driver in Vietnam is required to carry it, along with a licence suited to the vehicle and the vehicle owner's civil liability insurance certificate. Asking to see it at handover is reasonable. It records the vehicle's identifying details, typically including the plate and the chassis and engine numbers, so it is the right paper to compare against the bike.

Honda Vietnam's owner manual for the Wave RSX FI explains why those two numbers matter. It says the frame number and the engine number identify your vehicle and are needed when registering it, and that they are needed when ordering spare parts. You do not need to read the numbers on a rental bike in detail, but if the shop is willing, compare the plate and the model on the certificate with the bike in front of you. If the paper shows a different engine size from the bike you were promised, ask why before you sign.

If a shop does not carry the paper to the handover, ask for a photograph of it in advance. Our guide to [what documents to carry when riding in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/documents-to-carry-riding-in-vietnam/) explains which papers you are expected to hold as the rider.

## If the shop offers a different bike

Substitutions happen. A bike may be in the workshop, or another renter may have kept it longer. That is not necessarily a problem, but it should be your decision. Ask:

- What is different about the replacement: engine size, gearbox, age, condition?
- Does the price change? The final price is confirmed on the actual bike, so ask for that figure before you agree.
- Does the replacement fit your licence and your plans, for example a ride out of the city?
- Can you wait for the original bike, and for how long?
- If you decline, are there any charges or lost deposit?

Do not sign the contract until the model, the plate and the price are written together. Our explanation of [what the clauses in a Hanoi rental contract mean](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-motorbike-rental-contract-explained/) shows where the bike's details usually appear.

## Electric and 50cc bikes: extra checks

For an electric scooter, the battery level and charging arrangements are confirmed at handover, so ask for the battery level in writing and the range the shop expects. For a 50cc bike, ask what the engine size is and whether the bike is a classic Cub-style semi-automatic or a twist-and-go scooter, because they ride differently. Our page on [50cc bikes](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/50cc-bikes/) and our overview of [electric scooters](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/electric-scooters/) explain what the shop offers.

## Common mistakes

- **Booking by photo of a model, not of a bike.** A stock photo of a Vision shows the shape, not the one you will ride.
- **Skipping the version.** "Air Blade" can mean more than one engine size.
- **Accepting "something similar" without asking what differs.** Ask for the differences in size, age and price.
- **Confirming only by phone call.** A written message is easier to refer back to.
- **Forgetting the licence link.** The right engine size for you depends on the licence you hold.

## FAQ: confirming your model

### Will the shop guarantee a specific model?

Nguyen Ha says the models on its price page are possibilities and the bike actually available is confirmed when you book. Ask for the confirmation in writing.

### How do I find a bike's engine size?

Ask the shop, and compare with the registration paper. Manufacturer websites give figures for new models, which is a guide to the class only.

### Does the model affect the price?

It can. The final price is confirmed on the actual bike before the contract is signed.

### Can I refuse the bike if it is not the model I asked for?

Ask about this before you book. Settle what happens to the deposit and any fee if you decline.

### Should I photograph the bike?

Yes. Photograph the plate, the whole bike and any marks, before you ride away.
