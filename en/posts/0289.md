Cau Giay is Hanoi's western office and campus belt, and it is a place to ride with a plan rather than on impulse: avoid the weekday commuter peaks, stay off the elevated decks that are closed to motorbikes, expect camera enforcement and leave the scooter only in a proper parking space. The roads are wide and the destinations, such as the Vietnam Museum of Ethnology, are easy to find, but the same few avenues carry thousands of commuters at once and kerb space is scarce. This guide covers what Cau Giay means after the 2025 reorganisation, when and where traffic builds, which fast roads you must not enter, how camera fines work for a rented bike, how to read parking rules under Law 36/2024/QH15 and how to fit a visit to the museum into a ride. It reflects sources read as of October 2026 and is general information, not live traffic advice.

## Cau Giay after the 2025 ward changes

Cau Giay used to be a district with eight wards. Resolution 1656/NQ-UBTVQH15 ended the district tier from 1 July 2025 and created 126 wards and communes across Hanoi. The Hanoi culture department lists three wards in this area: Cau Giay, Nghia Do and Yen Hoa. Older guides still say "Cau Giay District", and the Vietnam Museum of Ethnology is still often described as being in Nghia Do Ward, Cau Giay District. Street names are what you use on the road, so search for those.

Cau Giay is on the west side of the city, on the opposite side from Nguyen Ha Motorbike Rental on the east bank of the Red River in Long Bien. Expect to cross the river and the city to get there, and read the [ring roads guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-ring-roads-highways-motorbikes-allowed/) before you set the navigation.

## Where and when the traffic builds

Cau Giay's character is office towers, apartment blocks, universities and ministries along a handful of arteries. VietNamNet's 2020 report on the Department of Transport's hotspot list described Xuan Thuy Street as a long-standing jam, especially from Lane 130 to the overpass crossroads, and described frequent congestion where Pham Hung Street meets the My Dinh bus station, with many buses adding to the flow. It also noted that Ring Road 3 between Thanh Tri bridge and the Mai Dich area was still clogged because of the apartment blocks along the route. That report is several years old, so use it as a pattern rather than a current map. A January 2026 Lao Dong report says Hanoi's traffic police have identified 51 points and routes that regularly jam,.

Practical consequences:

- **The peaks are real.** Roughly 07:00 to 09:00 and 16:30 to 19:00 on weekdays are the periods to avoid if you can.
- **Mid-morning and mid-afternoon are calmer,** and weekends are lighter in the office zones, though the museum and malls fill up.
- **Junctions near bus stations and metro stations are the pinch points.** Buses pull in and out, pedestrians cross in bunches and motorbike taxis stop at the kerb. Our [intersection guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/crossing-hanoi-intersections/) explains how to merge calmly.

If the route is long, leave earlier and accept that a slow ride is the normal ride in this district.

## Fast roads that are closed to bikes

Cau Giay is next to the Ring Road 3 corridor and its elevated deck. Our ring road guide, which cites Vietnamese press reports, explains that the elevated deck is car-only while the ground road beneath it is open to motorbikes. The principle for expressways is written into Law 36/2024/QH15, Article 25: motorbikes and mopeds are not allowed on expressways, apart from road-management vehicles and personnel. Law 36 has been in force since 1 January 2025.

Hanoi's elevated deck is not formally an expressway, but a sign at the ramp is the deciding factor. Look for the round red-bordered motorbike prohibition sign and the ground-level lane markings. If a navigation app tells you to turn up a ramp, ignore it and read [how to check whether an app is suggesting an expressway](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-check-if-a-navigation-app-suggests-an-expressway/). Missing a turn at ground level costs minutes. Following a car up a ramp can cost a fine and a delay.

## Cameras and what they mean for a rental

Hanoi has been rolling out AI cameras that detect violations and notify the vehicle's registered owner. A November 2025 Lao Dong report on the traffic police's pilot AI cameras at the Pham Van Bach and Hoang Quan Chi junction, which is in the Cau Giay area, said the system detected 93 cases of riders without helmets in one 24-hour period. Later Lao Dong reports in early 2026 describe regular lists of motorbikes fined remotely under Decree 168.

For a renter the point is simple. On a rental bike the registered owner is normally the shop, not you, so a camera notice reaches the shop first. How it is handled afterwards depends on your rental contract, so ask before you sign. The way to avoid the question is to ride as the rules require: helmet on and fastened, stopping lines respected, red lights obeyed, speed kept within posted limits. Our guides to [helmet rules](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/helmet-rules-in-vietnam/), [traffic fines that catch foreign riders out](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/traffic-fines-vietnam-foreign-riders/) and the [rental contract clauses](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-motorbike-rental-contract-explained/) explain how notices usually reach the renter. This article gives no fine amounts because they change with amendments.

## Parking in an office district

Parking is the real challenge here, because the kerb is crowded with office workers' bikes and the rules are enforced. Law 36/2024/QH15, Article 18, sets the basics. On city streets you may stop or park only at the kerb or pavement on the right in your direction of travel, with the nearest wheel no more than 0.25 metres away, without obstructing pedestrians or vehicles. You must not stop or park on the left of a one-way street, on a pedestrian crossing, at a junction or within 5 metres of its edge, in front of an office or organisation gate with a vehicle entrance or within 5 metres either side, at a bus stop or passenger pick-up point, or where the vehicle hides a traffic sign or signal. Article 77 says pavements are for pedestrians and roadways for traffic, so a bike on a walkway that has not been designated for parking is a problem even if others are doing it.

Article 69 allows traffic police to move a vehicle that is parked illegally and causes an obstruction, and the owner or driver pays for the removal. Our [parking guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-motorbike-in-hanoi/) explains what happens in practice and what to keep.

What this means in Cau Giay:

1. **Use a basement, building lot or marked bay** where the office tower or mall has one open to visitors, and ask the attendant about hours.
2. **Do not park across a tower's driveway or gate,** even for a minute.
3. **Do not leave the bike on the walkway beside a bus or metro stop.**
4. **Lock the steering.** The shop provides no extra lock, so choose a supervised place.
5. **Photograph the spot and any ticket.** If the bike is moved, the photo shows where you left it.

For paying small charges on the day, see [how to pay for fuel, parking and tolls](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/paying-fuel-parking-tolls-hanoi-visitor/).

## Visiting the Vietnam Museum of Ethnology

The museum is the best-known visitor destination in the area, at 1 Nguyen Van Huyen Street, Nghia Do. Its official English page lists opening hours of 08:30 to 17:30 every day except Mondays and the New Year holiday. Check the site for the ticket price and any holiday changes before you go.

Ride there off-peak, park in the museum's own area if the attendant directs you, and plan to leave before the evening peak builds. A museum visit is also a good way to let a commuter rush pass. Photograph the parking location and keep your ticket.

## Metro as a pressure valve

Hanoi Metro Line 3 now runs an elevated section from Nhon to Cau Giay, and a Wikipedia summary lists operating hours of about 05:30 to 22:00, with the underground extension toward the centre still under construction in June 2026. If your plan is a day in the office belt, you can park at the edge and take the train for the central part. For other transport costs and rules, see the [Hanoi areas guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/areas/).

## The Ring Road 1 plan and Cau Giay

Hanoi's low-emission zone plan applies to the area inside Ring Road 1, and a VnEconomy report on the draft lists the nine wards involved: Hoan Kiem, Cua Nam, Ba Dinh, Giang Vo, Ngoc Ha, Hai Ba Trung, O Cho Dua, Tay Ho and Van Mieu - Quoc Tu Giam. Cau Giay is not among them. As of October 2026, the pilot covers only parts of Hoan Kiem ward. Reports say the zone will widen to the whole of Ring Road 1 from 2028, which matters if your trip continues towards the centre.

## FAQ: Cau Giay by scooter

### Is Cau Giay a good area for a first-time rider?

It is manageable off-peak, but the commuter peaks are demanding. Choose mid-morning and avoid the elevated decks.

### Can I ride on the Ring Road 3 elevated deck?

No. Reports and our ring roads guide say it is car-only. Use the ground road beneath it.

### Where can I park near an office tower?

Use a marked bay, basement or lot open to visitors. Under Law 36, do not stop within 5 metres of a junction or in front of a gate with a vehicle entrance.

### Will a camera fine reach me?

Camera notices go to the registered owner, which is the rental shop, and can be passed on under the contract. Ride as the rules require.

### When is the Vietnam Museum of Ethnology open?

The official site lists 08:30 to 17:30, closed Mondays and the New Year holiday. Confirm before you go.
