Before you reserve a rental scooter in Hanoi, ask the shop eight things in writing or on the phone: which bike you will actually get, what the quoted price includes, how much deposit is held and when it comes back, which documents you must show, how fuel and late returns are handled, who helps if the bike breaks down, and what cover exists if something goes wrong. A good shop answers plainly. A vague answer is information too, and it is far cheaper to hear it before you travel than at the counter with a bag on your shoulder.

This guide is a question list, not a legal opinion. Where we describe how Nguyen Ha Motorbike Rental works, the facts come from the shop's own published terms; for any other shop, treat the same question as something to ask.

## Why the questions matter more than the photo

Rental listings often show one pretty scooter and one starting price. What you ride away on is a specific bike with its own condition, and what you pay depends on terms that are rarely on the listing. Asking in advance does three useful things. It turns a marketing price into a total you can plan around. It shows how the shop deals with questions, which is a good preview of how it will deal with problems. And it leaves a short written record you can refer back to if a handover is rushed.

Keep the conversation friendly and specific. Shops in Hanoi are used to visitors who ask about deposits and fuel; nobody is offended by a clear list.

## Question 1: Which bike will I get, and is it confirmed?

"Honda Vision" on a listing can mean several bikes of different ages. Ask for the model, the approximate age and whether the booking is for that exact model or "a scooter of that type".

At Nguyen Ha Motorbike Rental the model names on the price page are the ones the shop may have, and the bike actually available is confirmed when you book. That is a sensible way to run a small fleet, and it is also the reason to ask. If you need a particular size, for example because you are tall or carrying a passenger, say so at the start. Our guide to [two riders and luggage on a Honda Lead or Vision](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/scooter-for-two-riders-and-luggage-hanoi/) helps you decide what to request.

Also ask whether you may see and choose among the bikes on arrival, and whether a different model can be swapped in if the first one is not ready.

## Question 2: What does the price include?

The price per day is only the beginning. Ask what is inside it and what is extra:

- **Helmet:** how many, and what quality. Nguyen Ha provides one basic helmet per rider at no charge. If you want a better helmet, bring your own.
- **Raincoat:** not included for short rentals, so bring one in the wet season.
- **Extra lock:** not provided, so plan to lock the steering and park in guarded places.
- **Delivery:** only for weekly or monthly agreements, within 10 km of Hoan Kiem Lake, at VND 50,000 per trip or VND 100,000 both ways, with anything farther agreed in advance. There is no airport service.
- **Fuel:** covered in Question 5.

Our [prices page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/prices/) shows the current ranges. The final price is confirmed on the actual bike before the contract is signed, so ask the shop to repeat the total before you commit.

## Question 3: How much is the deposit, and when does it come back?

A deposit is normal and it is separate from the rent. At Nguyen Ha the cash deposit runs from VND 2,000,000 to VND 5,000,000 depending on the bike and the contract, and it is not a no-deposit service. Ask:

1. The exact figure for the bike you are reserving.
2. In what currency and form it is taken.
3. What is written down about the bike's condition at handover.
4. When and how it is returned, and what, if anything, can be deducted.

Be wary of any arrangement where the shop keeps your passport as security. Nguyen Ha checks and photographs identity documents and the licence for the contract but does not keep the originals. Our article on [how a motorbike rental deposit works and how to get it back](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbike-rental-deposit-hanoi/) goes through the return step by step.

## Question 4: Which documents do you need from me?

Ask this before you fly, not at the counter. The shop will want to see your passport and a driving licence, and the licence has to be valid for the bike. Vietnam's Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (Law 36/2024/QH15), in force from 1 January 2025, requires a rider to carry a licence that matches the vehicle being ridden, and says a holder of an international driving permit issued by a Vienna Convention country must carry both the permit and the national licence for the right category. The law lists licence class A1 as covering two-wheeled motorcycles up to 125 cm3 or an electric motor up to 11 kW.

That is a summary of the official text as of October 2026, not a ruling on your own situation. Rules and enforcement can change, so check the current text and ask the shop what it accepts. Our guide to [documents to carry when riding in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/documents-to-carry-riding-in-vietnam/) lays out the full list, and the next section covers what to prepare.

## Question 5: What are the fuel and return rules?

Fuel is a common source of small disputes, so ask for the exact rule. At Nguyen Ha a petrol bike is handed over with about 0.5 to 2 litres and should come back with about 0.5 to 1 litre. An empty tank at return costs VND 20,000. For an electric scooter, the battery level and charging are confirmed at handover.

Then ask about the clock:

- How long is a rental day? At Nguyen Ha it is 24 hours, and shorter use is charged as a full day.
- What happens if you are late? From 1 to 5 hours late it is VND 30,000 per hour; from 6 hours it is one extra rental day.
- Is there a refund for returning early? At Nguyen Ha, unused rent is not refunded.

The shop's opening hours are 09:00 to 21:00 daily, so check that your planned return fits inside them. ## Question 6: What happens if the bike breaks down?

Nobody plans for a flat tyre, so ask before you need the answer. Useful questions are:

- Whom do I call, and is a phone number answered during my planned riding hours?
- Does the shop come to me, send a mechanic, or expect me to find a repair shop?
- Who pays for a puncture, a lost key or a dead battery, and what is the shop's rule for wear compared with damage?
- Is a replacement bike offered if the first cannot be repaired?

## Question 7: What cover do I have if there is an accident or theft?

Do not assume the rent includes cover for the bike or for you. Ask plainly what the shop's insurance, if any, pays for, who bears the cost of damage or theft, and what the contract says about the renter's responsibility. A travel policy often excludes riding a motorbike without the right licence, and some exclude rented motorbikes entirely, so read your own policy too. Our guide on [what travel insurance usually does not cover for motorbike rentals](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/travel-insurance-motorbike-rental-vietnam/) explains the usual gaps, and the article on [what the clauses in a rental contract mean](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-motorbike-rental-contract-explained/) helps you read the contract itself.

## Question 8: Can I see the contract before I arrive?

Ask for the terms in advance, in English if possible, or at least read them on the day before you sign. Check four things: the deposit, the condition record at handover, who pays for damage, and the late-return rule. If any point differs from what you were told on the phone, ask the shop to correct it in writing and take a photograph of the amended page.

## A short script you can copy

> Hello, I would like to reserve a scooter from [date] to [date]. Please confirm: which model I will get, the total price for those dates including the helmet, the deposit and when it is returned, which documents you need to see, the fuel level at handover and return, your late-return rule, and who I call if the bike breaks down. Is that confirmed for my dates?

Keep the reply. If the answer changes at the counter, you have a reference.

## Red flags and green flags

| Green flag | Red flag |
|---|---|
| Names the model and says when it is confirmed | "Any scooter, no problem" with no detail |
| States deposit, fuel and late rules without being pressed | Refuses to give figures until you arrive |
| Photographs documents for the contract and returns the originals | Asks to keep your passport |
| Gives a working phone number and opening hours | Only a messaging account with no hours |
| Explains what is not included, such as raincoat and extra lock | "Everything included" with no list |

## FAQ about reserving a rental scooter

### How far ahead should I reserve?

There is no universal rule. In the busy tourist months it is sensible to ask a few days ahead, and for a weekly or monthly rental or a particular model, earlier. The shop confirms the actual bike when you book, so ask for a confirmation that names your dates.

### Is a deposit always required?

At Nguyen Ha it is, and it is a cash deposit of VND 2,000,000 to VND 5,000,000 depending on the bike and contract. Other shops vary, so ask for the amount and the return conditions.

### Can I pay for the whole rental in advance?

Ask. Payment terms are the shop's own, and nothing here promises any particular method. What matters is that you get a clear total and a record of what you paid.

### Should I rent a scooter if I have never ridden in Asia?

That is a personal judgement. Hanoi traffic is dense and unpredictable, and a licence that technically covers the bike does not make you comfortable in it. If you are unsure, ask the shop about a quieter test ride in the area before you leave, and consider a lower-powered or electric bike.
