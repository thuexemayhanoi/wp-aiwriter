As of October 2026, the Hanoi Metro does not serve the Old Quarter, Hoan Kiem Lake or the east bank of the Red River, so for most central sightseeing the real choice is between a scooter and walking, taxis or ride-hailing, with the metro as a useful extra for specific trips in the west and south-west of the city. Two lines run: Line 2A from Cat Linh to Ha Dong and Yen Nghia, and the elevated part of Line 3 from Nhon to Cau Giay. The underground link that would carry Line 3 into the centre is still being built. This guide explains what each line covers, where the metro helps, where a scooter is better and how to decide for a given day.

## What is actually running

Hanoi has two operating metro sections, and the two do not connect to each other.

| Line | Route | Length and stations | Opened |
|---|---|---|---|
| Line 2A | Cat Linh to Ha Dong and Yen Nghia | About 13 km, 12 elevated stations | 6 November 2021 |
| Line 3 (elevated section) | Nhon to Cau Giay | 8.5 km, 8 stations | 8 August 2024 |

Both lines run from 5:30 to 22:00 daily according to Hanoi Metro. A February 2026 notice set peak hours at 6:30 to 9:00 and 16:30 to 19:30 with a train every six minutes, off-peak trains every 10 minutes, a train every 15 minutes between 19:30 and 22:00, and a uniform 10-minute interval on weekends and public holidays. Check the operator for the timetable that applies on your day, because the schedule has changed before.

The planned Line 3 underground section from Kim Ma to Hanoi Railway Station, with stations at Kim Ma, Cat Linh, Van Mieu and Hanoi Station, is not open. A Hanoi Moi report of August 2026 says the 8.5 km elevated section is not yet connected to the underground section or integrated with Line 2A. Wikipedia's Line 3 page lists the underground section as 79.5 per cent complete as of June 2026, with a planned opening in late 2027. On 19 October 2026 Hanoi held a commemorative plaque ceremony for Line 3 at Cau Giay station, and the EU is funding preparation for a further underground extension towards Hoang Mai, but neither changes what you can ride this week.

## Where the metro helps a visitor

Line 2A is the more useful of the two for a visitor because its Cat Linh end is closest to the middle of the city. Hanoi Metro's own station page describes Cat Linh as the first and largest station, at the junction of Cat Linh, Hao Nam, Giang Vo and Giang Van Minh streets, with interchange to buses and the Nui Truc BRT. Wikipedia lists the station in O Cho Dua ward. It is west of the Old Quarter and not within easy walking range of it, so check the distance on a map before you assume you can walk on from the station.

Situations where the metro is a sensible choice:

- **Travelling along the Line 2A corridor** towards Thai Ha, Lang, Thuong Dinh or Ha Dong, where the elevated track skips the traffic below.
- **Getting to Cau Giay and western Hanoi** on Line 3, for example to university areas or the Cau Giay district.
- **A wet or very hot day** when you want a dry, air-conditioned journey. Our guide to [riding in Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) covers the other side of that decision.
- **An evening out when you may drink.** Public transport or a taxi is the sensible choice when you will not ride, and our guide to [drink-driving rules for motorbike riders](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/drink-driving-rules-motorbike-vietnam/) explains why. Do not forget the last trains leave before 22:00.
- **Avoiding parking.** A metro trip needs no parking search.

Operator figures quoted in the Hanoi Moi report say Line 2A had handled over 380,000 train journeys by the end of July 2026, with schedule completion and on-time rates above 99 per cent on both lines. Those are the operator's own numbers, but they suggest the service is dependable.

## Where the metro does not help

A scooter, a taxi or walking is the better answer for most of central sightseeing, because the metro does not reach it:

- **Old Quarter and Hoan Kiem Lake.** No station serves them. Our guide to [riding in Hanoi's Old Quarter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-old-quarter/) explains when to ride and when to walk.
- **The east bank, Long Bien and Gia Lam.** Neither operating line crosses the Red River. If you stay near Nguyen Ha's shop at Alley 5 Nguyen Van Cu Street, Bo De Ward, Long Bien District, the metro is not a practical way to start your day.
- **Tay Ho and West Lake, Ba Dinh and the Mausoleum area.** No operating station is described as serving these areas in the sources used for this guide, so assume you need another way to get there.
- **Day trips out of the city.** Metro lines do not go to Ninh Binh, Tam Dao or Ba Vi. Our guide to [taking a rental motorbike out of Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/take-rental-motorbike-out-of-hanoi/) covers that.
- **Late nights.** The last trains finish at 22:00, a point to remember when you plan a long dinner.

## A side-by-side view

| Factor | Scooter | Metro (Line 2A or Line 3 elevated) |
|---|---|---|
| Coverage in the centre | Almost any street you can ride and park on | Only the west and south-west corridors |
| Hours | Whenever you choose, within your own limits | 5:30 to 22:00 |
| Parking | You must find a guarded place | None needed |
| Weather | Exposed to rain, heat and wet roads | Mostly protected |
| Luggage | Limited | Easier for normal bags |
| Transfers | None, but you navigate traffic | Walking or another vehicle at each end |
| Cost | A rental, fuel and parking | A fare per trip; see the operator's site |
| Flexibility | High | Fixed lines and timetable |

## How to choose for a given day

Ask these questions in order:

1. **Where are you going?** If the answer is the Old Quarter, Hoan Kiem, Long Bien, Tay Ho or the Mausoleum area, the metro is not the main answer.
2. **How many stops will you make?** Many short stops in different areas favour a scooter. One long trip along a metro corridor favours the metro.
3. **When are you travelling?** After about 21:30, the metro is a poor bet. Check whether the last train matches your plan.
4. **What is the weather?** A red-light rain day favours public transport, a taxi or staying indoors.
5. **Are you carrying bags or travelling with a passenger?** A scooter has limited space, and our guide to [carrying a passenger and luggage on a scooter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/scooter-for-two-riders-and-luggage-hanoi/) explains the limits.
6. **How confident are you in traffic?** If city traffic worries you, a metro trip, a taxi or a rented bike used only for quieter areas is a sensible middle path.

## Combining both without overpromising

Many visitors use a mixed approach, for example the scooter for a day around the east bank and Hoan Kiem, and the metro or a taxi for a trip to the west. There are two cautions:

- **Do not assume parking near a station.** This guide did not find confirmed motorbike parking details for individual stations, so ask or check before planning to leave a bike there. Our guide to [parking a motorbike in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-motorbike-in-hanoi/) explains how to recognise a legitimate facility.
- **A rental is charged by the day.** One day is 24 hours, and shorter use is charged as a full day, so a scooter that sits unused while you take the metro still costs the same. For longer stays, the weekly and monthly packages are priced separately; the [prices page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/prices/) has the ranges and the shop can confirm.

If you decide on a scooter for the days you want freedom and public transport for the rest, a weekly or monthly plan may be cheaper than many single days, and our guide to [weekly and monthly rental questions](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/weekly-monthly-motorbike-rental-hanoi/) lists what to ask.

## Ticketing and practicalities for visitors

From February 2026 Hanoi Metro applied an integrated ticketing system with personal identification, electronic authentication and biometric recognition on Line 3.1, after introducing it on Line 2A in November 2025. Passengers can pay through the Hanoi Metro app with domestic bank accounts or cards, international Visa cards and e-wallets. The February report did not say how a visitor without a Vietnamese identity document uses these systems, so check at the station and with the operator before relying on any one payment method, and keep a second way of getting where you are going.

## FAQ: scooter or metro in central Hanoi

### Can I take the metro to the Old Quarter?

Not as of October 2026. The operating lines are in the west and south-west, and the underground stations planned nearer the centre are not open.

### Do the two lines connect?

No. The elevated section of Line 3 is not yet connected to the underground section or to Line 2A, according to an August 2026 report.

### When does the metro run?

From 5:30 to 22:00 daily on both lines, with trains every 6, 10 or 15 minutes depending on time and day.

### Is the metro cheaper than renting a scooter?

For a single trip, yes, but they do different jobs. For fares, check metrohanoi.vn, and for rental ranges check the prices page.

### Can I bring a scooter onto the metro?

Not that this guide found. Treat the metro as a passenger service and check the operator's passenger guide for what you may bring.

### Which is better on a rainy day?

On a red-light rain day, public transport, a taxi or an indoor plan is usually better than riding.
