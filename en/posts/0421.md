If a rental motorbike gets a puncture, stop safely, do not ride on the flat tyre and phone the shop before you pay anyone for a repair. The shop is the first contact because it owns the bike, knows what the contract says about punctures and may prefer a particular repairer, send someone or tell you to go to a nearby stall. In Hanoi, small tyre-repair stalls are common and can fix many punctures quickly, but you should ask the shop first, agree the price before the work starts and keep the receipt. Who pays for a puncture depends on the contract and on what caused it, and Nguyen Ha's published terms do not set a fixed puncture charge, so ask rather than assume. This guide covers the steps in order, whom to contact and why, what a roadside repair involves, how to keep safe on the roadside, what the Civil Code says in general terms and how to avoid the next one.

## What a puncture looks like

A puncture is a hole that lets air out of the tyre. It can be slow, so that the tyre gradually goes soft, or fast, so that it deflates in seconds. Signs include:

- **The bike feels heavy or sluggish,** and the steering feels vague.
- **The rear end wobbles** or the bike pulls to one side.
- **A flapping or thumping sound** from a wheel.
- **A visibly flat or soft tyre** when you look down at it.

Honda's guidance on tyres says that tyre air pressure matters, that low pressure shortens tyre life and that worn tyres slip more easily in the rain. It also says to stop using a motorcycle if its tyres show cracks or scratches. A flat tyre is not something to ride on, even slowly. Riding on a flat tyre can damage the tyre beyond repair and can damage the wheel rim.

## Step 1: stop safely

As soon as you suspect a puncture, ease off the throttle, brake gently and stop in a safe place. Do not brake hard or swerve, because a flat tyre reduces grip.

Under Article 18 of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (Law No. 36/2024/QH15), in force since 1 January 2025, a driver whose vehicle has to stop because of a technical fault, and who is left partly on the carriageway or where stopping is not allowed, must warn other road users with hazard lights or a warning sign behind the vehicle. Many small bikes have no hazard switch, so the practical answer is to move the bike completely off the carriageway.

- **Pull over** to the side, away from junctions, bends and busy lanes.
- **Switch off the engine** and take the key.
- **Put the bike on its stand** on firm, level ground.
- **Step away from the traffic.**
- **Look at the tyre,** without touching anything sharp.

If you are on a busy road or a bridge, push the bike to a safer place, as far as that is possible. Do not ride it.

## Step 2: record the problem

A minute of evidence can save an argument later.

- **Photograph the flat tyre,** the whole bike and the place.
- **Photograph any object in the tread,** such as a nail or screw, before it is removed.
- **Note the time, the place and the odometer reading.**
- **Do not remove the object yourself** if you can avoid it. If a repairer removes it, ask to keep it or photograph it.

If you notice a cut or a bulge in the sidewall, photograph it carefully. That kind of damage often means the tyre cannot be repaired and needs replacing.

## Step 3: call the shop first

Call Nguyen Ha on 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969), open 09:00 to 21:00 daily. Tell the shop:

- your name and the plate number;
- where you are, with a map pin if possible;
- that you have a puncture and which wheel, front or rear;
- whether the bike can be pushed to safety;
- a photograph of the tyre.

Then ask:

- "Do you want me to take it to a repair stall, or will you send someone?"
- "Is there a stall you recommend near me?"
- "Who pays for the repair?"
- "Should I keep the receipt?"

Calling first matters for three reasons. The shop may have a preferred repairer or may prefer to collect the bike. The shop can tell you what the contract says about punctures. And a repair that the shop has agreed to is easier to claim or to discuss afterwards. Our guide to [lost keys, flat tyres and breakdowns with a rental bike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-bike-breakdown-hanoi-what-to-do/) gives the general order of action.

## Step 4: who does the repair

You have several options. The shop will usually tell you which.

| Option | When it fits | Points to check |
|---|---|---|
| The shop sends someone or collects the bike | You are far from a repairer, or the tyre looks badly damaged | Ask how long it will take |
| A roadside tyre-repair stall | A simple puncture near a stall | Get the shop's agreement and agree the price first |
| A motorbike workshop | A damaged tyre, rim or valve, or a repair that needs more tools | Ask the shop which workshop |
| Replacing the tyre or tube | The tyre is cut, split or worn out | Ask the shop to approve it |

Roadside stalls are a fixture of Hanoi, and for a simple puncture they are often quick. But a puncture repair is only as good as the repairer. Ask the shop before you hand over the bike, agree the price before work begins and watch what is done.

## Tubed or tubeless: why it matters

Many small manual bikes use tyres with inner tubes. Honda's Wave RSX FI manual describes the tyres as having inner tubes, and our guide to [reading a Honda Wave tyre size and pressure label](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-read-a-honda-wave-tyre-size-and-pressure-label/) explains how to check. With a tubed tyre, a puncture is usually repaired by patching or replacing the tube. With a tubeless tyre, a puncture is usually plugged or patched from the inside. Some scooters are tubeless and some are not, so ask the shop or the repairer which kind the bike has. Our guide to [tubes, punctures and what to ask](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/choosing-tyres-for-a-manual-bike-rental-in-wet-weather/) in wet-weather tyre choice explains the difference in more detail.

## Agree the price before the repair

Do this every time, before any work starts.

1. **Ask what the repair will cost,** and ask it in a way that gets a clear number.
2. **Ask what the number includes:** a patch, a new tube, labour, air.
3. **Tell the repairer the shop has agreed,** if it has.
4. **Do not agree to extra work** such as a new tyre unless the shop says so.
5. **Watch what is done,** and check that the correct wheel is repaired.
6. **Pay, and ask for a receipt.** If no printed receipt is available, ask for a written note or take a photograph of the stall, the repairer and the price.

Nguyen Ha's published terms do not list a standard price for a puncture repair, and we do not give figures here, because roadside prices vary. Settle the price with the repairer and the shop, not with a figure from an article.

## After the repair

- **Check the pressure.** A repaired tyre may need air. Honda says to check pressure with a gauge and to do so when the tyres are cool. Ask the repairer or the shop for the right pressure for your bike. Our guide to reading a tyre label gives the Wave RSX FI figures from Honda's manual, and other models differ.
- **Check the other tyre.** A nail picked up on a road may not be alone.
- **Look at the valve** and cap.
- **Ride gently for the first few kilometres,** and check the tyre again.
- **Tell the shop** what was done, send the receipt and the photographs and ask how it will be settled.

## Who pays

This is the question everyone asks, and there is no universal answer.

Vietnam's Civil Code (Law No. 91/2015/QH13), in force since 1 January 2017, treats a motorbike rental as a lease of property. As of October 2026, Article 477 says the lessor must keep the property in the agreed condition and fit for its purpose and must repair damage and defects, except minor damage that custom requires the lessee to repair. Article 479 says the lessee must preserve and maintain the property and carry out minor repairs, and is not liable for natural wear. Article 477 also says that if the lessor has been told and does not repair, the lessee may repair at reasonable cost, must tell the lessor and may claim the cost.

Whether a puncture is "minor damage that custom requires the lessee to repair" or an ordinary risk of use that the shop bears is not answered by the Code. It depends on the contract, on what caused it and on local practice. Some shops expect the renter to pay for a simple puncture repair, and others treat it as part of running costs. Ask before you sign, and ask again when it happens. If the tyre is worn out or defective, a replacement is more likely to be the shop's responsibility, as our guide to [requesting a replacement bike after finding a safety defect](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/requesting-a-replacement-bike-after-finding-a-safety-defect/) explains. If the tyre was damaged by misuse, it may be yours. Our guide to [what counts as normal wear on a rented Honda Wave](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-counts-as-normal-wear-on-a-rented-honda-wave/) explains how wear and damage are separated. This is general information, not legal advice.

## Keep the paperwork

- **The receipt or note** for the repair.
- **The photographs** of the tyre, the object and the place.
- **The messages** with the shop.
- **The amount paid,** and what it covered.

## How to avoid a puncture

- **Check the tyres at pickup** for cuts, objects and the wear indicator. Our [10-minute inspection checklist](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-scooter-inspection-checklist/) covers it.
- **Check the pressure** now and then. Honda advises using a gauge at least once a month and checking when tyres are cool.
- **Avoid riding over debris,** building sites and broken glass where you can.
- **Slow down on rough surfaces** and over potholes. Our guide to [riding a manual motorbike over speed bumps](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-a-manual-motorbike-over-speed-bumps/) explains how.
- **Look at the tyres after hitting something.** Honda says to inspect the tyres after contact with a kerb or large bumps.

## A quick checklist

1. **Stop safely and move off the carriageway.**
2. **Photograph the tyre, the object and the place.**
3. **Call the shop before paying anyone.**
4. **Agree who repairs it and the price first.**
5. **Get a receipt or a note.**
6. **Check the pressure and the other tyre.**
7. **Tell the shop what was done.**

## FAQ: a puncture during a rental

### Who should I call first?

The shop. It can advise on a repairer, send help and tell you what the contract says.

### Can I use a roadside repair stall?

Usually yes, with the shop's agreement. Agree the price before the work starts and keep the receipt.

### Who pays for the puncture?

It depends on the contract and the cause. Nguyen Ha's published terms do not set a fixed puncture charge, so ask before you pay.

### Can I ride on a flat tyre to the shop?

No. It can ruin the tyre and the rim and is unsafe.

### What if the tyre is cut or has a bulge?

It probably needs replacing. Tell the shop and ask for approval before replacing it.

### What if the shop does not answer?

Move the bike to a safe place, try again, send a message with photographs and keep a record of what you do and spend.
