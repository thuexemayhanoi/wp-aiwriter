Shared charging stations can suit a rental electric scooter, but only as a backup that you have checked in advance, not as the plan for your day. Public charging posts in Vietnam are run by operators such as VinFast's network, and the published steps assume that you bring your own charger, use the operator's app and stay with the scooter while it charges. Battery-swap cabinets are a separate system for bikes built to take swappable batteries. Whether either works for your rental depends on the exact bike, the shop's agreement and the time you can spare, and a full charge on a standard charger takes hours rather than minutes. This guide sets out the questions to ask before you rely on one.

## What "shared" charging can mean

People use the phrase for three different things, and they behave differently.

- **Public charging posts.** A post with several ports, where you connect the scooter's own charger and pay through an app. VinFast describes this system for its e-scooters.
- **Battery-swap cabinets.** A cabinet where you take out a spent battery and fit a charged one. These need a bike built for swappable batteries and normally a battery subscription.
- **Shared sockets.** A cafe, hotel or office that lets you plug in. This is informal and set by the host.

Only the first two are "stations" in the proper sense, and each has conditions. The third is covered in our guide to [charging an electric scooter in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/charging-an-electric-scooter-in-hanoi/), which also deals with hotels and timing.

## How a public charging post works

VinFast's published guide for its e-scooters describes the steps. In summary:

1. Move the scooter to a free post with an available port, shown by a green light.
2. Turn off the vehicle, open the compartment, connect the charger to the scooter and plug it into a socket on the post.
3. Log in to the VinFast E-Scooter app, scan the QR code on the post, choose the port and confirm. Charging starts when the port light turns red.
4. The app shows charging time, energy used and a provisional price, and you can stop the session from it.
5. When charging ends, unplug the charger, store it in the compartment and move the scooter away.

Two things follow for a renter. First, the process needs your own charger, because the post supplies power, not a charger. Second, it needs an account in the operator's app. VinFast's separate answer on its charging network says that the service is for VinFast electric vehicle customers. Ask the shop whether a renter can use the network and how, rather than assuming.

## Questions to settle before you rely on one

1. **Is the bike compatible?** Public posts described by VinFast serve VinFast scooters. An electric bicycle or another brand may not be served.
2. **Do I have the bike's own charger?** Without it, a post is no use. Ask the shop which charger comes with the bike and where it is kept.
3. **Whose app account is used?** Ask who pays and how, and whether the shop agrees to the use.
4. **Where is the nearest post to my route?** Check the operator's app on the day. Do not assume a map is current.
5. **Can I stay with the bike?** The advice from fire-safety officials is not to leave a vehicle charging unattended.
6. **How much time do I have?** See the next section.
7. **Is it within the contract?** Ask the shop whether use of third-party charging is allowed.

Nguyen Ha Motorbike Rental lists electric bicycles, 50cc-equivalent electric scooters and some VinFast electric motorbikes as possible rentals, and confirms the battery level and charging arrangement for each bike at handover. That is the time to ask about public posts.

## How long a charge takes

A charging stop is long, so plan around it. VinFast's own specification page for the Evo200, one model, says a standard 400 W charger takes about 10 hours from empty to full and about 8 hours from 20 per cent, while a 1,000 W charger takes about 4 hours from empty. The owner's manual describes the standard charger supplied with that model as drawing about 400 W.

Those figures are for the Evo200, not for every bike you might rent, and the rate at a public post depends on the post and the charger you connect. The sensible reading is that a public post is a stop of an hour or several hours, not the five minutes of a petrol station. If you need to be back in two hours, it will not rescue a flat battery.

## What the manufacturer says about care while charging

The same manual contains advice that matters at a shared post:

- Use only the supplied charger or an equivalent supplied by VinFast.
- Do not charge when the battery is at or above 45 degrees Celsius, and wait about an hour after riding for the battery to cool.
- Keeping the battery between 20 and 80 per cent is better for its life, and the manual says not to hesitate to top up often.
- Turn on the battery switch under the seat before plugging in, or the vehicle will not charge.
- Do not leave the charger in the compartment while it charges.
- After plugging in, wait at least 30 seconds before switching on the ignition, and if the charger is pulled out suddenly wait 10 seconds before reconnecting.

VinFast's FAQ for public posts adds that you should plug in with swift, firm movements, unplug the power first and then the charging port, and wait at least 30 seconds before turning on the electric lock.

## Fire safety at shared stations

Charging is where most electric two-wheeler fires start, and fire-safety bodies say so. Hanoi's fire-prevention unit, in advice reported in September 2026, warns against charging overnight or with nobody present, against charging a battery that is still hot from use, and against unsuitable chargers, and advises dry, ventilated places away from flammable material. The national fire-prevention department's advice, reported in 2025, includes waiting about 20 minutes after use and not charging for more than 8 hours continuously.

The Ministry of Public Security, answering a question in May 2026, said that charging stations and battery-swap stations are managed under fire-safety rules according to their scale and hazard, and that charging areas should have rules banning incompatible chargers. These are the kinds of rules that make an operator's station safer than a corridor full of plugs, but they do not replace your own attention.

## Battery-swap cabinets and rental bikes

Battery swapping is growing in Vietnam, but it works differently. Vietnam News reported in August 2025 that VinFast was launching swappable-battery motorbikes with a two-compartment bay, so that riders could fit or remove two 1.5 kWh packs at public cabinets, with the battery rented by subscription and home charging still possible. Electrek reported in March 2026 that these models use dual removable packs of about 85 km each.

For a short rental this matters in two ways. A scooter with a fixed battery cannot use a cabinet, and a bike that does take swappable batteries belongs to a subscription arrangement that the shop, not the renter, would have to hold. Do not expect to walk up to a cabinet with a rental scooter and swap a pack. Ask the shop first. Our guide to [renting an electric scooter in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/renting-an-electric-scooter-in-hanoi/) covers range and charging on a typical rental.

## When a shared station is worth trying

| Situation | Worth it? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| You are on a VinFast scooter, have the charger and the shop agrees | Yes, as a backup | Fits the published steps |
| You can wait a few hours near the post | Yes | Charging is slow |
| You have a tight schedule | No | A charge will not be quick |
| You do not have the charger | No | Posts need your own charger |
| The bike is an electric bicycle | Ask first | The network is aimed at scooters |
| You hope to swap a battery | Ask the shop | Needs a swap-ready bike and a subscription |
| You would leave the bike unattended | No | Fire-safety advice is against it |

## A sensible way to use shared charging

1. Start the day with the battery as full as the shop's charging allows.
2. Plan a route that stays well within the range the shop gives you. Our guide to [charging an electric scooter in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/charging-an-electric-scooter-in-hanoi/) explains how to build a charging window into the day.
3. Treat a shared post as a margin of safety, not part of the plan.
4. Keep your phone charged, since the app needs it.
5. Save the shop's number, 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969), open 09:00 to 21:00 daily.
6. If in doubt, ride back to the shop and charge there.

## FAQ: shared charging stations and rental bikes

### Can I use a public charging station with a rental electric scooter?

Possibly, if the bike is compatible, you have its charger, the operator's app can be used and the shop agrees. Ask before you rely on it.

### Do I need my own charger at a public post?

For the VinFast posts described by the manufacturer, yes. You connect the scooter's charger to the post.

### How long does it take to charge at a shared station?

Hours, not minutes. VinFast gives about 10 hours from empty on a 400 W charger and about 4 hours on a 1,000 W charger for the Evo200.

### Can I swap a battery with a rental scooter?

Only if the bike is built for swappable batteries and the subscription is arranged, which is unusual for short rentals. Ask the shop.

### Is it safe to leave the scooter charging at a station?

Fire-safety advice is to stay nearby, avoid overnight charging and use the right charger.

### What if I have no charger and the battery is low?

Phone the shop. A post cannot help without a charger.
