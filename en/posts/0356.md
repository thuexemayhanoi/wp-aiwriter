Riding from Hanoi to Hai Phong is possible on a motorbike, but only on the old National Highway 5, never on the fast expressway, and it is a demanding ride because of the distance and the heavy truck traffic. Published figures put the road distance between about 105 and 122 km, which means a round trip of roughly 210 to 245 km in a day on a small rental bike. Law 36/2024/QH15 bars motorcycles and mopeds from expressways, and the World Bank describes National Highway 5 as a truck-heavy corridor that is a major cause of fatal crashes. Both facts shape the safe plan: leave early, ride within your limits, build in rests, and be willing to turn back or finish the trip by train. This guide covers the hazards in order of seriousness and gives a checklist.

## Why this ride is riskier than its distance suggests

A hundred kilometres sounds modest, but several factors combine. The road is a national highway shared with container trucks, coaches and local traffic; it passes through towns where speed limits and junctions change; and the ride is long enough for tiredness to degrade your judgement on the return leg, which is when many riders are most at risk. The destination is also a large port city, where heavy vehicles are everywhere.

Since 1 July 2025 Hai Phong includes the former Hai Duong province, according to the city's own news. It now covers about 3,194 square kilometres, so signs and maps may place you in Hai Phong city earlier than you expect. This changes the names, not the road.

## Which road: National Highway 5 and the expressway

Two main routes link the two cities. Travel sites describe the Hanoi to Hai Phong expressway (CT04, also called 5B) as the fast, tolled option for cars, and National Highway 5 as the slower toll-free road that motorbikes use. Time estimates quoted online range from about 1 hour 30 minutes on the expressway to about 2 hours or more on Highway 5. One travel guide estimates about three hours on Highway 5 for a motorbike. Treat those as rough.

The rule is not a matter of preference. Article 25 of Law 36/2024/QH15 says motorcycles, mopeds, similar vehicles, non-motorised vehicles and pedestrians must not travel on expressways, other than people and vehicles serving the road's management and maintenance. That means:

- Do not follow a navigation route that uses the expressway. Our guide to [checking whether a navigation app suggests an expressway](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-check-if-a-navigation-app-suggests-an-expressway/) shows how to spot and correct it.
- Look for signs at on-ramps; do not enter if motorbikes are prohibited.
- If you end up on one by mistake, do not reverse or turn round, which the same law also bars on expressways. Follow any instructions from road staff and leave at the next exit when it is safe.

See also the [guide to which Hanoi highways allow motorbikes](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-ring-roads-highways-motorbikes-allowed/).

## The hazards in order of seriousness

| Hazard | Why it matters | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Heavy trucks and containers | The World Bank links Highway 5's truck volume to fatal crashes and congestion | Keep a long gap, never ride beside a truck's rear wheels, avoid overtaking on blind bends |
| Fatigue on the return | Tired riders misjudge gaps and react late | Rest every hour or so; turn around early |
| Low sun and dusk | Glare in the morning and dusk visibility on the way back | Start early; be off the highway before dark |
| Rain and wet surfaces | Reduced grip and visibility | Pull over and wait, see below |
| Town sections and junctions | Sudden stops, vehicles crossing, shop frontages | Slow down, cover your brakes |
| Vehicle faults | A rental bike you do not know | Inspect before leaving |

## Time, distance and fatigue

A ride of this length is a different undertaking from a Hanoi city loop. As a planning allowance, count on roughly three hours each way once you include traffic, fuel and short stops, though published estimates vary. A day that starts at 09:00 when the shop opens and finishes in Hai Phong by midday leaves only a few hours before you should start back.

A simple structure helps:

1. **Depart by mid-morning at the latest,** with a full tank.
2. **Stop every hour or so,** even for five minutes, to stretch, drink and check the bike.
3. **Set a hard turnaround time** and stick to it. For example, decide before leaving that you will be on the road home by mid-afternoon.
4. **Do not ride the return in the dark.** Read [riding at night in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-at-night-in-hanoi/) for why that leg is the weakest part of the day.
5. **Stay overnight if you are tired,** rather than ride home on fumes.

For how to space your stops sensibly, see our [guide to planning breaks on a long ride](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-plan-breaks-on-a-long-semi-automatic-bike-ride/) and [when to refuel on a day trip](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/when-to-refuel-a-manual-motorbike-during-a-day-trip/). Petrol bikes at Nguyen Ha are handed over with only about half a litre to two litres, so fill up at the first station you reach.

## Riding technique on a truck-heavy road

These are general defensive-riding habits, not a substitute for experience.

- **Stay visible.** Ride where drivers' mirrors can show you, and avoid hanging back in a truck's blind zone directly behind or alongside it.
- **Expect trucks to pull out and drift.** Heavy vehicles often swing wide at junctions and when passing slower traffic. Give them room instead of squeezing through.
- **Overtake only with a clear view.** On a two-way highway, a pass that looks short can become long if the vehicle ahead speeds up. If in doubt, stay put.
- **Respect speed limits and posted signs,** which change through towns; our guide to [speed limits for motorbikes in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/speed-limits-motorbikes-vietnam/) explains the categories.
- **Brake early and smoothly,** and use both brakes. A small bike loaded with a backpack takes longer to stop than you expect.
- **Keep your eyes moving,** checking mirrors, the shoulder and the junctions ahead, because crossing mopeds and bicycles are common near towns.

If at any point the traffic feels beyond your comfort, pull into a safe lay-by or forecourt, take a break and reconsider the plan. Turning back early is a good decision, not a failure.

## Weather, storms and flooding

Hai Phong is on the northern coast, which tropical storms can affect from summer into autumn. As general guidance, check the forecast before a long ride in the summer and autumn storm season, because strong wind and heavy rain make a highway full of trucks far more dangerous. If heavy rain or strong wind is forecast, postpone. If rain starts on the road, slow down, increase your following distance and find shelter rather than push on; our guide to [riding in Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) explains how wet roads and visibility change the ride.

## Arriving in Hai Phong

The old centre of Hai Phong, south of the Cam river, is busy and, according to the World Bank, congested by through-traffic from Highway 5. Plan where you will park before you arrive, and use guarded parking if you can; do not leave the bike unattended on a busy street. Remember that the shop does not provide an extra lock, so lock the steering. Our [guide to parking in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-motorbike-in-hanoi/) lists the general principles, and the same habits apply elsewhere.

If your trip continues to the island of Cat Ba, that is a separate question with its own ferry and rental issues, and the ride there adds to an already long day.

## Coming home, and the late-return cost

The shop's late-return terms are simple: VND 30,000 per hour for one to five hours late, and an extra rental day from six hours. That is not a reason to rush on a highway full of trucks. If you are running late, call the shop on the number above instead of pushing the speed. ## If you would rather not ride

A travel site notes the train from Hanoi to Hai Phong takes roughly two and a half to three hours, and coaches run frequently. For many visitors a bus or train is the safer and less tiring choice, and you can still explore Hanoi on a rental bike before and after. ## FAQ: Hanoi to Hai Phong by motorbike

### Can I ride a motorbike on the Hanoi to Hai Phong expressway?

No. Law 36/2024/QH15 prohibits motorcycles and mopeds on expressways. Use National Highway 5.

### How far is it?

Published figures range from about 105 to 122 km one way, depending on the start and end points.

### How long does it take on a motorbike?

One guide suggests about three hours on Highway 5. Allow more for stops and traffic.

### Is it safe for a beginner?

Not recommended. The route is long and truck-heavy, which makes it a poor choice for a new rider.

### Is Hai Duong part of Hai Phong now?

Yes, administratively, since 1 July 2025.

### Should I do it in a single day?

Only if you are an experienced rider, start early and set a firm turnaround time. Many travellers prefer an overnight stay or public transport.
