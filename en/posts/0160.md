Carrying a passenger in slow traffic is mostly a matter of making every input smaller and earlier. Take one passenger at most, brief them before the engine starts, let them hold the rear grab handle or your waist, and ask them to lean with you and keep their feet on the footrests even at a standstill. Then ride as if the scooter were heavier at the back, because it is: pull away gently, leave a longer gap, use both brakes together and avoid squeezing through gaps that a solo rider might try. Slow traffic is where a loaded scooter wobbles, drags a foot or gets bumped, so the habits below matter more at walking pace than at speed. This guide uses Honda's Vietnamese owner's manuals and rider training material, plus Vietnam's 2024 road traffic law, and it is general information rather than instruction in person.

## What a second person changes at low speed

A scooter is light and nimble. Add a passenger and the combined weight sits further back, the rider has less feel through the handlebar, and every movement of the passenger becomes a movement of the scooter. At speed this is a small effect. At walking pace it is large, because a two-wheeler stays upright through balance and small steering corrections, and a shifting passenger disturbs both.

Honda's rider training document for Vietnam lists the balance technique for slow, crowded or narrow roads: keep your head up and looking well ahead, use your hips and abdomen to keep the weight centred, and add a little throttle if the speed becomes so low that the scooter starts to fall away. With a passenger those corrections need more room and more notice, which is why the sections below focus on smoothness.

Slow traffic also brings more stopping and starting. Each pull-away and each stop is a moment when the passenger can lurch against you or put a foot down at the wrong time. Agreeing a few simple rules in advance prevents most of that.

## The thirty-second briefing before you move

Say these things out loud before you start the engine. Honda's Vision owner's manual describes the same points for the passenger, and a short spoken briefing is easier to follow than a long explanation at a junction.

| Ask your passenger to | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Wear a properly fastened helmet before getting on | Honda's manual stresses a standard helmet and fastened strap for rider and passenger |
| Hold the rear grab handle or your waist | Honda's manual names these two grips |
| Keep both feet on the footrests, even when you stop | Honda's manual says so explicitly, including when stationary |
| Lean the same way you lean in a turn | Honda's manual asks the passenger to lean with the rider |
| Sit still and avoid sudden movements | A shifting passenger unbalances the scooter at low speed |
| Keep bags in front of them or in the under-seat space, not swinging | A swinging bag changes the balance |
| Tap your shoulder or speak up if something feels wrong | Gives you an early warning to stop |

The Vision manual also says to avoid loose clothing that can catch on parts of the scooter. Long scarves, flapping coats and loose bag straps are worth tucking in before you set off. ## Getting on and off without a wobble

The safest moment to mount is when the scooter is held still. Honda's training document describes getting on with the rider holding the front brake, the scooter upright and the side stand handled deliberately, with a safety check behind before mounting. In practice that gives a clear order for two people:

1. The rider sits first, holds the front brake and keeps at least one foot on the ground, which is also how Honda's manual describes getting on from the left.
2. The rider confirms the side stand is up and the rear brake lock, if the scooter has one, is released.
3. The passenger mounts from the left once the rider says so, using the grab handle or the rider's shoulder for balance, and finds the footrests before the rider moves.
4. When stopping, the passenger stays seated until the rider says the stand is down and the scooter is steady.

Never let a passenger climb on while the rider is already rolling or while the rider's feet are off the ground. At a kerb, choose a spot where the passenger can step off onto the pavement rather than into the lane.

## Pulling away in stop-and-go traffic

A passenger's weight makes a scooter feel slower to respond, and the temptation is to open the throttle more to compensate. Resist it. Honda's manuals say to open the throttle slowly to accelerate and to release it to decelerate, and that is more important with two people on board.

A rhythm that works in slow traffic:

- **Start earlier, not harder.** Begin rolling a moment after the vehicle in front moves, then settle at a steady crawl.
- **Keep your hands light.** Hold the grips firmly but avoid jerking the throttle, because a twitch becomes a lurch with a second person aboard.
- **Look through the traffic, not at the bumper.** Honda's training advice is to look far ahead, which gives you time to coast.
- **Use the whole gap.** Do not close up on the vehicle ahead just to stop a few seconds later.

If the scooter hesitates when you open the throttle, ease on slowly rather than twisting harder. Sudden acceleration is a separate topic and a worthwhile one to read before a loaded ride.

## Braking: bigger gaps and both brakes

Honda's Vietnamese training document describes braking in three steps: close the throttle, apply both brakes together with gradually increasing force, with slightly more front than rear, then stop and put your foot down. It also warns that braking suddenly with the front brake can lift the rear wheel and braking suddenly with the rear brake can lock the wheel, so the smooth, combined approach is the rule.

With a passenger, allow more distance. The same document gives a two-second rule for the gap behind another vehicle and a four-second rule in poor visibility. The distances it lists are 11 m at 20 km/h and 17 m at 30 km/h for two seconds, doubling for four seconds. Those numbers are well beyond the gaps you will see in a Hanoi crowd, so treat them as the ideal and keep as much space as the traffic honestly allows.

Honda's Vision manual adds that the scooter has a combined brake that shares braking force between the wheels and that using both brakes together gives the best braking. A passenger who is warned in advance will be less likely to slide forward into your back. A useful habit is to say "stopping" aloud, or to agree a squeeze of the shoulder, before a firm stop.

## Feet, stands and standing still

In a queue it is natural for a rider to put a foot down and for the passenger to do the same. Honda's manual says the passenger's feet stay on the footrests even when the scooter is stopped. There are two reasons. A foot on the ground can be hit by another scooter, and a passenger who steps down shifts weight onto one side.

Rider technique matters too. Honda's training document says that after the scooter stops, the right foot keeps the brake applied and the left foot goes down. That leaves you free to hold the scooter upright if the passenger shifts.

When you park, ask the passenger to dismount before you put the scooter on its stand, unless the stand is already down on firm ground. Putting a scooter on the centre stand with a person still on it is hard and risky.

## Turns, U-turns and weaving

Slow turns with a passenger are where balance problems most often appear. Honda's manual asks the passenger to lean in the same direction as the rider. Honda's training document says to reduce speed before the turn, look where you are going and add throttle gently as you leave the bend.

Practical rules:

- **Slow before the turn, not in it.** Reduce speed by easing off the throttle and using the brakes before you start to lean.
- **Take wider arcs.** A scooter with a passenger needs more room to turn and a larger radius in an alley or at a junction.
- **Avoid tight U-turns on slopes or cambered roads.** If a U-turn is needed, find a wider spot.
- **Do not weave through gaps.** A passenger's knees, elbows and bags make you wider than you feel, so a gap that looks fine to the rider may not be wide enough for both.

## Load, tyres and what a scooter can carry

Passengers and luggage share the scooter's carrying limit. The Honda Vision manual in the version we checked lists a maximum carrying capacity of 170 to 176 kg depending on the variant, covering the rider, passenger, luggage and accessories together. It also lists a maximum of 15 kg of luggage, 10 kg in the under-seat box, 1.5 kg on the hook and front compartment and 3 kg on the rear grab handle. Other models, such as the Air Blade or the Lead, have their own figures, so ask the shop for the number that applies to the scooter you are given.

The manual's tyre table is worth noting. For the Vision, it gives the same cold-tyre pressures for a rider alone and for a rider with a passenger: 200 kPa front and 225 kPa rear. Other models differ, and the Lead manual lists a lower front pressure. Check the label or ask the shop rather than guessing, and let the tyres cool before checking, because Honda notes that warm tyres can show a higher reading than they really have.

## A brief note on the rules

As of October 2026, the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (No. 36/2024/QH15, effective 1 January 2025) says that the driver of a two-wheeled motorcycle or moped may carry one person, with a short list of exceptions that includes children under 12 years of age. It also says that people carried on the vehicle may not carry bulky objects, use umbrellas, cling to or push other vehicles, or stand on the seat or carrier. Both rider and passenger must wear a standard helmet with the strap fastened.

That is a summary of one article, not legal advice, and exceptions have conditions. If you are unsure, take one passenger and no more.

## When to stop, swap or call it off

Agree in advance how the passenger can stop the ride. A tap on the shoulder, a spoken word or a shout works better than leaning or pointing, because the rider cannot always see what the passenger sees. Stop, safely and off the road, if:

- the passenger feels unwell or frightened;
- the scooter feels unusually heavy or unstable at low speed;
- a bag or strap is loose;
- rain begins and the road turns slippery, as our guide to [riding in Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) explains.

## Pillion checklist

- Helmets fitted and strapped on both people.
- Loose clothing and bags secured.
- Passenger briefed on grip, feet, leaning and signals.
- Rider mounts first, holds the front brake and has a foot down.
- Rear brake lock released and side stand up.
- Pull away slowly, with a long gap ahead.
- Both brakes together, passenger's feet staying on the footrests.
- Slow, wide turns and no weaving.
- Load within the scooter's stated limits.

## FAQ: carrying a pillion passenger in slow traffic

### Can I carry two passengers on a scooter in Hanoi?

The 2024 road traffic law allows one passenger, with limited exceptions such as children under 12 years of age. For a typical rental trip, the safe answer is one passenger.

### Should my passenger hold me or the grab handle?

Honda's manual says either the rear grab handle or the rider's waist. Choose whichever the passenger can hold steadily without moving around.

### Should my passenger put their feet down when we stop?

No. Honda's manual says the passenger's feet stay on the footrests even when the scooter is stationary.

### Do tyre pressures change with a passenger?

On the Honda Vision in the manual we checked, the pressures are the same for one or two people, but other models differ. Check the label or ask the shop.

### Is slow traffic or fast traffic harder with a passenger?

Slow traffic is usually harder for balance, because a passenger's movement has more effect at low speed. Fast traffic is harder for braking distance.

### What if the scooter feels too heavy with two people?

Ask the shop before you leave about a different model. Do not try to compensate with more throttle or sharper turns.
