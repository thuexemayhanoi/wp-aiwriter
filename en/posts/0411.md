If you want to be on the road before the shop opens at 09:00, the straightforward plan is to collect the bike during opening hours the day before, agree the due time that results from that handover, park it somewhere guarded overnight and fuel it before you go to bed. Nguyen Ha is open 09:00 to 21:00 daily, a rental day is 24 hours from the handover time in the contract, and the shop does not publish an early-opening or out-of-hours handover service, so a dawn start has to be arranged around those facts. This guide shows how the 24-hour clock works in your favour, what an evening-before pickup involves, what to do about parking and fuel overnight, what to ask the shop to confirm and when it is better simply to start later.

## The facts that shape an early start

Three published facts set the boundaries.

- **Opening hours.** The shop at Alley 5 Nguyen Van Cu Street, Bo De Ward, Long Bien, is open 09:00 to 21:00 every day, and bookings are taken by phone or Zalo on 0334 699 969 or on WhatsApp at +84 334 699 969 within those hours.
- **The 24-hour day.** A rental day runs for 24 hours from the handover time written in the contract. A bike collected at 10:00 is due back at 10:00 the next day unless the contract says otherwise.
- **Collection at the shop.** Daily and short hires are collected at the shop. Delivery is offered for weekly and monthly rentals only, within 10 km of Hoan Kiem Lake at VND 50,000 per trip, and the time and place are agreed in advance. There is no airport service.

Nothing in the published terms promises a handover before 09:00. If you need one, ask the shop directly and treat it as a favour to be confirmed, not as something you can rely on. The rest of this guide assumes the standard hours.

## The clock works in your favour

Because the day is counted from the handover time and not from midnight, the due time always falls on the same clock time on the following day. If you collect between 09:00 and 21:00, the due time will also fall between 09:00 and 21:00, so the return lands inside opening hours automatically. That is a useful property for early starters.

Suppose you collect the bike in the late afternoon and leave at dawn the next morning. Your rental day began at the handover, so the 24 hours run through the night and the following day. A ride that starts early and returns in the afternoon of that following day sits comfortably inside the first rental day, with time to spare. This is an illustration of how the 24-hour rule works, not a promise of a particular schedule, so check the exact due time against your contract.

The other side of the coin is that you pay for the evening and the night as part of the same day. If you collect at 18:00 and start riding at 05:30, you have used the night for parking and not for riding, and the day you paid for ends at 18:00 the next day. For a long excursion with a late return, a handover very late in the day gives you a due time late in the next day, which is the best fit. Our guide to [choosing a rental period when plans may change](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/choosing-a-rental-period-when-your-travel-plans-may-change/) explains how to weigh a second day.

## Options for an early start

| Option | How it works | Points to check |
|---|---|---|
| Collect the evening before | Take the bike during opening hours the day before and ride at dawn | Overnight parking, fuel, light for the handover check |
| Collect at 09:00 and start later | Normal handover and a later departure | Shorter riding day, but the simplest plan |
| Ask about an early handover | Request a handover before opening | Not a published service; the shop decides |
| Delivery (weekly or monthly rentals only) | Bike brought to an agreed address within the distance limits | Time and place agreed in advance; fee applies |

Most day-trippers choose the first option. Longer renters who already hold a bike do not need any of these, because the bike is already with them.

## Doing the handover check in daylight

An evening-before pickup has one hazard: the light. The handover inspection of brakes, horn, headlight, indicators, mirrors, tyres and bodywork is easy in daylight and hard in the dark. Our [10-minute inspection checklist](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-scooter-inspection-checklist/) lists what to look at. Photograph any scratch or dent so the handover condition is clear to both sides.

Check the sunset time for your date and aim to collect well before it. If you can only collect after dark, ask the shop to let you check under good lighting, and test the headlight and indicators first, because you will depend on them at dawn. The shop confirms the final bike and its condition before you sign, so use that moment, not the roadside the next morning, to raise anything unusual.

## Overnight parking and security

A bike kept overnight needs a safe place. The shop does not provide an extra anti-theft lock, so plan to lock the steering and use guarded parking. Some hotels and homestays keep motorbikes in a lobby or a covered area, so ask yours when you book and ask whether the bike can be brought in before the entrance is closed. If you park on the street, use a guarded lot or an attendant who gives you a numbered ticket; our guide on [parking a motorbike in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-motorbike-in-hanoi/) describes how tickets and guarded lots work.

Do not leave the helmet on the seat or any bag on the bike overnight. Take the key and your documents with you, and do not leave the contract copy in a storage box.

## Fuel before dawn

Petrol bikes leave the shop with about 0.5 to 2 litres and should come back with about 0.5 to 1 litre, and an empty tank at return costs VND 20,000. Do not rely on finding an open station in the dark before the ride. The shop's rental guide notes that fuel stations are staffed and that you stay on the bike while the attendant fills it, and that cash is accepted. Opening hours of individual stations were not confirmed for this guide, so treat early opening as unknown and fill the tank in the evening, when you can see the gauge and the attendant can see you. Our guides on [fuel in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/fuel-in-hanoi-petrol-stations-rental-scooter/) and on [when to refuel during a day trip](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/when-to-refuel-a-manual-motorbike-during-a-day-trip/) give the detail.

## Riding in the early hours

Riding at dawn has its own conditions. Visibility can be low, the road surface can be damp and the first part of the day belongs to delivery vehicles, runners and market traffic. Switch on the headlight, wear the helmet properly fastened and keep to a speed that lets you stop within the distance you can see. Our guide to [riding at night in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-at-night-in-hanoi/) lists the main risks, and many of them apply before sunrise.

If your destination is outside the city, tell the shop when you book, so that it can suggest a suitable bike and confirm the return time. Motorbikes are not allowed on Vietnam's expressways, so a dawn route uses national roads; check the route before you start with our guide on [how to check if a navigation app suggests an expressway](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-check-if-a-navigation-app-suggests-an-expressway/). Longer routes such as [Hanoi to Ninh Binh by motorbike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-to-ninh-binh-by-motorbike/) are described in our route guides.

## What to agree with the shop

Before you pay, confirm these points and keep the answers in a message or on your copy of the contract.

1. **The handover time and the exact due time.** Both should be written in the contract.
2. **Whether an evening-before pickup suits your dates.** Availability is confirmed at booking, not promised in advance.
3. **What happens if the return runs close to closing time.** The shop closes at 21:00, so ask what is arranged for a late return.
4. **Whether the same bike will be held for your dawn start.** Ask for the model in advance.
5. **Delivery, if you rent weekly or monthly.** The address, the time and the fee.

The Civil Code, in Article 482, says that the return place for movable property is the lessor's residence or head office unless the parties agree otherwise. In practice, the shop's contract and published terms set the place and time, which is why it matters that they are written down. Late returns are charged at VND 30,000 per hour for the first 1 to 5 hours and one extra rental day from 6 hours, and advance notice does not waive the charge, so an early start that leads to a late finish is not free.

## FAQ: starting early with a rental bike

### Can I collect the bike before 09:00?

The published hours are 09:00 to 21:00, and an earlier handover is not a published service. Ask the shop; do not assume it.

### Can I collect it the evening before?

Within opening hours, yes in principle, subject to availability and to the contract. Your rental day then runs from that handover time.

### Do I pay for a second day if I collect the evening before?

A rental day is 24 hours from handover, so the evening and the night count towards the same day. A return later than the due time is charged as late.

### Will the shop deliver the bike to my hotel for an early start?

Delivery is for weekly and monthly rentals only, within 10 km of Hoan Kiem Lake, with the time and place agreed in advance. There is no airport service.

### What if petrol stations are closed at dawn?

Fill the tank the evening before. Station opening hours were not confirmed for this guide.

### Is a helmet included?

Yes, one basic helmet per rider, free of charge.
