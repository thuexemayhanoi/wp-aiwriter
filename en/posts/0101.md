If you already ride manual bikes at home, the best first-day plan in Hanoi is a staged loop that starts with ten minutes of slow drills beside the shop, moves to the ordinary streets of Long Bien, adds a short stretch of faster riding on the Red River dyke side and ends back at the shop for a debrief. Each stage has a pass mark, and you move on only if you meet it. The point is not distance. A rented Honda Wave, Yamaha Sirius or Honda Blade is a different machine from a clutch bike, and Hanoi traffic is a different environment from most home roads, so the first day should calibrate both before you head out of town. This guide lays out the stages, the drills for each one, the traffic habits worth copying from local riders and the signs that tell you to stop and shorten the day.

## What "experienced" does and does not carry over

Skill on a clutch motorcycle helps with balance, braking and reading a road, but the bikes you rent here are mostly semi-automatic. Honda Vietnam describes the Wave RSX FI gearbox as a mechanical four-speed "return" type, which means a pedal that clicks through the gears with no clutch lever. The Wave RSX FI manual adds that you should ease off the throttle before changing gear, that you cannot shift straight from fourth into neutral while moving and that you should keep your foot off the shift lever while riding.

So three habits from clutch bikes need adjusting: reaching for a lever that is not there, rolling the throttle through shifts and using the clutch to creep. Our guides to [riding a semi-automatic Honda Wave without a clutch](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-ride-a-semi-automatic-honda-wave/) and [Honda Wave gears: starting, shifting and stopping smoothly](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/honda-wave-gears-starting-shifting-and-stopping-smoothly/) explain the mechanics. This page is the practice plan that uses them.

What does carry over is the thing that matters most: you already know how a bike feels when it is about to do something you did not ask for.

## Before you set off

Spend five minutes on the bike before the route begins, with the shop's staff beside you.

- **Controls.** Find neutral, the horn, the indicator switch and both brake levers while the bike is stationary.
- **Mirrors and levers.** Adjust them to your height. Our guide to [adjusting mirrors on a Honda Wave](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-adjust-mirrors-on-a-honda-wave-rental/) shows how.
- **Brakes and lights.** Run the routine in our [10-minute inspection checklist](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-scooter-inspection-checklist/).
- **Starting method.** Ask whether the bike has an electric starter and a kick starter, and use the one the staff recommend.
- **Phone.** Mount it or keep it in a pocket, and save the shop's number: 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969).

Ask staff which streets near the shop are quiet and which to avoid at the hour you are riding. They know the local traffic, and their answer is more reliable than a general guide.

## The shape of the day

| Stage | What | Suggested time | Pass mark |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Low-speed drills on a quiet stretch near the shop | 15 to 20 minutes | Smooth starts, stops and U-turns without a foot down |
| 2 | Local streets around Nguyen Van Cu with junctions | 30 to 45 minutes | Calm gear changes and no hard braking in traffic |
| 3 | Dyke-road stretch or Chuong Duong approach, in daylight | 30 to 45 minutes | Steady speed, relaxed hands, correct lane |
| 4 | Return, park and debrief | 10 to 15 minutes | Notes ready for the shop |

The times are a suggestion for planning, not a promise. Experienced riders sometimes find stage 1 takes five minutes. Do not skip it anyway. Everything that goes wrong in stage 2 is easier to fix in stage 1.

## Stage 1: low-speed drills beside the shop

Find a quiet, level, clean stretch with no parked cars where you can pull a U-turn, and use it for six drills.

1. **Neutral to first.** Start the engine in neutral (Honda's manual says to check the neutral indicator), then select first and pull away with a small throttle opening. Repeat ten times until it feels smooth.
2. **Up through the gears.** Run from first to fourth and back down, easing the throttle each time before the shift. Listen for the engine note rather than looking at the dial.
3. **Slow-speed control.** Ride in first at walking pace, using the rear brake lightly to keep the bike steady, as you would on a clutch bike.
4. **Stopping from speed.** Brake with both controls from a moderate speed and stop in a straight line. Our guide to [front brake versus rear brake on small manual motorbikes](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/front-brake-versus-rear-brake-on-small-manual-motorbikes/) explains the feel.
5. **U-turn and figure-eight.** Do both ways, looking where you want to go.
6. **Park and kick the stand.** Park on the side stand and restart without the bike rolling.

**Pass mark:** you can start, stop and turn at walking pace without a foot down and without the bike lurching. If it lurches on gear changes, read our guide to [avoiding lurching when changing gears](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-avoid-lurching-when-changing-gears/) before moving on.

## Stage 2: the streets of Long Bien

Now ride the ordinary streets around the shop at traffic speed. Nguyen Van Cu Street itself is a roughly two-kilometre road running from the Cau Chui junction to the northern end of Chuong Duong Bridge, according to a local street directory, and its surroundings give you a typical mix of buses, trucks, delivery riders and school traffic in a place where the pace is lower than in the Old Quarter.

Pick a loop of ordinary streets, preferably a circuit you can ride clockwise and then counter-clockwise. Use it to practise the habits that make Hanoi riding work:

- **Predictability beats speed.** Hold a steady line and speed so that others can fit around you.
- **Look two vehicles ahead.** The brake lights of the vehicle in front of the one in front give you the earliest warning.
- **Cover the brakes.** Keep fingers and toe near both controls at junctions.
- **Signal early and leave the signal on long enough** for riders behind to read it.
- **Merge gradually.** Slide into gaps rather than cutting across.
- **Stay in second or third.** Fourth gear at city speed leaves no pickup for a gap.

Our guide to [crossing Hanoi intersections](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/crossing-hanoi-intersections/) explains how junction flow works and how to merge. If you must make a right turn across traffic, take the quieter junction rather than the biggest one.

**Pass mark:** you finish the loop with no hard braking, no stalled engine and no moment when you felt you were being pushed along. If you stall, read our note on [why a manual motorbike might stall at a junction](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/why-a-manual-motorbike-might-stall-at-a-junction/).

## Stage 3: a short faster stretch

Once stage 2 feels dull, add one section where you hold a steady speed in a higher gear. Two reasonable options on this side of the river are the Red River dyke roads and the approach to Chuong Duong Bridge, a road bridge 1,213 metres long with two lanes in each direction. Hanoi's tourism pages describe riding from Chuong Duong along the winding dyke towards the village of Bat Trang, and our guide to [the east bank and Bat Trang by scooter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/gia-lam-east-bank-bat-trang-by-scooter/) goes further.

Stay in daylight and well off the peak. Hanoi's own traffic notices around Chuong Duong Bridge have used roughly 06:00 to 09:30 and 16:00 to 19:30 as the rush-hour windows, so mid-morning or early afternoon is calmer. Do not ride onto an expressway or an elevated ring-road deck: Law 36/2024/QH15 bars motorcycles and mopeds from expressways, and our guide to [Hanoi's ring roads and highways](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-ring-roads-highways-motorbikes-allowed/) explains which parts are closed. If you meet a road signed against motorcycles, stop somewhere safe and re-plan.

Practise three things here: holding third or fourth gear at a steady speed, downshifting before a junction rather than braking and shifting at the last moment, and reading wind and surface on an exposed road. Obey the posted limits. Under Circular 38/2024/TT-BGTVT, effective 1 January 2025, the general limit for motor vehicles in built-up areas is 60 km/h on divided roads and 50 km/h on two-way roads without a median, and the signs on the day take priority.

**Pass mark:** relaxed hands, a steady line, no crowding by trucks and you are happy to ride the stretch twice.

## Stage 4: return and debrief

Return to the shop before you are tired. A first ride that ends with sore hands and a rushed return teaches the wrong lessons. When you arrive, park, switch off and make notes while they are fresh:

- Does the throttle feel sharp or soft?
- Are the lever positions right, or do they need adjusting?
- Did the bike ride true, or did it pull to one side?
- Is the brake bite where you expected it?
- Did anything rattle?

Tell the shop what you found. If a lever needs moving or the seat feels wrong, ask while the bike is in the shop's hands. If you liked the bike, that is a useful answer too, and if you did not, ask whether a different model is available. Do not assume one is.

## What to leave out on day one

A good first-day plan includes things you decide not to do:

- **A passenger.** Our guide to [riding a manual motorbike with a passenger in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-a-manual-motorbike-with-a-passenger-in-hanoi/) explains the changes in handling.
- **Luggage and a long day.** Learn the bike unloaded first.
- **Night riding.** See [riding at night in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-at-night-in-hanoi/) for when it is sensible.
- **Rain.** Our guide to [riding in Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) explains why wet roads need extra margin.
- **Hills and mountain roads.** Save them for day two or later.
- **A long ride out of town.** Decide that after the practice day, not before.

## Stop rules

End the plan early if any of these happens:

1. You stall more than twice in a row in traffic.
2. You feel tension in your shoulders or a tight grip that does not ease.
3. A near miss leaves you shaken.
4. The bike does something unexpected, such as a slipping gear or a noise you did not hear at pickup.
5. The weather turns or light fades.

For a slipping gear or a gear that will not engage, read our notes on [what a slipping gear feels like](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-a-slipping-gear-feels-like-on-a-rented-motorbike/) and what to do [if a rental motorbike will not shift gears](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-to-do-if-a-rental-motorbike-will-not-shift-gears/). ## FAQ: the first-day practice route

### How long should a first-day practice ride take?

Plan for two to three hours including the debrief. That is a planning suggestion, and fast learners may finish sooner.

### Do I need a quiet place to practise?

Yes, for stage 1. Ask the shop which stretches near it are quiet at the hour you are riding.

### Should I cross Long Bien Bridge on day one?

It is an option for later. Our guide to [the Long Bien and Red River first ride](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/long-bien-red-river-first-ride/) covers the crossing and notes that arrangements change.

### Is a manual bike harder in traffic than an automatic?

For a clutch-bike rider the semi-automatic is straightforward but different. Our article on [when a semi-automatic bike is easier than an automatic scooter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/when-a-semi-automatic-bike-is-easier-than-an-automatic-scooter/) compares them.

### What should I carry on the first day?

Phone, wallet, water and the documents the shop asked for. Leave heavy bags at the hotel.
