On an automatic scooter, use the side stand for short stops and quick parking, and the centre stand when you want the bike upright on both wheels, on firm level ground, for longer stops or for the checks the shop asks you to do. For the side stand: switch off, flip the stand down with your foot, lean the scooter slowly to the left until its weight rests on the stand, then turn the handlebar fully to the left and lock the steering. For the centre stand: stand on the left, hold the left grip and the rear grab rail, press the stand's foot down with your right foot and pull the scooter up and back. This guide covers both, the engine cut-off that surprises new riders, how to get off a stand safely and what to check at handover. It is general information, so follow the manual or the shop for your exact bike.

## Two stands, two different jobs

Most automatic scooters rented in Hanoi, such as the Honda Vision, Air Blade, Click or Lead, carry a side stand. Many, though not every variant, also carry a centre stand. Honda's Vietnamese manual for the Lead 125, for example, shows a centre stand on the listed variants and notes that some variants do without it. Whether the bike you are handed has one is something to look at before you ride away, because the answer changes how you park it and how you handle a flat tyre or a stuck rear wheel.

The two stands do different jobs:

- **Side stand:** holds the scooter leaning to the left, on one wheel pair and the stand's foot. It is quick and suits short stops, but it is the less stable of the two and it depends on the ground being firm and level.
- **Centre stand:** lifts the rear wheel off the ground and holds the scooter upright. It is steadier, keeps the rear wheel free to turn and is the stand Honda's manuals use when a procedure needs the rear wheel clear, such as inspecting the rear brake lever.

If the scooter has both, a sensible habit is to use the side stand for a coffee stop and the centre stand for anything longer, on ground you trust.

## How to put a scooter on the side stand

Honda's owner's manuals give a consistent short sequence, and the Vietnamese manuals for the Lead 125 and Vario 125 cover the first four steps below:

1. **Switch the engine off.** Do not leave it running while the stand is moving.
2. **Push the side stand down** with your foot until it is fully out and its foot sits flat on the ground.
3. **Lean the scooter slowly to the left** until the whole weight rests on the stand. Let go only when it is clearly stable.
4. **Turn the handlebar fully to the left.** Honda's Winner R manual adds that turning the handlebar to the right reduces stability and can make the bike fall over.
5. **Turn the ignition to Lock** and remove the key, or switch the smart key system off as the manual describes, and take it with you.

Mount and dismount from the left side, which is also the side the stand leans toward. Do not sit on a scooter that is on its side stand and rock it about, because the stand can slip.

### Why the steering goes left

With the front wheel turned to the left, the weight of the scooter pushes the stand foot against the ground and the bike leans back onto it. With the wheel straight or turned right, a small push can tip the bike off the stand. It also helps a little when a wind gust or a passing bag catches the scooter.

## The engine cut-off that surprises new riders

Many Honda scooters have a side stand ignition cut-off. Honda's Air Blade manual for Vietnam states that the engine cannot start while the side stand is down and that lowering the stand while the engine is running will shut the engine off. Other Honda scooter manuals say the same.

This is a safety feature that stops a rider moving off with the stand still down. In daily use it explains three situations people often misread as a fault:

- The engine will not start after you step on the starter, because the stand is still down.
- The engine dies at a standstill as you lower the stand, which is normal.
- Idling Stop scooters that stopped the engine at a red light will not restart if you drop the side stand in the meantime. The Honda PCX manual explains that the engine does not restart even if the throttle is opened once the side stand is moved.

Before riding off, you can test the cut-off with staff beside you. Raise the stand and start the engine. Lower the stand and the engine should stop. If the scooter keeps running with the stand down, tell the shop and ask for another bike.

## How to put a scooter on the centre stand

The centre stand takes technique rather than strength, and the commonest mistake is trying to lift the bike with your arms. Honda's Metropolitan manual describes the method: stand on the left side of the vehicle, hold the left handle grip and the rear grab rail, press down on the tip of the centre stand with your right foot and, at the same moment, pull the scooter up and back.

In practice:

1. Stop the engine and pick ground that is firm and level.
2. Stand beside the scooter on the left, with your feet apart.
3. Hold the left grip and the grab rail behind the seat.
4. Place your right foot on the stand's tip, which is a flat tab at the end of the stand.
5. Press down with the foot and pull back and up in one smooth movement. Use your body weight, not your arms.
6. When the stand locks, the rear wheel is off the ground. Check that both stand feet are flat.

On a heavier scooter such as a Lead or Air Blade, a sharper pull is not needed. If the scooter fights you, check that the side stand is raised and the ground is even, and ask staff to show you once.

### Taking the scooter off the centre stand

Honda's PCX manual describes moving off in a specific order: push the vehicle forward off the centre stand, squeeze the rear brake lever, keep the throttle closed and make sure both the side stand and the centre stand are up. Then mount from the left, keeping at least one foot on the ground, and release the brake lever. Pushing forward matters, because the stand folds up as the scooter rolls off it, and the brake lever stops the scooter rolling further than you intend.

## Choosing the ground

Both stands are only as good as the surface beneath them. Look for:

- **Hard, level ground.** A slight slope that does not bother a car can make a scooter on its side stand roll or tip. If you must park on a slope, our guide on [safely parking a semi-automatic motorbike on a slope](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/safely-parking-a-semi-automatic-motorbike-on-a-slope/) explains how facing and gear choice help, though automatic scooters have no gear to hold them.
- **No soft patches.** Sand, loose gravel, wet soil and broken paving let the stand foot sink.
- **No gaps.** Do not park with the stand foot over a gutter grate, a manhole or a kerb edge.
- **Shade, if you can.** A hot sun softens asphalt, and a stand foot can sink into it over several hours.
- **Space around the scooter.** In crowded lanes bikes get brushed and knocked, so leave room and park where an attendant can see it. Our [guide to parking a motorbike in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-motorbike-in-hanoi/) covers guarded lots and tickets.

## A one-minute stand check at the shop

Before you accept the scooter, spend one minute on the stands with staff beside you:

| What to check | What good looks like | If not |
|---|---|---|
| Side stand spring | Stand snaps back firmly when you lift it | Report a weak spring before riding |
| Side stand foot | Flat, not bent, not worn through | Ask for another bike |
| Cut-off | Engine stops when the stand goes down | Report it |
| Centre stand, if fitted | Folds up fully and stays up | Ask the shop to look at it |
| Rubbing noise | No scrape on corners | Report it |
| Ground clearance | Stand does not hang down when raised | Report it at once |

A side stand that sags down a little after you ride off can touch the road at the first corner. That is why the spring check matters. Our [10-minute rental scooter inspection](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-scooter-inspection-checklist/) places the stand check in the full routine.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | What happens | Better habit |
|---|---|---|
| Riding off with the stand down | Engine cuts out or the stand scrapes | Look down and flip it up before moving |
| Handlebar straight or turned right | Scooter more likely to tip | Steering fully left |
| Parking on a kerb edge | Stand foot slips | Park on the flat |
| Lifting with the arms on the centre stand | Strain and an awkward drop | Foot down, pull back and up |
| Leaving a heavy bag on the hook | Bike tips when you walk away | Take it with you |
| Parking beside a moving lane | Scooter is knocked | Choose a protected spot |
| Rocking a scooter on its stand | Stand slips | Stay off it |

## Locking up

Standing the scooter safely is one part and securing it is another. Our shop does not provide an extra lock, so the guidance is to lock the steering and park in guarded places. Turn the handlebar fully left, turn the ignition to Lock and keep the key or smart key with you. A scooter with a smart key system can be started by anyone carrying the fob within range, according to Honda's Vietnamese manual, so keep the fob away from the bike when you leave it.

## When a stand is the wrong choice

Some situations call for walking away from the idea of a stand:

- **Heavy rain on a sloping street,** where water runs under the stand foot.
- **Uneven pavements around the Old Quarter,** where the best option is another spot. Our guide to [riding in the Old Quarter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-old-quarter/) explains why walking in can be easier.
- **Loaded scooters.** A heavy bag in the front hook can pull the bike off the stand, so remove it first.

## FAQ: scooter side stand and centre stand

### Which stand should I use most of the time?

The side stand, for short stops. Use the centre stand when you want the scooter steadier or need the rear wheel off the ground.

### Why does the engine stop when I put the stand down?

Many Honda scooters have a side stand cut-off, so the engine stops when the stand is lowered and will not start while it is down.

### Why must the handlebar be turned left?

It keeps the scooter leaning onto the stand. Honda's manual warns that turning the bar right reduces stability.

### Can a scooter without a centre stand be parked safely?

Yes, on the side stand, on firm level ground, with the steering turned left and locked.

### Is it hard to lift a scooter onto its centre stand?

It is a technique, not a strength test. Press down with your foot and pull back and up smoothly.

### What if the stand will not stay up?

Do not ride. Ask for another bike or a mechanic to look at the spring.
