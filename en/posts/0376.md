When daylight is short, plan the route in layers and put the demanding roads in the middle of the day. Start with the one stop you really want, then add a core loop that ends well inside Hanoi's built-up area, then list optional extras you will drop if you are behind. Keep unfamiliar, hilly or unlit stretches for the bright hours between mid-morning and mid-afternoon, and leave the last hour for roads you know. In Hanoi the sun sets around 5:15 to 5:45 pm from October to December, so a rental that starts at the shop's 09:00 opening has about eight usable hours. This guide explains how to read those numbers, order the stops, deal with low-sun glare and dusk traffic, and shape a route that still feels full. It is general planning advice for a scooter or other rental bike, not a fixed itinerary.

## What "limited daylight" means in Hanoi

Hanoi sits at about 21 degrees north, so the days never get as short as in Europe, but they shrink noticeably between early autumn and the December solstice. These figures come from timeanddate.com and time.now for 2026.

| Date | Sunrise | Sunset | Day length |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 October | 5:47 am | 5:44 pm | about 11 h 56 min |
| 1 November | 5:59 am | 5:21 pm | about 11 h 22 min |
| 1 December | 6:16 am | 5:14 pm | about 10 h 57 min |
| 22 December | 6:29 am | 5:20 pm | about 10 h 51 min |

Timeanddate notes that the solstice day is about two hours and 33 minutes shorter than the day at the June solstice, so a summer day in Hanoi runs to more than 13 hours of daylight. The point is that the same route can be comfortable in June and cramped in December, so plan by the season, not by the route.

Two details matter more than the day length itself. First, the sun sets at its earliest in early December, not on the shortest day, so the tightest evenings come a few weeks before the solstice. Second, the light does not vanish at sunset. Timeanddate lists civil twilight as ending about 22 to 23 minutes later on the dates above, and after that you are riding on streetlights and your own headlight.

## Count the light you can actually use

A rental day begins when the shop opens at 09:00. If you are renting by the week or month and already have the bike, you can start earlier, but a one-day renter starts at 09:00 and should ask the shop whether an earlier handover is possible rather than assume it.

From 09:00 to sunset, the usable window runs to about 8 hours 45 minutes on 1 October, 8 hours 20 minutes on 1 November and 8 hours 15 minutes on 1 December. Take off the time to leave the city, queue at a petrol station and eat. Take off a buffer of about 45 minutes before sunset, so the final stretch is not a race. That leaves something like six hours of real riding and stopping, which is more than enough for a good local loop but not for a long rural run.

Our guide to [riding at night in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-at-night-in-hanoi/) explains what changes after dark, and it is the reason to protect that buffer.

## Build the route in three layers

1. **The anchor.** One place or stretch that makes the day worthwhile, such as a lakeside loop, a craft village or a bridge at golden hour. Decide its position in the day first.
2. **The core.** The roads that link the anchor to the start and finish. These should be roads you can read easily on a map and should never take you deep into unfamiliar territory late in the day.
3. **The extras.** Optional stops, such as a second cafe, a detour or a viewpoint, that you add only if you are ahead of time.

If you are running late, you drop the extras and keep the anchor and the core. That is easier than dropping a main goal, because you have already decided what is optional.

## Put the hard roads in the bright middle

Light is not equal across the day. Early in the morning the sun is low and the roads are busy with school and work traffic. In the afternoon it is low again, and in cool months it sets quickly. The most useful light is in the middle. This is when you want the stretches that demand attention:

- **Hill and mountain roads,** where a bend, a pothole or a truck is harder to read in poor light.
- **Narrow rural lanes** with mixed traffic and few street lamps.
- **Unfamiliar junction clusters** on the edge of the city.
- **Anywhere you might need to stop and check a map.**

Leave the end of the day for familiar, lit streets and a short run back to the shop. Our guide to [manual bikes and steep mountain roads](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/manual-bikes-and-steep-mountain-roads-when-to-choose-another-vehicle/) explains why mountain roads suit neither a late start nor a small engine.

## Think about the sun's direction

Timeanddate's data gives the direction of the sun, and it matters on a bike. In November and December the sun rises in the east-southeast, around 105 to 115 degrees on the compass, and sets in the west-southwest, around 247 to 255 degrees. At solar noon in early December it is still 47 degrees above the horizon, and in the afternoon it drops quickly into the southwest.

That has practical consequences:

- **Riding west or south-west in the last hour of light puts a low sun in your eyes.** A visor, sunglasses or a cap helps, but glare is still a hazard, especially with wet or dusty road surfaces.
- **A low sun behind you lights the road well,** so a homeward leg heading east in the late afternoon is easier than one heading west.
- **Tall buildings and hills throw long shadows,** and the light on a road can drop much sooner than the sunset time.

Sunrise-sunset.org notes that behind hills, mountains or buildings the sun appears later than the table suggests, and the reverse applies at the end of the day, so in a valley or beside a ridge you lose light earlier. When you plan a route with a late leg, look at the map for hills on its western side.

## Choose loops or out-and-backs on purpose

On a short day, a loop tends to use the light better than an out-and-back, because you see new things on the way home and do not retrace the same stretch. The trade-off is that a loop commits you to its far side. If something goes wrong halfway round, going forward may be as far as going back.

An out-and-back gives you an easy exit at every point. If you are behind, you turn at the next good place. You can also build exits into a loop: note, for each junction, the quickest road back.

A good short-day compromise is a loop that stays inside or near the built-up area, such as one around West Lake or along the river, where you are never far from a main road.

## Plan exits and shortcuts before you need them

For each leg, write down one exit. An exit is a road that leads back toward the city faster than the planned route, ideally onto a major street rather than a lane. Mark it on your map app before you leave, because deciding while the light fades is much harder.

If you rely on a navigation app, check its suggested route before you start. Some apps choose faster roads that may not suit a small bike. Our guide to [checking whether a navigation app suggests an expressway](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-check-if-a-navigation-app-suggests-an-expressway/) explains how, and [Hanoi's ring roads and highways](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-ring-roads-highways-motorbikes-allowed/) lists which ones allow motorbikes.

## Dusk is also a traffic problem

Visibility is only half of the dusk problem. Roads in the capital often fill in the late afternoon and early evening as people go home, which on a short winter day coincides with the loss of light. That is a general observation, not a measured figure, but it is a good reason to be back in your final, familiar stretch before the busiest time. Our guide to [crossing Hanoi intersections](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/crossing-hanoi-intersections/) explains how traffic merges and flows.

Honda's UK safety advice for night riding is useful here: adjust your speed to what you can see, take extra care at intersections and curves where obstacles are harder to spot, and keep your visor and lights clean.

## Check the bike for the light you will have

Before you leave, spend a few minutes on the points that matter when light is short. Switch on the headlight and check that it works on both settings, check the brake light and indicators, wipe the mirrors and any visor, and make sure the horn works. Our guide to a [10-minute rental scooter inspection](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-scooter-inspection-checklist/) lists the full set. If anything is faulty, ask the shop to fix it or change the bike.

Carry a charged phone and consider a small battery pack, because a navigation app and a torch both drain it faster at dusk.

## Weather also shortens the day

In winter, cloud and drizzle can make the light dim well before sunset. One local tourism guide lists January as the cloudiest month, with about 50 hours of sunshine in the whole month, and describes light drizzle. Treat that as a general climate note and check the forecast on the day. A grey afternoon can feel like early evening, so build in more margin on a cloudy day.

## When to choose a different day

If your only free day is short on light, consider a different type of trip: stay in the city, take a shorter loop, or move a longer ride to a day with more daylight. ## FAQ: planning a route with limited daylight

### How much daylight do I have in December?

About 10 hours 51 minutes to 10 hours 57 minutes in Hanoi, with sunset around 5:14 to 5:25 pm.

### Should I avoid riding after sunset?

Where you can, yes. Plan to be on a familiar, lit road by sunset, with a buffer.

### Is a loop or an out-and-back better?

A loop uses light well but commits you. An out-and-back gives easy exits. Near the city, a loop is fine.

### What should I check on the bike?

Headlight, brake light, indicators, mirrors and horn, and ask the shop to fix anything faulty.

### Does the sun's direction matter?

Yes. A low sun in the southwest in late afternoon can dazzle you on a westbound road.

### Can I start before 09:00?

The shop opens at 09:00. Ask whether an earlier handover is possible, and do not assume.
