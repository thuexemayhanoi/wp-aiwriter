To check a 50cc bike's mirrors and horn, sit on the bike with the engine running, set the mirror glass until you can see the lane behind you past your own shoulder, wiggle each mirror stem to confirm it holds its position, then press the horn button once, briefly, and listen for a clear, steady note. If a mirror is loose, cracked badly, points at the sky or shows only your arm, or if the horn is silent, weak or stuck, ask the shop to fix it or swap the bike before you leave. A 50cc moped is the slowest vehicle in most Hanoi streams, so what you can see behind you and what other riders can hear matter more than they would on a faster bike.

## Why these two checks matter more on a slow bike

A 50cc moped in Hanoi spends much of its time being passed. Faster scooters, delivery riders and buses close up from behind, often with little warning, and the person on the moped is the one who has to read that traffic and stay predictable. A mirror gives you the rear picture before you move sideways to avoid a pothole, pull out of a parking spot or turn into an alley. A horn does the opposite job: it tells the rider about to cut across you, or the pedestrian stepping off a kerb, that you are there.



Honda makes the same point in its August 2026 guidance on pre-ride inspection: the owner's manual makes the check the user's responsibility, because routine checks help prevent dangerous malfunctions. Neither mirrors nor horn is difficult to test, and both are cheap to repair, which is why a rental shop is the right place to find a problem rather than the middle of a junction. The wider routine is in our [10-minute rental scooter inspection](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-scooter-inspection-checklist/). This guide covers only the mirrors and the horn, and applies to the Cub-style 50cc bikes and the twist-and-go 50cc scooters that Nguyen Ha lists for rent, with the exact bike confirmed when you book.

## What the Vietnamese rules say about both items

As of October 2026, the rules on equipment and horn use come from three official documents. Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, in force since 1 January 2025, lists a fine for riding a motorbike or moped that has no horn, or whose horn or left-hand mirror is missing or does not work. The wording concerns the mirror on the rider's left, which means a moped does not have to carry two. Vietnam's mirror standard, QCVN 28:2024/BGTVT, as summarised by the legal portal Thu Vien Phap Luat, asks for at least one mirror on the left of the rider on two- and three-wheeled mopeds.

The third document is Law 36/2024/QH15, whose Article 21 limits horn use. A rider may sound the horn to warn of a danger or to signal an overtaking move, and must not honk repeatedly, use a horn of the wrong volume, or use it between 22:00 and 05:00 in residential areas and near medical facilities. A sign marked P.128 bans the horn on a stretch of road. None of this needs memorising; the practical point is that a working horn and a working left mirror are a basic expectation, and that restraint with the horn is part of riding well. ## Checking the mirrors, step by step

Do this on the stand or with the bike held upright, engine off or idling, with the staff member nearby.


1. **Look at the mounting.** Each mirror sits on a stem that screws or clamps into the handlebar or fairing. Take hold of the housing and try to twist it. A good mirror turns when you push firmly and then stays where you put it. A mirror that flops, droops back to a different angle or rattles when you press the handlebar is loose.

2. **Check the glass.** Look for a crack, a missing piece or a spider-web pattern. A mirror with a cloudy or heavily scratched surface is also poor, because glare at night turns it into a blur. Small marks at the edge are normal on a used bike.

3. **Check the stem and housing.** A bent stem, a mirror cover held on with tape or a housing with a gap where the glass should sit are all signs of an earlier drop. They are not automatically unsafe, but ask about them.

4. **Sit properly and adjust.** Sit on the seat with both hands on the grips, back straight, as you will ride. Move each mirror until you see the road behind and to the side, with a sliver of your own shoulder or elbow in the edge of the glass, not a view dominated by your arm.

5. **Look both sides.** The standard asks for one on the left, but a mirror on each side gives a better rear picture when a vehicle is overtaking on the right.

6. **Test the hold.** With the engine running, rock the bike slightly and watch the image. If it shakes so much that you cannot read it, the stem or its mounting needs attention.

For a Honda Wave, the steps are explained in more detail in [how to adjust mirrors on a Honda Wave rental](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-adjust-mirrors-on-a-honda-wave-rental/), but a 50cc moped usually has smaller mirrors, so be ready for a narrower picture.

## What a good mirror setting looks like

Set the mirrors so the picture is useful at the speed you will actually travel. On a moped that is slow, so the lane behind you is the part of the road that matters. Aim for three things: the lane directly behind you, the lane on your side that a passing rider would use, and a small piece of your own body as a reference point.

Do not tilt the mirrors upwards to see the far distance. A mirror aimed too high shows the sky and the tops of buildings and hides the scooter a metre behind you. Do not aim it at your own back either. Many riders find that a mirror turned slightly outwards gives the best view of the lane beside them, with a short head turn covering the blind spot behind the shoulder.

Whatever the setting, a mirror does not replace a head check. Look over your shoulder before changing lanes or pulling away from the kerb, especially on a bike with small mirrors.

## Checking the horn

The horn test takes seconds, but it must be done properly.


- **Turn the key or switch to the on position.** On some bikes the horn works only with the ignition on, and on many bikes it is happier with the engine running, because the battery is under less strain.

- **Press the button once, briefly.** Give a short tap, not a long blast. A good horn gives a clear, steady, reasonably loud note straight away.

- **Listen to the sound.** A weak, wheezy, buzzing or cracked noise means a poor connection, a flat battery or a tired horn. A sound that comes late, or only when you press harder, points to a worn switch.

- **Check the button's return.** The button should spring back immediately. A horn that keeps sounding or sticks is a hazard and an annoyance, and the shop should fix it before you ride.

- **Try it with the engine idling.** On a 50cc scooter the horn can sound noticeably quieter when the battery is low.

- **Find the button without looking.** It is usually on the left handlebar switch cluster, beside the turn signal switch. Honda Vietnam's Wave Alpha owner's manual lists the horn button among the handlebar switches, though that bike is a different model from a 50cc Cub or scooter, so the layout may differ. Learn where it is before you pull away.

If the horn is silent, check the shop's response. A missing horn is a reason to ask for a different bike, since it is both a safety tool and a basic equipment item under the decree above.

## How to use the horn on a 50cc moped

A short tap is the Hanoi norm. Riders use it to say "I am here", not "get out of my way". A quick double tap at a blind alley mouth, before overtaking a slower rider or when approaching a pedestrian who has not seen you, is usually enough. A long blast tends to startle people and does not make a gap appear.

Use it with the law in mind. Do not honk continuously, do not honk at night in residential areas, and watch for the P.128 sign. Hospitals, schools and quiet lanes are places where restraint is expected. If you are riding near the Old Quarter at night, a headlight flash or slow, careful riding does more than the horn. Our [night riding guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-at-night-in-hanoi/) covers the lights side.

A moped's own character matters too. A light 50cc bike accelerates gently, so a rider cannot rely on speed to leave a bad situation. Hearing a horn behind you is a cue to hold your line and keep calm, not to swerve.

## Problems you may find and what to ask

| What you notice | Likely meaning | What to ask the shop |
|---|---|---|
| Mirror turns by itself | Loose stem or worn joint | Tighten it or swap the mirror |
| Mirror shows only your arm | Needs adjusting, or stem is bent | Adjust it; if the picture stays poor, ask for another mirror |
| Cracked or missing glass | Earlier drop or damage | Replace it, or choose another bike |
| Mirror vibrates and blurs at idle | Loose mounting | Re-seat or tighten |
| Horn silent | Wiring, switch or battery | Ask for another bike, or a repair before leaving |
| Horn weak or crackly | Low battery or tired horn | Ask for it to be checked |
| Horn sticks on | Faulty switch | Have it repaired before you ride |
| Horn works only sometimes | Loose connector | Ask for a check |

If a mirror is slightly misaligned but otherwise firm, you can adjust it yourself. If something is bent, cracked or loose, leave it to the shop.

## Checking again during a longer rental

On a weekly or monthly rental, mirrors loosen with vibration and the horn can weaken if the battery ages, so give both a quick check at the start of each week. Mirrors also move after the bike is knocked while parked, so look at them each time you get on. ## FAQ: mirrors and horn on a 50cc rental

### Does a 50cc bike need two mirrors?

Under the standard summarised by Thu Vien Phap Luat, a moped must have at least one mirror on the rider's left. Two is better for visibility, and many bikes come with a pair.

### Can I ride with a cracked mirror?

A crack that spoils the picture is a reason to ask for a replacement. Tiny marks at the edge are usually fine, but ask the shop.

### How loud should the horn be?

Clear and steady, loud enough for a pedestrian a few metres away to notice. A weak or crackly note needs checking.

### When am I not allowed to use the horn?

Law 36/2024/QH15 prohibits continuous honking, an improper volume, and use between 22:00 and 05:00 in residential areas and near medical facilities. A P.128 sign also bans the horn locally.

### What if the horn stops working during the rental?

Stop somewhere safe, check that the key is on and the button is not stuck, and call the shop.

### Should I adjust the mirrors myself?

Yes, the angle is yours to set before you ride. Anything bent, cracked or loose should be left to the shop.
