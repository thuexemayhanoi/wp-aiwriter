The ride from central Hanoi to Duong Lam ancient village is about 44 km one way, according to Vietnam's national tourism authority, and it can be done on a rental motorbike in a single long day if you accept two things. First, you must choose a road that motorbikes may use, because the quickest-looking line on a map often follows a car-only carriageway. Second, you need to treat the day as a ride with a village at the far end, not a village visit with a ride attached. Vietnam Airlines' travel guide names two motorbike routes, one by Thang Long Avenue and Route 21 and one by Highway 32 through Son Tay. This guide compares them, explains which roads to avoid, and builds a timetable around a shop that opens at 09:00.

## Distance, time and who this ride suits

Sources disagree slightly on the distance because they measure from different starting points. The national tourism authority puts the village 44 km from the centre of Hanoi, a Hanoi city tourism page says about 44 km to the west, and other travel pages give 45 to 50 km. From the east bank of the Red River, where our shop is, expect to add several kilometres and a river crossing.

On a small rental motorbike that means a ride of roughly 90 km for the round trip, plus movement around the village. That is comfortable for a rider who has already spent a few days on Hanoi's streets and who is used to the bike. It is a poor choice for someone on their first morning with a scooter, because most of the effort is not in the distance but in the traffic at both ends.

As a planning allowance rather than a quoted figure, allow around two hours each way from the east bank in daytime traffic, and revise it after the first half hour. If that sounds too long, the closer rides in our [Hanoi to Ba Vi guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-to-ba-vi-by-motorbike/) and our [Tam Dao guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-to-tam-dao-by-motorbike/) are alternatives in a similar spirit.

## Route A: Thang Long Avenue, the Hoa Lac junction and Route 21

Vietnam Airlines describes the first motorbike route as Thang Long Avenue, then the Hoa Lac intersection, then Route 21, through Son Loc to Duong Lam. A Klook guide gives the same idea in plainer words: follow Thang Long Avenue, turn right at the Hoa Lac junction, then keep to road 21 until you reach the old village.

The catch is the word "avenue". Thang Long Avenue has a fast central carriageway that is closed to motorbikes and parallel service roads that are open to them. A rider has to use the service roads, which have more junctions, occasional works and more stop-start driving than the main lanes. Our guide to [which ring roads and highways allow motorbikes](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-ring-roads-highways-motorbikes-allowed/) explains how the two layers work.

Pick this route if you like a wide road with clear signs and are happy to read your map carefully near Hoa Lac, where several roads fork.

## Route B: Highway 32 through Son Tay, then Route 21

The second route in the same guide is Highway 32 to Son Tay, then Route 21 to the village. A state news report on the Son Tay citadel describes Highway 32 as the road that takes you west from Hanoi for about 45 km to Son Tay town. Road listings place the highway's start near the Mai Dich and Cau Giay side of the city and trace it through Hoai Duc and Dan Phuong before Son Tay, which fits that description.

This route passes through ordinary towns, with shops, bus stops, traffic lights and schools. That makes it slower than Route A on paper, but easier to understand: there is one main road, plenty of places to stop for water, and a town of real size, Son Tay, at the end where you can eat, refuel and check the bike.

Pick this route if you prefer a continuous road with places to stop, or if you may want to add the Son Tay citadel to the day.

## Which route for which rider

| Your situation | Likely better choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Confident, wants wide roads | Route A | Fewer small towns, but read the map at Hoa Lac |
| Wants to stop often | Route B | Towns and fuel stations along the way |
| Plans to see the Son Tay citadel too | Route B | The citadel is in the town you pass through |
| Nervous about junctions | Route B | One main road, easier to follow |
| Returning late in the afternoon | Either, but the one you know | Do not try a new road in the dusk |

Whichever you choose, ride it once in daylight before you need to return along it in fading light. Our guide to [riding at night in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-at-night-in-hanoi/) explains why unfamiliar roads after dark are a bad plan on a rental.

## The roads that are not for you

Motorbikes and mopeds are not allowed on expressways in Vietnam. This is set out in Article 25 of the Road Traffic Order and Safety Law, Law 36/2024/QH15, which took effect on 1 January 2025 and lists motorcycles and mopeds among the vehicles that may not use an expressway. Hanoi has several of them, and a navigation app set to "car" or left on default can offer one as the quickest option.

On a ride to the west, the practical dangers are the car-only central lanes of Thang Long Avenue and the elevated decks of the ring roads. Before you start, check the route type in your map app. Our short guide on [how to check if a navigation app suggests an expressway](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-check-if-a-navigation-app-suggests-an-expressway/) shows what to look for. If a ramp is signed for cars only, do not follow a car up it, even if the turn is easy to take.

## A timetable that starts at 09:00

Most sample timetables for this trip begin before dawn. A rental shop that opens at 09:00 cannot offer that, so build the day around the opening time. If you want an earlier handover, ask the shop in advance rather than assuming one is possible.

| Time | What to do |
|---|---|
| 09:00 | Collect the bike, inspect it, confirm the fuel level |
| 09:30 | Leave, with a full tank if you can fill up before the city edge |
| About 11:30 | Arrive near the village, park at the edge and walk in |
| 11:30 to 14:00 | Village gate, lanes, one or two relics, lunch |
| 14:00 to 15:00 | Rest, drink, check the bike and the weather |
| By 15:00 | Start back, aiming to be off the unfamiliar road before the light fades |
| Evening | Return the bike before 21:00, the shop's closing time |

Vietnam Airlines suggests visiting in the early morning, between 07:00 and 09:00, or the late afternoon, between 15:00 and 17:00, because midday sun makes walking less comfortable. A 09:00 pickup rules out the early slot, so the useful lesson is the second half: do not plan to walk the lanes at noon in summer if you can help it, and keep some time for the shade of the afternoon.

## What is at the end of the road

Duong Lam is not one village but a group of neighbouring ones. The tourism authority's description names Mong Phu, Dong Sang, Cam Thinh, Doai Giap and Cam Lam as the villages forming the recognised ancient area, with Mong Phu the largest. Its main features are the laterite houses, a gate, a well, a banyan and a communal house. The commune also contains the Phung Hung Temple, the Ngo Quyen Tomb, the Giang Van Minh Temple and Mia Pagoda.

Laterite is the key to the look of the place. The walls, gates and wells are built of a rust-brown stone that is soft when cut and hardens in the air. The Mong Phu gate is the best-known photographic spot; sources differ on exactly how old it is, with one Hanoi culture department page tracing it to the Le dynasty in the sixteenth century and a Vietnam Airlines guide describing it as over three centuries old. Mia Pagoda, in Dong Sang village, is described in the same sources as holding around 280 or more Buddha statues.

The village is lived in, so the standard advice applies: park at the edge, walk the lanes and ask before entering a courtyard. The village's own website lists an electric car service for visitors who do not want to walk, and a food booking service, so there are ways to see more without taking the motorbike into the lanes. I could not find an official statement of parking arrangements or opening times, so check on the day and follow any signs at the entrance.

## Season and weather

Vietnam Airlines names October to April as the ideal period, with cool and dry weather suiting a walk through the lanes. It adds that May is the time to see the lotus lake at the village gate, if you can tolerate the heat. The same open-country road that is pleasant in autumn can be hot in summer and slippery after a sudden storm. Our guide to [riding in Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) covers the gear and the decision to wait.

Because daylight is short in winter, give yourself a larger margin between your planned departure and the sunset, which falls in the late afternoon in the coldest months.

## FAQ: Hanoi to Duong Lam by motorbike

### How far is Duong Lam from Hanoi?

The national tourism authority says 44 km from the centre. Other sources say up to 50 km, depending on where they measure from.

### Which road should a motorbike use?

Vietnam Airlines names two: Thang Long Avenue, the Hoa Lac junction and Route 21, or Highway 32 through Son Tay and then Route 21. Use service roads rather than the car-only central lanes of Thang Long Avenue.

### Can I ride a motorbike on an expressway to get there faster?

No. Motorcycles and mopeds are not allowed on expressways under Law 36/2024/QH15, in force since 1 January 2025.

### Is it too far for a single day?

Not for a confident rider with a reliable bike and an early start, but it is a full day. Add buffer for the return ride.

### What should I do once I arrive?

Park at the edge, walk the lanes, ask before entering courtyards and check any signs for rules on the day.
