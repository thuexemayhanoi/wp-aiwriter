If you lose the key to a rental motorbike, retrace your steps for a few minutes, then call the shop and tell them exactly where the bike is. Do not ask a street locksmith to cut a copy or bypass the ignition, and do not leave the bike unattended in the open if you can avoid it. The shop will tell you whether it has a spare, whether it can send someone and what a replacement involves. How much a replacement costs, and who pays, is a matter for your contract, and the published terms do not list a fee for a lost key, so you should ask before you agree to anything.

This guide sets out the steps in order, explains the difference between a traditional key and a smart fob, covers what to say when you report the loss, how to keep the bike safe while you wait and how to avoid losing a key in the first place. It builds on our general guide to [lost keys, flat tyres and breakdowns with a rental bike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-bike-breakdown-hanoi-what-to-do/) with more detail on keys.

## Step 1: retrace your steps

Most lost keys are not lost for good. They are in another pocket, on a table, in the bike's own ignition or in a place you visited an hour ago. Spend five minutes on this before you raise the alarm.

- **Check the bike.** Look in the ignition, the seat compartment and the front basket or hook, if there is one. Riders sometimes leave a key in the lock.

- **Check your clothes and bag,** including every pocket, the bottom of the bag and any small zip pocket.

- **Check the last places you stopped.** Cafes, shops, guarded car parks and hotel receptions often hold a key that was left behind or dropped nearby. Ask the person on the counter, not just the table.

- **Ask the parking attendant.** If you left the bike in a guarded car park, the attendant who gave you a ticket is a good first person to ask.

- **Look on the ground** where you parked and where you walked.

- **Ask anyone who was with you.**

If you find the key, take a photograph of it and where it was, and tell the shop only if the bike was left unlocked or the key was out of your control for a long time.

## Step 2: call the shop

If the key is gone, call Nguyen Ha on 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969), open 09:00 to 21:00 daily. Tell the shop:

- your name and the plate number of the bike;

- the exact place the bike is parked, with a map pin if possible, and whether it is in a guarded car park;

- whether the bike is locked or unlocked and whether the steering is locked;

- when and where you last had the key;

- what type of key it is: a metal key or an electronic fob;

- whether you have any spare key with you.

Be honest about how it happened. Staff deal with lost keys regularly, and a clear account helps them decide what to do. If it is outside opening hours, send a message and call when the shop opens. ## Step 3: keep the bike safe while you wait

A bike without a key is a bike you cannot ride, but it can still be taken. Think about how to keep it safe until the shop arrives.

- **Leave it in a guarded place.** If it is already in a guarded car park, tell the attendant that you will return with the shop's help.

- **Do not leave it on a quiet street overnight** if you can avoid it. If the shop cannot come at once, ask whether you can wait with the bike or whether someone can come for it.

- **If the steering is locked,** leave it locked. A locked steering is a basic security measure, and Nguyen Ha does not provide an extra lock.

- **If the steering is unlocked,** do not try to push the bike a long way, and do not force the lock.

- **Photograph the bike where it is,** with the plate and the surroundings visible.

- **Take your own belongings** and any documents you carry.

## Traditional key or smart fob: why it matters

Replacement depends on what kind of key the bike uses.

**A traditional metal key** is inserted and turned. A copy can sometimes be cut, but a key cut by a stranger may not fit well and does not necessarily match the bike's security. The shop may have a spare, or may be able to arrange a copy.

**A smart fob** is a small electronic device that the bike recognises when it is nearby. Honda's Vision manual describes a two-way check between the scooter and the registered fob, and says an unregistered fob cannot activate the system. Our guide to [smart key versus traditional key on rental scooters](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/smart-key-versus-traditional-key-on-rental-scooters/) explains how it works. Honda's help pages include questions about replacing a lost smart key on other scooter models, which indicates that replacement involves registering a new fob, usually through an authorised workshop, not a simple copy at a street stall. Our guide to [what to do if a scooter smart key battery runs low](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-to-do-if-a-scooter-smart-key-battery-runs-low/) covers a related problem.

In both cases the shop is the right person to decide. It knows what keys it holds, which bike you have and what a replacement involves.

## Do not use a street locksmith

You may see locksmiths and repair stalls that offer to cut a key or "open" the bike. Avoid them.

- **They can damage the ignition or the steering lock,** which makes the bike harder to secure and can lead to a larger repair bill.

- **A forced lock can damage a smart-key system.** Our guide to lost keys, flat tyres and breakdowns explains why a forced lock can damage the ignition and the bike's security.

- **Any new key is for the shop to approve.** A key made without the shop's knowledge may not match its records, and it can raise questions about security.

- **It may breach your contract,** which usually says you must use the bike as agreed and must not modify it. Vietnam's Civil Code (Law No. 91/2015/QH13, in force since 1 January 2017) says a lessee must preserve the leased property and use it according to its purpose and as agreed, so a forced ignition is risky.

Wait for the shop's instructions.

## What a replacement involves

How a key is replaced varies by bike and by shop. The shop may:

- **Bring a spare key** to where you are.

- **Collect the bike** and take it to the shop.

- **Arrange a new key or fob** through its usual supplier or a dealer.

- **Swap the bike** for another.

Ask the shop which of these it will do, how long it will take and what it will cost. The published terms at Nguyen Ha do not list a fee for a lost key, so the answer comes from your contract and from the shop. Our guide to [reading a motorbike rental agreement in English](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/reading-a-motorbike-rental-agreement-in-english/) explains where to look in the paper.

## Costs and who pays

The Civil Code says that a lessee must preserve leased property and compensate if it is lost or damaged, and the cost of a key is normally dealt with in the contract. As of October 2026, what you can reasonably do is:

1. **Ask what the replacement will cost, before it is done.**

2. **Ask whether the cost will be taken from the deposit,** which Nguyen Ha holds against damage, loss and unpaid charges.

3. **Ask for the amount in writing,** with the reason.

4. **Ask to see the new key or fob and any receipt.**

5. **Keep every message.**

If the shop says the cost is something you cannot agree with, say so calmly, and ask how it was calculated. ## After the key is replaced

- **Test it.** Check that it starts the bike, locks the steering and opens the seat.

- **Ask how many keys now exist,** and who holds them.

- **Note the change on the contract,** and keep a photograph of the new key.

- **Check the old key.** If the old key turns up, hand it to the shop. An old key that could still start the bike is a security risk if it is lost.

## How to avoid losing a key

- **Photograph the key at pickup,** and note how many keys or fobs come with the bike.

- **Attach it to something you will notice,** such as a lanyard or a bright tag, and keep it on you, not in a bag you may put down.

- **Never leave the key in the ignition.**

- **Use one pocket for the key,** every time.

- **Take it when you walk away,** even for a minute.

- **Lock the steering every time.**

- **For a smart fob, keep it out of range when you leave the bike.** Honda warns that anyone carrying the registered fob within range can start the engine, so do not leave it on a cafe table next to the bike.

## A quick checklist

1. **Retrace your steps for five minutes.**

2. **Call the shop with the location and key type.**

3. **Keep the bike safe, ideally in a guarded place.**

4. **Do not use a street locksmith.**

5. **Ask how replacement works and what it costs, before it is done.**

6. **Get the amount and the reason in writing.**

7. **Test the new key, and note it on the contract.**

## FAQ: lost rental bike keys

### What should I do first?

Retrace your steps for a few minutes, then call the shop with the bike's exact location.

### Can I get a copy cut on the street?

It is better not to. A street locksmith can damage the ignition, and a replacement should be approved by the shop.

### Is there a fee for a lost key?

The published terms of Nguyen Ha do not list one. Ask the shop what applies under your contract, and ask for it in writing.

### What if I have a smart fob and lose it?

Replacement usually involves registering a new fob, which the shop or a dealer arranges. Do not try to bypass the system.

### Can the bike be left where it is?

If it is in a guarded car park, usually yes while the shop arranges help. Otherwise, ask the shop whether it can be collected.

### What if I think the key was stolen?

Tell the shop at once. If you cannot find the bike, treat it as a theft and report it to the police.
