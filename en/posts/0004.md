Every rider and every passenger on a motorbike, scooter or moped in Vietnam must wear a helmet, and the rule is enforced far more consistently than most visitors expect. Police checkpoints in Hanoi stop bare-headed riders as a matter of routine, and a helmet worn with the strap hanging loose counts as no helmet at all. The law is simple, the fine is modest but real, and the practical questions are the ones that trip people up: which helmets are legal, whether a child needs one, what happens with two passengers, and whether the helmet handed over with a rental bike is good enough. This guide answers them with the rules in force since 1 January 2025 under Vietnam's Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (Law 36/2024/QH15) and the penalty decree that accompanies it (Decree 168/2024/ND-CP), as of October 2026.

## The rule in one sentence

Article 33 of Law 36/2024/QH15 requires the rider and every person carried on a two- or three-wheeled motorcycle or a moped to wear a helmet that meets the national technical standard, with the chin strap fastened properly. The obligation has three parts, and all three matter at a checkpoint: a helmet must be worn, it must be a helmet made for motorcycle use that carries the conformity mark, and it must be fastened. Electric scooters and electric mopeds fall under the same classes of vehicle as their petrol equivalents, so the rule applies to them as well. Bicycles are not covered by the article, but anything with a motor that moves you along a public road is.

The same article limits how many people a motorbike may carry: the rider and one passenger, with a second passenger allowed only in a few listed cases such as carrying a child under 12 or taking a patient to hospital. A family of four on one scooter is a common sight in Hanoi, but it is not legal, and a rental contract will not cover it.

## What counts as a proper helmet

Vietnam has a national standard for motorcycle helmets, QCVN 2:2021/BKHCN, issued under Circular 04/2021/TT-BKHCN of the Ministry of Science and Technology and in force since 1 August 2021. Since 1 January 2024 every helmet made in or imported into Vietnam has had to meet it before it can be sold. A compliant helmet carries the CR conformity mark, printed on the shell or on a waterproof label fixed to it, together with a label naming the manufacturer or importer, the size and the month and year of manufacture. The standard applies to helmets for motorcycles, mopeds, electric motorbikes and electric bicycles alike.

What the standard does not bless is the thin, cap-shaped "fashion helmet" sold for a few dollars' worth of dong at markets and roadside stalls. Those shells have no energy-absorbing liner and no CR mark, and police treat them as not being helmets. Three legal shapes are common in Hanoi: the half helmet that covers the top of the head, the three-quarter or open-face helmet that also covers the ears and the back of the head, and the full-face helmet with a chin bar. All three are legal if they carry the mark; the open-face and full-face styles protect far better, and a visor keeps the dust and rain out of your eyes on a longer ride.

Fit matters as much as the mark. A helmet should sit level, grip the head without pressure points and stay in place when you shake your head with the strap undone. The strap is then fastened so that no more than a finger or two fits between it and your chin. A loose strap is what most riders are actually fined for.

## The fines

Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, in force since 1 January 2025, sets the penalties. A rider who wears no helmet, or wears one without fastening the strap properly, is fined VND 400,000–600,000 (Article 7, clause 2, point h). A rider who carries a passenger without a helmet, or with the strap unfastened, is fined the same VND 400,000–600,000 (Article 7, clause 2, point i), except when the passenger is a patient being taken for emergency care, a child under six, or a person being escorted after an offence. The passenger without a helmet is fined separately, again VND 400,000–600,000 (Article 12, clause 5). In practice a couple stopped with one helmet between them can therefore face two fines at once.

Fines are paid at the State Treasury or through the police's online channels, not in cash to the officer; ask for the written decision, and read our guide to the [traffic fines that catch foreign riders out](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/traffic-fines-vietnam-foreign-riders/) for how a roadside stop works. A helmet offence does not cost licence points, but it is the most common reason a visitor is stopped at all, and once stopped you will also be asked for your licence and the bike's papers.

## Children and passengers

The law makes no exception for children: a child carried on a motorbike must wear a helmet that meets the standard, fastened properly. The decree only spares the rider the fine when the child is under six, which is why you see small children riding bare-headed in Hanoi, but a child's skull is not safer for it. Children's helmets with the CR mark are sold in the same shops as adult ones, in sizes from toddler upwards; if you plan to ride with a child, buy one before you set off rather than hoping the rental shop has a small size.

An adult passenger needs a helmet of their own, and so does a second rider on a weekly rental who takes the bike out alone. Tell the rental shop when you book that two people will ride, so that the right number of helmets is ready at handover, and check the strap on each one.

Whichever helmet you wear, keep it with you when you park. Helmets left hanging on a mirror disappear, and most scooters have room under the seat for one half or open-face helmet; a full-face helmet usually has to come with you.

## Buying a better helmet in Hanoi

Helmet shops cluster along the main shopping streets of every district and carry Vietnamese brands as well as international ones. Look for the CR mark and the manufacturer's label before anything else, then try sizes until one fits without rocking. A visor or a pair of clear glasses is worth having in the dry season, when the dust on the ring roads is constant, and in the rainy season a visor is the difference between seeing and squinting. Keep the receipt: a helmet bought in Vietnam is also the helmet you can hand back to the shop in a damage dispute as proof you wore your own.

## FAQ

### Do passengers really need a helmet in Vietnam?

Yes. Article 33 of Law 36/2024/QH15 applies to everyone carried on a motorcycle or moped, and Decree 168/2024/ND-CP fines both the rider who carries a bare-headed passenger and the passenger. The only exceptions to the rider's fine are an emergency patient, a child under six and a person being escorted after an offence.

### Is a half helmet legal?

Yes, if it carries the CR conformity mark of the QCVN 2:2021/BKHCN standard and you fasten the strap. Open-face and full-face helmets protect much better, but they are not required by law.

### Does a child under six need a helmet?

The law requires every passenger to wear one; the penalty decree only exempts the rider from the fine when the child is under six. Buy a child's helmet with the CR mark if you intend to ride with a small child.

### Can I wear a bicycle helmet?

No. The standard covers helmets for motorcycles and mopeds, and a bicycle helmet carries no CR mark for that use. Police treat it as no helmet.

### Does the rental shop provide helmets for two?

Nguyen Ha includes one basic helmet with each rental; say when you book that two people will ride so that a helmet is ready for each. A passenger without a helmet is fined just like the rider.
