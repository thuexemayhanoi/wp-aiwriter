Phung Hung mural street is a short, free, open-air stop that takes about 30 to 45 minutes on foot, so the best way to visit by scooter is to ride to the edge of the Old Quarter, park once in a guarded place and walk the murals. The paintings cover roughly 200 metres of old railway arches, opened to the public in early February 2018, and they are best seen in morning or late-afternoon light on a weekday. Do not try to ride along the street looking at the walls. This guide covers what you are actually going to see, the condition of the murals in 2026, the timing, the approach from the east bank of the Red River, a parking routine and a simple walking plan.

## What the murals are, and what they are not

The murals line Phung Hung Street, which runs beside the elevated railway that leads towards Long Bien Bridge. They were painted on the walls that close the stone railway arches, under a community art project funded by the Korea Foundation, UN-Habitat and the Hoan Kiem authorities, with Vietnamese and Korean artists and the painter group of Nguyen The Son. VOV World describes scenes of Hang Ma Street at Mid-Autumn, the Trang Tien department store, an Old Quarter corner with street vendors and a schoolgirl on her way to school, along with an old motorbike and a public water tap with a queue from the subsidy period.

Sources give slightly different counts, from 17 to about 19 or 20 artworks, and a street length of about 200 metres. Treat those as approximate. What matters for planning is that this is a compact outdoor gallery of old-Hanoi memories, not an attraction with gates, tickets or a visitor centre. Nobody guides you, and the street keeps working as an ordinary city street beside it.

It is also a different place from Train Street. Train Street is a narrow residential lane where access has been restricted and changeable, and our guide to [why Train Street access and parking rules matter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/visiting-hanoi-train-street-why-parking-and-access-rules-matter/) explains why you should not plan a whole day around it. The mural street is a normal public road beside the arches, which makes it the easier of the two to include.

## What to expect in 2026

Be realistic about condition. A Vietnam.vn report from August 2026 says the Phung Hung mural project of 2017 to 2018 created a highlight of nearly 20 decorated arches but has now deteriorated, and that the Hoan Kiem ward authorities are preparing a separate urban design project for the arch area along Phung Hung Street. The same report describes the wider arch system, 131 stone arches built by the French more than a century ago, as largely closed off or filled in, with some used as parking and informal trading.

In plain terms, you may find faded paint, peeling sections and stalls or parked bikes in front of some panels, and you may also find the street changed by works in the coming years. VinWonders says the paintings were made on a wooden-style background with golden acrylics, with an expected durability of 5 to 10 years, which fits that picture. A visit is still worthwhile for the setting and the old-Hanoi themes, but it is better to go expecting a lived-in street than a pristine gallery.

## Choose the day and the hour

The street is public, so there is no opening time. These are practical suggestions rather than rules:

- **Weekday morning.** Quieter pavements, softer light and fewer people stopping for photographs. A good option if you are riding in from the east bank before the Old Quarter fills up.
- **Late afternoon.** The arches catch warmer light, but traffic around the railway and Old Quarter edges is heavier and tired riders make mistakes.
- **Weekend evenings.** The streets around Hoan Kiem Lake are closed to vehicles during the walking-street period, which is reported as Friday 19:00 to Sunday midnight. Traffic reroutes onto the surrounding roads, so allow extra time and read signs. Our guide to [parking near Hoan Kiem Lake](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/where-to-park-a-scooter-near-hoan-kiem-lake/) covers that pattern.
- **Public holidays and commemorative days.** Popular photo spots in the Old Quarter draw crowds on these days, so pick another time if you dislike crowds.

If rain is forecast, postpone. Wet arch walls, slick painted road markings and splashing traffic make the visit less pleasant, and our guide to [riding in Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) explains how to decide.

## Getting there from the east bank

Nguyen Ha's shop is at Alley 5 Nguyen Van Cu Street, Bo De Ward, Long Bien District, on the east bank of the Red River, so the mural street is a short hop across the river rather than a long ride. Two things matter on the crossing.

First, choose the crossing that suits your vehicle on the day. Long Bien Bridge is a historic railway bridge with separate lanes for pedestrians, motorbikes and light vehicles. Vietnam+ reported that after a repair closure from 28 March, motorbikes, light vehicles and pedestrians were allowed back on those lanes from 28 May 2026, in both directions between Bo De and Hoan Kiem. That is history, not a promise: look at the signs and any barriers when you arrive, and have a second crossing in mind on your map. Our guide to [a first ride between Long Bien and the Red River](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/long-bien-red-river-first-ride/) describes the approach in more detail.

Second, do not rely on a navigation app to know which lanes your scooter may use. Set the destination as Phung Hung Street, zoom the route before you go and follow road signs over the app. This article does not give turn-by-turn directions because lane arrangements and works change.

## Park once, then walk

The murals sit on a busy edge of the Old Quarter, and it is the walk that makes the visit work. A sensible routine:

1. **Pick a guarded place before you arrive.** Look for a marked, attended motorbike parking area, not a gap on a pavement. Our guide to [guarded lots, tickets and towing in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-motorbike-in-hanoi/) explains how to recognise one.
2. **Do not park in front of the arches.** The Vietnam.vn report notes that parts of the arch frontage are used as parking, and a bike left in front of a mural or an arch opening is the kind of stop that causes problems with residents and officials. Another bike being there is not a permission.
3. **Lock the steering and take the key.** The shop does not provide an extra lock, so use the steering lock and a place with an attendant.
4. **Take your valuables.** Photograph the bike's condition before you leave it, as you would at any handover.
5. **Save the location.** Drop a pin on your map and note the lot's closing time.

Walk from the lot to the street. Because the sources disagree on the exact ends of the mural stretch, with one naming Phan Dinh Phung and Hang Bong and another Hang Bong and Tran Nhan Tong, simply follow the railway arches and you will see where the paintings start and finish.

## A 45-minute walking plan

| Time | What to do |
|---|---|
| 0 to 5 min | Walk from the parking area to the nearest end of the mural stretch. |
| 5 to 25 min | Walk the full length once without stopping for more than a few seconds, so you know what is there. |
| 25 to 40 min | Walk back and stop at the panels you liked, keeping the pavement clear. |
| 40 to 45 min | Choose a cafe or snack stop in the Old Quarter, or continue towards Dong Xuan Market or Hang Ma Street, both mentioned by VinWonders as nearby. |

If you have more time, add the Hoan Kiem area on foot, and see our guide to [riding in Hanoi's Old Quarter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-old-quarter/) for when it is better to walk than to ride.

## Manners and safety at the arches

- **Stay on the pavement and off the railway.** The rails above the arches are active, and the street below carries traffic and bikes. Do not climb for a photograph.
- **Do not block doorways.** People live and work behind and beside the arches.
- **Watch the road, not the viewfinder.** If you want a photograph with the street behind you, stand against a wall away from traffic.
- **Be careful with stallholders and vendors.** Hanoi authorities have publicly said that unmarked prices in the Old Quarter are a problem, so ask the price before you take something and decline politely if you do not want it.
- **Respect residents' privacy.** Do not photograph into windows or private doorways.

## If the street is different when you arrive

Works, a festival, a closure or a crowd can change the plan. Do not push on. Choose a legitimate parking spot, check signs, and have a fallback within a few minutes: the Hoan Kiem Lake area on foot, a cafe, or a second look on another day. ## FAQ: Phung Hung mural street by scooter

### Is there an entrance fee?

No. It is a public street beside the railway arches, so there is no ticket office or gate.

### How long should I allow?

About 30 to 45 minutes on foot, plus time to find parking and walk to the street.

### Are the murals still in good condition?

Reports in 2026 say the 2017 to 2018 project has deteriorated and that renovation planning for the arch area is under way. Expect some wear.

### Can I ride along the street to see the murals?

You can see them from a moving bike, but you will not see much and it is hard to ride safely while looking. Park and walk.

### Is this the same as Train Street?

No. Train Street is a separate residential lane with changeable access. The mural street is the road beside the arches.

### Can I visit on a weekend evening?

Yes, but the Hoan Kiem walking-street arrangements change traffic, so check the signs and plan parking first.
