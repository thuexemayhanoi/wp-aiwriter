An intercity bus is the safer choice whenever the ride would put you outside what you can control or what is lawful: you do not hold a licence that covers the bike, the trip would run into darkness, fog or storms, the road is a long mountain route you have never ridden, you are tired or have been drinking, or you are carrying more people and luggage than a small scooter handles well. In those situations the question is not whether a bus is exciting but whether you would be a competent, legal and alert rider for the whole route. This guide sets out seven situations where the bus is the better answer, what evidence supports the caution, where the motorbike is still a good choice and how to combine the two. It offers general guidance as of October 2026, not a safety guarantee for either mode.

## What the evidence does and does not say

No source we read gives a per-kilometre comparison of crash risk between intercity coaches and rental scooters in Vietnam, so this article does not claim a number. What the available data does show is the setting: the World Health Organization's 2023 country profile for Viet Nam lists 75,353,485 registered vehicles in 2021, of which 70,113,559 were powered two- and three-wheelers. It reports 5,699 road deaths in official statistics for 2021 and a WHO estimate of 17,229, a rate of 17.7 per 100,000 population.

VietNamNet reports that Le Van Dat of the Institute for Transport Strategy and Development told a National Traffic Safety Committee workshop in February 2025 that about 65 to 70 percent of road accidents involve motorcycles and that more than 90 percent of traffic deaths are related to motorcycle accidents. The committee's deputy chief put registered motorcycles at 77 million at the end of 2024, so most traffic is two-wheeled and a rider is exposed to mixed traffic on every journey. The same WHO profile records that Vietnam has a national law on driving time and rest periods for professional drivers, which is aimed at professional drivers and does not apply to a visitor on a rental bike.

That is context, not proof that buses never crash. The point is narrower: on certain days and routes, a professional driver on a fixed route has structural advantages over a visitor on an unfamiliar bike.

## Situation 1: you do not hold a licence that covers the bike

This is the clearest case. Under the Road Traffic Order and Safety Law (36/2024/QH15, effective from 1 January 2025), Article 9 prohibits driving a motor vehicle without the licence required by law, and Article 56 requires a rider to hold a licence that suits the vehicle and to carry, among other papers, the registration certificate, the licence and the compulsory civil liability insurance certificate. If you are not sure your licence covers a motorcycle over 50cc, the bus is the lawful way to cover the distance. Our guide to [licence rules for foreigners](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbike-licence-rules-for-foreigners-in-vietnam/) explains what is recognised, and our guide to [documents to carry](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/documents-to-carry-riding-in-vietnam/) lists what to have with you.

Riding without a valid licence is not just a paperwork problem. Many travel policies treat it as grounds to refuse a claim, as our guide to [travel insurance and motorbike rentals](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/travel-insurance-motorbike-rental-vietnam/) explains.

## Situation 2: the ride would run into darkness

October daylight is short. Timeanddate lists Hanoi sunrise at about 05:47 on 1 October 2026 and sunset at about 17:44, with sunset near 17:21 by the end of the month. A trip that needs a return ride after about 17:00 means riding in the dark on rural roads with trucks, unlit vehicles and animals. Our guide to [riding at night in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-at-night-in-hanoi/) explains why visibility matters even in the city. If your plan only works if nothing goes wrong, take a bus for the outbound or return leg.

## Situation 3: fog, heavy rain or a storm

Wet roads lengthen braking distances, reduce visibility and make bends less forgiving, especially on a small bike. Our guides to [riding in Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) and [riding a semi-automatic bike in heavy rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-a-semi-automatic-bike-in-heavy-hanoi-rain/) explain the issues. Hill roads can have fog at any time of year. If the forecast is poor or the weather changes after you set out, a bus, a train or a night in a hotel is a better decision than pressing on. Remember that the shop does not include a raincoat with short rentals.

## Situation 4: a long mountain route you have not ridden

Hanoi to Ha Giang is about 300 kilometres by road according to travel sources, and sleeper buses and limousines are generally quoted at around five and a half to seven hours. A bike takes much longer once you add fuel, meals and mountain bends, and our article on [riding from Hanoi or renting in Ha Giang](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-to-ha-giang-ride-or-rent-there/) explains why many visitors take the bus north and arrange a bike at the other end. Sapa is similar: a bus takes roughly five to six hours according to ticket sites.

The same logic applies to shorter hill rides if you are new to mountain bends. Our guide to [manual bikes and steep mountain roads](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/manual-bikes-and-steep-mountain-roads-when-to-choose-another-vehicle/) explains when another vehicle is the better choice. The distance and time figures here are estimates from commercial sources, and actual times vary with traffic, season and operator.

## Situation 5: you are tired, unwell or have been drinking

Riding demands attention and balance for hours. If you slept badly, have a long flight behind you, feel ill or are tired after a big day, hand the driving to a professional. Article 9 of the traffic law also prohibits driving with any alcohol in the blood or breath, so the bus is the sensible option after a social evening. Our guide to [drink-driving rules for motorbike riders](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/drink-driving-rules-motorbike-vietnam/) explains the rule.

## Situation 6: more people, luggage or children than a scooter handles well

A small bike with a passenger and bags is less stable and takes longer to brake. Honda's manuals set load limits for each model, and our guides on [carrying a passenger and luggage](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/scooter-for-two-riders-and-luggage-hanoi/) and [child passengers on a motorbike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/child-passengers-motorbike-vietnam/) explain the limits. On a long intercity journey, the combination of fatigue, weight and traffic is a good reason to choose a bus.

## Situation 7: the fast road is closed to motorbikes

Article 25 of the traffic law says motorbikes and mopeds may not use expressways. A bus can use the expressway where a scooter cannot, so on some corridors the bus has a safer and faster road available than the one open to you. Our guide to [checking whether a navigation app suggests an expressway](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-check-if-a-navigation-app-suggests-an-expressway/) shows how to avoid a restricted road, and the alternative road may be a busy trunk route.

## Where the motorbike is still the better choice

The motorbike suits short, flexible rides in good light where you want to stop often: a Long Bien and Gia Lam morning, a lake loop, a short run to a craft village. It also suits experienced, licensed riders on a known route with margin in the plan. A quiet eastern ride, such as the one in our [guide to the east bank](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/gia-lam-east-bank-bat-trang-by-scooter/), is much lower risk than a 300-kilometre trunk road. The decision is about the route and the rider, not the vehicle's reputation.

## How to choose and use a bus well

Buses vary. Use an operator you can identify, buy a ticket through a reputable channel, and check where it departs and arrives. Hanoi has several stations: one booking site lists My Dinh for the west and north, Gia Lam for the north-east and Ninh Binh and Giap Bat for the south, and Yen Nghia as a large hub, while many tourist buses pick up in the Old Quarter. The shop is near Gia Lam bus station, which is handy if you want to leave from the east side.

Practical habits help. Choose a daytime departure for a first trip, because night buses reach their destinations in the early hours and you must find a place to stay. Wear a seatbelt if one is fitted. Keep your valuables with you. If a driver is speeding or using a phone, say so to the operator. We could not find an official operator safety rating to recommend, so rely on recent reviews and your own judgement.

## Combine the two

You do not have to choose one mode for the whole trip. A common pattern is to rent a bike for city days, take a bus for the long leg and rent again, or use a guide, at the other end. ## FAQ: bus or rental motorbike

### Is a bus always safer than a motorbike?

Not always, and we found no source that quantifies it. A bus is the better choice in particular situations: no suitable licence, darkness or bad weather, fatigue, long mountain legs and heavy loads.

### Can I ride a rental if I only have a car licence?

Not for a motorcycle over 50cc. A car licence does not cover it. Check our licence guide before you rent.

### Which bus station is closest to the shop?

The shop is near Gia Lam bus station. Check which station serves your destination, because different stations serve different directions.

### What if the weather turns during a ride?

Stop somewhere safe, wait or change plan. A bus, a taxi or a night's stay is a better decision than riding on in poor visibility.

### Does the shop help me store the bike while I travel?

The shop's terms do not mention storage, so ask before you rely on it. Returning the bike early does not refund unused rent.
