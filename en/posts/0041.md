Vietnam's traffic law tells every rider to carry three things: a driving licence valid for the class of bike, the bike's registration certificate and the compulsory civil-liability insurance certificate for the bike. On a rental, two of those three belong to the shop, so the practical question for a visitor is which papers the shop hands over, which ones you must bring yourself and what the penalty decree really punishes. The answer is less obvious than most travel forums suggest, because the fines for having no licence or no registration are heavy while the decree's fixed "forgot to carry it" fines are aimed at commercial transport bikes. This guide sets out the position as of October 2026, based on the text of the law and the decree, and gives a simple way to organise the papers so a roadside check takes two minutes. It is general information, not legal advice.

## The short list

As of October 2026, a rider on a rental motorbike should have on them or on the bike:

- **A driving licence** that covers the bike's engine size. For a foreign visitor that normally means a home licence with a motorcycle category plus an International Driving Permit issued under the 1968 Vienna Convention, or a Vietnamese licence. Our guide to [licence rules for foreigners in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbike-licence-rules-for-foreigners-in-vietnam/) explains which countries' permits count.
- **The bike's registration certificate**, the small card with the plate number, the frame and engine numbers and the registered owner.
- **The compulsory civil-liability insurance certificate** for the bike, a paper slip or electronic certificate that names the plate and the cover period.
- **Your passport or a clear copy** of the data page and visa. The traffic law does not list it, but an officer who checks your licence will want to match it to a person.

The shop supplies the registration card and the insurance certificate because both belong to the bike. You supply the licence, the IDP and your identification.

## What the 2025 law says

Article 56 of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (Law No. 36/2024/QH15), in force since 1 January 2025, says that a driver must have a licence that is valid, still has points and matches the vehicle being driven. It then lists the papers to carry when taking part in road traffic: the vehicle registration certificate (or a certified copy with the lender's confirmation if the bike is mortgaged), the driving licence for that vehicle type, the technical-safety and environmental inspection certificate for motor vehicles where the law requires one, and the compulsory civil-liability insurance certificate of the vehicle owner.

The inspection item matters little to a rental rider today. The only inspection scheme for motorcycles that we found is the emission-testing roadmap in Prime Minister's Decision No. 13/2026/QD-TTg, which starts on 1 July 2027 in Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City and later elsewhere. So in October 2026 there is no motorbike inspection certificate for a rental rider to carry. If a shop does hand you one, carry it with the rest.

Article 56 also says that papers already integrated into a Vietnamese electronic identification account (VNeID) can be shown and checked through that account. That helps residents whose licences and registration records live on the app. A short-stay visitor has no VNeID account, so for you the physical licence, the IDP and the physical certificates are what count.

## What the penalty decree really punishes

The fines come from Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, also in force since 1 January 2025 and amended by Decree 238/2026/ND-CP from 15 August 2026. We read the text on the Government portal. The amendments change other articles, and the wording of Articles 14 and 18 touched by Decree 238 concerns number plates and young riders, not the document fines below. The rules for a motorbike rider are these:

- **No licence, or the wrong class of licence.** Under Article 18, riding a motorbike of up to 125 cc without a licence, or with one that does not match the bike, is fined VND 2,000,000–4,000,000. For a bike above 125 cc the fine is VND 6,000,000–8,000,000.
- **An IDP without your national licence.** Holding a 1968-convention IDP but not carrying the national licence that goes with it is fined in the same bands as having no licence: VND 2,000,000–4,000,000 up to 125 cc and VND 6,000,000–8,000,000 above it.
- **No valid insurance.** Under Article 18, a motorbike rider without a valid compulsory insurance certificate is fined VND 200,000–300,000.
- **No registration certificate, or an expired one.** Under Article 14, riding without a registration certificate, or with an expired one, is fined VND 2,000,000–3,000,000 and costs two licence points. Using an altered certificate or one that does not match the bike's frame and engine numbers falls in the same band.
- **Did not carry a document you hold.** The decree's fixed fines of VND 200,000–300,000 for not carrying the insurance certificate, the registration certificate or the licence are written for motorbikes used in commercial transport. For an ordinary private or rental rider we found no separate fine for a document you hold but left at the hotel.

That last point is a gap in the penalty table, not permission to leave papers behind. The law's duty to carry the documents applies to every driver. An officer who cannot see a licence cannot confirm that you have one, and the heavy fines above apply if you turn out not to. Treat carrying the full set as the rule.

## The driving licence: the document that is yours

The licence is the one document a rental shop cannot give you. Since 1 January 2025, Article 57 of the law defines class A1 as two-wheeled motorbikes up to 125 cc or electric motors up to 11 kW, and class A as bikes above 125 cc or above 11 kW, including everything in A1. A Honda Vision, Air Blade 125, Wave or Sirius sits inside A1. A 150 cc sport bike such as a Honda Winner or Yamaha Exciter does not. A foreign licence is read the same way, so the motorcycle category on your home licence and IDP must cover the bike.

Carry the physical licence and the IDP booklet together. A photo on your phone is not a substitute, because the IDP only works alongside the home licence it translates. If your country is not party to the 1968 convention, or your licence has no motorcycle category, you are riding unlicensed as far as Vietnamese law is concerned, and the fine is of a different order from forgetting a paper.

## The registration certificate: the shop's card, your responsibility

Every motorbike in Vietnam has a registration certificate issued by the police. On a rental the registered owner is the shop or a person connected with it, not you. That is normal, and an officer expects it.

Shops differ in what they hand over. Some give the original card, others a laminated copy while the original stays at the shop. Ask which you are getting and photograph the card at handover, so the plate number and owner's name stay on your phone even if the card goes missing. Because Article 14 punishes riding without a registration certificate far harder than the insurance slip, treat the card as the one paper you cannot lose.

Our article on [what the clauses in a Hanoi rental contract mean](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-motorbike-rental-contract-explained/) explains why many contracts make the renter responsible for the card and the key. Keep the card with your passport rather than loose under the seat.

## The insurance certificate: what it covers and what it does not

Vietnamese law requires every motor-vehicle owner to hold compulsory civil-liability insurance. The certificate is a small printed slip or an electronic certificate listing the plate number and the validity dates. It must be current on the day you ride, because the fine under Article 18 is for having no valid certificate.

That insurance covers the owner's liability for injury or damage caused to third parties, within statutory limits. It does not pay to repair the rental bike, replace it if stolen or treat your own injuries. The rental contract and your own travel insurance deal with those. Our guide to [travel insurance and motorbike rentals](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/travel-insurance-motorbike-rental-vietnam/) explains the usual exclusions, including the licence condition most insurers apply.

Check the dates on the slip at handover. A certificate that expired last week is the shop's problem, but at a checkpoint it becomes yours.

## Passport, visa and hotel registration

The traffic law does not require you to carry a passport, but identification is part of any police interaction. Carry the passport if you are comfortable doing so, or a good colour copy of the data page and the visa or e-visa approval. Many hotels in Vietnam ask to keep the passport during a stay for their own registration duties. If yours does, take the copy and keep a photo on your phone.

A rental shop will also ask to see your passport and licence when you sign. At Nguyen Ha Motorbike Rental, staff check the identity documents and the licence and photograph them for the contract. The originals are not kept, and the shop's security is a separate cash deposit, not your passport. ## How a roadside check usually goes

Traffic police in Hanoi run checks at fixed points and sometimes stop riders for a visible offence such as no helmet, running a red light or riding in a lane closed to motorbikes. When you are waved in, stop where indicated, switch off the engine, take off your helmet if asked and hand over the documents one at a time as requested. The officer will normally ask for the licence first, then the registration card and the insurance certificate.

Keep the three papers together in one wallet or zipped pouch that you can reach without unpacking a bag. Riders who dig through luggage, or who offer a phone photo of an IDP they left at the hotel, turn a short stop into a long one. If a penalty is issued, ask for the written record and an official receipt for any payment, and keep both. Our article on [traffic fines that catch foreign riders out](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/traffic-fines-vietnam-foreign-riders/) walks through the process in more detail.

If a paper is missing because the shop kept it, phone the shop from the roadside. A photo of the card, or the shop confirming the plate number and owner, can settle a registration question on the spot. It will not stand in for a licence you do not have.

## A practical way to organise the papers

Treat the documents as one kit that you assemble at the shop and never split up:

1. **Licence and IDP** in a slim wallet in a zipped front pocket, not under the seat where they can vanish with the bike.
2. **Registration card and insurance slip** in the same wallet. Photograph both, including the plate number, before you leave the shop.
3. **Passport or copy** in the same pocket or a money belt, with a photo of the data page and visa on your phone.
4. **The shop's phone number** saved in your contacts. Nguyen Ha is reachable on 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969) from 09:00 to 21:00 daily.
5. **The rental contract** in the seat compartment, with a photo of it on your phone as well.

Do not leave any of these in the seat compartment overnight. The compartment is not a safe, and a bike stolen with its papers is a worse outcome than a bike stolen on its own.

## Mistakes that cause real trouble

A few patterns come up again and again with visiting riders:

- **Assuming an IDP alone is enough.** It only works with the home licence it accompanies, and the home licence needs a motorcycle category.
- **Renting a bike above 125 cc on an A1-equivalent licence.** Since 1 January 2025, anything above that line needs class A or its foreign equivalent.
- **Leaving the registration card under the seat,** then losing the key. The card and the key tend to disappear together.
- **Not checking the insurance dates.** The shop should supply a current certificate, but the rider is the one fined if it has lapsed.
- **Handing the passport to a shop as security.** Keep it. A cash deposit is the normal and safer arrangement.

None of these is exotic. They are the ordinary result of treating the paperwork as the shop's problem rather than yours for the length of the rental.

## FAQ: documents for riding a rental motorbike in Vietnam

### Do I need to carry the original registration card or is a copy acceptable?

Article 56 refers to the registration certificate, or a certified copy together with the lender's confirmation when a bike is mortgaged. Many shops hand over a laminated copy and keep the original. Ask which you are receiving, photograph it and keep the shop's number handy in case an officer wants to confirm the plate and owner.

### Can I show my licence as a photo on my phone?

Not as a visitor. The VNeID provision in the 2025 law applies to documents integrated into a Vietnamese electronic identification account, which short-stay foreigners do not have. Carry the physical licence and the IDP.

### What is the fine for riding without the right documents?

As of October 2026, riding without a valid licence for the class is fined VND 2,000,000–4,000,000 up to 125 cc and VND 6,000,000–8,000,000 above it. No valid insurance is fined VND 200,000–300,000, and no registration certificate VND 2,000,000–3,000,000, all under Decree 168/2024/ND-CP.

### Is there a fine for simply forgetting a document at the hotel?

The decree's fixed fines for not carrying a document are written for motorbikes in commercial transport, and we found no separate fine for an ordinary rider. The law still requires you to carry the papers, and an officer cannot verify what you cannot show, so do not rely on the gap.

### Does the rental shop give me the insurance certificate?

It should, because the compulsory civil-liability insurance belongs to the bike and must be current. Check the plate number and dates on the slip before you ride off.

### Should I carry my passport while riding?

Carry the passport or a clear colour copy of the data page and visa. The traffic documents are the licence, the registration card and the insurance certificate, and identification is what makes the licence check work.
