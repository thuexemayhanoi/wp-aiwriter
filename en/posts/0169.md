To confirm that a rental bike is in the moped class, check three facts against three pieces of evidence: the registration certificate must show a petrol displacement of no more than 50 cm3 (or an electric power of no more than 4 kW), the model must have a design speed of no more than 50 km/h according to the manufacturer's data, and the plate and numbers on the certificate must match the bike in front of you. Under the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (Law 36/2024/QH15, effective 1 January 2025), only a vehicle that passes all three counts as a moped, which a rider of 16 or over may ride without a motorcycle licence.

If the shop's word is the only evidence, you have not confirmed anything. This guide sets out a six-step routine for the rental counter, the records worth keeping, the warning signs, how electric bikes change the check, and what to do if the bike fails, as of October 2026.

## Why the check falls to the renter

A rental shop describes its bikes in everyday terms, such as 50cc, Cub or scooter, and those labels are not the legal test. The law looks at the vehicle's actual characteristics. If the shop hands a licence-free rider a bike that is in fact a larger motorcycle, the rider is the one riding without a licence that covers the bike, and most travel insurance policies will not respond to an accident in that situation. Our guide to [renting a 50cc scooter in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/renting-a-50cc-scooter-in-hanoi/) covers the age and licence rules that sit behind the moped class.

None of this implies that shops mislabel bikes as a rule. It means that two minutes of checking turns an assumption into a record, and that the habit is worth having whichever shop you use.

## The three tests and where the evidence comes from

Article 34 of Law 36/2024/QH15 defines a moped as a two- or three-wheeled motor vehicle with a design speed of not more than 50 km/h and, for a petrol engine, a displacement of not more than 50 cm3 or, for an electric motor, a power of not more than 4 kW. Each test has its own best source.

| Test | Best evidence | What you can do yourself |
|---|---|---|
| Displacement not above 50 cm3 (petrol) | The Dung tich line on the registration certificate | Read it, and compare with the model code and the label on the bike |
| Power not above 4 kW (electric) | The Cong suat line on the certificate | Read it, and ask for the electric model's specification |
| Design speed not above 50 km/h | The manufacturer's or importer's specification for the model | Ask the shop for the sheet or the model name, and check it against the maker's website |

The registration certificate for motorcycles and mopeds, issued under Circular 79/2024/TT-BCA (effective 1 January 2025), has a block of vehicle characteristics that includes the brand, model code, vehicle type, displacement, power, and engine and chassis numbers. This guide concentrates on the routine of checking those lines against the bike, rather than on what each line means.

## A six-step check at the counter


1. **Ask to see the registration certificate, or a clear photograph of it.** A shop that rents out bikes should be able to produce it. If the original is kept elsewhere, ask for a photograph, and treat the lack of any paper as a warning.

2. **Read the displacement line yourself.** Look for a figure of 50 or lower. If the line shows 110, 125 or anything else above 50, the bike is not a moped, whatever it is called.

3. **Match the plate.** The plate on the bike should be the plate on the certificate. The certificate's QR code carries the plate, the plate colour and the engine and chassis numbers, which is why a mismatch is a serious sign.

4. **Match the model.** Compare the model code and brand on the certificate with the badge and model label on the bike. A certificate for one model and a bike that is plainly another is a red flag.

5. **Ask for the design speed.** The certificate does not give it. Ask the shop what the model's design speed is and where that comes from, then check the model on the manufacturer's page where one exists. Honda Vietnam's model pages, for example, give engine size and specifications for the scooters it sells.

6. **Ask for the finding in writing.** Have the shop note in the rental contract or on the handover sheet that the bike is a moped of not more than 50 cm3, with its plate number. Nguyen Ha confirms the exact bike and terms when you book, and you can ask on 0334 699 969 between 09:00 and 21:00 daily.

If the shop cannot or will not do step 1 or step 6, move on to another bike or another shop.

## Records to keep during the rental

Evidence is useful only if you can produce it. Take these photographs at handover and keep them on your phone for the whole rental:

- The registration certificate, both sides, with the displacement line readable.
- The plate on the bike, with the certificate beside it.
- The model label or badge on the bodywork.
- The odometer and fuel gauge, as for any rental.
- The page of the contract where the bike and plate are written.

These photographs also help if there is a dispute about damage or a question at a roadside check. The shop photographs your own documents for the contract, so the exchange of evidence runs both ways.

## Red flags and what each one means

| Warning sign | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| The certificate shows 110 cm3 or more on a bike called a 50cc | The bike is a motorcycle, not a moped |
| No certificate is produced, or only a handwritten note | You cannot verify the class |
| The plate does not match the certificate | The paper may belong to another vehicle |
| The bike looks much larger than a typical 50cc | A larger engine may have been fitted |
| The displacement line is blank, smudged or altered | The figure cannot be trusted |
| The shop says "it is basically 50cc" | A vague answer is not evidence |

A bike that has been modified is a separate problem from a bike that was mislabelled. Decree 168/2024/ND-CP treats an owner's unauthorised change of the brand or paint colour from what the registration shows as an offence, and an engine change is a larger departure still. A bike that no longer matches its paper cannot be confirmed from that paper.

## Electric bikes: extra checks

Electric bikes are sold under many names, and the moped test for them is power and design speed, not displacement. For an electric scooter marketed as the equivalent of a 50cc, read the Cong suat line and look for a figure of no more than 4 kW, then ask for the design speed. Law 36/2024/QH15 excludes motor-assisted bicycles from the moped definition, so an electric bicycle is a different class, with different rules. ## If the bike turns out not to be a moped

Do not ride it. If you hold a licence that covers the bike, the bike may still be fine to ride, but you must then meet the licence rules for its class, as our [guide to licence rules for foreigners](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbike-licence-rules-for-foreigners-in-vietnam/) explains. If you do not, ask the shop for a bike that does pass, or end the booking before the contract is signed. After signing, the contract terms, including early return, are set out in our [rental contract guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-motorbike-rental-contract-explained/), and it is far easier to change bikes at the counter than after a day of riding.

## What this check cannot do

Be realistic about the limits. You can read the card and compare it with the bike, but you cannot measure an engine's size by looking at it, and you cannot test its design speed. The check removes the obvious problems, not every possibility. Only the authorities and the manufacturer can rule definitively on a vehicle's class. If you are unsure, asking the shop to put the bike's class in writing and photographing the evidence is the sensible limit of what a renter can do.

## FAQ about confirming the moped class

### Can I tell a moped from the bike's appearance alone?

Not reliably. Size and badges are hints, but the registration certificate and the manufacturer's data are the evidence.

### Do I need the original registration certificate?

A clear photograph, taken with the shop's agreement, is the practical standard. The shop may keep the original with the bike or on file.

### What if the certificate shows exactly 50 cm3?

Law 36/2024/QH15 says not more than 50 cm3, which includes 50, though other texts use "50 or more" for motorcycles. Ask the shop to confirm the class in writing, or choose a bike that shows a lower figure.

### Does a moped need a licence plate?

Mopeds are registered vehicles with plates, and the certificate and plate should match the bike you are given.

### What if I am under 18?

A rider of 16 or 17 may ride a moped but not a motorcycle. Decree 168/2024/ND-CP penalises a 16- or 17-year-old riding a larger motorcycle, so for that rider the check matters even more. Parents or guardians should talk to the shop before booking.
