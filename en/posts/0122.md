Uneven brake feel means the two brakes, or the two sides of one brake, do not respond in a matching, predictable way: one lever travels much further than the other, a lever feels spongy or pulses, the scooter pulls to one side under braking, or a wheel drags when you let go. On a Honda Vision or Air Blade the front brake is a hydraulic disc and the rear is a drum worked by a cable lever, so each wears and fails in its own way. Honda also fits a combined braking system, and the Air Blade 160 manual describes front-wheel ABS, which adds sensations that are normal and some that are not. Test slowly in a quiet, empty place, compare against what Honda's manuals say to expect, and report anything odd before you ride into traffic.

## First, what "even" should feel like

Even brake feel does not mean both levers feel the same. A front disc and a rear drum are different machines. It means each brake behaves consistently from one pull to the next and in proportion to your hand.

Honda's manuals describe the layout. On the Vision, the Air Blade and similar scooters, the front lever (normally on the right) works the front disc brake through brake fluid, and the rear lever (normally on the left) works the rear drum brake through a cable. The owner's manuals say that using both brakes together gives the best braking, and that the scooters have a combined braking system that shares braking force between front and rear. Honda says the split of braking force differs depending on whether you use only the front brake or only the rear brake.

So "even" means that:

- the front lever has a firm, steady feel with a clear point where the brake bites;
- the rear lever has a small, even free play and then bites progressively;
- neither lever pulses, creeps closer to the grip as you hold it or springs back slowly;
- the scooter slows in a straight line without pulling;
- both wheels spin freely when you let go.

## Spot 1: lever travel that differs from what you expect

Honda gives a number for the rear lever. The Vision and Air Blade manuals both state a free play of 10 to 20 mm, measured at the tip of the rear brake lever, which is the distance the lever moves before the brake starts to work. Too little free play can make the shoe rub, and too much makes the scooter slow to respond.

You can feel this without a ruler. Put the scooter on its stand, pull the rear lever gently until you feel resistance and note how far it travelled. Then do the same on the other scooter if the shop has two. If the rear lever travels almost to the grip before it bites, the cable has stretched or the shoes have worn. If it has no free play at all and the wheel does not spin freely, it may be too tight. Both are adjustments that the manual describes in half-turn steps, and both are for the shop to do, not you.

The front lever has no adjustable free play in the same way. Honda's manuals say that if the brake fluid is below the LOWER mark, or if the lever's free play exceeds the standard, the pads should be checked, and if they are not worn there may be a fluid leak that needs a Honda dealer.

## Spot 2: spongy, soft or sinking levers

A soft front lever that you can squeeze a long way, or one that slowly sinks under steady pressure, points to air in the brake system, low fluid or worn pads. Honda UK's maintenance page lists air in the brake lines as a cause of a spongy feel and says only a trained technician should deal with it. Honda Global notes that brake fluid deteriorates with heat and moisture and must be replaced periodically, and that pads, shoes and discs are consumables.

A renter cannot repair this. The renter's job is to say it clearly: "the right lever feels soft and goes further than it did at handover." A soft lever is a reason to ask for another scooter. Do not ride off, because a front brake that fades under repeated use is dangerous in a stop-and-go city.

## Spot 3: pulsing, shuddering or vibration

A lever that pulses under steady braking has several possible causes:

- **ABS working.** The Air Blade manual says the front-wheel ABS stops the front brake locking in a hard stop, that the front lever may pulse slightly when it works and that this is normal, and that ABS does not act below about 10 km/h and does not shorten stopping distance. A pulse during a hard stop on the ABS model is therefore not a fault by itself.
- **A warped or uneven disc**, which gives a pulsing or vibration even in gentle braking.
- **Uneven pad wear or dirt** on the disc face.

The key difference is the kind of braking. A pulse in a hard, deliberate emergency stop on the ABS model is expected. A shudder in gentle braking at moderate speed is not, and it should be reported. Ask the shop whether the scooter you are riding has ABS, because the Vision manual describes a combined system and the Air Blade manual lists ABS for one type.

## Spot 4: pulling to one side or an uneven slowing

If the scooter drifts or the handlebar tugs to one side when you brake, suspect something other than the brake itself first: uneven tyre pressure, a bent handlebar or fork, or a loose wheel. Brake pads sticking on one side can also cause tugging.

Test it on a flat, empty strip at walking speed. Brake gently with the front lever only, then with the rear only, then with both, and watch the line the scooter takes. It should slow straight. A scooter that always drifts the same way under a gentle brake needs a mechanic. Check tyre pressure first, since the manual's label gives the correct cold pressures.

## Spot 5: dragging, heat and noise

A brake that does not release fully gives away its presence in simple ways:

- the wheel does not spin freely on the stand after you release the lever;
- the brake or hub area smells hot or feels much hotter than the other wheel after a short ride (take care, do not touch hot parts; Honda's manuals warn against touching brake areas until they cool);
- the scooter feels slow and heavy and uses more throttle than usual;
- a steady rub, scrape or squeal even when you are not braking.

Noise needs careful reading. Honda UK's maintenance page lists squeaking or grinding as common signs that brakes need service. A light squeal in damp weather can be harmless, and Honda's manual even says damp brakes work less well and should be dried by using them gently at low speed. Metal-on-metal grinding, however, is a sign of pads or shoes worn to the limit and should be treated as urgent.

## Spot 6: the checks Honda says to do before every ride

Honda's pre-ride list for the Vision tells riders to check brake operation, the front brake fluid level and pad wear, and the rear brake shoe wear and free play. Honda Global's daily inspection advice says to check the brakes by pushing the scooter forward while holding each lever, and to check lever play.

| Check | What you do | What is worrying |
|---|---|---|
| Hold test | Hold each lever and push the scooter forward | Wheel rolls easily, lever sinks |
| Rear free play | Pull the rear lever gently | Almost no resistance, or no play at all |
| Fluid level | Look at the front reservoir on the right handlebar | Level below the LOWER mark |
| Pad wear | Look for the wear mark on the front pads | Pads worn to the indicator |
| Rear shoe wear | Pull the lever and look at the indicator mark on the brake plate | Mark reaches the reference mark |
| Free spin | Spin each wheel on the stand | Drag, rub or noise |

Honda says front pads are replaced as a pair, and that rear shoe wear is shown by an indicator on the brake plate. Do not attempt to adjust a combined brake cable yourself. The Vision manual warns that adjusting the cable is for an authorised Honda dealer.

## A safe test routine at the shop

1. Stay on the stand for the hold test, free-spin test and lever travel check.
2. Ask staff to point out the fluid reservoir and the wear indicators.
3. Move to a quiet, level, dry spot with no traffic and walk the scooter forward.
4. Brake gently with the rear lever, then the front, then both, and watch for pulling or noise.
5. Ride a short distance at a low speed and repeat, noticing whether the lever feel is the same each time.
6. Repeat once in the opposite direction on a flat road.

Never test a brake by braking hard at speed on a public road. ## Wet weather and heat

Brakes behave differently in rain. Honda's manuals say that wet brakes are less effective and that you should use them gently at low speed to dry them. In Hanoi's rainy periods allow a longer stopping distance and brake earlier. Our guide to [riding in Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) covers it. Honda also warns that continuous braking on a long descent can overheat the system, so use both brakes in short applications on hill roads.

## FAQ: uneven scooter brake feel

### Should the front and rear levers feel the same?

No. They are a hydraulic disc and a cable drum. What matters is that each is consistent and proportional.

### Is a pulsing front lever normal?

Only on an ABS model during a hard stop, according to the Air Blade manual. A pulse in gentle braking should be reported.

### How much free play should the rear lever have?

Honda's Vision and Air Blade manuals give 10 to 20 mm at the lever tip.

### What does a spongy lever mean?

Often air in the system, low fluid or worn pads. A mechanic must inspect it.

### Can I adjust the brakes myself?

Not on a rental. Honda says cable and fluid work is for a dealer or technician.

### Is a squeal always dangerous?

No, but grinding is. Report any new noise.
