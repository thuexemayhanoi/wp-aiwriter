If you are staying in a Hanoi hotel, guesthouse or serviced apartment and want to rent an electric scooter, settle the charging question before you book the room, not when the battery is low. Most guests end up with one of three options: charging the scooter at a socket in the lobby, courtyard or parking area under staff supervision; carrying a removable battery or small battery pack to a permitted spot; or skipping charging by choosing a lodging or a vehicle that does not need it. This guide approaches the problem from the guest's side of the desk, with the questions to ask the hotel, a short message you can copy, what to do when the answer is no and the basic habits that keep a charging session safe. It deliberately avoids repeating the fire-prevention and battery-swap detail in our general charging guide.

## Match the lodging to a charging method

Different kinds of accommodation give different answers, and knowing which you have saves a lot of back-and-forth.

| Lodging type | Typical charging situation | What to confirm |
|---|---|---|
| Small hotel with a ground-floor lobby | A socket near reception or the bike parking | Which socket, and who watches it |
| Guesthouse or homestay in an alley | Often a family-run space with a courtyard or hall | Whether the family is comfortable with it |
| Serviced apartment in a tower | Building rules, often strict | Whether the management allows charging at all |
| Rented flat in an apartment block | Depends on the building, not the host | Written permission from the host and the building |
| Larger hotel with a basement car park | Policies vary, and may exclude two-wheelers | Where electric bikes may be parked |

The reason apartment towers differ is that rules are still catching up. A VnExpress International report from December 2025 said that, without clear regulations for charging and parking electric vehicles, apartment complexes in Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City were struggling to reconcile residents' needs with fire-safety risks, and that many buildings made their own rules. It added that fire-safety regulations did not prohibit parking or charging electric vehicles indoors, but that the lack of specific guidelines had led many apartments to set their own policy. A hotel's answer is therefore usually a house rule, not a national one, so ask and accept the answer.

## Ask before you book the room

Put these questions to the property by message or email, ideally before you pay for a stay of several nights.

1. Can an electric scooter be parked on the premises overnight?
2. Is there a socket where it can be charged, and where exactly?
3. Is that spot indoors, covered or outside, and is it dry?
4. Will someone be around while it charges, such as reception staff?
5. Does the hotel allow a removable battery to be brought into a room?
6. Is there a charging fee, or a rule about how long it can charge?
7. Who should I speak to if there is a problem, and at what hours?

A hotel that answers all seven quickly is used to this and probably has a routine. A hotel that does not know the answers may still be fine, but give it time to check with the manager, because the person at the desk may not be the one who decides.

## A message you can copy

Keep it short and specific. Staff are more likely to say yes to a clear request than to a vague one.

> Hello, I will stay from [date] to [date] and plan to rent an electric scooter from a local shop. Could you tell me whether I can park it at the hotel and charge it? The charger is the one supplied with the bike. I would charge it only while someone is present, and never overnight or unattended. If charging is not allowed, please let me know so I can make other plans. Thank you.

The last two sentences matter. They show you understand the risk and make it easy for the hotel to give a simple answer.

## Where charging can happen

If the hotel agrees, the spot makes a difference to how comfortable everyone is.

- **A ground-floor lobby, courtyard or parking area with a socket.** This is the most common arrangement, and the best, because the scooter stays outdoors or near the entrance and staff can see it.
- **A room with a removable battery.** Some scooters and most electric bicycles have a battery that can be taken off and charged indoors. Many hotels are reluctant, and the decision is theirs. Do not carry a battery into a room without clear permission.
- **A staff area or storeroom.** Occasionally a hotel offers to charge it for you. Accept only if they are happy to, and agree the time when you will collect it.
- **A neighbouring cafe or shop.** Possible in a pinch, but only with the owner's permission, and never as a regular plan.

Avoid stairwells, corridors, fire exits and the area under a staircase. They are the places where a small fire does the most harm.

## How long a charge takes, and what that means for a guest

Charging times matter because they decide whether the scooter can be charged during the hours someone is present. VinFast's published specification for the Evo shows a full charge of one 1.5 kWh battery in about 4.5 hours, and about 9 hours for a two-battery set. A report on the Feliz S gives about 6 hours. Those are other models than the one you may be handed, so ask the shop for your bike's figure.

If a charge takes around 4 to 6 hours, a guest who returns to the hotel in the afternoon can plug in for the evening while staff are on duty, and unplug before going to bed. If it takes 9 hours or more, either a longer stay at the socket or a partial charge is needed. ## Safe charging habits for a guest

The US National Fire Protection Association (NFPA), whose guidance is for American users but applies in general terms, warns that e-bike and e-scooter battery fires have been associated with faulty charging equipment, improper charging practices and overloaded circuits. Its advice for people who already own or use such a device includes:

- **Do not keep charging once the battery is full.**
- **Never use charging equipment that did not come with the device.**
- **Stop using the device if the battery shows signs of damage,** such as an unusual smell or a change in colour.
- **Have repairs done by a qualified professional.**

The NFPA fire code, in the case of charging more than five devices indoors or near a building, also says that extension cords or power strips cannot be used, that charging cannot take place within 10 feet, about 3 metres, of combustible materials and that charging cannot block an exit. A single rented scooter is a different case, but those rules are a sensible template for a guest: plug straight into a wall socket, keep the area clear and never block a door.

In practice:

1. Use the charger supplied with the bike, never a substitute.
2. Plug directly into the wall, not through an extension reel or a multi-way adapter.
3. Keep the cable off beds, curtains and walkways.
4. Stay nearby, and stop the charge before you leave or sleep.
5. If the battery or charger gets unusually hot, swells, smells or looks damaged, unplug it and call the shop.

## What the rules may look like soon

Regulation is moving. A Vietnam.vn report on Circular 31/2026 from the Ministry of Construction says that, from 15 December 2026, apartment buildings must provide separate parking areas for electric cars, and sets limits on charging stations and charging power in underground parking, with monitoring and fire alarms. The Prime Minister has also directed ministries to develop national standards for charging in apartment buildings. These rules apply to apartment buildings, not to hotels, and the details are for the building's operator. For a guest, the practical result is that a serviced apartment or a rented flat may soon have a clearer, stricter policy than a small hotel. Ask the host to confirm the current rule in writing.

## If the hotel says no

A refusal is not a disaster, and it is better to hear it early.

- **Choose a different bike.** A petrol scooter needs no socket, and a bicycle with a small battery may be easier to accommodate.
- **Ask the shop.** Tell staff on 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969), open 09:00 to 21:00 daily, that your lodging cannot charge, and ask what they suggest. Do not assume the shop can charge it for you; ask.
- **Plan short days.** If you can start with a full battery and ride well within the usable range, you may not need to charge at all.
- **Change the lodging.** For a long stay, a guesthouse with a courtyard may serve you better than a tower.
- **Do not improvise.** No stairwells, no borrowed chargers, no charging while you sleep.

Parking is part of the same question, since a hotel that cannot charge may still park the bike safely. Our guide to [parking a motorbike in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-motorbike-in-hanoi/) explains guarded lots and tickets.

## Arrival checklist

- Tell reception that you have an electric scooter and ask where it should go.
- Photograph the battery display at arrival.
- Confirm the exact socket, and test it with the supplied charger while someone is watching.
- Agree the hours you will charge and who will check.
- Unplug before you leave or sleep.
- Photograph the battery before you return the bike.

## FAQ: charging as a hotel guest

### Will every hotel in Hanoi let me charge a scooter?

No. Policies vary by property, and some do not allow charging at all. Ask before you book.

### Can I bring the battery into my room?

Only if the hotel clearly allows it. Some bikes have removable batteries, but many hotels do not want them in rooms.

### Is it safe to charge overnight?

Local fire-prevention advice, covered in our general charging guide, discourages charging when nobody is present. Plan to charge in the evening while someone is around.

### Can I use an extension cord?

It is better not to. Plug the supplied charger straight into a wall socket, which is the NFPA's advice for indoor charging.

### What if my charger stops working?

Do not use a different charger. Call the shop.
