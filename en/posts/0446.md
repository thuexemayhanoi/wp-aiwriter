When you rent a motorbike, check what actually comes with it before you pay. At Nguyen Ha the published terms say one basic helmet per rider is included at no charge, that a raincoat is not part of short rentals but may be provided on a monthly rental, and that no separate anti-theft lock is provided, so you are expected to lock the steering and park in guarded places. Mirrors are part of the bike itself and are inspected at handover. A phone holder is not mentioned in the published terms, so if you want one, ask whether the shop offers it, or bring your own and mount it in a way that does not alter or damage the bike.

The rule that ties it all together is simple. Anything you are given should be written on the contract, photographed at handover and handed back at the end. This guide explains each item in turn, what to ask, how to check it, what you can and cannot add to the bike, who is responsible if an item is lost or damaged and how to return everything cleanly. It is general information, not legal advice.

## Why accessories cause disputes

A bike is the big item, and accessories are the small ones that are forgotten. A helmet left in a taxi, a mirror knocked at a parking spot, a phone holder that left a mark on the handlebar: none of these is large, but each can turn into an argument at return if nothing was written down at the start. Nguyen Ha's published terms say that before leaving the shop both sides should inspect brakes, tyres, lights, mirrors, bodywork, accessories and the fuel or charge level and record the handover time. That is the moment to settle what counts as an accessory.

## The helmet

The helmet is the one accessory that matters for safety, and the one most likely to be a poor fit.

- **Check the fit.** It should sit level on your head, feel snug without pain and not move when you shake your head. The chin strap should fasten and hold.
- **Check the condition.** Look at the shell and the inside for cracks, dents, loose padding and a worn strap. Our guide to [what to do if a helmet does not fit at pickup](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-to-do-if-a-helmet-does-not-fit-at-pickup/) explains what to ask for if it fails.
- **Ask about a passenger.** The published terms speak of one basic helmet per rider, so if someone rides with you, ask what is provided for them.
- **Wear it fastened,** every ride. Our guide to [helmet rules in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/helmet-rules-in-vietnam/) explains what riders and passengers must wear.
- **Photograph it** at handover, inside and out.

Our guides to [choosing a helmet that fits for a manual motorbike journey](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/choosing-a-helmet-that-fits-for-a-manual-motorbike-journey/) and [moped helmets: fit matters at lower speeds too](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/moped-helmets-fit-matters-at-lower-speeds-too/) explain why a basic helmet still has to fit.

If you prefer to bring your own helmet, tell the shop, because a helmet you own is yours to look after and nobody else can say what it looked like.

## The mirrors

Mirrors are part of the bike, not an optional extra, but they are among the parts most often loose, turned or cracked.

1. **Check that both are present.** A missing mirror should be written on the contract.
2. **Check that each is secure.** It should not wobble or drop when you press it.
3. **Check the glass.** Look for cracks, chips and a loose or fogged surface.
4. **Adjust them from the riding position.** Sit on the bike, on its wheels and not on the stand, and set each so that you see the lane behind you with only a small part of your own arm in view.
5. **Check them again after any stop** where the bike could have been knocked.

## The phone holder

Many visitors want a holder so that they can follow a map. The published terms do not mention one, so there are three possibilities.

- **The shop offers one.** Ask whether it is on the contract, how it fits and what happens if it is lost or damaged.
- **You bring your own.** Ask the shop before you attach it, because it may not want clamps or adhesive on its handlebar or panels.
- **You use none.** Plan the route before you set off, and stop to check it rather than looking down while riding.

If you do fit a holder:

1. **Fit it where it does not alter the bike.** Do not drill, cut or glue anything.
2. **Keep it clear of the controls, the instrument panel, the lights and the mirrors.**
3. **Make sure it is firm,** so the phone cannot fall into the wheel or the road.
4. **Remove it before return,** and check for marks.
5. **Photograph the mounting point** at handover and at return.

Keep your eyes on the road, and stop somewhere safe to look at the screen.

## The raincoat

The published terms say a raincoat is not part of short rentals but may be provided on a monthly rental. So:

- **For a short rental,** bring your own, or buy one. Our guide to [how to carry a raincoat and helmet on a small manual bike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-carry-a-raincoat-and-helmet-on-a-small-manual-bike/) explains where to stow it.
- **For a monthly rental,** ask whether one will be provided, and have it written on the contract.
- **Check that it is not too long,** because loose material can catch on a wheel or a footrest.

## The lock and keeping the bike secure

The published terms say no separate anti-theft lock is provided, and that you should lock the steering and park in guarded places.

- **Check the steering lock works** before you leave the shop. Turn the handlebar fully, turn the key and test that the lock holds.
- **Park in a guarded place** where possible. Our guide to [parking a motorbike in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-motorbike-in-hanoi/) explains guarded lots and tickets.
- **Do not leave valuables on the bike,** including the helmet, if you can carry it.
- **Take the key with you,** and keep it safe. ## Getting it written down

Anything handed to you should be on the contract.

1. **List each item:** key or keys, helmet or helmets, raincoat if provided, phone holder if provided, any other item.
2. **Note its condition:** "helmet, black, small scuff on the left" is better than "helmet".
3. **Photograph each item,** and the full set together.
4. **Ask who is responsible if one is lost or damaged,** and what the replacement cost would be. The published pages do not list prices for accessories, so ask and write down any amount.
5. **Keep your copy** of the contract and the photographs until the deposit has been returned.

## Who is responsible for an accessory

Vietnam's Civil Code (Law No. 91/2015/QH13), in force since 1 January 2017, says the lessee must preserve and maintain the leased property and compensate if it is lost or damaged, apart from natural wear (Article 479), and must return it in the condition in which it was received, apart from natural wear (Article 482). We read the lease articles in the text published by the Tuyen Quang Department of Justice. As of October 2026, the general position is that an item you were given with the bike is part of what you must look after and return. The contract decides the detail, including any amount for a lost item. This is general information, not legal advice.

In practice, that means:

- **A helmet that is lost or badly damaged** is likely to be your cost.
- **Ordinary wear,** such as a strap that has softened with use, is not.
- **A mirror broken by a fall** is damage, to be assessed by its cause and the contract.
- **A mark from a phone holder you fitted** is damage you caused, so avoid it.

The published pages say that damage from a collision, an accident or misuse is priced by its cause, the actual condition of the bike and the contract, and that the renter may bear a cost in proportion to the real damage.

## What to do at return

At return, hand back everything on your list.

1. **Gather the items** before you arrive: key or keys, helmets, any raincoat, any holder.
2. **Remove your own holder,** and check for marks.
3. **Empty the under-seat compartment.**
4. **Photograph the items** with the bike, as in our guide to [what to photograph when returning a rental scooter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-to-photograph-when-returning-a-rental-scooter/).
5. **Go through the list with staff,** and ask for it to be marked as returned.
6. **Ask for any charge to be itemised** with its reason.

## FAQ: rental motorbike accessories

### Is a helmet included?

Yes. The published terms say one basic helmet per rider is included at no charge. Ask what is provided for a passenger.

### Is a raincoat included?

Not for short rentals. The published terms say it may be provided on a monthly rental.

### Does the shop provide a lock?

No separate anti-theft lock is provided. Lock the steering and park in guarded places.

### Can I use my own phone holder?

The published terms do not mention one. Ask the shop before you fit it, and do not drill, cut or glue anything.

### What if I lose a helmet or a mirror breaks?

Report it at once. The Civil Code says a lessee must look after the property and compensate for loss or damage, and the contract decides the amount. Ask for any cost to be itemised.

### Should I bring my own helmet?

You may, and some riders prefer to. Tell the shop, and make sure it fits and is fastened.
