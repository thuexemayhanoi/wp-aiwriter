If you have no motorcycle licence, both an electric bicycle and a 50cc scooter can be ridden legally in Vietnam, but they are different vehicles for different visitors. An electric bicycle has working pedals, a motor that stops assisting at 25 km/h, and no plate or minimum age in the text of the law. A 50cc scooter is a petrol moped with a registration plate, a minimum rider age of 16 and a 40 km/h limit. Choose the bicycle if you are happy on a pedal bike and want the gentlest start; choose the moped if you want a motor that does the work and can keep up with traffic. This guide compares them for a visitor, as of October 2026, and notes where Nguyen Ha's published information stops and the shop's answer begins.

## The two vehicles side by side

| | Electric bicycle | 50cc petrol scooter |
|---|---|---|
| Legal class | Motor-assisted bicycle, a non-motorised vehicle | Moped (xe gan may), a motor vehicle |
| Driving licence | None required | No licence class exists for the moped class, so none required |
| Minimum age | None in the law; children under 7 must be accompanied | 16 |
| Number plate | No | Yes, the vehicle is registered |
| Motor | Assists only while you pedal, cuts out at 25 km/h | Petrol engine up to 50cc, design speed up to 50 km/h |
| Speed cap on the road | About 25 km/h from the assist | 40 km/h for mopeds under Circular 38/2024/TT-BGTVT |
| Helmet | Required | Required |
| Energy | Battery, plus your legs | Petrol |
| Effort | Pedalling with help | Twist and go, or a small gearbox on Cub types |

The "no licence" row needs a caution. It describes Vietnamese traffic law for these two classes. It does not mean every shop will hand over any bike without questions, and it does not mean a bike labelled "50cc" is one, as the section on mislabelled bikes explains.

## What the law says, in brief

Law 36/2024/QH15, in force since 1 January 2025, defines the moped, called xe gan may, as a two- or three-wheeler with a design speed of no more than 50 km/h and, if it has a petrol engine, a displacement of no more than 50 cm3. An electric moped is the same class if its motor is no more than 4 kW. The law excludes motor-assisted bicycles from this definition, and treats them, electric ones included, as bicycles whose motor assistance stops when the rider stops pedalling or at 25 km/h.

Article 59 of the law says a person aged 16 or over may ride a moped. The licence classes in Article 57 begin with class A1 for two-wheel motorcycles up to 125 cm3 or an electric motor up to 11 kW, and the sources summarising the law say that mopeds, as the lower class, need no licence. For the speed, Circular 38/2024/TT-BGTVT, effective 1 January 2025, sets a maximum of 40 km/h for mopeds and similar vehicles on roads other than expressways. Our guide to [renting a 50cc scooter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/renting-a-50cc-scooter-in-hanoi/) covers these points from the moped side, and the one on [electric bikes in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/electric-bikes-in-hanoi-rules/) covers the bicycle side.

## Which visitor are you?

Rules do not decide this. Your own riding history does.

### You have never ridden anything with a motor

Choose the electric bicycle if you are comfortable on an ordinary bicycle. Its speed is limited by design, it is light, and you control it with the same skills you already have. A moped is easier than a manual motorbike, but it still asks you to manage throttle, braking and balance at traffic speed with a motor you have never felt. Our guide to [crossing Hanoi intersections](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/crossing-hanoi-intersections/) is worth reading before either.

### You ride scooters at home, but not a licensed class here

Choose the 50cc moped, or an electric moped, if you are used to a throttle and want the motor to do the work. A rider who already uses a small scooter at home may find the moped natural. It carries a bag, climbs a bridge without effort and does not need you to pedal in Hanoi's heat. It will not keep up with a 110cc scooter, so ride at the speed it gives you and keep right.

### You are 16 or 17, or travelling with young riders

The law lets a 16-year-old ride a moped. In 2026 a government decree on traffic penalties, Decree 238/2026/ND-CP, took effect on 15 August 2026, and the Thu Vien Phap Luat portal explains that it did not ban 16- and 17-year-olds from mopeds or electric mopeds. A shop may nevertheless set stricter conditions for younger renters, so ask before you plan around it. An electric bicycle has no age rule in the law, though a child under seven must be accompanied by an adult on the road.

### You have an international licence that does not cover motorcycles

You are in the same position as a visitor with no licence: the moped and the electric bicycle are the lawful options. Our guide to [motorbike licence rules for foreigners](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbike-licence-rules-for-foreigners-in-vietnam/) explains what a licence and an International Driving Permit must cover.

## Speed and Hanoi traffic

The 25 km/h cut-out on an electric bicycle and the 40 km/h moped limit are the two numbers to compare with Hanoi's flow. In the centre, streets are slow and a bicycle fits in. On the wider roads, dyke roads and bridges, faster traffic passes both vehicles. Neither is built for fast roads, and neither should be on expressways or the faster ring-road stretches. Our guides to [speed limits for motorbikes](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/speed-limits-motorbikes-vietnam/) and [ring roads and highways](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-ring-roads-highways-motorbikes-allowed/) explain the roads.

A practical difference is how each behaves when it is slower than the traffic around it. A moped at its 40 km/h limit sits near the right edge and lets faster vehicles pass. A bicycle does the same at a lower speed and needs more attention to junctions, because a car or scooter turning across you has less time to see you. If that makes you nervous, the moped's extra speed is a safety margin as well as a convenience.

## Effort, hills and heat

A bicycle asks for your legs. The motor helps on hills, bridges and headwinds, but on a hot day you still sweat, and the battery drains faster if you lean on the highest assist. A moped asks for nothing but attention. If you are travelling in the hot months or you tire quickly, that matters. If you want exercise or a quiet ride around a lake, the bicycle is the pleasanter vehicle.

## Fuel and charging

A 50cc moped needs petrol. At Nguyen Ha, petrol bikes are handed over with about 0.5 to 2 litres and come back with about 0.5 to 1 litre, and an empty tank at return costs VND 20,000. Our guide to [fuel in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/fuel-in-hanoi-petrol-stations-rental-scooter/) explains stations and filling up.

An electric bicycle needs a socket. The shop confirms battery level and charging at handover. For a short rental you may never plug it in; for a longer one you will, and our guide to [charging an electric scooter in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/charging-an-electric-scooter-in-hanoi/) explains the options.

## Weekend evenings in the Old Quarter

Hanoi began piloting a low-emission zone on 1 July 2026, and the reports differ on the details, so read the signs on the day. VOV described the launch as covering a zone that includes the Hoan Kiem Lake walking area, the Old Quarter and the night market, with all motorbikes, motorcycles and cars banned from 7 p.m. to midnight on Fridays, Saturdays and Sundays. Earlier proposals were framed as limits on petrol motorbikes on 11 streets in Hoan Kiem, from 6 p.m. on Fridays and from 6 a.m. on weekends, and AFP reported in May 2026 that the plan had been scaled back.

The safe reading for a visitor is this. If you want to ride in the Old Quarter on a weekend evening, expect a petrol moped to be restricted. VOV's description would cover electric motorbikes too, while the earlier proposals named only petrol ones, so an electric moped is not a safe workaround. Ask the shop, and check the signs. An electric bicycle also has the advantage that you can push it. Our guide to [riding in the Old Quarter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-old-quarter/) explains when to walk instead.

## Papers, helmets and insurance

The shop checks and photographs identity documents for the contract and keeps no originals. If you have no licence, say so when you book, so the shop can offer a bike in a class you may ride. One basic helmet per rider is included free, and our guide to [helmet rules in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/helmet-rules-in-vietnam/) explains the standard.

Travel insurance is a separate question. Many policies pay for a motorbike accident only if the rider held the licence the policy requires, and that can apply even when the law does not require one. Read the clause before you leave home. Our guide to [travel insurance and motorbike rentals](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/travel-insurance-motorbike-rental-vietnam/) explains what is usually not covered.

## When the label is wrong

A bike described as "no licence needed" is only in the licence-free class if its engine and design speed fit the definitions above. A 110cc scooter sold as a 50cc is a motorcycle, and riding it without the right licence is a problem for you and the shop. An "electric bike" with a flat floor and no pedals is a moped or motorcycle, not a bicycle. Ask to see the registration paper and the maker's specification for the actual bike, and read the engine size or motor power from the paper, not from a sticker.

## A five-question decision

1. Can you ride a bicycle confidently in light traffic? If not, the moped is probably easier.
2. Do you want a motor that does the work in the heat? If so, choose a moped.
3. Do you plan routes that stay quiet and short? If so, a bicycle is enough.
4. Will you ride on weekend evenings in the Old Quarter? If so, check the zone rules first.
5. Will you keep the vehicle for more than a few days? If so, ask the shop about weekly pricing and about fuel or charging arrangements.

If you are still unsure, ring Nguyen Ha on 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969), open 09:00 to 21:00 daily, and say plainly that you have no licence.

## FAQ: electric bicycle or 50cc scooter without a licence

### Can I ride a 50cc scooter in Vietnam without a licence?

Under Law 36/2024/QH15, the moped class has no licence class, and a person aged 16 or over may ride one. Every other rule of the road still applies.

### Can I ride an electric bicycle without a licence?

Yes. It is classed with bicycles, with no plate and no minimum age in the law, but a helmet is required.

### Which is safer for a beginner?

It depends on your skills. A bicycle is slower and lighter; a moped is faster and easier on the legs. Start on quiet roads either way.

### Does an electric scooter count as an electric bicycle?

Only if it has working pedals and the assist stops at 25 km/h. A pedal-less scooter is a moped or motorcycle.

### Is a 50cc scooter allowed in the Old Quarter on weekends?

Check the low-emission zone signs and ask the shop, because reports about the restrictions differ.
