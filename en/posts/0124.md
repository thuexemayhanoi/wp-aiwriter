Honda Vietnam lists the current Air Blade 125 and 160 with a seat height of 775 mm, a wheelbase of 1,286 mm and a weight of 110 to 113 kg depending on version, which makes it a mid-sized, planted scooter and not a tiny one. 

Those numbers describe the scooter, not you, so a rental fit test has to cover two things: whether your feet reach the ground at a stop, and whether your arms, hands and eyes reach the grips, levers, switches and mirrors without strain. This guide gives the published figures, explains what each control does to your reach and offers a routine of about ten minutes to run before you sign. The shop confirms the exact scooter at booking, so treat the figures as a guide.

## Seat height: the foot-down part of the test

The figure of 775 mm is measured to the seat on a stationary scooter. Your feet have to reach the road past a seat that is padded, tapered and wide at the back. Run the quick version:

1. Wear your riding shoes.
2. Take the scooter off its stand. Honda's manual says to board from the left with at least one foot on the ground.
3. Sit upright and put both feet down. Note whether you are flat-footed, on the balls of the feet or on one toe.
4. Roll a metre, brake and stop. Can you hold it level?

If you are borderline, the reach test below matters more, because a comfortable upper body can make up for slightly limited foot reach, and the reverse is rarely true. The Vision comparison above covers the foot-down side for shorter riders too.

## Reach is the part spec sheets leave out

Honda's panel lists length, width, height and wheelbase but not the distance from seat to grips, the angle of the levers or the position of the mirrors. You have to find those on the scooter itself. Five reach questions decide whether a scooter suits you on a longer ride:

- **Grips.** With your back upright and your shoulders relaxed, can you hold both grips with your elbows slightly bent? Arms fully straight or pulled in too tight both signal a poor fit.
- **Levers.** Can you pull both brake levers fully with your hand on the grip, without letting go or shifting your wrist? Short fingers often struggle to reach the lever tip.
- **Switches.** Can your thumbs reach the turn signal, horn, starter and the idling stop switch without moving your grip?
- **Mirrors.** Can you see the lane behind you without leaning out? Our guide to [adjusting mirrors on a rental bike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-adjust-mirrors-on-a-honda-wave-rental/) describes the method for any scooter.
- **Legs and floor.** Can you place your feet on the floor with your knees comfortably apart, and not jammed against the front panel?

None of these needs a tool. They need you in the seat, in your shoes, wearing the helmet you plan to ride in.

## The levers: why the left hand matters on this scooter

On an automatic scooter there is no clutch, so the left lever works the rear drum brake and the right lever works the front disc. Honda's Air Blade manual states a free play for the rear lever of 10 to 20 mm at the lever tip, and it explains that the scooter has a combined braking system. Because both hands have a brake job, lever reach matters twice.

Check that you can pull each lever with two or three fingers and still keep a firm hold of the grip. Then put the scooter on its stand and pull each lever until it bites, noting the distance. A lever that reaches close to the grip before it works is not a fit problem but a brake adjustment issue, and the shop should be told about it. If it is a reach problem, a small hand may need a different scooter.

## A ten-minute rental fit test, in order

Run these steps on the scooter you are being given, not on a sample.

1. **Stand-side check (2 minutes).** With the scooter on its stand, sit and hold the grips. Check elbows, shoulders and lever reach as above. Turn the handlebar fully left and right to feel the limits.
2. **Mirror and view check (1 minute).** Set the mirrors so you can see the lane behind without leaning. Do it with your helmet on.
3. **Off the stand (1 minute).** Lift it off the stand and walk it a few steps. Honda's manual tells riders to release the rear brake lock before moving off.
4. **Foot-down check (1 minute).** Both feet down, then one foot, as you would at a junction.

5. **Slow-speed handling (2 minutes).** In a quiet area ride a slow figure of eight and a gentle U-turn. A scooter that fits you turns without you straining to reach or leaning your body.
6. **Brake and control check (1 minute).** At low speed apply each lever, then both. Do not test hard braking on a public road.
7. **Passenger check, if relevant (1 minute).** If you will carry a passenger, check that you still reach the grips with someone behind you. Our guide to [carrying a passenger and luggage on a scooter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/scooter-for-two-riders-and-luggage-hanoi/) covers the load side.
8. **Decide.** If two or more steps feel wrong, ask for another scooter before you ride off.

## Pushing, parking and the heavier body

The Air Blade's published weight is higher than the Vision's, and the difference shows in the moments you handle it without the engine. Practise rolling it backwards out of a parking row, turning it on the spot and putting it on its stand. If you cannot do these calmly, you will find every parking stop stressful, and our guide to [parking a motorbike in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-motorbike-in-hanoi/) shows how often Hanoi asks you to do exactly that.

## Posture and what it says

A good fit is easy to recognise from outside. Your head is up, your shoulders are loose, your elbows are slightly bent and your wrists are straight. You do not grip hard, lean forward, reach for the levers or stretch with your toes.

Signs of a poor fit show within the first kilometre: tension in the neck, pressure on the palms, numb fingers, a dragging rear lever hand, an urge to shift forward and back on the seat. If these show up, stop and adjust or swap scooters rather than endure them. A day in Hanoi traffic is long, and discomfort invites mistakes.

## How the shape of the scooter affects the fit

Honda describes the 2026 Air Blade as a modern, sporty design. Honda's own scooter article says that scooters give a relaxed riding posture with legs forward, but body panels and floor shape differ between scooters and versions, so how the front panel sits against your knees is something to test and not to assume from photographs.

The seat is also part of the equation. Check whether you slide forward when braking or backward when accelerating, because a seat that holds you poorly makes the reach feel longer.

## Wet days, night riding and long distances

Fit matters more as conditions get harder. In rain, gloves and wet grips change how you hold the levers, and a wet road makes slow-speed balance more important. Our guide to [riding in Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) covers it. At night, you look further ahead and need your mirrors set correctly, as in the guide to [riding at night in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-at-night-in-hanoi/). For longer rides such as a day trip out of the city, minor discomfort grows quickly, so run the fit test even if you have ridden before.

## FAQ: Air Blade seat height and reach

### What is the Air Blade seat height?

Honda Vietnam lists 775 mm for the Air Blade 125 and 160.

### Is the Air Blade heavier than a Vision?

Honda Vietnam lists 110 to 113 kg for the Air Blade, against 97 kg for the current Vision.

### Can I adjust the handlebar or levers?

Not on a rental. The mirrors can be adjusted, so ask for help with them.

### How do I know if the reach is too long?

Straight arms, tension in the shoulders, trouble pulling the levers or sliding forward on the seat all signal a poor fit.

### Is a 160 different from a 125 for fit?

Honda lists the same seat height and wheelbase, so the fit is similar. The 160 is a few kilograms heavier.
