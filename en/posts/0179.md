Two different age rules apply when a young traveller wants to rent a 50cc bike in Hanoi, and they do not have to match. The legal minimum for riding a moped in Vietnam is 16, set by Article 59 of Law 36/2024/QH15, in force since 1 January 2025. A rental shop is a private business, and it can ask for more than the law requires, such as a minimum age of 18 or a signature from a parent or guardian for anyone younger. As of October 2026 the safe approach for a family is to treat 16 as the point at which riding becomes legally possible and the shop's terms as the point at which renting becomes actually possible. This guide explains both layers, what changes at 18, what the penalties are for getting it wrong and what to ask a shop before you book.

## The legal layer: what the law sets

Law 36/2024/QH15 sets age limits by type of vehicle and licence:

- **16 years or older:** may ride a moped (Article 59, paragraph 1a). A moped is a two- or three-wheeler with a design speed of no more than 50 km/h and, with a petrol engine, no more than 50 cm3, or, with an electric motor, no more than 4 kW (Article 34).
- **18 years or older:** may be issued a licence of class A1, A, B1, B or C1 (Article 59, paragraph 1b). Class A1 covers motorcycles up to 125 cm3, which includes the ordinary 110 and 125 cc rental scooters.

Between those two ages sits a gap that surprises many families. A 16- or 17-year-old can ride a moped, but cannot be issued a licence for a motorcycle, so a 110 cc scooter is out of reach until 18 however experienced the rider is. Foreign licences do not change this, because the rule concerns the vehicle class and the age requirement of the licence. Article 56, paragraph 4 adds that a moped rider must understand the traffic rules and be able to control the vehicle, and Article 59, paragraph 2 requires the health conditions suited to the vehicle.

There is no upper age limit for a moped in the law, only the health condition, so older travellers are not excluded by age.

## What happens below 16, and between 16 and 18

Published summaries of Decree 168/2024/ND-CP set out the consequences for riders who are too young:

- **Aged 14 to under 16:** a warning for riding a motorcycle or moped.
- **Aged 16 to under 18, on a motorcycle above 50 cm3 or an electric machine of 4 kW or more:** a fine of VND 400,000 to 600,000.

Decree 238/2026/ND-CP, in force from 15 August 2026, changed the wording of the second rule from engine-size figures to "motorcycles and similar vehicles". Some reports read that as extending the fine to every two-wheeler. The Traffic Police Department responded that the change aligns terminology with the traffic law and does not alter what is penalised, so 16- to 17-year-olds may still ride mopeds but not motorcycles. This is a recent change and the wording of the amendment is not always reported consistently, so check current guidance if the question matters to your trip.

## The owner's side: why shops are careful

A rental shop owns the bike, and the law does not leave the owner out. Decree 168/2024/ND-CP provides a fine of VND 8,000,000 to 10,000,000 for an individual who owns a motorbike and hands it to a person who is not entitled to ride it, with a higher amount for an organisation. Published summaries note this applies to giving a bike to someone who is under age as well as to someone without the right licence.

That explains much of what a shop does at the counter: checking an identity document for a date of birth, asking for a licence on larger bikes and in some cases declining to rent to young riders at all. It is not unfriendliness. It is the owner managing a legal risk that falls on the owner.

## The commercial layer: what a shop can add

On top of the legal minimum, a shop can set conditions for its own reasons. Common examples include:

| Shop condition | Typical reason |
|---|---|
| A higher minimum age, such as 18 | A simpler rule, and no question about contracts with minors |
| A parent or guardian signing for a renter under 18 | The contract is easier to enforce with an adult party |
| An identity document showing the date of birth | The shop must be able to verify the age |
| A licence on every bike above 50cc | Avoids the owner's penalty |
| A shorter or more supervised first rental for young renters | Practical risk management |

## Why a signature from a parent or guardian is common

Vietnam's Civil Code treats people under 18 as minors. In general, a person from six to under eighteen needs the consent of a legal representative, usually a parent, to enter into and perform a civil transaction, with an exception for the small transactions of daily life that suit the age group. A rental contract with a deposit is not a small daily purchase, so a shop that wants a contract it can stand behind will commonly ask a parent or guardian to sign. This is a general principle, not advice on any particular contract, but it explains why a shop might accept a 16-year-old only with a parent present.

For a family travelling together, this is usually easy: the parent signs, carries the papers and stays involved. For a teenager travelling with a school group or friends, it may not be possible, and the realistic answer may be no rental.

## Four situations and what usually follows

1. **A 15-year-old.** Not legally permitted to ride any motorbike or moped in Vietnam. Choose another way to get around, such as walking, a taxi or a guided tour.
2. **A 16- or 17-year-old with a parent.** Legally permitted to ride a genuine moped. The shop may agree if the parent signs and the bike is a real 50cc. A 110 cc scooter is not an option.
3. **A 16- or 17-year-old travelling without an adult.** Legally permitted on a moped, but many shops will not rent without a guardian's signature. Ask in advance.
4. **An adult of 18 or over.** The law allows a moped from 16 and a licensed motorcycle from 18, and the shop's licence and identity checks apply. ## Before a teenager rides: a safety conversation

Meeting the age threshold is not the same as being ready for Hanoi traffic. A young rider who has never ridden on a busy road will find junctions, buses and scooters coming from every angle demanding. Parents should consider:

- **Practice first.** A quiet area such as the riverside near Long Bien or an empty car park is a better place to learn than a crowded street. Our guide to [Long Bien and the Red River as a first ride](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/long-bien-red-river-first-ride/) describes a gentle start.
- **A properly fitting helmet.** The helmet rule applies to mopeds, and a loose helmet is little protection. Our guide to [helmet rules in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/helmet-rules-in-vietnam/) explains what must be worn.
- **The speed ceiling.** A moped is limited to 40 km/h outside expressways. Our guide to [speed limits for motorbikes in Vietnam, including mopeds](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/speed-limits-motorbikes-vietnam/) covers it.
- **Daylight and short routes.** Avoid night riding and long journeys until the rider is confident.
- **The alternative.** A parent can ride while the teenager is a passenger, and a guided tour or a walk may be a better choice for the first day.

## FAQ: rider age and shop age rules

### What is the minimum age to ride a 50cc bike in Vietnam?

16, under Article 59 of Law 36/2024/QH15. The vehicle must be a genuine moped: a design speed of no more than 50 km/h and an engine of no more than 50 cm3, or an electric motor of no more than 4 kW.

### Can a 17-year-old rent a 110 cc scooter?

No. A licence for the class that covers 110 and 125 cc scooters, A1, is issued from 18, so a 17-year-old cannot legally ride one, whatever licence they hold at home.

### Can a shop refuse to rent to a 16-year-old even though the law allows it?

Yes. A shop can set its own minimum age or ask for a guardian's signature. The law sets the minimum for riding, and the shop decides whether to enter a rental contract.

### Does a foreign teenager follow different rules?

No. The Vietnamese age rule applies to anyone riding on Vietnamese roads, and a home country that permits younger riders does not change it.

### Is there a maximum age for a 50cc rental?

The law sets no upper age for riding a moped, only the health conditions suited to the vehicle. A shop may ask about experience or fitness in practice.
