If a tyre loses air while you are riding, grip the handlebars firmly, keep the bike straight, ease off the throttle gradually, avoid hard braking, let the bike slow on its own, signal and steer gently to a safe place at the edge of the road, then stop and switch off. Published motorcycle guidance describes the signs as a wobble, heavier or vaguer steering, a humming noise and a loss of power, and warns that braking hard or over-correcting the wobble is what usually causes the crash. A flat front tyre makes steering difficult, and a flat rear tyre makes the back feel unstable and reduces drive. Do not carry on riding on a flat tyre.

This guide explains the warning signs, the steps to take in the first seconds, where to stop under Law 36/2024/QH15, what to do afterwards and how to contact the shop, how repairs usually work, how to avoid the problem with a daily check and what to ask before you rent. It is general information based on published riding guidance and the law as of October 2026, not an engineering opinion.

## Warning signs

A tyre rarely goes from fully inflated to flat in an instant, though a blowout can be sudden. Watch for these signs.

- **A wobble or weave** at the back or the front that you do not cause.

- **Steering that feels heavy or vague,** especially at the front.

- **A humming or flapping sound,** or a thump with each wheel turn.

- **A loss of power,** because an underinflated tyre has more rolling resistance.

- **The bike pulls to one side** or leans oddly in a turn.

- **A soft feel** when you hit a bump.

- **A visible bulge or a nail or screw** in the tread when you stop.

If you notice any of these, begin to slow down gently and look for a place to stop. Riding even a short way on a deflating tyre can damage the wheel and make the bike harder to control.

## What to do in the first seconds

Published guidance for motorcyclists is consistent on the main points.

1. **Stay calm.** Panic leads to sudden movements, and sudden movements cause crashes.

2. **Hold the handlebars firmly but not rigidly.** Keep your arms and body loose enough to move with the bike. Do not fight the wobble. One guide notes that it is usually the rider's over-correction, not the tyre, that causes loss of control.

3. **Keep the bike straight and upright.** Do not try to steer sharply.

4. **Ease off the throttle gradually,** and let the bike slow through its own rolling resistance. Do not close the throttle abruptly.

5. **Avoid hard braking.** One guide says to stay off the brakes at first, and that a deflated tyre can unseat from the rim if braked hard. Another says to apply the brakes only gently, once speed has dropped a lot. If you must brake, use gentle, even pressure, with the working wheel doing most of the work.

6. **Signal and look.** Check your mirror, use your indicator or a hand signal, and keep an eye on traffic behind you.

7. **Steer gently towards the edge,** to a place where you can stop safely.

8. **Come to a stop,** put the stand down on firm ground and switch off the engine.

With a flat front tyre, steering is the problem, so keep your grip firm and your movements small. With a flat rear tyre, the back may sway and the bike may lose drive, so keep the bike straight and let it slow down.

## Where to stop

Stop where you are safe from traffic and not obstructing it. Article 18 of Law 36/2024/QH15 bans stopping and parking on pedestrian crossings, within 5 metres of the edge of intersections, on bridges, near bends with obscured visibility, under overpasses and in many other places. It also says that if a technical incident forces a vehicle to stop on part of the carriageway or in a place not designated for parking, the operator shall send out an emergency light signal or place a warning sign behind the vehicle. Our guide to [where to stop safely after a motorbike breaks down](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/where-to-stop-safely-after-a-motorbike-breaks-down/) explains how to choose the spot, and our guide to [rental bike breakdown in Hanoi: what to do](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-bike-breakdown-hanoi-what-to-do/) covers the wider steps.

If you are on a bridge, in a tunnel or on a bend, keep going slowly to a safer place if you can do so without risk, since the law bans stopping on a bridge except where allowed. Do not ride far on a flat tyre.

## After you stop

1. **Switch on the hazard lights** if the bike has them, and keep yourself and any passenger off the traffic side.

2. **Look at the tyre.** Check for a nail, a screw, a cut, a bulge, a valve problem or a bent rim.

3. **Do not ride on.** A flat tyre can destroy the tyre and the wheel and leave you with no control. One published guide says you should never drive with a flat tyre, even if it has been plugged, as it can lead to a blowout.

4. **Call the shop.** Nguyen Ha can be reached on 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969), open 09:00 to 21:00 daily. The shop's published pages say to contact the shop if there is a mechanical issue, puncture, accident or unexpected damage, and not to assume the cost or repair approach without checking the agreement. They do not publish a recovery fee or a repair price for a puncture, so ask what applies before you act.

5. **Photograph the tyre, the damage and your location,** and note the time. Our guide to [how to report damage that appears during your rental](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-report-damage-that-appears-during-your-rental/) explains how.

6. **Decide on a repair plan with the shop.** Options may include a nearby repair stall, the shop sending someone, or recovery by a vehicle. Do not agree to a price or to a repair that the shop has not approved, and keep any receipt.

7. **Push the bike, do not ride it,** if you must move it a short distance, and only on the road edge or a footpath that you may use on foot, with the engine off. Our guide to [how to avoid riding on footpaths and pedestrian streets](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-avoid-riding-on-footpaths-and-pedestrian-streets/) explains the difference between pushing and riding.

## How repairs usually work

Whether a tyre can be repaired depends on its type and the damage.

- **Tubeless tyres** can often be repaired with a plug or patch for a small puncture in the tread, according to published guidance, though a plug is a temporary measure and a shop should check the tyre afterwards.

- **Tyres with inner tubes** need the tube patched or replaced, which means taking the wheel off. That is hard to do at the roadside, so roadside help may be needed.

- **Sidewall damage, bulges and large cuts** usually mean the tyre must be replaced.

- **Sealants and spray repairs** are sometimes advised against, because they can make later repair harder.

Do not try to repair a rental yourself unless the shop tells you to. ## Prevent it: the daily tyre check

Published motorcycle guidance says that poor inflation is the most frequent cause of tyre failure, and that regular checks of pressure and general condition prevent most trouble. Honda's maintenance guidance on tyres likewise covers checking condition and pressure.

1. **Look at both tyres** for nails, screws, glass, cuts, cracks, bulges and uneven wear.

2. **Check the pressure,** if you can, with a gauge, or ask a tyre stall to check it when the tyres are cold. Ask the shop for the right pressure for your bike and load, and for two people. Our guide to [how to read a Honda Wave tyre size and pressure label](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-read-a-honda-wave-tyre-size-and-pressure-label/) shows where it is printed.

3. **Check the valve caps** are in place.

4. **Look at the tread depth.** A worn tyre is more likely to puncture and slips more in the wet. Our guide to [choosing tyres for a manual bike rental in wet weather](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/choosing-tyres-for-a-manual-bike-rental-in-wet-weather/) explains.

5. **Press the tyre with your thumb.** It should feel firm, not soft.

6. **Record the condition at handover** with photographs.

7. **Avoid debris,** such as glass, nails, potholes and roadworks. Our guides to [dealing with potholes on a rental motorbike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/dealing-with-potholes-on-a-rental-motorbike/) and [how to ride near construction sites and temporary barriers](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-ride-near-construction-sites-and-temporary-barriers/) explain how.

8. **Do not overload the bike.** Excess weight stresses tyres.

9. **Check again after a long ride or a hard knock.**

Older tyres are more likely to fail. One article notes that manufacturers often recommend replacing tyres at about five years old, and that old rubber is more prone to punctures. You cannot judge a rental tyre's age easily, so ask the shop and look for cracks.

2. **Grip firmly, keep straight, ease off the throttle, no hard braking.**

3. **Signal, look behind, steer gently to a safe place.**

4. **Stop lawfully, away from crossings, junctions and bridges; switch on hazards.**

5. **Do not ride on a flat tyre.**

6. **Photograph the damage, and call the shop before agreeing any repair.**

7. **Check tyres, pressure and valves before every ride.**

8. **Report cracks, bulges and worn tread at handover.**

## FAQ: a tyre losing air

### Should I brake hard if my tyre goes flat?

No. Published guidance says to ease off the throttle and slow gradually, avoiding hard braking, since a deflated tyre can unseat or the bike can skid. Use gentle braking only once the speed has dropped a lot.

### Can I ride to a repair shop on a flat tyre?

No. It can damage the tyre and the wheel and make the bike unsafe. Push the bike if it is a short distance, or ask the shop to arrange recovery.

### Who pays for a puncture on a rental?

The shop's published pages do not say, and tell you to check the agreement. Call before you pay for anything, and keep the receipt.

### Is a front or a rear flat worse?

Both are dangerous. A front flat affects steering and a rear flat makes the back unstable and reduces power.

### Can a puncture be repaired at the roadside?

A small tread puncture in a tubeless tyre can sometimes be plugged, but it is a temporary fix, and a tyre with an inner tube is harder to repair. Ask the shop.

### How often should I check the tyres?

Before every ride, and again after a long ride or a hard knock.
