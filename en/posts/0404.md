Check three things before you travel and get each one in writing: the shop's hours on your actual pickup date, the exact door or alley where the bike is handed over, and a phone number that is answered when you arrive. For Nguyen Ha Motorbike Rental the published hours are 09:00 to 21:00 daily and the address is Alley 5 Nguyen Van Cu Street, Bo De Ward, Long Bien District, Hanoi, near Gia Lam bus station, but a careful renter still confirms the date, the arrival time and the pin on the map. Listings go out of date, holidays change hours and Vietnamese alley addresses are easy to mistype into a map app. Ten minutes of checking at home saves an hour of wandering with luggage.

## The three facts to confirm, and where to get each

Most pickup problems come from three small gaps: an hours listing that was right last month, an address typed into a map without its ward or district, and nobody answering the phone at the door. Close each gap with a source you trust.

| Fact | Where to look first | Second check | What a good answer looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opening hours on your date | The shop's own contact page | A short message asking about your date | A reply that names the date and the time window |
| Pickup point | The address on the shop's contact page | The map pin, compared with the written address | The same street, ward and district in both |
| A working phone | The number on the shop's own pages | A call or message a day before | Someone answers or replies within a few hours |

Treat any one source as a claim and two matching sources as a fact. If they disagree, the shop's own contact page and a direct message beat a third-party listing.

## How to read an opening-hours listing

Map listings are convenient, but they are only as current as the last time someone edited them. Google's own help for business owners explains that hours entered on a Business Profile describe a normal week, and that unusual days such as holidays or special events are meant to be set separately as special hours. It also notes that on certain holidays a warning can appear telling users that hours might differ. In other words, a listing that shows a regular weekly pattern does not by itself prove that the shop is open on a public holiday.

Look for three things in a listing:

- **The day-by-day pattern.** Check that your pickup day appears and that it is not marked closed.
- **Any holiday or special-hours notice.** If you see one, ask the shop directly.
- **How recent the information feels.** Recent reviews, recent photos and a reachable phone number all point to a live listing.

The shop's own pages are the better reference. Nguyen Ha publishes 09:00 to 21:00 daily on its [contact page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/contact/), and the [rental guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-guide/) lists its basic terms. Treat anything that differs from those pages as a reason to ask.

## Match the hours to your own timetable

A shop being open is not the same as your plan fitting inside its hours. Work through these points before you book flights, trains or a hotel check-in.

### Arrival time

Count backwards from the shop's closing time, not forwards from your landing time. Allow for immigration queues, luggage, traffic between your arrival point and Long Bien, and the handover itself, which includes an inspection, a contract and a deposit. If you will arrive close to 21:00, ask whether collecting the next morning is easier. Nguyen Ha does not run an airport service, so plan the journey from the airport or station separately.

### Return time

The shop's hours also decide when you can hand the bike back. A rental day at Nguyen Ha is 24 hours and late returns are charged by the hour for the first five hours, then as an extra day from six hours, so a return after closing time is a real cost, not a formality. Our guide to [returning a rental motorbike in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/returning-your-rental-motorbike-hanoi/) explains the steps and what staff check.

### Holidays and Tet

Public holidays and the Lunar New Year period, usually in late January or February, are the days when shops most often change hours or close for a few days. Ask early and ask by date: "Are you open on 30 April and 1 May?" is clearer than "Are you open on holidays?". Do not assume that a shop that is open every day of the year in a listing will staff the counter during the holiday itself.

### Hours versus delivery

If a weekly or monthly agreement includes delivery, the handover time is agreed with the shop in advance and is not just the counter's opening hours. Confirm the exact delivery window in writing.

## Reading a Vietnamese alley address

The shop's address is given as Alley 5 Nguyen Van Cu Street. In Hanoi, a side lane off a main street is called a ngo, translated here as alley, and a smaller lane off that alley can be a ngach. Addresses often use a slash to show the layers: an address such as 23/66 means house number 23 inside alley 66 of the named street. Vietnam Discovery's guide to addresses adds that the same street name can appear in different wards, districts or cities, so the ward and district matter as much as the street.

This is why the full line is worth copying exactly: Alley 5, Nguyen Van Cu Street, Bo De Ward, Long Bien District, Hanoi. A search for "Nguyen Van Cu" alone can lead to a different street in another city, so include Hanoi and Long Bien every time. The landmark that helps most is Gia Lam bus station, which the shop gives as its nearest well-known point.

## Test the pin before you travel

You can check a pickup location without leaving your hotel or your sofa.

1. **Copy the full address** from the shop's contact page, not from a review or an old listing.
2. **Paste it into your map app** and compare the dot with the written address. It should sit in Long Bien District, Hanoi, not in another city or district.
3. **Zoom in on the entrance to the alley.** Look for the main street, the nearest junction and any landmark the map labels.
4. **Save the pin offline** if your map app allows it, in case your mobile data is not working on arrival.
5. **Ask the shop to confirm the pin**, for example by sending a screenshot of your pin and asking "Is this the right place?".
6. **Note a second landmark**, such as a bus station or a large shop on the main street, so you can describe where you are over the phone.

If you plan to ride away into the Old Quarter or Hoan Kiem afterwards, see our overview pages for the [Long Bien area](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/long-bien/) and the [Gia Lam area](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/gia-lam/) to place the shop on the map first.

## What to have in your hand at the door

Arriving prepared makes the handover quick. Staff at Nguyen Ha check identity documents and the licence and photograph them for the contract at handover, and they do not keep the originals. Vietnam's Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (Law 36/2024/QH15) requires a driver to carry a licence suited to the vehicle together with the vehicle's papers, and the shop asks to see your licence for that reason. Our guide to [what documents to carry when riding in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/documents-to-carry-riding-in-vietnam/) explains the list, and the shop confirms its own requirements when you book.

Bring a charged phone with the shop's number saved, your passport or identity card, your licence and any permit, and the deposit in the form the shop has told you to bring. The deposit at Nguyen Ha is cash, set at VND 2,000,000 to 5,000,000 depending on the bike and contract, so ask the amount in advance. The [prices page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/prices/) shows how the shop sets out its rates.

## If the shop looks closed when you arrive

Do not wait silently outside. Work through these steps.

1. **Call the shop** on 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969) and say that you are at the address.
2. **Send your map pin** if the call is not answered, and describe the nearest landmark.
3. **Check the time.** Your phone clock may differ from Vietnam time, which is seven hours ahead of UTC all year, so confirm that it is really inside 09:00 to 21:00 in Hanoi.
4. **Look for the alley number** on the wall or the gate. Several alleys on one street can look alike.
5. **Wait somewhere safe and lit** if staff say they are coming, and do not hand a deposit to anyone you cannot connect to the shop you booked.

If you cannot reach the shop at all, the sensible fallback is to find a place to stay nearby and collect the bike in the morning, rather than accepting a bike from a stranger who offers to help.

## Questions to put in your message

A short message does the whole job. Copy and adapt:

> Hello Nguyen Ha. I plan to collect a bike on [date] at about [time]. Please confirm that you are open then, and that the pickup point is Alley 5 Nguyen Van Cu Street, Bo De Ward, Long Bien. I will send my map pin. Who should I call when I arrive, and what should I bring?

Keep the reply. If plans change, send a new message the same day and keep that reply as well. Our article on [how weekly and monthly rentals work](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/weekly-monthly-motorbike-rental-hanoi/) explains the extra steps for longer bookings.

## FAQ: hours and pickup location

### What are the shop's opening hours?

Nguyen Ha Motorbike Rental lists 09:00 to 21:00 daily. Confirm the hours for your specific date, particularly around public holidays and Tet.

### Where do I collect the bike?

At Alley 5 Nguyen Van Cu Street, Bo De Ward, Long Bien District, Hanoi, near Gia Lam bus station. Compare the written address with your map pin before you travel.

### Can I collect the bike outside opening hours?

Do not assume so. Ask in advance and get any agreed time in writing.

### Is my map app reliable for finding an alley?

It is a good start, but check the ward and district and keep the shop's number ready, because the same street name can exist elsewhere.

### Does the shop deliver to my hotel?

Delivery is available only for weekly or monthly agreements, within 10 km of Hoan Kiem Lake, and is agreed in advance. Shorter rentals are collected from the shop.
