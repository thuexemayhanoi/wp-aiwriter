A safe right turn on a scooter has five parts: move to the right-hand side of your lane early, signal and keep the signal on, slow down before the corner, give way to pedestrians and anyone on your inside, then turn smoothly into the correct lane of the new road. Law 36/2024/QH15 sets the legal frame in Article 15. Before changing direction you must observe the surroundings to ensure a safe distance from the vehicle behind, slow down, signal, and gradually move to the lane closest to the direction you will turn. You must keep the signal on throughout the change, and you may change direction only when safety conditions are met and the manoeuvre is not likely to obstruct people or other vehicles.

This guide turns that rule into a routine. It covers where to be on the road, what to check, who to give way to, how large vehicles change the picture, how wet roads and darkness change it again, what to do at junctions without lights and what the common mistakes and fines are. It is general information based on the law as of October 2026, not legal advice.

## Why right turns deserve their own guide

In a country where traffic keeps to the right, a right turn is the shorter and simpler turn, and that is exactly why people get careless. You cross no oncoming lane, so it feels safe. But a right turn takes you through the places where pedestrians cross, where scooters undertake on the inside and where large vehicles swing wide. The risks on a right turn tend to come from the people and vehicles on your right and behind you, rather than from traffic in front. Our guide to [crossing Hanoi intersections](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/crossing-hanoi-intersections/) describes how junction traffic flows, and this guide concentrates on the turn itself.

## What Article 15 requires

The Law's rules on changing direction apply to a right turn as much as to a left turn or a U-turn.

- **Observe before you move.** Look at the surroundings and ensure a safe distance with the vehicle behind.

- **Slow down.**

- **Signal.** Use the direction indicator, or a hand signal if the vehicle has no indicator. Use the signal continuously during the change.

- **Gradually move to the lane closest to the direction you will turn.** For a right turn, this is the right-hand lane.

- **Turn only when it is safe,** and when the turn is not likely to obstruct people and other vehicles.

- **Give way** to pedestrians, rudimentary vehicles and oncoming traffic when changing direction.

Article 12 adds that operators must observe the surroundings and slow down or stop when they change direction, and at road intersections. These are legal duties, and they are also the basis of a safe technique.

## Step 1: choose your lane early

The mistake that causes most trouble is the late move. A rider who realises at the last moment that the turn is coming cuts across two lanes of scooters and arrives at the corner with no room.

1. **Look for the turn well ahead.** Check your route before you start, and read signs as you approach. Our guide to [understanding common motorbike road signs in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/understanding-common-motorbike-road-signs-in-vietnam/) explains the signs you may see.

2. **Move right gradually,** one lane at a time, as Article 13 requires for lane changes: signal, check the vehicles in front, behind and on both sides, and then move.

3. **Be in the right-hand lane,** or the lane marked for right turns, before the junction. Road markings may show which lanes turn. Our guide to [how to read motorbike lane markings in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-read-motorbike-lane-markings-in-hanoi/) explains the lines.

4. **If you miss the turn, go on.** Carry on to the next safe place and come back. A missed turn costs minutes, and a forced one can cost far more.

## Step 2: signal early, and keep it on

Signalling is where many riders cut corners.

- **Start the signal before you slow,** so that the rider behind has time to react.

- **Check that it is flashing.** On a rental scooter, a stuck or faulty indicator is easy to miss. Test it before you leave. Our guide to [how to check a scooter's horn and turn signals](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-check-a-scooters-horn-and-turn-signals/) explains how.

- **Keep it on until the turn is done,** as Article 15 requires. Turning it off early tells those behind you that you have finished when you have not.

- **Cancel it afterwards.** Many scooters do not cancel the signal themselves, and a signal left on misleads others.

Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, as read on the Government portal, sets a fine of VND 600,000 to 800,000 for turning without observing, without a safe distance, without slowing down or without a signal, or for not keeping the signal on during the turn. As of October 2026 that is the range in the decree, and you should check the current text if a figure matters to you.

## Step 3: slow down before the corner

A scooter that enters a corner too fast either runs wide, into the lane beside the new road, or brakes late and loses balance. So:

1. **Reduce speed before you reach the corner,** with both brakes, in a straight line, and not while leaning.

2. **Keep your speed low and steady** through the turn.

3. **Look through the turn,** to where you want to go, and not at the kerb.

4. **Do not brake hard mid-turn.** If you must stop, straighten up first where you can.

5. **Keep a margin for wet roads,** where painted lines and manhole covers can be slippery. Our guide to [riding in Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) explains how.

## Step 4: pedestrians first

A right turn passes straight through the crossing on the side road, and pedestrians there are often looking at the traffic on their own side.

- **Slow down for crossings.** Article 12 requires you to observe and slow down or stop where there are road markings or other signals for pedestrians, or where pedestrians or wheelchair users are crossing.

- **On a green light,** the law says operators must slow down or stop to give way to pedestrians or wheelchairs crossing the roadway (Article 11).

- **Do not stop on the crossing,** and do not edge into the people.

- **Expect hesitation,** from older people, children and people carrying loads. Article 30 requires that children under 7 cross with an adult and that people help those who may find crossing difficult.

- **Make eye contact where you can,** and wait for the person to complete the crossing.

If you are turning right on a red light because a green arrow or supplementary sign permits it, the pedestrian rule applies in full. ## Step 5: the vehicles on your inside and behind

The danger on a right turn is usually on your right and behind you.

- **Scooters and bicycles on the inside.** A rider who comes up on your right as you turn can find themselves in your path. Check your right-hand mirror and make a quick look over your shoulder before you move, and again before you turn.

- **The vehicle behind.** Article 15 requires a safe distance from the vehicle behind, so check your mirror and slow smoothly.

- **Bicycles and rudimentary vehicles.** The law requires you to give way to them when changing direction.

- **Oncoming traffic turning left into the same road.** At a junction you may meet vehicles turning in from the opposite direction. Slow and be ready to give way.

Do not assume that a rider who is level with you has seen your signal. Slow down and let them go.

## Large vehicles: buses, trucks and cars

A large vehicle turning right behaves differently from a scooter.

- **A long vehicle may swing out left before turning right,** which leaves a gap on its right that looks like space for a scooter. Do not enter it.

- **A large vehicle has blind spots** on its right-hand side, close to its body. If you cannot see the driver's mirror, the driver may not see you.

- **Stay behind rather than beside** a large vehicle that is signalling a right turn, and give it the whole corner.

## Junctions without lights

At a junction without signals, the Law's rule on giving way applies. Under Article 22, at an intersection between a non-priority road and a priority road, or between a feeder road and a main road, the vehicle coming from the non-priority or feeder road must give way to vehicles on the priority or main road. At an intersection without a "roundabout ahead" sign, a vehicle must give way to the vehicle approaching from the right.

For a right turn, this means:

- **If you are turning right from a minor road into a major road,** give way to the traffic on the major road.

- **If you are on the major road and turning right into a minor road,** slow down and look for vehicles emerging from it.

- **At an unsigned junction of equal roads,** give way to the vehicle approaching from your right, which includes vehicles that will turn into your path.

Alley mouths are particularly risky because they open onto the road with no warning. Treat each one as a junction, and slow down.

## Night, rain and poor visibility

A right turn takes more care when visibility is poor.

- **At night,** use your low beam, as Article 20 requires when changing direction at intersections. Our guide to [riding at night in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-at-night-in-hanoi/) covers lights and visibility.

- **In rain,** slow down more than you think necessary, and avoid turning over painted markings and metal covers.

- **When the view is blocked** by a parked vehicle or a stall at the corner, stop short, look and creep forward until you can see.

## Common mistakes

1. **Turning from the wrong lane,** usually the middle one.

2. **Cutting the corner,** into the path of oncoming traffic on the new road.

3. **Signalling late or not at all.**

4. **Leaving the signal on after the turn.**

5. **Not slowing for the crossing.**

6. **Not checking the inside.**

7. **Turning on a red light** where no arrow, sign or officer allows it.

8. **Stopping on the crossing** to wait for a gap.

## A quick checklist

1. **Be in the right-hand lane early.**

2. **Signal before you slow, and keep it on.**

3. **Slow down before the corner, straight and upright.**

4. **Give way to pedestrians and bicycles.**

5. **Check the inside and behind.**

6. **Stay clear of large vehicles that are turning.**

7. **At unsigned junctions, give way to priority roads and to vehicles on your right.**

8. **If you miss the turn, go on.**

## FAQ: right turns on a scooter in Hanoi

### What does the law require before a right turn?

You must observe the surroundings, keep a safe distance from the vehicle behind, slow down, signal, move gradually to the lane closest to the turn and turn only when it is safe and will not obstruct others (Article 15).

### Who must I give way to when turning right?

Pedestrians, rudimentary vehicles such as bicycles, and oncoming traffic, and at an unsigned junction the vehicle approaching from your right.

### Do I need to signal for the whole turn?

Yes. Article 15 says the signal must be used continuously during the change of direction.

### Can I turn right on a red light?

Only where a green arrow, a supplementary sign or a traffic officer permits it. Otherwise red means stop.

### What is the fine for turning without a signal?

VND 600,000 to 800,000 for turning without observing, a safe distance, slowing down or a signal, or without keeping the signal on, under Decree 168/2024/ND-CP. Check the current text.

### What if I miss my turn?

Go on to the next safe place and come back. Do not cut across lanes at the last moment.
