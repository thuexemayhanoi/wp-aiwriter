Postpone a day trip from Hanoi when there is an official storm or flood warning for the north, when heavy rain is forecast over the hills you plan to cross, when thunderstorms are expected for most of the day, or when the ground is still saturated after days of downpours. A shower in the city is not a reason to cancel, but a typhoon, a rising river or a mountain road after prolonged rain is. Different routes fail in different ways: hill roads suffer landslides and rockfall, low delta roads flood, and coastal roads take wind and heavy rain. This guide explains the season, what each type of route risks, which official sources to check and when, a clear set of postpone signals, and what to do instead. It is general planning advice, not a forecast, and conditions on the day always decide.

## What the rainy season looks like around Hanoi

Northern Vietnam's wet season generally runs from about May to September, with the heaviest rain in July and August, according to Vietnam Airlines' travel guide, which describes sunny mornings often followed by afternoon showers and thunderstorms. One local tourism guide puts Hanoi's annual rainfall at about 1,680 mm, mostly between May and September.

The storm season is longer. The US Embassy in Vietnam says the typhoon season runs from June through the end of November. Rain can arrive late, too. Vietnam news reports show the circulation of Typhoon Matmo bringing moderate to heavy rain across Hanoi on 7 October 2025, with a flooding risk, so October is not automatically safe.

For a rider, that means two different problems. In the core months you often face afternoon thunderstorms that pass. In storm weeks you may face a system that closes roads for days. Planning for the first, and postponing for the second, is the heart of the method.

## What each type of route risks

| Route type | Examples | Main rainy-season hazard |
|---|---|---|
| Hill and mountain roads | Tam Dao, Ba Vi, Mai Chau, Ha Giang | Landslides, fallen rock, slick bends, poor visibility |
| Low delta and river roads | Roads towards Ninh Binh, dyke roads by the Red River | Standing water, flooded underpasses and low sections |
| Coastal and estuary roads | Ha Long Bay direction | Strong wind, heavy rain bands, storm effects |
| City streets | Old Quarter, low-lying districts | Short flooding, slippery paint and manhole covers |

### Hills: landslides and saturated ground

Northern Vietnam has many landslide-prone mountain slopes. An older JICA study for Vietnam's transport strategy (published in 2000) notes that large landslides occur in the northern mountains during heavy rainfall, and that national roads there are regularly damaged by flash floods and landslides. A more recent example: when Typhoon Narra stalled off the northern coast in August 2026, the national forecasting centre said rainfall since 21 August had topped 500 mm in parts of Quang Ninh and 520 mm in Lang Son, saturating the soil and raising the risk of flash floods and landslides in mountainous areas.

The lesson for riders is that the danger does not end when the rain does. Saturated slopes can slide hours or days later. A small, lightly built bike has no margin if rock or mud is on a bend. Our guide to [manual bikes and steep mountain roads](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/manual-bikes-and-steep-mountain-roads-when-to-choose-another-vehicle/) explains why mountain roads already demand care.

### Delta roads: flooding and standing water

Low-lying roads flood quickly in heavy rain. When Typhoon Kajiki hit in August 2025, VietnamPlus reported inundation depths of 30 to 50 cm in places in Hanoi that could last 30 to 60 minutes or longer in low-lying areas, and authorities advised vigilance in flood-prone suburban areas. After Typhoon Yagi in September 2024, The Guardian reported thousands of residents near the swollen Red River being evacuated in Hanoi, and a landslide sweeping away a village in the north.

Water depth is hard to judge on a road, and a small scooter can stall or lose control in water far shallower than it looks. The US Embassy's storm advice is blunt: unexpected flooding can occur a considerable distance downstream from a storm, and you should not walk or drive across flooded areas. See our guides to [riding a manual motorbike through standing water](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-a-manual-motorbike-through-standing-water/) and [riding in Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/).

### Coast: wind and heavy rain bands

Typhoons often make landfall on the northern coast and then move inland, as Yagi did in 2024 before reaching Hanoi. Even a storm that does not strike directly can bring torrential rain and strong gusts. A ride towards the coast in storm season should be the first to go when a warning is issued.

## Where to check, and when

You do not need to be a meteorologist. Check at four moments:

1. **Three days out.** Look at the general outlook. If a tropical storm is forming, consider choosing another date.
2. **The evening before.** Read the next day's forecast and any warnings for the provinces on your route.
3. **The morning of the ride.** Look at the sky and the latest forecast before you collect the bike.
4. **During the ride.** Watch the clouds and listen for thunder, and turn back early.

Useful sources include:

- **Vietnam's National Center for Hydro-Meteorological Forecasting.** It has an English-language page at nchmf.gov.vn that shows weather by location, with sea forecasts mostly in Vietnamese. A browser translation tool works.
- **The US Joint Typhoon Warning Center,** which the US Embassy recommends monitoring during a storm's approach.
- **Local news** and your hotel, which can tell you about closed roads.
- **The rental shop,** which knows local conditions. Ask on 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969), open 09:00 to 21:00 daily.

The US Embassy adds that storm paths can change with little notice, so check again rather than rely on one look.

## The postpone signals

Treat any one of these as a reason to postpone a hill or long-distance trip:

- **An official storm or typhoon warning** affecting the north or the coast.
- **A flood alert** on the rivers near your route.
- **A forecast of very heavy rain over mountain provinces** you will cross.
- **Heavy rain in the previous days,** because slopes and roads are still unstable.
- **All-day thunderstorm forecasts,** not just an afternoon shower.
- **News of road closures, landslides or flooding** on your route.
- **Your own doubts.** If you are not comfortable, the trip can wait.

Treat these as reasons to shorten or change a trip:

- A single afternoon shower in the forecast
- Light drizzle with no thunder
- A wet but open city street

## Lightning and when to stop

Thunder changes everything. The US National Weather Service says that if you can hear thunder you are within striking distance, that there is no safe place outside, and that the best shelter is a substantial building or hard-topped vehicle. For motorcyclists it advises pulling over near a safe building and waiting 30 minutes after the last rumble. If you hear thunder, head for a petrol station forecourt or a solid building, not a roadside shelter or a tree.

Honda UK's rain-riding advice adds that wet roads reduce grip and lengthen stopping distances, that you should keep braking and steering smooth, and that you should keep your lights on and allow more time to react. ## What to do instead on a wet day

Postponing a trip does not need to ruin the day. Options include:

- A short, dry-weather loop within the city once the rain has passed, with a plan to be back early. See our guide to [riding in the Old Quarter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-old-quarter/).
- Museums, covered markets and cafes, using the bike only for short hops.
- Shifting the long ride to a clearer day, and treating the wet day as a rest day.
- If the weather has already turned on a trip, stopping early. Our guide to [what to do if a rental bike breaks down](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-bike-breakdown-hanoi-what-to-do/) helps if something goes wrong.

Be wary of riding beside a river that is rising. A riverside road that was pleasant last week can be under water this week.

## A one-page decision guide

| Condition | Decision |
|---|---|
| Typhoon or tropical storm warning for the north | Postpone all long trips |
| Heavy rain forecast over the hills you will cross | Postpone hill trips, choose a city loop |
| Thunderstorms forecast all day | Postpone, or stay in the city and be ready to shelter |
| Heavy rain in the last day or two, mountain route | Postpone, as slopes may still slide |
| Single afternoon shower forecast | Go, start early, carry rain gear, be back before it starts |
| Clear forecast after a wet spell | Go, but watch for debris and mud on roads |
| Roads reported closed or flooded | Choose another route or day |

## FAQ: postponing in the rainy season

### When is the rainy season in Hanoi?

Generally May to September, with peak rain in July and August, and the typhoon season runs from June to the end of November.

### Should I cancel for any rain?

No. A passing shower is part of riding in the season. Cancel for storm warnings, floods, landslide risk or all-day thunderstorms.

### Is it safe to ride on mountain roads after the rain stops?

Not always. Soaked slopes can slide later, so be cautious for a day or two after very heavy rain.

### Where can I check official forecasts?

Vietnam's National Center for Hydro-Meteorological Forecasting publishes weather and warnings, and the US Joint Typhoon Warning Center tracks storms.

### What if I am already out when thunder starts?

Get to a substantial building or forecourt and wait 30 minutes after the last thunder.

### Does the shop give refunds if I cancel mid-rental?

Unused rent is not refunded on early return. Ask the shop about options before the rental begins.
