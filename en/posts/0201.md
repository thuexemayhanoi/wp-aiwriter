The electrical side of a 50cc bike can be checked in about five minutes at the rental counter: turn the key and watch the lights, start the engine with the button and, if there is one, with the kick lever, listen to how the starter turns, then walk around the bike testing the headlight, tail light, brake light, indicators and horn. A bike that starts promptly, holds steady lights at idle and shows every lamp working is usually in good electrical order, while a slow crank, dim lamps or a brake light that only works from one lever are reasons to ask for a different bike. This checklist is ordered as you would do it in practice, explains what each sign usually means and uses Honda's own wording where Honda has published it. It is a renter's inspection, not a diagnosis, and it will not make anyone a mechanic.

## Why small bikes need an electrical check

A 50cc bike has a small engine and a small electrical system. The battery is modest, the generator is modest and the lamps are chosen to match. That is fine when everything is healthy, but it leaves little margin. A tired battery that still starts a bike on a warm morning may fail on a cold evening, and a headlight that looks acceptable in a bright shop may be too dim on an unlit road. Because the bike is slow, the consequences are usually inconvenience rather than danger, yet a dead tail lamp or brake light on a Hanoi street at night is a real hazard because other riders judge your speed by it.

The good news is that the checks are visual and audible. You need the key, a few minutes and a willingness to ask the staff to hold the bike while you walk round it. Our wider [10-minute rental scooter inspection](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-scooter-inspection-checklist/) covers tyres, brakes and fuel; this page goes deeper on the starting and lighting systems only.

## Step 1: key on, before the engine starts

Turn the key to the On position without pressing the starter. On most bikes a set of dashboard indicators lights up briefly as a self-test.

- **Dashboard indicators:** the neutral light on a gear bike, the turn-signal indicator when you use the switch, the high-beam light when you select it. Our guide to [semi-automatic dashboard lights](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/semi-automatic-motorbike-dashboard-lights-explained/) explains the usual symbols.
- **Position lamp:** Honda's news release for its Super Cub 50 Pro notes that the position lamps can be switched on with the key in the On position even when the engine is stopped. Where a bike has such a lamp, it is a quick way to test that part of the system without running the engine.
- **A dead dash:** if nothing lights at all with the key on, the battery may be flat or disconnected, or a fuse may have blown. Do not try to fix it. Ask for another bike.

Note what you see, because a missing light at this stage is not always a fault, and you should know the normal behaviour of this particular bike before you ride it.

## Step 2: the electric starter

Most modern 50cc scooters have an electric starter button. Many Cub-style bikes also have a kick lever. Honda's Wave RSX FI manual, which is for a 110cc model and is used here only as an example of Honda's wording, gives this routine for starting with the button: key On, gearbox in neutral with the neutral light lit, throttle fully closed, press the starter switch. Bikes differ, and some scooters also want a brake lever held, so ask the staff how this one is meant to start.

Listen to the first second:

| What you hear | What it usually means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Quick, even crank and the engine catches | Healthy battery and starter | Carry on |
| Slow, laboured crank | Weak battery or a poor connection | Ask for another bike or for the battery to be checked |
| Rapid clicking, no crank | Very low battery or loose connection | Do not keep pressing; ask the shop |
| Silence, with lights on | Switch, relay or fuse problem | Check the stand and kill switch, then ask |
| Crank but no start | Fuel or ignition issue, not the battery | Ask the shop to start it |

### The five-second rule

Honda's manual says that if the engine does not start within five seconds, you should switch the key off, wait about ten seconds and then try again. The pause allows the battery voltage to recover. Repeated long presses of the button are a good way to flatten a small battery. If the bike needs more than two attempts at the counter, do not accept it on the promise that "it is only cold".

## Step 3: the kick lever, if there is one

A kick lever is a useful backup on an older or smaller bike. Honda's manual describes the method: press the lever gently until you feel light resistance, let it return to the top of its travel, close the throttle fully, then kick down quickly in one continuous stroke. It adds that you should not kick the lever while the bike is moving, should not kick it too hard and should fold the lever back in once it has returned. Our guide to [using the kick starter on an older rental motorbike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-use-the-kick-starter-on-an-older-rental-motorbike/) describes it in more detail.

What to check at the shop:

- The lever moves through its full stroke and returns on its spring.
- It does not slip, so that you feel it bite and then give way.
- The engine starts within a few kicks when warm.
- The lever folds in and stays out of your ankle's way.

A kick lever that slips or does not return is a mechanical fault and has nothing to do with the battery. Report it.

## Step 4: read the battery signs

You cannot test a battery properly without tools, but you can notice its habits.

- **Slow cranking** on a cold start or after the bike has stood for a day.
- **Dim lights at idle** that brighten when the throttle is blipped. On a healthy bike the generator keeps the lamps steady.
- **Weak horn** or a horn that fades while you hold it.
- **White or green crust** on the terminals, if the seat or cover is already open. Honda's manual says that terminals showing corrosion with a white deposit can be washed with warm water and wiped, and that heavily corroded ones need cleaning and polishing. It also says that this work is best done by a skilled mechanic wearing eye protection, because a battery produces explosive gas.
- **A battery that needs charging every few days** for a bike that is ridden daily, which is an old battery or a charging fault.

Honda describes its battery as maintenance-free: do not remove the strip over the cap, and do not check the electrolyte or add water. For a renter that is another way of saying that the battery is the shop's business. If you see a bulging case, leaking liquid or loose wires, tell the shop and ask for another bike.

## Step 5: walk-round lamp test

Ask a staff member to stand beside the bike, with the key on and the engine running, and test each lamp in turn.

1. **Headlight, low beam.** Is the beam steady and white or yellow, not flickering? Shine it on a wall ten metres away to check that it points down the road, not into the sky or the ground.
2. **High beam.** Does the dash light come on, and does the beam change?
3. **Position lamp or pilot light,** if fitted.
4. **Tail light.** Visible from behind, not cracked, not full of water.
5. **Brake light from the right-hand lever.** Squeeze it and watch the rear lamp.
Now test the brake light from both brakes, since each has its own switch.

6. **Brake light from the other brake,** whether it is the left lever or the foot pedal. Honda's manual lists the brake light switch among the items to check, and a switch that works from one brake only is a common fault.
7. **Turn signals, front and rear.** Left and right, and check that each blinks at a steady rate and cancels. A rapid blink often means a bulb is out. Honda says to fit only amber bulbs to turn signals.
8. **Horn.** A clear, loud tone. Our guide to riding at night covers why it matters, and Honda's pre-ride list includes lights, indicators and the horn together.
9. **Number plate light,** where fitted.

Cracked lenses, plastic taped over a lamp or a loose headlight unit should be reported and photographed. Our [night riding guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-at-night-in-hanoi/) explains why a dim lamp matters more after dark.

## Step 6: fuses, spares and accessories

A fuse protects the wiring, and a blown fuse stops some part of the system dead. Honda's manual says that if a component does not work, the fuse should be checked, that the replacement must have the same rating as the old one and that a higher rating can make electrical damage more likely. It also warns that non-Honda electrical accessories can overload the system and run the battery down.

For a renter, the lesson is practical:

- Do not open the fuse box or swap a fuse yourself.
- Do not plug in extras such as phone chargers or spotlights unless the shop agrees they are fitted correctly.
- Do not accept a bike with taped or loose wiring hanging under the seat or behind the headlight.

If the shop wants to replace a fuse or bulb at handover, that is a good sign: it shows they are looking. Ask them to do it, rather than doing it yourself.

## Step 7: a short test ride

After the stationary checks, do a short test ride on a quiet street.

- Does the engine restart immediately after you stop and switch off once? A bike that starts well cold but not warm has a different problem from one that struggles in the cold.
- Do the lights stay steady as you accelerate and slow?
- Does the horn work as you ride, and do the indicators cancel properly?
- Does the bike idle without stalling when you stop at a junction?

Stop, restart and try the starter again before you return. If anything has changed, say so at once.

## Record what you found

Take a short video of the bike starting, with the dashboard in view, and photographs of each lamp lit. Write down the bike's plate number and the time. If a problem shows up later, the record shows the condition at handover. ## Quick checklist to copy

- [ ] Key on: dashboard lights up, no warning lamps stuck on
- [ ] Starter: starts in under two seconds of cranking, twice in a row
- [ ] Kick lever (if fitted): moves, returns, bites, folds in
- [ ] Idle: steady, lights do not dim
- [ ] Headlight low and high beam work, aimed correctly
- [ ] Tail lamp and number plate lamp work
- [ ] Brake light works from both brakes
- [ ] Turn signals work front and rear, amber, steady rate
- [ ] Horn loud and clear
- [ ] No taped or exposed wiring, no leaking or bulging battery
- [ ] Photographs and a start-up video saved

## FAQ: 50cc starter, battery and lights

### How many times should I try the starter at the shop?

Two or three short tries. Honda advises switching off and waiting about ten seconds if the engine does not start within five seconds of cranking.

### Is a slow start always a bad battery?

No. It can be the battery, a loose connection, an old plug or a fuel issue. Either way, a bike that starts slowly at the counter is likely to cause trouble later.

### Do all 50cc bikes have a kick starter?

No. Cub-style bikes often do, and many twist-and-go scooters have only the button. Ask which applies to the bike you are given.

### Can I change a bulb myself?

It is better not to. Honda's manual says to switch off, let the bulb cool, use only the specified type and test the new one before riding. On a rental, ask the shop to do it.

### Is a dim headlight a problem if I only ride by day?

It still deserves a report, because a dim lamp can mean a weak battery or poor charging, and rain or tunnels can make a day ride dark.

### What if the brake light only works from one lever?

Report it and ask for the switch to be fixed or another bike to be provided before you ride.
