Before you ride onto Nhat Tan Bridge on a scooter, check four things: which lane at the foot of the ramp is signed for motorbikes, whether a hard divider separates you from the cars, what the lane-specific speed signs say, and whether the route you are following continues onto the car-only Vo Nguyen Giap road toward Noi Bai Airport, which motorbikes may not use. The bridge itself carries motorbikes in a separate strip, but the approaches are where mistakes happen, because lanes split, signs change and the road beyond the bridge is a different type of road. Hanoi authorities were also preparing new warning signs and lane-specific speed rules in September 2026. This guide explains the layout, the 2025 and 2026 changes, the rules behind the signs and a short check to make before every crossing.

## What the bridge and its approaches look like

Nhat Tan is a cable-stayed bridge across the Red River that links Tay Ho on the city side with Dong Anh on the north bank. It opened on 4 January 2015 as part of a new highway toward Noi Bai Airport. According to figures published by Vietnam News Agency in 2024, the whole structure is 9.17 kilometres long, of which the bridge proper is 3.9 kilometres, including a 1.5 kilometre span over the river, and the approach viaducts account for 5.27 kilometres. The deck is 33.2 metres wide and carries eight lanes in both directions, which that source divides into four motor-vehicle lanes, two bus lanes and two mixed strips, plus a central divider and a pedestrian path.

For a rider, the number that matters is how long the approaches are. You spend several minutes on raised ramps before you reach the river, with limited places to stop and no easy way to turn around. A wrong lane choice at the bottom of a ramp therefore stays with you for a long time.

Our [guide to ring roads and highways open to motorbikes](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-ring-roads-highways-motorbikes-allowed/) puts Nhat Tan in the wider picture of Hanoi's river crossings. This article concentrates on the ramps and approach roads.

## Lane by lane: where a scooter belongs

Describing every lane marking would go out of date quickly, but the logic is stable:

- **Motorbikes use the mixed strips** named in the official figures, not the car or bus lanes. Look for the painted motorbike symbol and the divider rather than assuming which edge the strip is on.
- **Cars use the four motor-vehicle lanes**, and the bus lanes are reserved for buses.
- **A hard divider** now separates the lanes that carry different types of vehicle, according to a September 2026 report in the Ministry of Construction's newspaper.
- **Bicycles** are allowed on the bridge only between 22:00 and 05:00, according to a 2024 report by the national road-traffic radio.

Vietnamese law says that on a lane reserved for one type or group of vehicles, other vehicles may not enter it, and that slower vehicles keep to the right. A scooter in a bus lane or a car lane is breaking the lane rule, and it is also exposed to much faster traffic. If the motorbike strip looks narrow, remember that separating slow from fast traffic is the reason it exists.

## The Tay Ho approach

On the city side the bridge is reached through the Phu Thuong and Vo Chi Cong area. This is a part of town with wide roads and large junctions, and drivers sometimes behave as though they are already on a highway. A June 2025 newspaper report described a queue stretching from the foot of the bridge back to the Vo Chi Cong and Nguyen Hoang Ton intersection at rush hour, and also described motorbikes hitting reflective boxes at the start of a newly installed median at the beginning of Vo Chi Cong Street.

The practical points are:

- **Choose your lane well before the ramp.** Changing at the last moment across a painted island is how riders end up in the wrong strip.
- **Treat new dividers as new.** Maps and memory can be out of date. Look at what is physically there.
- **Expect a queue.** Allow extra time and keep your distance in stop-and-go traffic. Our guide to [crossing Hanoi intersections](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/crossing-hanoi-intersections/) explains how the merge usually works.

## The Dong Anh side and the road to the airport

Past the river the highway runs on toward Noi Bai. Hanoi traffic police told the road-traffic radio in 2024 that the stretch of Vo Nguyen Giap road from Song Thiep Bridge to near the airport is reserved for cars, that pedestrians, bicycles, electric bicycles, motorcycles and mopeds are banned from it, and that it is designed for speeds of 80 to 90 kilometres per hour. The same report says the road has side roads (service roads) on each side for motorbikes and non-motorised vehicles.

For a scooter rider the rule is simple: do not follow a car-style route on a phone, and do not keep going straight when the signs send two-wheelers to the side road. When in doubt, take the service road and accept a slower journey. Our guide to [checking whether a navigation app suggests an expressway](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-check-if-a-navigation-app-suggests-an-expressway/) shows how to catch an unsuitable suggestion before you reach the ramp.

The law itself is explicit on expressways: Article 25 of the 2024 Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety says that motorcycles, mopeds and similar vehicles may not go onto an expressway, apart from vehicles serving its management and maintenance. Whether a particular road counts as an expressway is shown by its signs, not by how fast the traffic is moving.

## What changed in 2025 and 2026

Three recent episodes show why a check on the day matters.

1. **July and August 2025: work on gantries and lane fences.** The city's Department of Construction announced that, between 25 July and 18 August 2025, the bridge and both approaches would be reorganised to build a sign gantry and lane-separation fences. At night, from 22:00 to 05:00, a third of the roadway was to be fenced at both ends, with motorbikes sharing the remaining width, and the whole structure was to close briefly from midnight to 04:00 for the gantry installation.

2. **June to August 2026: collisions.** A September 2026 report describes multi-vehicle collisions on the bridge and notes that on 19 June a concrete mixer on the approach in Phuc Thinh used the motorbike lane instead of the car lane. The report says the traffic police and the Department of Construction surveyed the bridge on 1 September.

3. **September 2026 plan.** The two agencies agreed to add distance-warning signs, adjust the speed limit lane by lane, add reflectors and repaint markings, with the changes expected in September. Police said they would first run an information period and then penalise violations, using AI cameras to detect speeding and short following distance.

The honest conclusion is that by late October 2026 you cannot be sure which signs are in place. Read the speed signs and the lane arrows as you reach them. A 2015 description of the highway gave 80 kilometres per hour for the car lanes and 40 for the two motorbike lanes, but that is a design figure from the opening year, not a current limit.

## Rules behind the signs

| Situation | What the 2024 law says | What it means on the approaches |
|---|---|---|
| Dedicated lane | Other vehicles must not enter a lane reserved for one type or group | Stay out of bus and car lanes |
| Slow vehicle | Slower vehicles keep to the right | Stay to the right in your own strip |
| Bridge | No stopping or parking on a bridge unless traffic organisation allows it | Never stop for photos on the deck |
| U-turn | No turning round on a bridge or at a bridge head | If you are in the wrong lane, continue to the next legal exit |
| Expressway | Motorcycles and mopeds may not use it | Use service roads where signed |

The law took effect on 1 January 2025. Penalties for breaking these rules are set separately in a government decree, so we do not quote amounts here; our [traffic fines guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/traffic-fines-vietnam-foreign-riders/) explains the general picture.

## A check before every crossing

1. **Route.** Open the map, then ask whether the route uses a car-only road. Choose the option that allows motorbikes, not the fastest line.
2. **Fuel.** There is no easy stopping place on the ramps. A rented petrol bike is handed over with a small amount of fuel, so top up beforehand; the [guide to refuelling during a day trip](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/when-to-refuel-a-manual-motorbike-during-a-day-trip/) gives a simple rule.
3. **Weather.** The deck is high and open. Wind and rain make a light scooter harder to hold. See [riding in Hanoi's rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/).
4. **Light.** Avoid unfamiliar approaches at night. Our [night-riding guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-at-night-in-hanoi/) lists the problems of low visibility and unlit dividers.
5. **Speed.** Match the posted figure for your lane. Speed limits for motorbikes are explained in our [speed limits guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/speed-limits-motorbikes-vietnam/).
6. **Distance.** Keep a generous gap. The reports behind the 2026 changes blame inattention, speed and short following distances.
7. **Exit.** Know your exit before you are on the ramp.

## FAQ about riding to Nhat Tan Bridge

### Can a scooter cross Nhat Tan Bridge?

Yes. Reports describe two mixed strips on the deck, which is where motorbikes ride, with a divider separating them from cars. Follow the signs for your direction.

### Can I ride from the bridge to Noi Bai Airport?

Not on the main car-only carriageway of Vo Nguyen Giap road. Reports say motorbikes use the side service roads. Check the signs and use a motorbike-appropriate route.

### Is it safe in the dark or in the rain?

It can be done, but the approaches are long and exposed. A first-time rider is usually better off crossing in daylight and dry weather.

### What if I take the wrong ramp?

Do not stop or reverse. Continue to the next place where you can turn legally, or follow the signs back. Turning round on the bridge or at the bridge head is not allowed.

### Does the bridge allow bicycles?

A 2024 report says bicycles are permitted on the bridge only from 22:00 to 05:00. That is a bicycle rule, not a motorbike one.
