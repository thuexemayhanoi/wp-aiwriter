The most reliable thing we can say about parking at Hoa Lo Prison is what the relic's own visitor rules say: park at the designated lot only. There is no published map of that lot on the official pages we read, so the practical method is to ride to 1 Hoa Lo Street, look for the attendant or the marked parking area near the gate and ask which spot is for two-wheelers that day. Travel guides agree that limited parking is available near the entrance and on adjacent streets as attendants direct, and one recent blog reports that the visitor entrance moved in April 2026, which changes where you should walk from the bike. This guide explains how to approach the site, what to do when the nearest space is taken, how to handle a helmet and bag during a visit that the relic's own rules treat with care, and how to avoid the common mistakes of parking on a busy corner.

## Where Hoa Lo Prison sits

The relic's official address is No. 1 Hoa Lo Street, in the Cua Nam area of central Hanoi, and its site says it opens from 8:00 to 17:00 every day, including festivals and holidays. Older pages give the ward as Tran Hung Dao in Hoan Kiem District, and local ward names have changed, so use the street name and the number as your anchor.

The official directions page gives some useful distances from other places: about 850 metres from one nearby landmark, along Nha Chung, Quang Trung and Hai Ba Trung streets, and about 1.1 km from Hoan Kiem Lake, via Dinh Tien Hoang, Hang Khay, Trang Thi, Quang Trung and Hai Ba Trung streets. In other words, the museum is in the area south of the lake, on a side street off Hai Ba Trung, and the approach from the lake runs through some of the busiest streets in the French-quarter grid.

## What the official rules say about parking

The relic publishes internal regulations for visitors. Among them: visitors should not carry weapons, explosives or flammable items, handbags have to be put in a designated place, smoking is prohibited and, most relevant here, parking is at the designated lot only. The relic also says that joss sticks and flowers may be offered only in the commemorative area, and that visitors should observe the guidance of staff when anything is unclear.

Two lessons follow. First, the museum expects you to use its own arrangements, so a spot on a nearby pavement is not the same thing. Second, the rules are short and general, which means the details of where and how much are settled by the attendants on the day. Ask them. We found no fee on the official pages, and we do not quote one.

## Check the entrance before you park

One recent travel blog reports that after restoration work the relic changed its visitor route on 6 April 2026: entry is now through a side gate on the Hoa Lo Street pavement, to the right of the old main gate, and exit is through a side gate opposite No. 60 Tho Nhuom Street. The same blog says that most guides online still show the old main gate and that some visitors stand in front of it wondering why it is closed. We could not confirm this on the official pages, which do not mention the route, so treat it as a reported change and check at the site.

Why this matters for parking:

- If you leave the museum by the Tho Nhuom side, you may come out some distance from where you parked. Plan the walk back before you go in.
- If you choose a parking place by the old gate, you may have to walk around to the actual entrance.
- Photograph the place where the bike is and a nearby shop sign or street number, so that you can find it from a different exit.

## Approaching on a scooter

The museum is in a part of the city where roads are narrow in places and busy at most hours. The streets named in the relic's own directions, Hai Ba Trung, Quang Trung and Trang Thi, are the likely approaches from the lake. A few habits make the last few hundred metres easier:

1. **Set your map pin to "Hoa Lo Prison Museum, 1 Hoa Lo Street".** A pin on the corner of Hai Ba Trung may leave you on the wrong side of the road.
2. **Look at the final street in satellite view the night before.** Note which side the gate is on and where a turn across traffic is possible.
3. **Slow down early.** Pedestrians, taxis dropping off passengers and tour groups all converge near the gate.
4. **Go round the block rather than stop in the carriageway.** If you miss the turn, take the next one.

If you have not yet ridden in a busy central district, read [crossing Hanoi intersections](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/crossing-hanoi-intersections/) and [riding in the Old Quarter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-old-quarter/), since the traffic patterns are alike.

## What to expect at the lot

Visitor guides describe parking "near the entrance or on adjacent streets as directed by attendants", and say that limited parking is available. One blog says motorbike parking outside the gate is free. Because we could not confirm that on the official pages, assume nothing: ask whether there is a charge, and take a ticket if one is offered. Whatever the arrangement:

- follow the attendant's direction on where the front wheel should point;
- lock the steering, since the shop does not provide an extra lock;
- keep the ticket or a photo of the number plate area with the ticket in it;
- take what you need, and leave valuables out of the seat compartment.

The general picture of how guarded lots, tickets and towing work in Hanoi is in [parking a motorbike in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-motorbike-in-hanoi/), and the shop's rental guide explains what to do if something goes wrong with the bike.

## Helmets, bags and the visit itself

The relic's rules require handbags to be put in a designated place. That is relevant to a helmet and a backpack too, so expect to be asked. You have three options for the helmet: carry it, ask whether it can be left with your bag at the designated place, or ask the parking attendant, understanding that anything left on a scooter is at your own risk. Our shop includes one basic helmet per rider, so you do not have a spare to leave behind, and you should look after it as you would your own.

Hoa Lo is a place of remembrance for prisoners held there under French rule and, later, for American pilots held there during the war. The site asks visitors to be orderly and quiet. A rider arriving in a loud mood with gear in hand should leave it at the bag place and enter calmly. That is a courtesy rather than a rule we can cite.

## If the nearest spot is full

At busy times the space near the gate fills. Do this:

1. Ask the attendant where two-wheelers should go next.
2. If you need another place, pick a guarded lot or a clearly marked parking area and not a gap by a no-parking sign, a blocked doorway or a pavement ramp.
3. Note the street name and take a photo of the bike and the entrance to the lot.
4. Walk to the museum by the route you will use to return.

Our guide to [where to park a scooter near Hoan Kiem Lake](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/where-to-park-a-scooter-near-hoan-kiem-lake/) lists the thinking behind choosing a lot in the lakeside area, which is a short walk from here by the figures above. Park once and walk if you plan to see several places in the neighbourhood: St Joseph's Cathedral is about 850 metres away according to one guide, and the lake is about 1.1 km by the museum's own figure.

## Timing: day and night visits

The relic gives its hours as 8:00 to 17:00 daily. A travel guide also mentions an evening experience on Friday, Saturday and Sunday at 19:00 with advance reservation, but that is not something we confirmed with the relic itself, so check before you plan around it. If you do go in the evening, read our guide to [riding at night in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-at-night-in-hanoi/), and keep in mind that the shop is open 09:00 to 21:00 daily, so agree a return time when you rent.

Guides suggest allowing 60 to 90 minutes, or up to two hours for a slower visit, which affects how long your parking lasts. If you are parking at a lot that charges by time or closes, ask when it shuts.

## FAQ: parking at Hoa Lo Prison Museum

### Is there official parking at Hoa Lo Prison?

The relic's visitor rules say parking is at the designated lot only. Guides report limited parking near the entrance or on adjacent streets as attendants direct.

### Is it free?

One blog says motorbike parking is free outside the gate, but we could not confirm it on the official site. Ask the attendant.

### Where is the entrance now?

A recent blog reports that since 6 April 2026 visitors enter through a side gate on the Hoa Lo Street pavement and exit opposite No. 60 Tho Nhuom Street. Check at the site.

### What are the hours?

The official pages give 8:00 to 17:00 every day, including festivals and holidays.

### Can I bring a bag or helmet inside?

The rules say handbags must be put in a designated place. Ask staff about helmets.

### Is it walkable from Hoan Kiem Lake?

The relic's directions give about 1.1 km from the lake, which is a walk of some minutes for most people, so parking once and walking is an option. Check [where to park near Hoan Kiem Lake](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/where-to-park-a-scooter-near-hoan-kiem-lake/).
