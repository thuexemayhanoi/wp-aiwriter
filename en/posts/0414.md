Record the fuel level at handover in three ways at once: photograph the gauge with the ignition on and the bike upright on level ground, ask the staff member to say how much petrol is in the tank and have that statement written on the contract or the handover sheet. A fuel gauge on a small bike is only a rough guide, so a photograph alone proves little. A photograph plus a number agreed in writing gives both sides something to compare when the bike comes back. This guide explains why fuel causes small disputes, what Nguyen Ha's published terms say about handover and return levels, how to take a record that will stand up, how to word the line on the paper and what to do at return. It also covers petrol bikes only, because an electric bike's battery level is recorded differently.

## Why fuel is worth recording

Fuel is one of the few parts of a rental that changes every day. Almost everything else about the bike should come back as it left, apart from ordinary wear, but the tank is meant to move. That makes it easy to argue about. A renter thinks the bike left with half a tank, and the shop remembers a quarter. Nobody has a record, so the discussion turns on memory.

A short record at pickup removes the argument. It also helps you, because small bikes carry little fuel. A Honda Wave RSX FI has a 4.0-litre tank, so a difference of one litre is a quarter of the tank. Our guide to [how a rental is returned](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/returning-your-rental-motorbike-hanoi/) explains how the return inspection compares the bike with the pickup record, and fuel is one of the things compared.

## What the published terms say

Nguyen Ha publishes its fuel terms, and they are the starting point for any record.

- **At handover,** a petrol bike is handed over with about 0.5 to 2 litres in the tank.
- **At return,** the bike is expected back with about 0.5 to 1 litre.
- **If the tank is empty at return,** a fee of VND 20,000 applies.
- **For electric bikes,** the battery level and the charging arrangement are confirmed at handover rather than measured in litres.

These are the shop's published terms as of October 2026, and your own contract is what counts. Two consequences follow. First, the bike will not necessarily be handed over full, so do not assume it is. Second, the return expectation is lower than the handover level, which means you are not expected to refill to the handover amount. The sensible aim is to bring the bike back with a little fuel, not with an empty tank. ## Set the bike up for an honest reading

A gauge reads differently depending on how the bike is standing. Before you take any photograph, make the conditions fair.

1. **Level ground.** The fuel in a small tank moves with the slope. A bike on its side stand leans, and the float in the tank can read high or low. Ask staff to hold the bike upright, or use a flat, firm spot, and let it settle for a few seconds.
2. **Ignition on.** The gauge needs power. Turn the key to the on position, and wait for the dashboard to finish its start-up check.
3. **Engine off or idling steadily.** Either is fine, but do not rev the engine, because vibration moves the needle.
4. **Wait for the needle to settle.** On bumpy roads the needle bounces, and even in a quiet yard it can take a moment.
5. **Look straight on.** Reading a needle from an angle shifts where it appears to point. Hold your phone square to the dashboard.

Our guide to [checking a scooter's fuel gauge before a longer journey](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/checking-a-scooters-fuel-gauge-before-a-longer-journey/) explains why a gauge measures a float's position and not litres, and why it can be optimistic or slow to move.

## What to photograph

Take these photographs and keep them together.

- **The dashboard with the gauge visible,** close enough to read the needle or bars, with the ignition on.
- **A wider shot of the dashboard,** so the odometer and any warning lights show too. It proves the bike and the time.
- **The bike from the side,** standing on level ground, so the viewer can see it was upright.
- **The fuel filler,** if staff open it for you, so the level in the tank is visible. Do not open a hot tank yourself, and let staff do it.

Phones record the time and date of each photograph. That timestamp is useful, so do not edit or crop away the information, and back the photos up to the cloud the same day.

## Ask the staff for a number

A gauge is a picture. A number is a statement. Ask the person handing over the bike something like: "How much petrol is in the tank now, roughly?" The published terms speak in half-litre steps, so an answer such as "about one litre" is the kind of statement to expect.

Then connect the two. If the needle shows about a quarter and the staff member says one litre, that is consistent with a 4.0-litre tank, because a quarter is about one litre. If the needle looks nearly full and the staff member says half a litre, something does not match, and you should ask.

Honda's information for the Wave RSX FI says that at the red mark on the fuel gauge about 1.2 litres remain. That is a useful reference point. If the needle is above the red mark, the tank holds more than about 1.2 litres. If it is at or near the red mark, the tank holds about that much or less. Our guide to [semi-automatic motorbike dashboard lights explained](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/semi-automatic-motorbike-dashboard-lights-explained/) shows what the red mark looks like.

## Get it in writing

A record on your phone is yours. A line on the contract is shared. Ask for the handover level to be written on the contract or the handover sheet, with the date and time, and ask both of you to initial it. Keep the wording simple and neutral. For example:

- "Fuel at handover: about 1 litre. Gauge photographed."
- "Fuel at handover: about 0.5 litre, near the red mark."
- "Fuel at handover: about 2 litres. Both parties agree."

If the form has no space, write it on your copy and take a photograph of the page with the staff member's signature or initials next to it. Our guide to [reading a motorbike rental agreement in English](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/reading-a-motorbike-rental-agreement-in-english/) explains how to add notes to the paper itself.

If the shop says the tank is "full" or "half", ask what that means in litres. The published terms are in litres, and a word like "full" can mean different things to different people.

## A sample handover exchange

This is the kind of short conversation that works well, in plain words.

**Renter:** "Could you stand the bike upright and let me photograph the fuel gauge?"

**Staff:** "Of course."

**Renter:** "About how much petrol is in the tank?"

**Staff:** "About one litre."

**Renter:** "Could we write that on the contract, with today's date?"

**Staff:** "Yes."

Most staff are happy to do this. It costs half a minute, and it protects them as well.

## At return: use the record

When you bring the bike back, repeat the process. Stand it upright, switch the ignition on, photograph the gauge and ask the staff member to confirm the level. Compare it with the handover line.

- **If the level is within the published range,** about 0.5 to 1 litre, there should be nothing to discuss.
- **If the tank is empty,** the published fee of VND 20,000 applies.
- **If you believe the gauge disagrees with the handover record,** show your photographs and the written line, and ask for the difference to be explained.

Do not argue about a litre in the doorway. Calmly show the evidence, and agree a figure in writing if there is a genuine difference. ## Electric bikes are different

An electric bike has no tank. The published terms say that the battery level and the charging arrangement are confirmed at handover. Record the battery percentage in the same way, with a photograph of the display and a written line. ## FAQ: recording the fuel level

### Will the bike be handed over with a full tank?

Not necessarily. The published terms say a petrol bike is handed over with about 0.5 to 2 litres.

### How much fuel should I return the bike with?

The published terms say about 0.5 to 1 litre. An empty tank at return costs VND 20,000.

### Is a gauge photograph enough?

It is useful, but a gauge is only a rough guide. Add a number from the staff member and a line on the contract.

### Why does the bike need to be upright?

A small tank's float reads differently on a slope. Level ground gives a fairer reading.

### What does the red mark on a Honda Wave mean?

For the Wave RSX FI, Honda says about 1.2 litres remain at the red mark. Treat it as a signal to refuel soon.

### What about an electric bike?

Record the battery level instead, with a photograph and a written line.
