Soc Son is a good scooter trip for riders who want green hills and lakes within a day, but the road to it needs more attention than the destination. Published guides place the district roughly 25 to 40 kilometres north of central Hanoi depending on where they measure from, reached via the Nhat Tan Bridge, the airport road and National Highway 3. The highlights are the Soc Temple complex with the giant Saint Giong statue on the mountain, several lakes at the foot of forested hills, and quiet lanes between them. This guide covers the approach road and its traffic, a sensible order for the stops, the bike and document checks to make before leaving, how to ride the hills and what to leave for another day.

## What Soc Son is, and why it needs planning

Soc Son is a district on the northern edge of Hanoi that mixes hills, plains, rivers and lakes. A 2024 VietnamNet feature describes a large forested area and says it takes less than an hour to reach from Hanoi, and the Center for Nature Conservation and Development lists Dong Quan, Dong Do and Ham Lon lakes and the Soc Temple relic complex as its main attractions. The same centre notes that the district also holds Noi Bai International Airport and a junction of major roads, which is a useful clue for a rider: this is not a quiet backroad, but a busy corridor with a green destination at the end.

Plan the trip around that. The scenery is gentle, but you are sharing the road with airport traffic, freight and buses for the first part of the ride.

## The approach: Highway 3

VietnamNet's 48-hour guide describes leaving Hanoi toward Noi Bai airport via the Nhat Tan Bridge and then taking National Route 3, calling the road comfortable with good views but warning riders to watch the speed limits. That matches how most visitors reach the Soc Temple area.

A December 2024 report in Lao Dong gives a more cautionary picture. It said National Highway 3 in parts of Soc Son was then only 8 to 9 metres wide and that accidents often occur, and that Hanoi planned to widen about 6.7 kilometres of it, from the junction with Highway 18 to the junction for the road to Soc Temple, in the period 2024 to 2027. Expect roadworks, narrowed lanes or diversions on that stretch, and check what is actually on the road when you go.

What this means on a scooter:

- Stay in the lane for two-wheelers where one is marked, and do not ride on the shoulder behind a stopped bus.
- Keep a wide gap behind lorries, and do not overtake on bends or near junctions.
- Expect abrupt stops where buses and coaches pick up passengers.
- Leave early. Traffic on a corridor like this usually builds through the morning, and the return leg is slower than the way out.

Do not let a navigation app choose a faster-looking road without checking. Article 25 of Law 36/2024/QH15, in force since 1 January 2025, bars motorcycles, mopeds and similar vehicles from expressways. Our guide to [checking whether a navigation app suggests an expressway](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-check-if-a-navigation-app-suggests-an-expressway/) and the summary of [Hanoi's ring roads and highways](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-ring-roads-highways-motorbikes-allowed/) explain how to avoid them.

## Traffic checks before you go

The title promises traffic checks, so here they are in two parts: checks on the bike and checks on yourself.

**The bike.** Do our [10-minute rental scooter inspection](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-scooter-inspection-checklist/) before you leave the shop, and give brakes, tyres, lights, horn and mirrors extra attention, because the trip includes fast highway sections and a hill. Fill the tank in the city. Our guide to [fuel in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/fuel-in-hanoi-petrol-stations-rental-scooter/) explains how.

**The paperwork.** Carry the documents the law expects and keep them where you can reach them. Our guides to [what documents to carry](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/documents-to-carry-riding-in-vietnam/) and [motorbike licence rules for foreigners](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbike-licence-rules-for-foreigners-in-vietnam/) go through them.

**The helmet.** Wear a helmet that fits and is fastened, and make sure any passenger does too. See the [helmet rules](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/helmet-rules-in-vietnam/).

**Permission to travel.** Ask the shop whether the bike can leave the city. Our guide on [taking a rental motorbike out of Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/take-rental-motorbike-out-of-hanoi/) lists the questions.

**Speed.** Circular 38/2024/TT-BGTVT sets the speed limits by type of road and vehicle. On two-way roads inside built-up areas the general limit is 50 km/h, and mopeds and similar vehicles have their own lower limit of 40 km/h on roads other than expressways. Posted signs always override the general figure. Our guide to [motorbike speed limits](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/speed-limits-motorbikes-vietnam/) goes through the rest. A small 50cc scooter is a weak choice for Highway 3 for this reason, and our guide to [renting a 50cc scooter in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/renting-a-50cc-scooter-in-hanoi/) explains what that class is suited for.

**Checkpoints.** If a traffic officer signals you to stop, stop in a safe place. Under Article 11 of Law 36/2024/QH15, the signals of the person directing traffic come before lights and signs. Stay calm, keep your documents ready and follow the instructions. This is general information, not legal advice.

## The stops, in a sensible order

Guides give different distances, since each measures from a different point, so use them for relative planning only.

### 1. Soc Temple and the Saint Giong statue

The Soc Temple complex, in Phu Linh commune, honours Saint Giong, the legendary hero who is said to have ridden to the top of Soc Mountain after defeating invaders and risen into the sky. VietnamNet describes a monument to him on the mountain, built in 2010 for the 1,000th anniversary of Thang Long-Hanoi, at an altitude of about 300 metres. An Origin Vietnam guide describes the statue as a bronze one about 15 metres high and says the walk up includes nearly 2,000 steps, with a steeper way at the front and a gentler way at the back. These details come from one commercial guide and should be treated as indicative.

A national tourism information site adds that a festival is held at the temple every year, that around Tet it is one of the country's biggest, and that the foot of the mountain is a popular picnic spot. Our advice is to avoid the festival days on a scooter. The roads are crowded and the parking is thin.

The same Origin Vietnam guide describes the nearby Non Nuoc Pagoda as close to the statue, with a large Tathagata Buddha figure over 8 metres high.

### 2. The lakes and forest edges

The lakes are the quiet part of the day. VietnamNet's guide suggests morning walks or jogs around Keo Ca Lake, Dong Quan Lake and, further afield, Dong Do Lake. VnExpress highlights Ham Lon Lake as a natural highlight, with people camping by Nui Bau Lake at the foot of Ham Lon Mountain. Origin Vietnam lists the Dong Quan lake ecological forest as a place for hill walking and camping, and Chom Nui, or Hoa Son, Lake in Nam Son.

Choose one or two of them rather than all of them. Lanes near the water are narrow and sometimes unsurfaced, so ride slowly and be ready to turn around.

### 3. Lunch

Dong Quan Lake is known for a braised fish dish that received an official certificate, according to Origin Vietnam. Restaurants around the lakes tend to serve outdoor, rustic meals. We cannot confirm opening hours or prices, so ask before you sit down.

## Riding in the hills

The hill roads are modest, but a few habits help.

- **Go down slowly.** On an automatic scooter, use both brakes gently and early, and do not hold the front brake alone. Hot, long braking fades.
- **Watch the surface.** Gravel at the edge of lakeside lanes and fallen leaves after rain can make the front wheel slide. Our guide to [riding in Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) explains what wet roads do.
- **Do not overload.** A pillion with a heavy bag makes a small scooter hard to hold on a slope. See our guide to [carrying a passenger and luggage on a Honda Lead or Vision](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/scooter-for-two-riders-and-luggage-hanoi/).
- **Park carefully on a slope.** Use the stand on firm ground and lock the steering.
- **Mind the light.** Light fades earlier under trees and on hill roads. See [riding at night in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-at-night-in-hanoi/).

If the route turns steep and technical, a larger or more powerful machine is not a fix. Our guide to [manual bikes and steep mountain roads](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/manual-bikes-and-steep-mountain-roads-when-to-choose-another-vehicle/) explains when to choose a different trip.

## What to leave for another day

Some ideas are poor fits for one scooter day:

- A full climb to the top and back plus several lakes. Pick one.
- Overnight camping on a rental bike. Check the shop's terms first.
- Late returns. Hill roads and Highway 3 are worse in the dark.
- Festival days, unless you plan to arrive very early or go by other transport.

## FAQ: Hanoi to Soc Son by scooter

### How far is Soc Son from central Hanoi?

Published guides give figures from about 25 to 40 kilometres, depending on where they measure from and which attraction they mean. Allow about an hour each way in good conditions.

### Which road do I take?

Guides describe the Nhat Tan Bridge, the airport road and National Highway 3. Check a map app and avoid expressways.

### Is the climb to the Saint Giong statue hard?

One guide says it involves nearly 2,000 steps, with a steeper and a gentler way. Wear sturdy shoes and plan for heat.

### Can I take a rental scooter there?

Ask the shop. Not every rental allows travel outside the city.

### Is Highway 3 safe on a scooter?

It is busy, and a 2024 report described parts as narrow with frequent accidents. Ride defensively and leave early.

### Should I go on a festival day?

Only if you enjoy crowds. Roads and parking are tight, so a normal weekday is easier.
