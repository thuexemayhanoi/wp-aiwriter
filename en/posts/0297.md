The simplest answer is that the Temple of Literature publishes its own advice for two-wheelers: the official visitor page lists Giam Garden as the parking area for motorbikes and bicycles, and lists "two-wheel parking" among the site's facilities. Cars and tourist buses are directed along Van Mieu Street instead. So on a rented scooter you do not need to hunt for a gap on the pavement of Quoc Tu Giam Street. Aim for the Giam Garden side of the complex, follow any attendant's directions, keep your parking ticket and remember which gate you will leave by, because the site has one entrance and several exits. This guide explains how the area is laid out, how to approach it, what to do with your helmet and bags and what to check before you walk in.

## How the site and its four streets fit together

The Temple of Literature, known locally as Van Mieu - Quoc Tu Giam, sits in the Dong Da side of central Hanoi, south of the old Thang Long citadel and roughly 2 to 3 km west of Hoan Kiem Lake. The whole complex covers 54,331 square metres, and the official description says it is made up of Van Lake, Giam Garden and the walled inner sanctuary. That matters for parking, because the walled temple is only one part of a block that is surrounded by roads on every side.

Travel guides describe the block as meeting four streets: Quoc Tu Giam at the main gate, Van Mieu Street, Nguyen Thai Hoc and Ton Duc Thang. Two of the guides we read add that many of the roads around the temple are one-way, so it is worth looking at the approach on a map before you ride rather than while you are in the junction. The practical consequence is that the street you arrive on may not be the street you want to park on, and a one-way system can turn a missed turn into a long loop.

## What the official page says about two-wheelers

The temple's official visitor-information page, in its directions section, gives separate parking lines for each kind of transport:

| Vehicle | Parking stated by the site |
|---|---|
| Private cars | Along Van Mieu Street |
| Motorbikes and bicycles | Giam Garden |
| Tourist buses | Along Van Mieu Street |

The same page lists the site's facilities, which include an information desk, audio guide, restrooms, a tea house and two-wheel parking. It does not publish a fee, a capacity or a closing time for the parking itself, so do not assume any of those. If the attendant quotes a price, that is the price for that day, and the sensible response is to ask for a ticket and keep it.

Because this page is the only source we found that comes from the temple's own management, we treat it as the baseline. Blogs and booking sites sometimes name side streets as parking spots, but street arrangements in Hanoi change, and a guarded garden run by the site itself is easier to rely on than a roadside tip from several years ago.

## Entrance, exits and why they matter for your scooter

The official page gives one entrance, at 58 Quoc Tu Giam Street, and two exits: Nos. 19 to 21 Van Mieu Street, and a path to Giam Garden opposite No. 40 Ton Duc Thang Street. In other words, a visit can end on a different street from where it began. Plan this before you go in:

1. Decide where the scooter will be. If it is in Giam Garden, the Giam Garden exit path is the natural way back to it.
2. Take a photo of the parking area, the attendant's booth or a nearby landmark, and of your ticket.
3. If you walk out of a different exit, you will need a minute or two to walk around to the bike, so allow for that if you are meeting someone or catching a booked slot.

During events or festivals the layout may change, so treat any attendant's instruction as more current than a guide or a map.

## Opening hours and how they affect when you park

The official site says the temple is open from 8:00 to 17:00 on all days of the week, with an evening programme from 18:00 to 22:00 on Wednesday, Saturday and Sunday. A different visitor site lists a night-tour schedule with other times, so check the official page for the day you plan to go rather than relying on any single number.

For parking, the main point is that the evening programme brings people back to an area that is quieter in the late afternoon. ## Approaching the temple on a rental scooter

Without making up a single "correct" route, here is a sensible way to prepare:

- **Set the destination as the Giam Garden or Van Mieu Street, not only the main gate.** A map pin on the Quoc Tu Giam frontage may put you on the wrong side of a one-way street for the parking area.
- **Look at the street view or satellite view beforehand.** Look at which side of the road the parking is and where you must cross traffic.
- **Allow extra time on the first approach.** The temple is close to the middle of the city, and the surrounding streets are busy in the morning and late afternoon.
- **Do not ride onto the pavement or stop on the carriageway to wait for a gap.** Go round the block instead.

## Locking, helmet and bags

The shop includes one basic helmet per rider and does not provide an extra lock, so the practical routine is to lock the steering and park in a guarded place. At the temple, the official rules ask visitors to keep quiet in sacred areas, not to bring food or drink in (a VnExpress guide states this for food and drink), and to follow security instructions.

That leaves the question of what to do with a helmet. You will be walking through the grounds for some time, and a helmet in your hand is awkward. Options include:

- carrying it on your arm and putting it down only on a bench you can see;
- asking the attendant whether it can be left with the scooter, understanding that this is at your own risk;
- using a helmet lock only if your own gear has one.

Do not leave a phone, passport copy, wallet or camera in the seat compartment. Take what you need and leave the rest at your accommodation. If your accommodation is somewhere you cannot easily return to, it is better to travel with fewer valuables than to hope that the compartment will protect them.

## If Giam Garden is full or closed

Occasionally a guarded area will be full, closed for an event or reorganised. In that case:

1. Ask the staff or attendant where they want two-wheelers to go. They know the current arrangement.
2. If you must find another place, choose a guarded lot or a clearly marked parking area, not a blocked doorway, a pavement ramp or a gap beside a no-parking sign.
3. Photograph where you left the bike and note the street name.
4. Do not leave the bike on a street where parking is clearly not intended, even for a few minutes. Vehicles that obstruct a pavement can be moved or ticketed, and our guide to parking in Hanoi explains how towing works.

This is general guidance rather than a rule we can cite for this exact street. For the shop's own approach to tickets and misplaced bikes, see the [rental guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-guide/).

## Combining the temple with other stops

Because the temple is a short distance from other central sights, many visitors do not just park once. One visitor guide places it about 700 m from the Ho Chi Minh Mausoleum complex. If you plan to combine the sights, you have two choices:

- **Park once and walk.** This is often the better choice when stops are close together, because each re-park costs time.
- **Ride between stops.** This suits sights that are farther apart. Check each site's own parking before you leave.

## FAQ: parking at the Temple of Literature

### Where do motorbikes park at the Temple of Literature?

According to the temple's official visitor page, motorbikes and bicycles park at Giam Garden, and the facilities list includes two-wheel parking. Cars and tourist buses are directed along Van Mieu Street.

### Is parking free?

The official page does not state a fee. Ask the attendant, keep your ticket and do not pay anyone who is not clearly part of the parking operation.

### Which gate do I use to go in?

The official entrance is at 58 Quoc Tu Giam Street. Exits are at Nos. 19 to 21 Van Mieu Street and by a path to Giam Garden opposite No. 40 Ton Duc Thang Street.

### Are the streets around the temple one-way?

Several travel guides say many of the surrounding streets are one-way. Check your approach on a map before you set off, and follow the signs on the day.

### Can I leave my helmet on the scooter?

You can ask the attendant, but any item left on a bike is at your own risk. Many riders carry a helmet in their hand or on their arm.

### Can I visit in the evening?

The official site lists an evening programme from 18:00 to 22:00 on Wednesday, Saturday and Sunday, with a separate ticket. Check it on the day, and plan your ride home before dark.
