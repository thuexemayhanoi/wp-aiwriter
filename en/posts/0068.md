Chain tension is one of the pre-ride checks Honda specifies, and before a longer ride on a rented Honda Wave or Yamaha Sirius it is worth a minute of your time. The check measures how much the chain moves up and down between the sprockets, and the figure is set by the manufacturer for each model. For the Honda Wave RSX FI, the owner's manual gives a chain slack of 20 to 30 millimetres. Too little slack stretches the chain and strains the sprockets, and too much lets the chain whip or come off. The adjustment itself belongs to a mechanic, not to a visitor. This guide explains what the check involves, what the manual says, what a renter can reasonably do with eyes and fingers, how to ask the shop to show you the slack, and what to look for if the chain is too tight or too loose. It is general information, not a substitute for the owner's manual of the exact bike.

## Why tension matters

Honda's aftersales guidance explains the problem in two sentences. A chain that is too tight stretches sooner. A chain that is too loose does not engage the sprocket properly. And even with the right slack, the chain and the sprockets wear together, which is why the engine-side sprocket also wears. Honda lists chain slack inspection among the pre-ride checks and says to take a chain that is too tight or too loose to a Honda dealer to be fixed immediately.

For a rider on a long trip, the stakes are practical. A chain that is too loose can jump off the sprocket and lock the rear wheel or leave you stranded. A chain that is too tight wears quickly and puts extra load on the gearbox and wheel bearings. Neither is something you want to discover at the top of a mountain.

## What Honda's manual specifies

Honda's Wave RSX FI owner's manual gives these details:

- **Chain slack:** 20 to 30 mm.
- **How it is checked:** shift to neutral and switch off the engine, put the bike on its centre stand on flat, firm ground, remove the inspection hole cap, move the chain up and down with your finger and spin the rear wheel to see whether the chain runs smoothly.
- **How often:** check the chain before every ride and lubricate it every 500 km, and more often if you ride on poor roads, at high speed or with repeated hard acceleration.
- **Chain type:** a 420-size chain with 104 links on that model.

The manual describes this as an owner's check. The adjustment itself is a workshop job that shops do with tools and care. As a renter you do not need to do it.

## What twenty to thirty millimetres looks like

Twenty to thirty millimetres is two to three centimetres, a little more than the width of a thumb. That is the total up-and-down movement of the chain at the point the manual specifies. It is a small amount, and it is easy to misjudge by eye, which is why Honda gives a number and a method. Chains also wear unevenly, so a link or two can be tighter than the rest. For that reason Honda's procedure includes turning the rear wheel and watching that the chain runs smoothly all the way round, not only measuring at one spot. If the slack changes noticeably as the wheel turns, mention it to the shop, because a chain with tight and loose patches often means wear.

## Other models differ

The 20 to 30 mm figure is for the Wave RSX FI. Other models, including the Wave Alpha, the Blade and the Yamaha Sirius, have their own specifications and their own measurement points. Honda's aftersales article on chain care notes that the measurement position and method differ between motorcycles, and that some are measured as a distance from the swing arm, so the owner's manual should be checked. Do not apply a number from one model to another. Ask the shop for the figure for the bike you have and ask them to show you the check.

## What a renter can do

You do not need tools to make a useful check before a longer ride. With the bike parked on level ground, in neutral, the engine off and the bike on its stand, you can:

1. **Look at the chain** along its visible length. It should have a thin film of oil, no rust, no stiff or kinked links and no thick layer of grit.
2. **Press the middle of the lower run** gently up and down with a finger. The chain should have a small amount of play, not so much that it hangs in a deep curve and not so little that it feels stretched like a bowstring.
3. **Turn the rear wheel by hand** if it is safe to do so and watch the chain move. It should travel smoothly, with no tight spots where the play changes.
4. **Listen as you turn it.** Grinding or clicking is worth reporting.
5. **Look at the sprocket teeth** if you can see them. Teeth that look hooked or pointed suggest wear.

If you do not know what normal looks like, ask the shop to show you on this bike. A mechanic can demonstrate the check in under a minute.

## How to ask the shop

Be specific and friendly. For example:

- "I am going on a longer ride tomorrow. Could you check the chain slack and show me what it should be on this bike?"
- "Has the chain been lubricated recently?"
- "Is the chain adjusted to the specification for this model?"

If the shop adjusts it, ask them to do so before you leave, not on the road. ## Signs the chain is too loose

- A visible sag that hangs low between the sprockets.
- A rattle or slapping when you open and close the throttle.
- A clunk when pulling away or changing gear.
- The chain touching the chain guard or the swing arm.
- Jerky drive that feels like a slip. Our guide to [what a slipping gear feels like on a rented motorbike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-a-slipping-gear-feels-like-on-a-rented-motorbike/) explains how to tell the difference.

## Signs the chain is too tight

- No play at all when you press the chain with a finger.
- A whine or hum that rises with speed.
- A rear wheel that feels stiff when you turn it by hand.
- The chain looks stretched like a taut string.
- Rapid wear on the chain and sprockets over a short time.

A chain that is too tight is harder to spot than a loose one, so ask the shop to measure if you are unsure.

## What not to do

- **Do not adjust the chain yourself.** It requires loosening the axle and aligning the wheel, and getting it wrong is dangerous.
- **Do not clean it with petrol.** Honda's manual says never to use petrol or a low-flash-point solvent.
- **Do not get lubricant on the brakes or tyres.** Honda warns to keep it off both, and not to apply too much.
- **Do not ride on a chain that you think is badly loose or tight.** Return to the shop.
- **Do not check the chain with the engine running or the bike in gear.** Honda's procedure is with the engine off and the bike in neutral.

## Re-checking on a longer trip

On a multi-day ride, check the chain by eye each morning and listen for changes during the day. Rain, dust and rough roads change its condition quickly. ## FAQ: chain tension

### How much slack should a Honda Wave chain have?

For the Wave RSX FI, Honda's manual gives 20 to 30 mm. Other models differ, so ask the shop for the figure for your bike.

### How do I check chain slack?

With the bike in neutral, the engine off and the bike on its stand on level ground, move the chain up and down with a finger and turn the rear wheel to check it runs smoothly. Ask the shop to show you on your bike.

### Can I adjust the chain myself?

No. Honda advises taking a chain that is too tight or too loose to a dealer. Ask the shop.

### How often should the chain be checked?

Honda lists chain slack among the checks before every ride and gives a lubrication interval of every 500 km.

### What happens if the chain is too loose?

It does not engage the sprocket properly and can whip, rattle or come off. It needs adjusting before you ride far.

### What happens if it is too tight?

It stretches and wears faster and strains the sprockets. Ask the shop to check it.
