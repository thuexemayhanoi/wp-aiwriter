A Honda Wave uses very little petrol, and the sum for a trip budget is short: distance in kilometres, divided by 100, times litres per 100 km, times the price on the pump. The only real uncertainty is the middle figure, because the manufacturer's number comes from a test and your ride will use more. This guide gives Honda's published figures for the Wave models, shows a simple formula and a table of litres for common day trips, suggests an allowance for real riding and explains how fuel works at a Hanoi rental shop. It deliberately leaves out pump prices, because they change and the board at the station is the only reliable source. For that side of the question, see our guide to [fuel in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/fuel-in-hanoi-petrol-stations-rental-scooter/).

## What Honda publishes

Honda Vietnam's product pages give a fuel-consumption figure for each model, measured on the manufacturer's test cycle. As of October 2026:

| Model | Fuel tank | Honda's figure |
|---|---|---|
| Wave Alpha 110 | 3.7 litres | 1.72 L per 100 km |
| Wave RSX FI | 4.0 litres | 1.56 L per 100 km |
| Blade 110 | 3.7 litres | 1.85 L per 100 km |

For comparison, Yamaha publishes 1.65 L per 100 km for the Sirius FI with a 4.0-litre tank. These are laboratory-style figures, and Honda's note on the Winner R shows that even the test method can change between model generations, so treat the numbers as the best case, not a promise.

## The formula

For any trip:

**Litres needed = distance in km ÷ 100 × litres per 100 km**

**Cost = litres needed × price per litre on the pump**

Add a margin for real riding. For example, a 120 km round trip on a Wave RSX FI at Honda's figure needs 120 ÷ 100 × 1.56, which is about 1.9 litres. With a 30 per cent allowance for traffic, hills, a passenger and luggage, plan on about 2.4 litres. Multiply by the price shown on the pump on the day.

The last two rows show why tank size matters. A 4.0-litre tank only just covers a 200 km day once you add the allowance, so plan at least one fill-up on a long trip. A 3.7-litre tank on the Alpha or Blade is no better.

## Why real use is higher than the test

Several things push consumption above the manufacturer's figure:

- **Stop-and-go traffic.** Accelerating from a stop uses more fuel than cruising.
- **Weight.** A passenger and bags add load, and Honda's manual lists maximum loads of 115 kg or 155 kg depending on version.
- **Hills.** A climb needs more throttle, and a descent does not give the fuel back.
- **Speed.** A small engine pushed near its top speed uses more fuel per kilometre than at a moderate pace. Our guide to [speed limits for motorbikes in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/speed-limits-motorbikes-vietnam/) explains the limits.
- **Headwinds and rain.** Both increase drag and rolling resistance.
- **Tyre pressure and chain condition.** A soft tyre or a dry, tight chain wastes energy. Honda's manual lists tyre pressures and chain care for the Wave RSX FI.
- **Idling and revving.** Honda warns that long idling or continuous revving can damage the engine and exhaust, as well as wasting fuel.

On a mixed Hanoi day with some traffic and a passenger, an allowance of 20 to 40 per cent over Honda's figure is a sensible planning range. On a steady ride on a flat road with one rider, the real figure may come closer to the test.

## Budgeting with the tank, not just the trip

A small tank changes how you plan more than a small price per litre. On the Wave RSX FI, Honda says that when the fuel needle is on the red mark about 1.2 litres remain, out of a 4.0-litre tank. At Honda's figure that is theoretically enough for about 75 km, but real riding gives less, so treat the red mark as the signal to refuel at the next station.

For a day trip, a useful rule is to fill up before you leave the city, plan a top-up when the gauge is at about half and never rely on the last quarter. In the countryside around Hanoi you are rarely far from a station, but on mountain roads they can be sparse. ## The rental shop's fuel rules

Fuel works differently at each shop. At Nguyen Ha Motorbike Rental, petrol bikes are handed over with roughly 0.5 to 2 litres in the tank and are expected back with roughly 0.5 to 1 litre. Returning a bike with an empty tank costs VND 20,000. The shop does not refund fuel left in the tank, so there is no benefit in filling right up at the end. Electric bikes are different: the battery level and charging are confirmed at handover. Our guide to [returning your rental motorbike in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/returning-your-rental-motorbike-hanoi/) explains how the return works, and our guide to [charging an electric scooter in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/charging-an-electric-scooter-in-hanoi/) covers that side.

The practical effect is that your fuel cost for a rental is the cost of the petrol you put in during the rental, less whatever you started with and plus whatever you leave. Because the start and end levels are similar, you can treat your spend as the fuel your riding burns.

## Which fuel to buy

Honda's Wave RSX FI manual says to use unleaded petrol only and gives a minimum octane that depends on the model version: RON 91 or higher for one version, 92 for another and 95 for another. It also says that petrol with up to 10 per cent ethanol is acceptable, and that more than 10 per cent can damage the paint, rubber fuel lines and tank and cause poor running. Yamaha has published a notice on E5 and E10 compatibility for its fuel-injected models. Because the right octane depends on the bike, ask the shop which fuel to use and do not guess. ## How to make fuel use lower

A few habits reduce consumption without slowing you much:

- **Keep tyres at the right pressure.** Our guide to [choosing tyres for a manual bike rental in wet weather](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/choosing-tyres-for-a-manual-bike-rental-in-wet-weather/) gives Honda's figures for the RSX FI.
- **Ride smoothly.** Gentle throttle and early braking use less fuel than sharp starts and late stops.
- **Use the right gear.** Staying in a gear that is too low revs the engine unnecessarily, and too high makes it labour. Our guide to [starting, shifting and stopping a Honda Wave smoothly](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/honda-wave-gears-starting-shifting-and-stopping-smoothly/) explains the technique.
- **Avoid long idling.** Honda warns against it.
- **Do not carry unnecessary weight.**
- **Plan the route.** Avoid doubling back and sitting in congestion.

## A trip-budget worksheet

1. **Estimate the distance** with a map app, and add 10 to 15 per cent for detours.
2. **Pick your litres per 100 km** from the table, and add an allowance for your conditions.
3. **Work out the litres:** distance ÷ 100 × litres per 100 km.
4. **Check the tank:** will the litres fit in one tank with a margin, or do you need a stop?
5. **Look at the pump price** on the day and multiply.
6. **Add the rental's own fuel rule:** start and return levels, and any empty-tank charge.

## FAQ: Honda Wave fuel economy

### How much fuel does a Honda Wave use?

Honda lists 1.72 L per 100 km for the Wave Alpha and 1.56 L per 100 km for the Wave RSX FI as test figures. Real use is higher.

### How far can a Wave go on a tank?

On Honda's figures, a 4.0-litre tank could cover over 250 km in theory, but plan to refuel at about half a tank.

### Does a passenger change fuel use?

Yes. Extra weight raises consumption, and Honda's manuals set maximum loads of 115 kg or 155 kg depending on version.

### What does the red mark on the fuel gauge mean?

On the Wave RSX FI, Honda says about 1.2 litres remain at the red mark. Refuel soon.

### Do I have to return the bike with a full tank?

Not at Nguyen Ha. Bikes come back with roughly 0.5 to 1 litre, and only an empty tank is charged, at VND 20,000.

### Which petrol should I use?

Unleaded, at the octane the shop specifies for your bike. Honda says up to 10 per cent ethanol is acceptable.
