When a rental scooter breaks down, the first job is to get it off the traffic lane and to a place where it will not be hit, and the second is to call the shop. Choose a spot close to the right edge of the road, with good visibility in both directions, away from bends, bridges, junctions and crossings, and switch on the hazard lights. Law 36/2024/QH15 lists the places where stopping and parking are banned (Article 18), and it says that if a technical incident or another force majeure event forces a vehicle to stop on part of the carriageway or in a place not designated for stopping, the operator must warn others with emergency lights or a warning sign behind the vehicle. Nguyen Ha's published pages say to contact the shop if there is a mechanical issue, a puncture, an accident or unexpected damage, and not to assume the cost or the repair approach without checking the agreement.

This guide explains how to decide where to stop while the bike still rolls, which places are banned or dangerous, how to make the bike visible, how to park on a slope, what to do on a bridge, at a level crossing, at night or in rain, who to call and what not to do. It is general information based on the law as of October 2026, not legal advice or a repair guide.

## What the law says

Article 18 of Law 36/2024/QH15 covers stopping and parking. Several points matter after a breakdown.

- **Where to stop.** On roads, operators may stop and park only in places with wide roadsides or land outside the carriageway. If the roadside is narrow or absent, they may stop and park only close to the right edge of the road in their direction. On streets, they must stop close to the right roadside or sidewalk, with the nearest wheel no more than 0.25 metre from it, without causing obstruction or danger.

- **Signal.** When entering or leaving a stopping place on the road, signal to other operators, and do not affect pedestrians and vehicles.

- **Banned places.** Operators may not stop or park on the left side of one-way roads, on bends or near crests with obscured visibility, on bridges, under overpasses, on pedestrian crossings, at intersections and within 5 metres of their edges, at pick-up and drop-off points, in front of gates and within 5 metres of them, where the road is wide enough for only one motor-vehicle lane, within the railway safety scope, where they would hide road signs or traffic lights, on roads reserved for buses and in several other places, except where stopping is expressly allowed.

- **Force majeure.** If a technical incident or other force majeure forces a stop on part of the carriageway or in a place not designated for parking, the operator must signal with emergency lights or place a warning sign behind the vehicle.

- **Slopes.** A parked vehicle on a steep road must have its wheels steered toward the roadside and chocked.

Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, as read on the Government portal, sets a fine of VND 600,000 to 800,000 for stopping or parking on a bridge, and other ranges for other banned places. A real breakdown is treated differently from convenience parking, but the safest course is to move out of a banned place as soon as you can. Check the current text if a figure matters to you. ## While the bike is still rolling

The decision about where to stop is made in the first seconds.

1. **Stay calm,** and keep both hands on the bars.

2. **Check your mirror,** and signal right.

3. **Switch on the hazard lights** if the bike has them and they still work.

4. **Slow smoothly** with both brakes. If the engine has died, you may still have brakes, but the lights and horn may be affected.

5. **Look for the best spot within reach,** not the first one.

6. **Coast to the right edge,** and stop with the nearest wheel close to it.

7. **Do not turn or swerve** across traffic to reach a spot on the other side.

If the engine cuts out, you can often roll for a surprising distance. Use that distance to reach a safe place instead of stopping where you are.

## The best places to stop

Look for a stopping place with these features.

- **A wide roadside, a paved shoulder or a space outside the carriageway.** Article 18 prefers these.

- **Good visibility in both directions,** so approaching traffic sees you in time.

- **A straight, level stretch,** not a bend or the top of a rise.

- **A guarded car park or a petrol station,** if one is close. Our guide to [fuel in Hanoi: petrol stations and rental scooters](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/fuel-in-hanoi-petrol-stations-rental-scooter/) explains stations.

- **A shop front or open yard** with room beside it.

- **A wide junction mouth or side street,** but only if you do not block it.

- **Somewhere you can stand safely,** off the road, behind a barrier or a kerb.

## The worst places to stop

Avoid stopping in these places if you can reach somewhere else.

| Place | Why it is dangerous |
|---|---|
| Bends and the tops of rises | Approaching drivers cannot see you in time |
| Bridges and the approaches to bridges | Article 18 bans stopping on bridges, and there is little room |
| Under overpasses | Banned except where allowed, and traffic and visibility are poor |
| Pedestrian crossings and within 5 metres of junctions | Banned, and you block people and turning vehicles |
| Level crossings and the railway safety scope | Banned, and trains cannot stop |
| Road tunnels | Banned, and there is little room |
| Single-lane stretches | Article 18 bans stopping where the road is wide enough for one motor-vehicle lane only |
| Bus stops and bus lanes | Banned, and buses need the space |
| The left side of a one-way street | Banned |
| Where you would hide a sign or a signal | Banned |

## If you are on a bridge

If the bike fails on a bridge, you cannot choose the ideal spot, and Article 18 bans stopping there. So:

1. **Try to reach the far end** while the bike still rolls.

2. **If you cannot, stop as far to the right as possible,** switch on the hazard lights and stay clear of the lane.

3. **Warn following traffic** if it is safe to do so.

4. **Call for help,** the shop first, and the emergency number 112 if anyone is in danger.

5. **Do not stand in the traffic lane.**

## If you are at a level crossing

If the bike fails at a railway crossing, Article 24 of the Law says that if a vehicle is damaged and cannot be moved out of the railway safety scope at once, the operator and people present must immediately signal to stop the train and take safety measures.

1. **Get everyone clear of the track at once.**

2. **Try to move the bike off the rails,** if you can do it in seconds.

3. **If you cannot,** leave it and move to a safe place, and warn the crossing keeper or the railway staff, and call 112.

4. **Never stay near the tracks** while a train may come.

## On a slope

Article 18 says a vehicle parked on a steep road must be steered toward the roadside and chocked. On a scooter, in practice:

- **Park across the slope,** if the road allows, close to the kerb, with the front wheel turned toward it.

- **Use the centre stand** if the bike has one, and the parking brake or the brake lock where fitted.

- **Leave a manual bike in gear,** if it is safe to do so.

- **Place a stone or a block behind the wheel** if you can find one.

- **Do not leave the bike where it could roll into traffic.**

## Making the bike visible

Article 18 requires emergency lights or a warning sign behind the vehicle where you stop on part of the carriageway or in a non-designated place.

- **Switch on the hazard lights.** If the battery is flat, they may not work.

- **Place something behind the bike,** if it is safe to walk there: a reflective warning triangle if you have one, or any bright object. Do not step into the traffic lane to do it.

- **Use your phone torch** at night to signal to approaching drivers, from a safe position at the roadside.

- **Move people away from the bike,** behind a barrier or onto the footpath.

On a scooter you probably do not carry a warning triangle, so the lights and your position are your main tools.

## At night and in rain

- **At night,** a stopped scooter with no lights is almost invisible. Get it off the road, switch on any lights that work and stand somewhere lit, away from the bike. - **In rain,** visibility is lower and the road is slippery, so stop early and gently, and shelter in a building if you can. Do not shelter under an overpass or a tree. ## Who to call, and what to say

1. **Call or message Nguyen Ha** on 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969), open 09:00 to 21:00 daily. The published pages say to contact the shop for a mechanical issue, a puncture, an accident or unexpected damage.

2. **Give your name, the plate number and your exact location.** Send a map pin by message.

3. **Describe what happened:** the noise, the smell, the warning lights, whether the engine will start.

4. **Say whether you are safe,** and where the bike is.

5. **Ask what the shop wants you to do,** and who will pay for recovery. The published terms do not state a recovery fee or a timeline, so ask and write down the answer.

6. **Take photographs** of the bike, the dashboard and the place.

7. **Call 112** if anyone is hurt or in danger. Decree 200/2025/ND-CP set up the national 112 emergency call centre, free of charge, 24 hours a day.

## What not to do

- **Do not leave the bike on the carriageway** and walk away.

- **Do not try to repair it in the traffic lane.**

- **Do not accept repairs or towing** from a stranger without agreeing the price first and telling the shop. Our guide to [how to keep receipts for repairs on a rental motorbike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-keep-receipts-for-repairs-on-a-rental-motorbike/) explains how to keep a record.

- **Do not push the bike across traffic.**

- **Do not stand between the bike and the traffic.**

- **Do not leave valuables on the bike.**

## A quick checklist

1. **Signal, slow and coast to the right edge.**

2. **Stop where visibility is good, off bends, bridges, junctions and crossings.**

3. **Switch on the hazard lights.**

4. **Move yourself and any passenger away from the traffic.**

5. **Call the shop, and 112 if there is danger.**

6. **Photograph the bike and the place.**

7. **Do not repair it or accept help without telling the shop.**

## FAQ: where to stop after a breakdown

### Where should I stop a broken-down scooter?

Close to the right edge of the road, outside the traffic lane if possible, on a straight stretch with good visibility, and away from bends, bridges, junctions and crossings.

### Can I stop on a bridge if the bike fails?

Article 18 bans stopping on bridges except where allowed, but if you cannot reach the end, stop as far right as you can, use hazard lights and get clear of the lane.

### Do I need hazard lights?

Article 18 says that where a technical incident forces you to stop on the carriageway or in a non-designated place, you must signal with emergency lights or place a warning sign behind the vehicle.

### Who pays for recovery?

The published terms do not state a recovery fee. Ask the shop before you agree, and keep the answer in writing.

### What if the bike breaks down at a level crossing?

Get everyone clear at once, signal to stop the train if you can and call 112. Article 24 says to signal the train to stop and take safety measures.

### Who do I call first?

The shop, on 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969), and 112 if anyone is in danger.
