A car door that opens into your path leaves almost no time to react, so the safest habit on a scooter is to treat every parked or stopped car as a door that might open, and to leave room, slow down and watch for occupants. The law puts the main duty on the person opening the door. Article 19 of Law 36/2024/QH15 says a vehicle's doors may be opened only when the vehicle has completely stopped or parked, and that before opening, the person must observe the front, the rear and the outer side of the door and open it only when it is safe. They must not leave the door open if that is unsafe. But the person who is hit is the rider, so the law is only part of the answer, and the rest is how you ride.

This guide explains the law, shows where and why doors open into scooter traffic, describes the warning signs, gives a technique for passing parked and stopped cars, covers taxis and ride-hailing pick-ups, and tells you what to do if a door hits you. It is general information based on the law as of October 2026, not legal advice.

## What the law says

Article 19 of Law 36/2024/QH15, which came into force on 1 January 2025, is short and clear.

1. **Doors may be opened only when the vehicle has completely stopped or parked.**

2. **Before opening a door, the person must observe the front, behind and the outer side of the door,** and open it and get out only when it is safe.

3. **The door must not be left open if that is unsafe.**

The duty applies to the person opening the door, which means passengers as well as the driver. As reported by Vietnamese media, a safe-driving specialist recommends that people alight on the kerb side where possible, look back before opening and open slowly. Under Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, as read in the text quoted by Vietnamese legal publications, a car driver who opens a door or leaves it open unsafely can be fined VND 4,000,000 to 6,000,000, and if the act causes a traffic accident the fine is VND 20,000,000 to 22,000,000, with licence points deducted. Those figures are in the decree for car drivers, and you should check the current text if you need a figure.

## Why it matters on a scooter

A scooter rider is narrow and quick, and passes close to parked and stopped cars. A door that opens a metre into the lane can be a wall. At normal city speeds, there may be no time to stop, and the choices are to hit the door, to swerve into the traffic beside you or to fall. Either of the last two can be worse than the door. The hazard is not only the car that is parked. It is also a car that has just stopped to drop off or pick up, and a car stopped in traffic.

## Where doors open into scooter traffic

- **Along streets with parked cars,** where a driver or passenger gets out onto the road side.

- **Where a taxi or ride-hailing car stops** to drop off or pick up, often without pulling fully to the kerb.

- **At traffic lights and in queues,** where a passenger leaves a car that has stopped in traffic.

- **Outside hotels, shops and offices,** where cars stop briefly.

- **In narrow streets,** where there is little room between the car and passing traffic.

- **At night,** when a door is harder to see.

## Warning signs that a door may open

Learn to read the car before you reach it.

| Sign | What it may mean |
|---|---|
| Brake lights just came on | The car has just stopped, and someone may get out |
| Hazard lights flashing | The car is stopped for a drop-off or a short wait |
| Engine running, a driver in the seat | The car may move, or the driver may get out |
| A passenger moving in the back seat | Someone is about to alight |
| A head turning or a hand on the door handle | A door is about to open |
| The car pulling in or stopping at a doorway | A drop-off |
| A taxi or ride-hailing car with its roof sign or a driver looking at a phone | A pick-up or drop-off |
| Interior light on at night | Someone is getting out |
| A wheel turned out | The car is about to pull out |

If you see any of these signs, slow down, and move out early, if the lane and the traffic behind you allow it.

## How to ride past parked and stopped cars

The technique has four parts: look, slow, space and signal.

1. **Look ahead.** Read the row of parked cars and the stopped vehicles far enough ahead to decide early.

2. **Slow down.** Reduce your speed before you reach the cars, so that you have time to react. Article 12 of the Law requires operators to keep a speed suited to the road, traffic density and conditions.

3. **Leave room.** Keep at least the width of a car door from the parked car where the lane allows it, and more for a stopped car with people in it. If you cannot leave that much, slow down further.

4. **Signal and check behind before you move out.** Article 15 requires a safe distance from the vehicle behind and a signal before a change of direction. Do not swerve out at the last moment, because the vehicle behind you is another hazard.

Do not ride tight against the car to leave room for other traffic, and do not cut in right after passing. Our guide to [how to follow a safe distance in Hanoi traffic](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-follow-a-safe-distance-in-hanoi-traffic/) explains how to keep your own space.

## The balance: door room versus traffic beside you

The difficult case is a narrow street where you cannot move out because of oncoming traffic or vehicles beside you. In that case:

- **Slow right down,** to walking pace if you need to.

- **Stay behind a vehicle** that is blocking your way, rather than squeezing past a row of cars.

- **Take the line that gives you the most room,** and ready your brakes.

- **Be prepared to stop completely.**

- **Do not accelerate** to get through. A slower pass that costs you a minute is better than a fall.

A door can open with no warning at all, and the only safe margin is speed and distance.

## Taxis and ride-hailing

Cars that stop to drop off or pick up are a particular hazard.

- **They often stop in the lane,** not at the kerb.

- **Passengers get out on the traffic side,** because it is quicker.

- **The driver may stop suddenly** when they see the passenger.

- **At night,** the car's lights may blind you or hide a door.

Treat any car that is slowing near a doorway or a junction as a car that is about to stop. ## If a door hits you

Stay calm, and put safety first.

1. **Stop where it is safe,** and get out of the traffic if you can.

2. **Check yourself and any passenger for injuries.** If anyone is hurt, call 112, the national emergency number, or 115 for an ambulance.

3. **Do not move the vehicles** unless they block traffic dangerously, and photograph the scene first if you can do so safely: the car, its door, your scooter, the plate and the road.

4. **Exchange details** with the other party, and note any witnesses.

5. **Call the police** if there is injury or significant damage. Article 80 of the Law requires people involved in an accident to stop, keep the scene and help victims.

6. **Tell the rental shop promptly,** with photographs. ## When you are the one opening a door

If you travel by car or taxi in Hanoi, the same law applies to you. Look back, check the mirror, wait for riders to pass and open the door slowly. Using the far hand, so that your body turns to look back, is a technique that safe-driving specialists suggest. If you leave your own scooter parked next to a car, do not leave it where a door can strike it.

## A quick checklist

1. **Treat every parked or stopped car as a door that may open.**

2. **Read the warning signs: brake lights, hazards, occupants, taxi stops.**

3. **Slow down early.**

4. **Leave at least a door's width, and more for stopped cars.**

5. **Signal and check behind before moving out.**

6. **If you cannot leave room, slow right down or wait.**

7. **After a hit, stop safely, get help and report it.**

## FAQ: car doors and scooter riders

### What does the law say about opening car doors?

Article 19 of Law 36/2024/QH15 says doors may be opened only when the vehicle has completely stopped or parked, after observing the front, the rear and the outer side, and not left open if unsafe.

### How much room should I leave?

At least the width of a car door where the lane allows, and more for a car with people in it. If you cannot leave that, slow down further.

### Who is responsible if a door hits a rider?

The person opening the door has the legal duty. The consequences for you, and who pays for damage, depend on the facts, the police assessment and your contract.

### What should I do after being hit?

Stop safely, get help for injuries, photograph the scene, exchange details, call the police if needed and report to the rental shop.

### Are taxis and ride-hailing cars more dangerous?

They stop often, in the lane and with little warning, so treat them with extra care.

### Is there a fine for opening a door unsafely?

Under Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, as quoted by Vietnamese legal publications, a car driver can be fined VND 4,000,000 to 6,000,000, and more if it causes an accident. Check the current text.
