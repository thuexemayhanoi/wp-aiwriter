When a rental scooter will not start, work through a short, safe order: put the bike on firm ground, check the side stand and brake lever, confirm there is petrol, make sure the key or smart key is recognised, follow the maker's start routine without cranking for more than about five seconds and then stop and call the shop. Honda's owner's manuals describe exactly this logic, and most "dead" scooters turn out to have a stand, a brake lever, an empty tank or a flat key battery behind them. This guide walks through each check in the order it is quickest to do, explains what Honda says about flooded engines and weak batteries, lists what a renter should never attempt and describes what to tell the shop. It is general information, because each model differs.

## Start with where you are

Before touching anything, make sure the scooter and you are safe. Push it out of the traffic lane if it will roll, put it on its centre stand on firm, level ground and switch the ignition off. A scooter that refuses to start in the middle of a busy junction is first a traffic problem and only then a mechanical one.

Take a breath and think about what changed. Did it start earlier today? Was it left in the rain, washed, knocked over or parked for hours in strong sun? Did the fuel gauge look low? Those answers often point to the cause before you check anything, and they are exactly what the shop will ask when you call.

## Check 1: the side stand and the brake lever

Many modern scooters have a safety cut-out that stops the engine from starting at all unless two conditions are met. Honda's Vision manual says the vehicle has a side stand ignition cut-off, so with the side stand down the engine cannot be started, and lowering it with the engine running stops the engine. It also says the starter motor works only when the brake lever is squeezed and the side stand is up.

So run through these small things first:

- Flick the side stand fully up and make sure it does not drop back down on its spring.
- Squeeze the rear brake lever, which on Honda scooters is the left lever, while pressing the starter button.
- If the bike has a rear brake lock, engage it when starting, as Honda's procedure suggests, and release it before you move off.
- Check the kill switch, if the bike has one, is not in the off position.

If a stand or lever was the cause, the scooter fires up at once. If not, go on.

## Check 2: is there petrol?

The next quickest check is fuel. Honda's Vision troubleshooting table, under the heading for a starter motor that turns but an engine that does not fire, lists checking that there is petrol in the tank. A needle gauge can stick, a digital gauge can mislead on a slope and a shop may have handed the bike over with only a small amount, so look at the actual reading and, if the seat can be lifted safely, at the tank.

Shops usually hand a petrol bike over with a small amount of fuel. Our guide to [fuel in Hanoi and filling up a rental scooter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/fuel-in-hanoi-petrol-stations-rental-scooter/) explains how to refill with the right grade, and our guide to [when to refuel during a day trip](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/when-to-refuel-a-manual-motorbike-during-a-day-trip/) helps you avoid being caught short. If the tank is nearly empty, put a little petrol in, then try again.

## Check 3: the key and the ignition

A traditional key has to be fully in and turned to the "on" position. If the key will not turn, the steering may be locked: move the handlebar slightly from side to side while turning the key. Do not force it.

A scooter with a smart key works differently. The system authenticates a small remote fob and lets the ignition knob turn without a key. Honda says the system can fail to work properly when the fob battery is depleted, when strong radio waves are nearby, when the fob is carried with a phone or laptop or when it is covered by metal. Carry the fob on your body, not in the underseat box or a bag with a metal object. If the fob's indicator does not respond, the battery is probably flat, and the shop can arrange a replacement. Our guide to [what to check on a manual bike's electric starter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-to-check-on-a-manual-bikes-electric-starter/) covers the equivalent for geared bikes.

## Check 4: follow the maker's start routine

Many "will not start" cases are just a start routine done slightly wrong. For a Honda Vision, the Honda Vietnam manual gives this order:

1. Put the scooter on its centre stand.
2. Squeeze the rear brake lever and engage the brake lock if fitted.
3. Turn the ignition to "on" and check that the engine warning light, the PGM-FI lamp, goes out.
4. With the throttle completely closed, press the starter button and release it as soon as the engine starts.

Keep your hand off the throttle while pressing the button. Honda's manual says that with the throttle fully open the engine will not start while the starter button is held, which is why the flooded-engine routine below uses it on purpose. If the engine starts but idles unevenly, Honda says to open the throttle gently.

## The five-second rule

This is the single most useful figure in the manuals. Honda's notice for both the Vision and the Wave RSX FI says that if the engine does not start within five seconds, you should turn the ignition off and wait ten seconds before trying again, to let the battery voltage recover.

Cranking again and again drains the battery, which is exactly what you cannot afford. A battery that gives a few good attempts will not give twenty. So count five seconds, release the starter button, switch off, wait ten seconds, then try a slightly different step. Never hold the button down for half a minute.

## If it cranks but will not catch

Honda describes a sequence for an engine that will not start. For the Vision it runs as follows:

1. Turn the throttle fully open and press the starter button for about five seconds. The engine will not start in that state, which is intended; it clears excess fuel.
2. Release the throttle and wait about five seconds without pressing the button.
3. Do a normal start.
4. If the engine starts but idles unevenly, open the throttle gently.
5. If it still does not start, wait ten seconds and repeat the sequence.

On the Wave RSX FI, with a kick starter, Honda's steps are to turn the ignition off, open the throttle fully, kick the engine over several times and then repeat the normal starting routine. If you are using the kick lever on an older bike, our guide to [how to use the kick starter on an older rental motorbike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-use-the-kick-starter-on-an-older-rental-motorbike/) explains it. Do not kick the lever while the bike is moving, and fold the lever back afterwards, as the Wave manual advises.

## If the starter does nothing at all

When you press the button and nothing happens, even a click, the manual points to the electrical side. For the Vision the list is:

- Check the start sequence again, including the stand and the brake lever.
- Check whether a fuse has blown.
- Check whether the battery connections are loose or the terminals corroded.
- Check the battery condition.

A renter should do the first item only. Fuses and battery terminals sit under panels and seats, and opening them on a rented scooter is the shop's job. A dim headlight, a dashboard that flickers or a horn that is weak while the starter is silent all point to a weak battery. Tell the shop exactly what you saw and heard.

A note on idling stop: some scooters, such as the Vision, stop the engine at red lights and restart when you twist the throttle. Honda warns that the battery can run low and fail to restart the engine; if that happens, press the idling stop switch to the "idling" position, so the system is off, and restart with the starter button. The same manual says to have the battery checked by an authorised shop.

## Warning lights to report, not ignore

If the engine warning lamp stays on after the ignition is on, Honda says to contact an authorised dealer as soon as possible. If it blinks, the manual's advice is to stop in a safe place, switch off and wait about ten minutes for the engine to cool. Should it blink again after restarting, ride slowly to the nearest authorised workshop at no more than 50 km/h, as Honda directs. ## What a renter should not do

Some things make a minor fault worse or leave you with a bill:

- Do not push-start a scooter, tow it with a rope or ask a passer-by to do so.
- Do not spray starting fluid into the air intake, open the carburettor or pull off the spark plug cap.
- Do not jump-start from another vehicle's battery.
- Do not dismantle panels, disconnect the battery or replace fuses without the shop's approval.
- Do not accept a roadside repair from a stranger without first telling the shop.

## Preventing the problem at pick-up

Most no-start surprises can be caught before you leave the shop. Start the bike twice with the shop's staff beside you, switch it off and start it again, because a bike that starts warm may not start cold. Watch that the dashboard lights come on and go off normally. On a smart-key scooter, ask how the key is carried, whether a spare fob exists and what to do if it stops responding. ## FAQ: scooter will not start

### Why does my scooter turn the key but do nothing?

On many scooters the side stand is down, the brake lever is not squeezed or the battery is weak. Check the stand and lever first.

### How long should I crank the starter?

Honda says that if the engine has not started within about five seconds, you should switch off and wait ten seconds before trying again.

### Is it safe to push-start a scooter?

It is not recommended. Honda's manuals do not describe it, and a rented scooter should not be towed or pushed to life by strangers. Call the shop instead.

### What if the smart key does not work?

Carry it on your person, away from phones and metal and check whether its indicator responds. A flat battery is a common cause, so tell the shop.

### Can I change the battery myself?

No. On a rental, leave the vehicle battery and fuses to the shop.

### Who pays for a breakdown?

It depends on the cause and the contract. Ask before you sign and tell the shop at once if something goes wrong.
