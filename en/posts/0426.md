After a serious accident on a rental motorbike, the first priority is life: call 115 for an ambulance or 112, the national emergency number, make the scene safe and help anyone who is hurt as far as you safely can. Do not leave, because Vietnam's traffic law requires people involved in an accident to stop, keep the scene, help the injured and stay until the police arrive unless they must leave for emergency care. Once people are safe, tell the shop, contact your embassy or consulate and your insurer's emergency line, do not sign anything you cannot read and keep every record.

Questions of fault, compensation and the rental contract come later. This guide sets out what to do in order, what the law requires, who to call, how medical care and insurance fit in, what to expect from the police and how to protect yourself afterwards. It is general information, not medical or legal advice, and anyone involved in a serious accident should seek professional help.

## What counts as serious

A serious accident is one in which someone is badly hurt or killed, a vehicle is badly damaged, a pedestrian or a child is involved, or the situation is dangerous or unclear. If you are unsure, treat it as serious. You can always scale down later, but you cannot go back and do the first minutes again.

## Step 1: stay calm and make the scene safe

Take a breath. Check that you are not in immediate danger from traffic, fuel or fire. Switch off the engine of your bike, and take the key. Ask someone to warn approaching traffic, or use hazard lights if the bike has them. Do not stand in a live lane.

Do not rush to move anyone. A person with a neck, back or head injury can be harmed by being moved, unless there is immediate danger such as fire or traffic.

## Step 2: call for help

Call the emergency numbers straight away, or ask someone nearby to do it. Say clearly where you are, what happened and how many people are hurt.

- **112** is the national emergency number, set up by Decree 200/2025/ND-CP, in force since 23 August 2025. It is free from fixed and mobile phones, answers 24 hours a day and is linked with the other emergency centres, so a call is passed to the right one.

- **115** is the medical emergency centre, for an ambulance.

- **113** is the police.

- **114** is fire and rescue.

If you do not speak Vietnamese, say the place, the word "accident" and "ambulance", and pass the phone to a local if you can. A map pin sent to the shop can help the shop assist.

## Step 3: help the injured

Help as far as you safely can, and without taking risks.

- **Check for danger and for a response:** is the person awake and breathing?

- **Stay with them,** keep them still and reassure them.

- **Do not remove a helmet** unless there is no other way to help with breathing, and leave that to trained people if they are coming.

- **Control heavy bleeding** with firm pressure on a clean cloth.

- **Do not give food or drink.**

- **Keep them warm,** and wait for the ambulance.

These are general first-aid points and not a substitute for training. Follow the instructions of the emergency operator.

## Step 4: the law says to stay

Vietnam's Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (Law No. 36/2024/QH15), in force since 1 January 2025, deals with accidents in Article 80. We read the text on the Government portal. As of October 2026, it says that the driver who causes an accident, the people involved and the people present must:

- stop immediately, warn of danger and keep the scene intact;

- help the injured and report to the nearest police, medical facility or People's Committee;

- stay at the scene until the police arrive, unless leaving to take a victim for emergency care or because of a threat to safety, in which case they must report immediately to the nearest police or People's Committee.

Bystanders must help protect victims' property and help with rescue. The police, for their part, must send forces to the scene, control traffic and handle the accident.

The penalty decree punishes leaving. Under Article 7 of Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, which we read on the Government portal, a rider who is directly involved in an accident and does not stop at once, keep the scene and help the injured is fined VND 1,000,000–2,000,000. A rider who causes an accident and fails to stop, stay at the scene or report to the police or local authorities is fined VND 8,000,000–10,000,000, and more serious consequences carry heavier penalties. Decree 238/2026/ND-CP, which amends the decree from 15 August 2026, does not change Article 7. If you must leave to get a victim to a hospital, report to the police as soon as you can.

## Step 5: record what you can

If you are able, and only after people are safe, take photographs and notes. If you are hurt, ask someone else to do it.

- **The scene,** including the positions of the vehicles and the road.

- **The damage** to all vehicles, and the plate numbers.

- **The road, signs and markings.**

- **Names and phone numbers** of witnesses.

- **Nearby cameras,** such as shops and car parks.

- **The time and place,** and what happened in your own words.

Do not argue about fault at the scene. State the facts when asked, and leave the question of blame to the investigation. ## Step 6: the police

When the police arrive, cooperate and follow their instructions. Expect to be asked what happened, to show your documents and to give a statement. A few points help.

- **Carry your passport, licence and the rental contract,** and show them when asked. Our guide to [what identity documents a rental shop may ask to see](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-identity-documents-a-rental-shop-may-ask-to-see/) explains what you should have.

- **Ask for an interpreter or a translation** if you are asked to give or sign a statement in Vietnamese. If none is available, ask for it to be translated by phone or by someone you trust, and say that you do not understand.

- **Do not sign a document you cannot read.** If you are pressed, say politely that you need a translation or advice first.

- **Ask for a record or reference number** of the report and for a copy if one is available.

- **Do not sign any settlement** until you understand it and have spoken to the shop and, if necessary, your embassy.

A serious accident can lead to a police investigation. That can involve interviews, evidence gathering and decisions by the authorities about responsibility. This guide does not cover criminal procedure. Anyone facing it should seek advice from their embassy and a qualified lawyer.

## Step 7: medical care

Go to a hospital or clinic, even if you feel fine. Adrenaline hides injuries, and symptoms from a head or internal injury can appear hours later. Keep every medical record, receipt and prescription. Ask for a written summary of your treatment and diagnosis, in English if you can. If you are on medication or have a condition, tell the staff.

Your own travel insurance may cover medical costs, but only if the policy covers motorbike riding and your licence, and many insurers want to be called at once. Our guide to [travel insurance and motorbike rentals](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/travel-insurance-motorbike-rental-vietnam/) explains the usual conditions. Call the insurer's 24-hour assistance line as soon as you safely can, and ask what documents it needs.

## Step 8: tell the shop

Call Nguyen Ha on 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969), open 09:00 to 21:00 daily, or message if you cannot call. Say what happened, who is hurt, where the bike is and what the police have said. The shop needs to know because the bike is its property, because it holds the compulsory insurance certificate and because it may need to be involved with the police. Do not wait until the end of the rental. Our guide to [when a rental motorbike needs to be collected](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/when-a-rental-motorbike-needs-to-be-collected/) explains what happens to the bike.

## Step 9: tell your embassy or consulate

Contact your embassy or consulate, especially if you or a companion is seriously hurt, if someone has died, if you are being investigated or if your passport is held or lost. They can advise on hospitals, interpreters and local lawyers, and can contact your family. Keep their emergency number with your documents.

## Insurance: what to expect

Insurance is where many visitors are surprised.

- **The compulsory insurance on the bike** is civil-liability cover. Under Decree 67/2023/ND-CP it compensates third parties for injury and property damage caused by the vehicle, within limits. It does not pay for your own injuries as the rider, repairs to the rental bike, theft or evacuation. The shop holds the certificate.

- **Your travel insurance** may cover your medical costs and evacuation, but it may exclude motorbike accidents if your licence is not valid for the bike, if you were not wearing a helmet or if you had been drinking.

- **The rental contract** governs damage to the bike.

Insurance claims need documents: the police report, medical records, receipts and photographs. Keep them in one place, and do not throw anything away. ## The rental contract and the deposit

Vietnam's Civil Code (Law No. 91/2015/QH13), in force since 1 January 2017, says a lessee must preserve the leased property and compensate if it is lost or damaged, apart from natural wear. The contract fills in the details, including how damage is assessed and how the deposit is applied. At Nguyen Ha the cash deposit of VND 2,000,000–5,000,000 is held against damage, loss and unpaid charges. After a serious accident, the amount owed for the bike may exceed the deposit, and other claims may arise from third parties. Do not agree a figure in the first days. Ask the shop to put its position in writing, keep every message and, if the amount is significant, take advice. ## What not to do

- **Do not leave the scene** unless you must for emergency care.

- **Do not move an injured person** without need.

- **Do not argue about fault,** or admit it in anger.

- **Do not sign documents you cannot read.**

- **Do not hand over your passport** to anyone but the police.

- **Do not post about it online** before the facts are clear.

- **Do not agree a payment or settlement** without advice.

- **Do not ride the bike away** if it is damaged.

- **Do not delay telling the shop and your insurer.**

## Keep a record

Start a single note or folder with everything: times, names, numbers, photographs, the report reference, medical records, receipts, messages. Back it up. Prices and terms are on the [prices page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/prices/).

## A quick checklist

1. **Protect life: call 115 or 112 at once.**

2. **Make the scene safe; do not move the injured needlessly.**

3. **Stay at the scene, as the law requires.**

4. **Record what you can, once people are safe.**

5. **Cooperate with the police; do not sign what you cannot read.**

6. **Get medical care, and keep the records.**

7. **Call the shop on 0334 699 969.**

8. **Contact your embassy and your insurer.**

9. **Do not settle or admit fault in a rush.**

## FAQ: a serious accident on a rental motorbike

### Who do I call first?

Call 115 or 112 for help, and the shop and your embassy once people are safe.

### Do I have to stay at the scene?

Yes, until the police arrive, unless you must leave to take a victim for emergency care, in which case you must report immediately.

### What is the emergency number in Vietnam?

112 is the national number. It is free, answers 24 hours a day and is linked with 113 police, 114 fire and rescue and 115 medical emergency.

### Will compulsory insurance cover my injuries?

No. It covers third parties, not the rider or the rental bike. Your travel insurance may cover your medical costs if its conditions are met.

### Should I sign a police statement in Vietnamese?

Not if you cannot read it. Ask for a translation or advice first.

### Who pays for the bike?

The contract decides. Do not agree an amount in the first days, and ask the shop to put its position in writing.
