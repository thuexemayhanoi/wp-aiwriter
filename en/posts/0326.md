To watch the sunset at West Lake by scooter without blocking the waterfront, arrive early enough to park properly, leave the bike in a marked or guarded place, and do your watching on foot. Sunset in Hanoi falls at about 17:30 in mid-October and about 17:15 in mid-November, and the light fades quickly afterwards, so the evening is shorter than most visitors expect. The roads beside the water are busiest just when the sky is best, which is why a scooter stopped in the carriageway for a photograph causes more trouble than almost anything else. This guide gives sunset times through the year, a timeline that works backwards from the sun, notes on glare and darkness, the stopping rules that apply, and a weekend warning if your night continues in the Old Quarter.

## When the sun actually sets

Hanoi sits close enough to the tropics that day length changes less than in Europe, but the shift is still enough to matter. According to the sunrise and sunset tables of the Alpenglow site, the sun sets at about 17:32 in the middle of October and 17:15 in the middle of November. The same source lists about 17:17 in mid-December, 17:35 in mid-January and 17:54 in mid-February. The longest days come in summer, with the latest sunset of 2026 at 18:42 on 5 July. Timeanddate's Hanoi table gives 17:31 for 16 October.

These times come from a calculation for the city, not a measurement at the lake, so treat them as accurate to a few minutes. Darkness follows quickly. For 19 October the Alpenglow table shows civil twilight ending at 17:52, 22 minutes after sunset, when outdoor activities need artificial light, and full darkness at 18:44. The practical consequence is that your ride back is often in the dark even if you leave while the sky is still glowing.

## Choosing a place to watch without needing the scooter

West Lake covers about 500 hectares of water, according to the national tourism board, which notes that many people regard it as the most beautiful place in the city for sunsets, so there are many places to watch. A few stand out in travel writing. The Thanh Nien causeway separates West Lake from the smaller Truc Bach Lake, and tourist listings describe it as a scenic tree-lined road between two lakes. The shoreline around Quang An has cafes and restaurants with lake views. Tran Quoc Pagoda is another popular spot.

The right choice is the one where you can leave the bike somewhere sensible. A cafe with its own bays, a guarded lot a short walk away or a marked roadside spot all work. A place with no parking within walking distance does not, however good the view. Our guide to [Tay Ho and West Lake by scooter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/tay-ho-west-lake-by-scooter/) describes the lakeside loop and the quieter streets, and our guide to [parking a motorbike in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-motorbike-in-hanoi/) explains tickets and guarded lots. Hanoi's city government also doubled the per-trip price ceiling for motorbikes at publicly funded roadside spots on 15 October 2026, so check the posted board and ignore older price lists.

## Why golden hour causes the jam

One travel guide describes locals parking their scooters along the railing of the Thanh Nien causeway from about 17:30 onward. That is part of the picture, and also the problem: when dozens of bikes pull over at once, pedestrians step into the road to take photographs and riders brake suddenly to look at the view, a narrow road becomes a bottleneck. You are not obliged to copy what the others are doing.

The simple discipline is to decide where you will stop before you reach the crowd, not after you have seen the view. If you reach a place and it is full, keep moving to the next legal option. A view from five minutes down the road is better than one from a position that blocks other people.

## The stopping rules, in plain language

Vietnam's Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (Law 36/2024/QH15, effective 1 January 2025) lists places where stopping and parking are not allowed. Article 18 includes pedestrian crossings, within 5 metres of the edge of a junction, passenger pick-up and drop-off points, curves and places near the top of a slope where the view is blocked, bridges unless traffic organisation allows it, and places where the carriageway is only wide enough for a single lane of motor vehicles. It also bars stopping on the left side of a one-way road. On a street you must pull in on your right, close to the kerb with your nearest wheel within 0.25 metres, and without endangering or obstructing others.

Article 77 says that roadways are for traffic and pavements are for pedestrians, with temporary use for other purposes only with permission. That rules out the idea of riding up onto a waterfront path to get closer, and it rules out treating a pedestrian promenade as parking. Signs on the spot override anything written here, so obey them.

## A timeline that works backwards from the sun

Pick a sunset time from the table and count backwards:

1. **About two hours before:** collect the bike, check brakes, lights and fuel, and decide your viewing place. Our [rental scooter inspection checklist](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-scooter-inspection-checklist/) shows what to look at.
2. **About 75 minutes before:** start riding. Allow for traffic, which can be slow as the day ends.
3. **About 45 minutes before:** be parked. This is the step that separates a calm evening from a stressful one.
4. **About 30 minutes before:** walk to the water, find a spot and wait without rushing.
5. **Sunset, then 20 minutes more:** the colour often deepens after the sun goes, and the crowds begin to leave.
6. **About 25 to 30 minutes after:** collect the bike, switch on lights and let the first wave of traffic thin out before joining the road.

The margins look generous, but they are what keep you from stopping in the road because you arrived late. If the sky is overcast, there is no shame in changing the plan to a cafe stop.

## Riding into the low sun and out into the dark

A sun low on the horizon creates glare on west-facing stretches, and the sun's direction at this time of year is slightly south of west. Slow down, keep a bigger gap than usual and use a visor or sunglasses that do not become a problem once the light drops. Take them off or swap them for clear lenses before it is dark. If your helmet has a visor, keep it clean, since smudges that are invisible at noon become a blur in low light.

When the light fades, switch on the headlight, even if the bike has a daytime running light, and check that the tail light works. Our [night riding guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-at-night-in-hanoi/) lists the visibility problems and the streets to avoid. Pedestrians and cyclists are harder to see at dusk, and the crowd around the water is looking at the sky, not the traffic.

## If your night continues in the Old Quarter

A West Lake sunset often leads to dinner in the centre. On weekends there is one new complication. Hanoi's low-emission pilot started on 1 July 2026, and in Area 1, which takes in the Hoan Kiem Lake pedestrian area, the Old Quarter and the night market, motorbikes and cars are barred from 19:00 to midnight every Friday, Saturday and Sunday. Phase one runs to 31 December 2026. West Lake is outside that first area, and the pilot does not restrict riding at the lake. If you are going on to Hoan Kiem on a Friday, Saturday or Sunday, park outside the zone before 19:00 and walk in. Our [Old Quarter riding guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-old-quarter/) explains the narrow streets, and the [Hoan Kiem parking guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/where-to-park-a-scooter-near-hoan-kiem-lake/) helps with the last step.

## Returning the scooter afterwards

Nguyen Ha is open from 09:00 to 21:00 daily, and the shop is in Long Bien, on the other side of the Red River from West Lake. Plan the return around the shop's hours, not around the sunset. A late return costs VND 30,000 per hour for the first five hours and an extra day from the sixth. Petrol bikes should come back with about 0.5 to 1 litre of fuel, and an empty tank costs VND 20,000, so fill up before the evening starts. ## FAQ: West Lake sunset by scooter

### What time is sunset at West Lake?

It is the same as for Hanoi: about 17:30 in mid-October, 17:15 in mid-November and 17:17 in mid-December, with later times in spring and summer.

### Can I stop on the causeway to take a photo?

Only where stopping is permitted and you can do it without obstructing others. Do not stop on the carriageway, within 5 metres of a junction or where signs prohibit it.

### How long after sunset is it dark?

On 19 October, civil twilight ended 22 minutes after sunset and full darkness fell at 18:44. Switch on your headlight as the light fades.

### Is West Lake affected by the low-emission zone?

Not in the first phase, which covers part of Hoan Kiem. The weekend evening bar on motorbikes applies around Hoan Kiem Lake and the Old Quarter.

### Should I ride around the whole lake?

Only if you have the time and confidence. A single well-chosen viewing point is often better than a full circuit at dusk.
