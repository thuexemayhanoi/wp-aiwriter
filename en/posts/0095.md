A small manual motorbike lurches when the drive to the rear wheel changes suddenly, and on a Honda Wave or Yamaha Sirius that almost always means the throttle was opened, closed or held at the wrong moment, not that the bike is broken. Honda's Wave RSX FI manual gives the basic rule: reduce the throttle before you change gear, and press the gear lever gently rather than stamping on it. Most lurches happen at one of four moments: pulling away, changing up, changing down and creeping in traffic. Each has a different cause and a simple fix. This guide helps you work out which moment is giving you trouble, explains what the bike is doing, sets out a short practice routine and says when a jerk is a sign of a fault you should report. It is general riding advice, not tuition.

## What the bike is doing when it lurches

A Wave has a four-speed gearbox that you change with your left foot, and its clutch works automatically, so there is no clutch lever. Honda describes the clutch as automatic, and the bike engages the drive as engine speed rises. That is convenient, because you cannot stall by releasing a lever too fast, but it takes the smoothing job away from your left hand and gives it entirely to your right wrist.

The result is that your throttle hand controls three things at once: how hard the engine pulls, how firmly the clutch engages and how the bike's weight shifts. A bike weighing under 100 kg reacts quickly to all three. A twist that would be unnoticeable on a heavy bike can make a Wave surge. Likewise, shutting the throttle sharply makes the engine suddenly hold the bike back, and the rider's body nods forward.

So the answer to lurching is almost always the same idea in different clothes: make the changes in drive gradual, and match the engine speed to the gear before the drive is reconnected.

## Find your moment

Use the table to work out where the jerk comes from. Be honest about which moment it is, because the fixes are different.

| When it lurches | What usually causes it | What to try |
|---|---|---|
| Pulling away from a stop | A sudden twist of the throttle, or a blip as you release the brake | Roll the throttle on slowly and steadily, release the brake as the bike begins to move |
| Changing up | Shifting with the throttle still open, or stamping the lever | Ease the throttle, press the lever gently, roll the throttle back on |
| Changing down | Dropping a gear at too high a speed, or while still on the throttle | Slow down first, then change, with the throttle closed |
| Creeping in traffic | Changing gear again and again, or surging with the throttle | Stay in one gear, hold the throttle steady, use the rear brake lightly for fine control |
| Coming off the throttle | Closing it suddenly | Roll off gradually |
| Both up and down, every time | A loose chain, a worn part or a fault | Report it to the shop |

## Pulling away without a jerk

The pull-away is where most renters feel the worst lurch, because the clutch is engaging from nothing and the rider's right hand is busy with both the throttle and the brake.

Honda's training document describes the order for a gear bike: ignition on, neutral, brake squeezed, start the engine, select the gear, then apply the throttle and move off. For the Wave, the clutch step is automatic, so the part you control is the throttle.

Three habits take the jerk out of a start.

**Release the brake as the throttle rises, not before.** If you let go of the brake first, the bike may creep or roll before you are ready. If you open the throttle first, the bike surges against the brake. Combine them: a little throttle, then the brake eases off, then more throttle.

**Roll, do not twist.** Think of rolling the grip a few degrees, waiting a moment for the bike to begin to move and then continuing. A quick, large twist makes the clutch grab.

**Keep the first few metres steady.** Do not blip the throttle in the first gear to "help" it along. A steady, slightly raised engine speed pulls the bike away gently. Our guide to [starting a semi-automatic bike on an incline](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-start-a-semi-automatic-bike-on-an-incline/) covers the harder case of pulling away on a slope, where the bike wants to roll back.

Honda's document also gives a foot position for gear bikes: the middle of the sole on the footrest, the right foot ready on the rear brake and the left foot not resting on the gear lever. A foot resting on the lever can change gear unintentionally when the road jolts it, and that is a lurch you did not choose.

## Changing up smoothly

Honda's Wave RSX FI manual says always to reduce the throttle before changing gear, and to press the gear lever gently. That is the core of an upshift. The sequence in practice is:

1. **Ease the throttle.** Not a full close, just enough to unload the drive.
2. **Press the lever gently** until the gear engages. Do not stamp on it, and do not hold the pedal down afterwards.
3. **Roll the throttle back on** smoothly as the gear settles.

The whole sequence takes a fraction of a second. If it feels like three separate actions, you are doing it slowly, and that is fine while you learn. With practice the three run together.

The same manual warns against needless shifting and against resting a foot on the lever while riding, because that can damage the gear-change mechanism. Treat the lever like a switch you operate on purpose, then leave alone.

If an upshift clunks hard every time even with a light touch and an eased throttle, mention it to the shop. ## Changing down without a surge

Downshifts cause the second most common lurch, and it happens for a simple reason. When you drop to a lower gear, the engine is asked to turn faster for the same road speed. If the road speed is still too high, the engine speeds up suddenly, the drive snaps in and the rear wheel is dragged. The bike pitches forward and the engine shrieks.

The fix is order. Honda's training sequence for slowing a gear bike starts with easing off the throttle and using the brakes to reduce speed. Changing down once the speed fits the lower gear is our practical advice that follows from it. In practice:

- **Close the throttle and brake first.** Slow the bike before you change the gear, not at the same moment.
- **Change down when the road speed suits the gear.** If the engine note rises sharply as you change, you were too fast.
- **Change one gear at a time,** with a moment between changes. If you need to lose a lot of speed quickly, use the brakes rather than the gearbox.
- **Roll the throttle on gently** after the change if you need to hold speed.

## Creeping in traffic

In Hanoi's stop-and-go traffic the jerks come from fidgeting: opening and closing the throttle, hopping between first and second gear and pulling the brake on and off. The bike then lurches in tiny steps that tire you and annoy anyone behind you.

A calmer approach is to choose first or second gear and stay in it, hold the throttle steady and use the rear brake lightly to hold the gap. Our guide to [handling stop-and-go traffic on a semi-automatic bike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-handle-stop-and-go-traffic-on-a-semi-automatic-bike/) describes the technique in detail. The point for lurching is that fewer changes mean fewer jerks. If you find yourself changing gear every few metres, you are probably in the wrong gear for the traffic.

## Your body can cause the lurch too

A jerk is not only the bike moving. Your body can make it worse.

- **Stiff arms.** A tight grip transmits every tiny throttle movement to the bike. Honda's advice for slow riding is relaxed arms and a light grip.
- **A nodding head.** When the bike lurches, your head snaps and you grab the bars. Keep your eyes up and your elbows soft.
- **Leaning on the bars.** Your weight should be on the seat and your knees against the tank, not on your wrists.
- **A passenger.** A passenger who is not expecting a start or a stop is thrown backwards or forwards. Tell them before you pull away, and make your starts and stops gentler than you would alone. ## When it is the bike, not you

If you have eased the throttle, pressed the lever gently and matched your speed and the bike still jerks, consider a fault. Signs worth reporting include:

- **A hard clunk on every gear change,** even with a light touch.
- **A clunk or surge when you open or close the throttle,** which can come from slack in the chain. Our guides to [recognising chain noise](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/recognising-chain-noise-on-a-honda-wave-or-yamaha-sirius/) and [checking chain tension before a longer ride](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/checking-chain-tension-before-a-longer-ride/) explain what to look for.
- **Engine speed that rises with no matching increase in road speed,** which may point to slipping.
- **A gear that will not engage,** or that jumps out. Our guide to [what to do if a rental motorbike will not shift gears](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-to-do-if-a-rental-motorbike-will-not-shift-gears/) gives a roadside routine.
- **A pull-away that is abrupt however gently you roll the throttle,** which a mechanic should look at.

A cold engine can idle higher than usual for a short time, which may make the first pull-away of the day more abrupt. That is general behaviour, not a figure from Honda, so give the bike a moment and be gentler with the throttle. If the problem does not go away when the engine is warm, report it.

## A 15-minute practice routine

Find a quiet, wide, dry, legal area with no traffic. Wear a helmet, long trousers and closed shoes.

**Minutes 1 to 5: pull-aways.** From a stop, practise rolling the throttle on slowly until the bike moves, then ride a few metres and stop again. Aim for no head nod. Repeat until the start is smooth every time.

**Minutes 6 to 10: upshifts.** Accelerate gently in first gear, ease the throttle, press the lever gently into second and roll the throttle back on. Then do the same into third. Listen for a soft change rather than a clunk.

**Minutes 11 to 15: downshifts and stops.** Slow down with the throttle and both brakes, change down once the speed is low enough and stop. Use the order Honda gives for stopping: release the throttle, use both brakes with the front stronger, then hold the brake with your right foot and put your left foot down. Our guide to [starting, shifting and stopping a Honda Wave smoothly](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/honda-wave-gears-starting-shifting-and-stopping-smoothly/) shows the full sequence.

## Quick checklist for smooth shifts

- **Left foot off the lever** between changes.
- **Right wrist relaxed,** rolling not twisting.
- **Throttle eased** before every change.
- **Lever pressed gently,** never stamped.
- **Slow first, then change down.**
- **One gear at a time** if you have lost a lot of speed.
- **Same gear in slow traffic,** as few changes as possible.
- **Tell your passenger** before you start or stop.

## FAQ: avoiding lurching when changing gears

### Why does my Honda Wave jerk when I pull away?

Usually because the throttle was opened too quickly, so the automatic clutch engaged abruptly. Roll the throttle on slowly and release the brake as the bike begins to move.

### Do I need to close the throttle when changing gear?

Ease it. Honda's Wave RSX FI manual says to reduce the throttle before changing gear, and to press the lever gently.

### Why does the bike surge when I change down?

Because the engine is asked to turn faster in the lower gear. Slow the bike first, then change down once the speed fits the gear.

### Is there a clutch lever to blame?

No. A Wave's clutch is automatic, so the smoothing is done by your throttle hand and your timing.

### Could a loose chain make the bike lurch?

It can cause a clunk or surge when the throttle opens or closes. Report it to the shop and do not adjust it yourself.

### How long does it take to shift smoothly?

Many riders improve noticeably after a short session in a quiet place, but it varies. Practise before you ride in heavy traffic.
