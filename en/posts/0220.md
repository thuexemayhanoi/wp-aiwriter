In Vietnamese law a moped and an electric bicycle are two different kinds of vehicle, and the dividing line is whether the motor does the work on its own. A moped (xe gan may in Vietnamese) is a two- or three-wheeler with a design speed of no more than 50 km/h, powered by a petrol engine of no more than 50 cubic centimetres or an electric motor of no more than 4 kW. An electric bicycle (xe dap dien) is a pedal bicycle with motor assistance that cuts out when you stop pedalling or when the bike reaches 25 km/h, and it is classed with bicycles, not with motor vehicles. This guide explains the definitions, what changes for the rider in practice, how to tell the two apart on a rental counter, and which one fits which kind of Hanoi trip, based on the rules in force as of October 2026.

## The quickest test: who is driving the wheel?

Ask one question: if you stop pedalling, does the bike keep going? On an electric bicycle the law says the motor power is cut off when the rider stops pedalling, and also when the bike reaches 25 km/h. The motor only assists. On a moped the engine or motor drives the wheel by itself, usually through a throttle, and pedals are not part of the definition.

That single difference explains almost everything else in this guide. Because the electric bicycle needs your legs and stops helping at 25 km/h, the law treats it as a bicycle with a small motor. Because the moped moves under its own power at up to 50 km/h, the law treats it as a motor vehicle, with an age limit, a plate and a different set of rules for carrying people.

A practical warning: shop signs and online listings use "electric bike" for everything from a pedal-assist cycle to a scooter that looks like a Honda. The words on the sign decide nothing. What counts is how the machine is built.

## What the road law actually says

Vietnam's Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (Law 36/2024/QH15), in force since 1 January 2025, sorts vehicles in Article 34. Two of its definitions matter here.

| | Moped (xe gan may) | Electric bicycle (xe dap dien) |
|---|---|---|
| Legal group | Motor vehicle (a two- or three-wheeler with a motor) | Non-motorised, rudimentary vehicle, listed with bicycles |
| What drives it | Petrol engine or electric motor on its own | Motor assists pedalling |
| Speed limit built into the definition | Design speed of no more than 50 km/h | Assist is cut off at 25 km/h |
| Engine or motor size | Petrol: no more than 50 cm3. Electric: no more than 4 kW | No size figure in the definition; it must be a pedal bicycle with motor assistance |
| Stops helping when | It does not; it is self-propelled | The rider stops pedalling, or the bike reaches 25 km/h |
| Minimum age in Article 59 | 16 | No age is set in that article |

Two details are worth noticing. First, the law says that mopeds do not include "xe dap may", the motor-assisted bicycle, which also covers the electric bicycle. So the two groups are deliberately separate, not a sliding scale. Second, a scooter that reaches 60 km/h is neither: it is a motorcycle, and it falls under the licence classes instead. ## Rules that are the same for both

Some rules do not depend on the label.

- **Helmets.** Article 31 says that the rider and any passenger on a motor-assisted bicycle must wear a helmet that meets the national standard, with the strap fastened properly. Article 33 says the same for moped riders and passengers. Our [helmet rules guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/helmet-rules-in-vietnam/) explains what counts as a proper helmet.
- **Dangerous riding habits.** Article 31 applies the same list of prohibited acts as Article 33, such as riding with both hands off the handlebars. A bicycle with a motor does not escape the ordinary rules of the road.
- **Everyday road rules.** Traffic lights, one-way streets, pedestrian crossings and the ban on riding along pavements apply to both.

In other words, nobody gets a free pass on safety because a vehicle is small or quiet.

## Rules that differ

**Passengers.** On a motor-assisted bicycle Article 31 allows one rider and no one else, with one exception: a child under 7 may be carried as well, making two people at most. On a moped Article 33 allows one rider, and a second person only in listed cases: taking a sick person to emergency care, escorting a person in custody, a child under 12, or an elderly or disabled person. In plain terms, neither vehicle is meant for two adult tourists, and a 50cc moped with two adults on board is a legal grey area to discuss with the shop, not an assumption to make.

**Age.** Article 59 sets 16 as the minimum age for riding a moped. The same article does not set an age for riders of non-motorised vehicles, so for a rental you should ask the shop what its own minimum is.

**Licence.** A moped does not need a driving licence under Vietnamese law. Article 56 says that moped riders must know the rules of the road, have the skills to control the vehicle and meet the age and health requirements. It does not require a licence class for them. Licence classes start with the motorcycle classes in Article 57, with A1 covering motorcycles up to 125 cc or 11 kW. An electric bicycle, as a non-motorised vehicle, is outside the licence system altogether. Our guide to [what a 50cc rental involves in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/renting-a-50cc-scooter-in-hanoi/) covers the 40 km/h moped limit and the rest of that picture.

**Registration and papers.** A moped is a motor vehicle, so expect a plate and a registration certificate, and ask to see them. An electric bicycle in the pedal-assist sense is not a registered motor vehicle in the same way. Ask what documents come with whatever you are being offered.

## When the label and the machine do not match

The trouble in a rental shop is not the law, it is the vocabulary. A few patterns to watch for:

1. **A scooter body with token pedals.** Some models look like small scooters and carry folding pedals. If the throttle drives the wheel without pedalling, it is functioning as a moped or a faster vehicle, whatever the pedals say.
2. **A "bike" that does more than 25 km/h on the throttle.** An electric bicycle in the legal sense stops assisting at 25 km/h. A machine that keeps accelerating is not that vehicle.
3. **A scooter advertised as "50cc equivalent."** That wording suggests the moped class, so ask for the maximum speed and the motor power in kW, and for the paperwork that shows them.
4. **A model labelled by its marketing name only.** Ask for the specification sheet or the registration, not the nickname.

If the answers are vague, treat the vehicle as the more demanding class until proven otherwise, and choose a different one if the shop cannot say.

## How they feel on a Hanoi street

These are general observations, not measurements, and individual models vary a lot.

A pedal-assist bicycle is light, quiet and slow. At 25 km/h at most it suits short, flat hops: a loop around West Lake, the streets near Long Bien Bridge, a morning along the river. It is physically active when the assist is low, and an unpractical choice for anyone who is hot, tired or carrying a bag in July.

A moped is heavier, faster and takes no effort. Its legal limit is below the speed of most scooters, so it travels at the pace of the slowest traffic and stays to the right. Neither vehicle suits long trips out of the city, and neither is meant for an expressway, which is closed to all two-wheelers of this kind.

Parking and handling also differ. A bicycle is easier to wheel and to squeeze into a narrow spot. A moped needs a guarded parking space like any motorbike, and our [guide to parking a motorbike in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-motorbike-in-hanoi/) explains how that works.

## Choosing between them

| If you want... | Better fit | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A short, gentle ride with some exercise and no licence worry | Electric bicycle | Slow, light and treated as a bicycle |
| To cover the city without pedalling, with no motorcycle licence | Moped (50cc or low-power electric) | Self-propelled, with no licence required, subject to the age rule |
| To carry a bag and ride at ordinary scooter pace | Neither; consider a 110 cc scooter if you hold the licence | A moped is limited to a lower speed than normal traffic |
| A first ride in Hanoi traffic | The one you find easiest to control | Handling matters more than the label |
| A trip beyond the suburbs | Neither | Both are too slow for rural roads |

## What to ask before you take either

- What is the maximum speed, and does the motor stop at 25 km/h or keep going?
- Is it petrol, and how many cc, or electric, and how many kW?
- Does it need pedalling to move, or does the throttle drive it?
- Which documents and plate go with it?
- What is the minimum age, and does the shop add its own conditions?
- Is a helmet included, and does it fit?
- For an electric model, what is the battery level at handover, and who charges it?

## FAQ: moped or electric bicycle

### Is an electric bicycle a moped in Vietnam?

No. Law 36/2024/QH15 puts the motor-assisted bicycle, including the electric bicycle, among non-motorised vehicles, and says that mopeds do not include it.

### How fast can each one go?

A legal electric bicycle stops assisting at 25 km/h. A moped has a design speed of up to 50 km/h and, in practice, a lower legal limit on the road, which our 50cc guide explains.

### Do I need a licence for either?

Not under the rules summarised here. A moped needs no driving licence but has a minimum rider age of 16, and an electric bicycle is outside the licence classes. A faster electric scooter is a different matter.

### Do I need a helmet on an electric bicycle?

Yes. Article 31 requires riders and passengers of a motor-assisted bicycle to wear a standard-compliant helmet with the strap fastened.

### Can two adults ride one?

Generally not. A motor-assisted bicycle may carry one extra child under 7, and a moped may carry a second person only in the cases listed in Article 33.

### Which is better for a first-time rider?

Whichever you find easiest to control and which the shop confirms you may legally ride. Start somewhere quiet and ask for a short test before leaving.
