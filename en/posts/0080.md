A small manual motorbike feels unstable for one of four reasons: how the rider is sitting and steering, how the bike is loaded, the condition of the tyres and steering parts, or what the road and wind are doing at that moment. Most of the time the cause is the first or second, which you can change in seconds, not a fault in the bike. A Honda Wave or Yamaha Sirius weighs under 100 kilograms, so a rider, a passenger and a bag can easily outweigh the bike by a factor of two, and small changes in how that weight sits have large effects on how steady it feels. This guide explains the common causes, gives a symptom guide for wobbles, twitches and pulls, shows what to do in the moment, covers what to check on the bike and says what to tell the shop. It is general information, not a diagnosis.

## Why a light bike is sensitive

A bike with 17-inch wheels, narrow tyres and a short wheelbase responds quickly to everything you do, which is part of its charm in traffic. The same quickness means a stiff arm, a lurching throttle, a badly placed bag or a soft tyre gets noticed at once. Honda's published specifications show how light these bikes are: about 96 to 99 kilograms for the Wave Alpha, Wave RSX FI and Blade. Yamaha lists 98 kilograms for the Sirius FI. A rider and a passenger together can weigh well over a hundred kilograms more, so the bike's behaviour depends heavily on the load it carries and how it is placed.

Instability does not mean the bike is dangerous. It means one of the inputs is wrong. Finding which one is the point of the rest of this article.

## Cause 1: the rider's posture and grip

Honda's safe-riding training document describes the posture it teaches. The hands wrap around the middle of the grips, the elbows are relaxed and slightly in, the shoulders are relaxed, the thighs lightly grip the seat, the hips sit in the middle of the seat and the eyes look far ahead. For a gear bike, the middle of the sole rests on the footrest, the right foot stays ready on the brake and the left foot does not press the gear lever.

Most stability problems in the saddle come from breaking one of those rules:

- **Death-gripping the bars.** Tense arms transmit every small movement into the steering, and a light bike starts to shake. Relax the grip and let the bike move slightly.
- **Looking at the front wheel.** The bike goes where your eyes go, and eyes on the wheel make the steering twitchy. Look far ahead.
- **Sitting too far back or forward.** A hip position away from the middle of the seat changes the balance and loads one wheel too much.
- **Leaning against the bars when braking.** A rider who braces on the handlebars locks the steering and pushes the bike off line.
- **Stiff knees.** Loose knees against the tank let the bike lean under you, and stiff ones fight it.

Honda also describes the balance technique for slow riding: use a low gear, keep your head level and looking straight ahead with a wide field of view, use your hips and abdomen to keep the bike and your body in balance, and add throttle to keep the bike upright if it is going too slowly. A bike that wobbles at walking pace is often being ridden too slowly in too high a gear.

## Cause 2: load, passengers and luggage

Honda's owner's manual for the Wave RSX FI says that overloading affects handling, braking and stability, and that luggage must be tied down tightly, balanced and as close to the centre of the bike as possible, away from the lights and the exhaust. It lists maximum loads of 115 kg or 155 kg depending on the version.

How a load sits matters as much as how much it weighs:

- **A heavy bag on the rear rack** lifts weight off the front wheel and makes the steering light and twitchy.
- **A heavy backpack on the rider's back** raises the centre of gravity and can pull the bike over in a slow turn.
- **A bag hanging from one handlebar** pulls the steering to one side.
- **A passenger who leans the wrong way or moves suddenly** is a moving weight that upsets the balance.
- **Loose items that shift** change the balance mid-ride.

## Cause 3: tyres and the bike's condition

Tyres are the first suspect when a bike feels unsteady. A soft tyre squirms, a worn one slips and a tyre with a bulge or a flat spot can make the bike shake. Honda's Wave RSX FI manual lists cold pressures of 200 kPa at the front and 225 kPa at the rear for a lone rider, and 280 kPa at the rear for rider and passenger. Honda's aftersales advice is to check pressure with a gauge at least once a month when the tyres are cool, and to replace a tyre when the wear indicator is visible. Our guide to [choosing tyres for a manual bike rental in wet weather](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/choosing-tyres-for-a-manual-bike-rental-in-wet-weather/) explains what to look for.

Other bike conditions that can cause instability, some of which only a mechanic can find:

- **A bent wheel or rim** after an impact, felt as a rhythmic wobble.
- **Worn rear suspension** that bounces after bumps.
- **Loose or worn steering bearings,** felt as a notchy or loose steering or a shake when braking.
- **A bent handlebar or fork** after a fall.
- **A loose or damaged chain** that makes the drive jerky.
- **A stuck or dragging brake,** which pulls the bike to one side.

If you feel a new instability after a bump or a fall, stop and check. Honda advises inspecting the tyres after hitting a kerb or riding over large bumps.

## Cause 4: the road, the wind and the traffic

Even a well-set-up bike feels unstable in some conditions:

- **Crosswinds,** especially on bridges and open stretches such as the Red River crossings, can push a light bike sideways.
- **Passing trucks and buses** create gusts and pressure waves that tug the bike as they pass.
- **Grooved, rutted or wet surfaces** make the front wheel follow ruts.
- **Painted lines and metal covers** are slippery in the wet.
- **High speed.** A small bike's steering gets lighter and more sensitive as speed rises.

The cure is mostly to slow down, relax and keep a straight, steady line. ## A symptom guide

| What you feel | Likely causes | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| A wobble at walking pace | Too slow in too high a gear, stiff arms, a heavy rear load | Use a low gear, add throttle, look ahead and relax your grip |
| A shake at moderate or higher speed | A soft or damaged tyre, a bent wheel, loose steering parts | Slow gently, stop safely and check the tyres; tell the shop |
| A pull to one side | An uneven tyre pressure, a dragging brake, a bag on one side, a bent fork | Stop and check the brakes, bags and tyres |
| A light, floaty front | A heavy rear load, a passenger leaning back, too much speed | Slow down and move weight forward and low |
| A bouncy, vague rear | Worn rear suspension or overloading | Reduce the load and tell the shop |
| A twitchy bike in gusts | Crosswinds or passing trucks | Slow down, relax and hold a straight line |
| A shudder under braking | A warped drum or disc, a loose wheel or steering bearing | Brake gently and tell the shop |

## What to do in the moment

If the bike starts to wobble:

1. **Stay calm and relax your grip.** A tight grip makes it worse.
2. **Do not brake hard.** A sudden brake can make a wobble worse.
3. **Ease off the throttle gently** and let the bike slow by itself.
4. **Keep the bike pointed straight** and look ahead.
5. **Slow to a stop in a safe place** and check the tyres, the load and the bars.

Only if the wobble is violent and not decreasing should you apply both brakes progressively, as Honda's braking sequence says, keeping the steering straight. After stopping, inspect the bike before continuing.

## A quick check you can do at any stop

- **Tyres.** Look for low pressure, bulges and objects. Press them with a thumb.
- **Load.** Are the bags tight, central and low? Is anything hanging from the bars?
- **Handlebars.** Do they turn smoothly without a notch or looseness?
- **Wheels.** Lift the front wheel with the stand if possible and spin it. Watch for a wobble.
- **Brakes.** Do both release fully when you let go?
- **Chain.** Is it dry, loose or noisy? Our guide to [checking chain tension before a longer ride](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/checking-chain-tension-before-a-longer-ride/) explains what Honda says.

## What to tell the shop

A clear description helps staff decide what to do. Say when the feeling happens (at low speed, at high speed, under braking, over bumps), whether you had a passenger or luggage, what the tyres and road were like and what you tried. For example: "At about 40 km/h on a smooth road the bars start to shake. It stops when I slow below 30. No passenger. The front tyre looks a little soft." ## FAQ: an unstable small motorbike

### Why does my bike wobble at low speed?

Often because you are going too slowly in too high a gear or gripping the bars too tightly. Use a low gear, add a little throttle and relax your arms.

### Can luggage make a bike unstable?

Yes. Honda's manual says overloading affects handling, braking and stability. Keep the load low, central and tied down.

### Can a soft tyre cause a wobble?

Yes. A tyre that is low on air squirms. Check the pressure when cool and ask the shop for the right figure.

### Why does the bike feel twitchy in crosswinds?

A light bike is easily pushed by wind and by the gusts of passing trucks. Slow down, relax and hold a straight line.

### Should I brake hard if the bike starts to wobble?

No. Ease off the throttle, keep the bike straight and slow gently.

### When should I swap the bike?

If the instability continues after you have checked your posture, load and tyres, or if you notice a bent part, ask the shop for another bike.
