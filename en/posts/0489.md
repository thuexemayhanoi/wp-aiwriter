In Vietnam the safe amount of alcohol before riding is none. Article 9 of Law 36/2024/QH15 prohibits operating a vehicle on the road with any alcohol in the blood or on the breath, and the thresholds in the penalty decree only set how large the punishment is. The practical rule is to decide how you will get home before the first drink, and never to ride after drinking, including the morning after a heavy night. Alcohol is not the only impairment: drugs, drowsy medicines and tiredness also slow you down, and Article 9 separately bans operating a vehicle with narcotic substances or stimulants in the body.

Our [drink-driving rules guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/drink-driving-rules-motorbike-vietnam/) explains the law and the fine bands in detail. This guide is about the decisions you make before you ride: how to plan the evening, how long alcohol stays in the body, why myths about sobering up fail, how to recognise other kinds of impairment, how to get home safely and what to do with a rented scooter. It is general information based on the law and published guidance as of October 2026, not legal or medical advice.

## The rule in one paragraph

Law 36/2024/QH15 bans operating vehicles on the road with alcohol in the blood or breath (Article 9).

It also bans operating them with law-banned narcotic substances or stimulants in the body, and it requires operators to meet health conditions suited to their vehicle (Article 59). Officers on duty may stop you and test you, and refusing a test is an offence. After an accident, medical facilities must test vehicle operators for alcohol, drugs or stimulants, and the investigation includes such tests (Articles 81 and 83). Under Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, as reported in our drink-driving guide, the fines for a motorbike rider range from a fine of VND 2,000,000 to 3,000,000 with 4 licence points deducted, to a fine of VND 8,000,000 to 10,000,000 with the licence revoked for 22 to 24 months, and refusing the test carries the highest band. Check the current text of the decree before relying on a figure.

## Plan before the first drink

The safest decision is made while you are sober.

1. **Decide how you will get home.** Taxi, ride-hail car, a bus, walking or a friend who is not drinking.

2. **Leave the scooter somewhere safe,** or do not take it at all. If you plan an evening with drinks, return the bike first. ## How long does alcohol stay in the body?

There is no safe waiting time that you can guarantee yourself. Public-health sources agree on several points.

- **Only time reduces alcohol in the blood.** A New York State traffic-safety brochure says that sleep, coffee, a cold shower, fresh air and exercise do not change how fast the liver breaks down alcohol.

- **The body clears about one standard drink an hour on average.** The same brochure and a UK charity both give roughly one drink or unit an hour, and note that the rate varies with size, sex, food, health and liver function.

- **You can still have alcohol in your system the next morning.** The UK charity Drinkaware warns that even if you have been to sleep, high levels may remain, and that the safest advice is to avoid alcohol altogether the night before you drive.

- **Impairment starts at low levels.** A Canadian addiction-health centre says that alcohol blunts alertness and motor coordination at levels below legal limits, and that people are poor judges of their own state.

In Vietnam the limit is zero, so the only reliable rule is not to drink before a ride, and not to ride the morning after a night of heavy drinking. If you are unsure, take a taxi.

## Myths that get riders into trouble

| Myth | Reality |
|---|---|
| "I only had one beer." | In Vietnam any alcohol in the blood or breath is an offence |
| "Coffee will sober me up." | Caffeine may make you feel more awake but does not lower alcohol in your blood |
| "A cold shower or fresh air will clear my head." | They do not change how fast your body clears alcohol |
| "I slept a few hours, so I am fine." | Alcohol may remain, and you can still be impaired |
| "It is a short ride." | A short ride can end in a fall or a stop at a checkpoint |
| "I feel fine." | Feeling fine is not the same as being unimpaired, and a test measures alcohol, not feeling |
| "Food will soak it up." | Food before or while drinking slows absorption, but does not remove alcohol already absorbed |
| "I know the roads." | Alcohol slows reactions on any road |

A health-information site that summarises NIAAA guidance puts it plainly: there is no way to sober up faster, and coffee, cold showers and walking do not change how quickly alcohol leaves the blood. ## Other kinds of impairment

Alcohol is the most common, but not the only, reason to stay off the bike.

- **Drugs and stimulants.** Article 9 bans operating a vehicle with banned narcotic substances or stimulants in the body. Do not ride after using them, and do not accept a ride from someone who has.

- **Medicines that cause drowsiness.** Some cold, allergy, pain, sleep and anxiety medicines can make you sleepy or slow. Our guide to [medicines and drowsiness: when not to ride a scooter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/medicines-and-drowsiness-when-not-to-ride-a-scooter/) explains how to check.

- **Tiredness.** Fatigue slows your reactions in similar ways. Our guide to [what to do if you feel fatigued on a long ride](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-to-do-if-you-feel-fatigued-on-a-long-ride/) explains the signs.

- **Heat and dehydration.** They cause dizziness and confusion. Our guide to [heat and dehydration on a motorbike day trip](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/heat-and-dehydration-on-a-motorbike-day-trip/) explains.

- **Combinations.** Alcohol with tiredness, heat or medicines is worse than any alone. Public-health guidance on medicines warns that alcohol can increase drowsiness.

- **Illness, strong emotion or shock.** If you are unwell, upset or dazed after an incident, you are not fit to ride.

If any of these applies, do not ride. Article 59 requires operators to meet the health conditions suited to their vehicle, and it is your responsibility to judge your own state.

## Getting home safely

- **Taxi or ride-hail car.** The simplest option. Order it before you leave the venue, not on the street.

- **Walking.** Often the best plan in the Old Quarter. Our guide to [Hanoi weekend walking streets: where to leave your scooter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-weekend-walking-streets-where-to-leave-your-scooter/) explains how to plan an evening around the lake without riding in.

- **Bus.** Check the last service.

- **A sober friend** who has a licence valid for the bike, not someone who has "only had a little".

- **Stay put.** If your hotel is close, walk, and collect the bike in the morning once you are sober, remembering that the morning after may not be clear either.

- **Do not accept a ride from a rider or driver who has been drinking.** The danger is the same for a passenger.

If you have left the bike somewhere overnight, tell the shop and take photographs of where it is parked. Our guide to [who is responsible for parking and traffic fines](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/who-is-responsible-for-parking-and-traffic-fines/) explains who is liable for what.

## If you are stopped after drinking

If an officer stops you and suspects alcohol, stay calm, stop and follow the officer's instructions. Article 72 of the Law requires operators to obey orders to stop and requests for inspection, and refusing a test is an offence that carries one of the heaviest fine bands. Do not run, argue or offer money, and call the shop. Our guide to [what to do if a traffic officer stops your motorbike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-to-do-if-a-traffic-officer-stops-your-motorbike/) explains your rights and duties. You may be separated from the bike, so ask for a record of what is taken.

## If a friend is about to ride

1. **Say something.** It is awkward for a moment, and far less awkward than a crash.

2. **Offer an alternative:** call a ride, offer a bed, walk with them.

3. **Take the keys** if you can do so safely and calmly.

4. **Do not ride with them.**

5. **If someone is unwell from alcohol,** do not leave them alone. Call 115 or 112. NIAAA guidance says that blood alcohol can keep rising after the last drink and that an intoxicated person should not be left to sleep it off alone.

## A quick checklist

1. **No alcohol before riding. In Vietnam the limit is zero.**

2. **Decide how you will get home before you drink.**

3. **Return the bike before the evening, or leave it safely parked.**

4. **Do not ride the morning after heavy drinking.**

5. **Coffee, showers and sleep do not make you safe.**

6. **No drugs, stimulants or drowsy medicines before a ride.**

7. **Do not ride when tired, hot, ill or upset.**

8. **Stop a friend from riding impaired.**

## FAQ: alcohol and impairment before riding

### Is any alcohol allowed before riding in Vietnam?

No. Article 9 of Law 36/2024/QH15 prohibits operating a vehicle on the road with alcohol in the blood or breath. The thresholds in Decree 168/2024/ND-CP set the size of the penalty, not an allowed amount.

### How long should I wait after drinking?

There is no guaranteed safe time. On average the body clears about one standard drink an hour, but it varies, and alcohol may remain the next morning. The safest course is not to drink before you ride, and to take a taxi the morning after a heavy night.

### Does coffee help me sober up?

No. It may make you feel more awake, but it does not reduce the alcohol in your blood.

### Can I ride after taking cold medicine?

Check the label and ask a pharmacist. Some cold and allergy medicines cause drowsiness. Our guide to medicines and drowsiness explains.

### What if I need to return the bike but have been drinking?

Do not ride. Call the shop, arrange a taxi home, and agree a time to return the bike when you are sober.

### What happens at a checkpoint?

Officers may require a test. Follow instructions, and see our guide to what to do if a traffic officer stops your motorbike.
