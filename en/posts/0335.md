On a wet day in Hanoi, leave the scooter parked when the rain is heavy, thunder is forecast, streets are flooding or you would be riding in the dark, and spend the day on sights where you park once and stay indoors. Light, short showers are different: a rider with proper rain gear and a short hop can carry on, but even then wet roads reduce grip and lengthen stopping distances. This guide is about the sightseeing decision rather than riding technique. It gives you a simple traffic-light test for the weather, a set of museums with hours taken from their own websites, a plan that clusters stops so one parking spot does the work, and a list of things to do with a rented bike while you wait out a storm.

## What October rain actually looks like

October is the end of Hanoi's wet season, not the start of a dry one. Weather-and-Climate gives roughly 128 mm of rain for the month, with daytime highs around 31 degrees Celsius, and Weathermonth quotes about 12 rainy days with 78 per cent humidity. Those are averages from commercial weather sites, not forecasts, but they tell you to expect a wet day or two in any visit.

Rain here often arrives as a sharp afternoon shower or thunderstorm, not all-day drizzle. The national hydro-meteorological forecasting centre, whose site is the official source for weather warnings, shows the pattern in a bulletin for 10 October 2026 covering the northern plains and midlands: cloudy with showers and thunderstorms in places, a few heavy falls locally, and a warning that thunderstorms may bring whirlwinds, lightning and strong gusts. That is the kind of line to read before you decide to ride.

The practical lesson is to check an official forecast in the morning and again before you leave a sight. The official forecast site is the National Centre for Hydro-Meteorological Forecasting at nchmf.gov.vn, where heavy-rain, thunderstorm and storm warnings are listed.

## A three-colour test for the day

| Colour | Signs | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Green | Dry or a few drops, good visibility, no warnings | Ride normally, keep the rain gear in the bike or bag. |
| Amber | Steady light rain, wet roads, showers forecast to pass within an hour | Make short hops only, leave extra distance, avoid the busiest junctions, or park and wait. |
| Red | Heavy rain, thunderstorm or storm warning, standing water, poor visibility, darkness plus rain, or you feel unsure | Leave the bike parked. Walk, take a taxi or ride-hailing car, or stay indoors. |

Honda's guidance for riding in rain is blunt: wet roads reduce tyre grip and increase stopping distances, so you should keep your movements smooth and give yourself more time to react, with lights on because visibility is reduced for you and others. Harley-Davidson's rider advice adds that the first minutes of a storm are among the worst because oil and dirt that built up on the road float to the surface, and that painted lines and manhole covers become especially slippery. Both support a conservative rule: if you are in doubt, you are in the red.

## Red-light moments when the bike stays put

Park and do something else when any of these are true:

- **An official heavy-rain, thunderstorm or storm warning covers Hanoi.** Do not ride to prove a point.
- **Streets are flooded or you cannot see the road surface.** Water hides potholes and open drains.
- **It is dark and wet.** Our guide to [riding at night in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-at-night-in-hanoi/) already asks for extra care; with rain it becomes a poor trade.
- **You are carrying a passenger or luggage and have no waterproof cover.** Wet clothes and a slippery seat make a ride miserable and harder to control.
- **You are tired, cold or new to riding in traffic.** Shivering reduces control.
- **Your helmet visor or glasses are fogging and you cannot clear them.**

The shop provides one basic helmet per rider, but a raincoat is not included for short rentals, so bring your own if the forecast is uncertain. Do not buy a flimsy cape that can flap into the wheel or block your mirrors.

## Indoor sights with published hours

These are places where the main experience is under a roof and where the official websites state opening hours. Hours and closing days can change, so check before you go.

| Place | Hours on its own website | Why it works in rain |
|---|---|---|
| Vietnam Museum of Ethnology | 8:30 to 17:30, closed Mondays and the New Year holiday | Indoor galleries on Vietnam's ethnic groups, plus an outdoor area for when the sky clears. |
| Vietnamese Women's Museum, 36 Ly Thuong Kiet | 8:00 to 17:00, Monday to Sunday | A multi-floor museum on women's lives; the website says it is near Hoan Kiem Lake and the Old Quarter. |
| Hoa Lo Prison Relic, No. 1 Hoa Lo | 8:00 to 17:00 every day, including festivals and holidays | A historic prison museum with exhibitions in the preserved site. |
| Vietnam National Fine Arts Museum | 8:30 to 17:00, Tuesday to Sunday, closed Monday | Galleries in a historic main building, and a cafe with indoor and outdoor seating. |

A few notes. The Women's Museum and Hoa Lo are both listed in Cua Nam ward on their own sites, so a single parking stop in that area can serve both on foot, and if you walk between them you do not need to ride in the rain at all. The Ethnology Museum is on Nguyen Van Huyen Street in Nghia Do, away from the Old Quarter, and is a case for a ride-hailing car or a longer wait for a break in the weather. Hoa Lo's regulations say to park at the designated lot only, and ask visitors to leave handbags in a designated place and not to smoke.

Other popular wet-weather picks, such as water puppet shows, cooking classes and cafes, are listed on travel sites, but those are commercial listings and you should check the venue's own timetable and booking rules.

## A one-parking-stop rainy-day plan

This plan assumes an amber day with the possibility of a red afternoon. It uses one guarded parking stop and a lot of walking under shelter.

1. **Morning.** Ride at your own pace to a guarded parking area in the Cua Nam and Hoan Kiem area, with rain gear on.
2. **Late morning.** Walk to the Women's Museum and spend an hour or two inside.
3. **Lunch.** Eat nearby, in a place with seating that is not on a pavement.
4. **Early afternoon.** Walk to Hoa Lo, which the official site says stays open through the day.
5. **Mid-afternoon.** Check the forecast again. If it is green or amber, ride home. If it is red, leave the bike where it is or use a car.
6. **Evening.** If the rain is still heavy, eat and wait for conditions to improve rather than riding in it. Ask the parking lot about its closing time before you settle in, because a guarded lot will close at some point.

## Leaving a rental bike in the rain

Leaving a bike parked for a few hours in bad weather is normal, but a few habits help:

- **Choose covered or guarded parking when you can.** A sheltered bay is better than an open kerb.
- **Lock the steering and take the key.** The shop does not provide an extra lock, so the steering lock and an attended parking place matter more.
- **Take photographs at parking and at pickup,** especially of any scratch or damage that already existed.
- **Wipe the mirrors and seat before you ride again.**
- **Check the brakes with a slow test.** Wet brake surfaces work less well at first, so make a gentle trial stop.
- **If the bike will not start after standing in heavy rain or water, do not keep cranking it.** Phone the shop on 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969), open 09:00 to 21:00 daily. ## FAQ: rainy-day sightseeing in Hanoi

### Is it okay to ride in light rain?

It can be, with proper gear, lights on, extra distance and short hops. Wet roads reduce grip, so go slower than usual. If in doubt, park.

### Which official source should I check for warnings?

The National Centre for Hydro-Meteorological Forecasting at nchmf.gov.vn lists heavy-rain, thunderstorm and storm warnings.

### What are good indoor sights?

Museums with published hours include the Vietnam Museum of Ethnology, the Vietnamese Women's Museum, Hoa Lo Prison Relic and the Vietnam National Fine Arts Museum. Check the hours before you go, because some close on Mondays.

### Does the shop provide a raincoat?

Not for short rentals. One basic helmet per rider is included, so bring your own rain gear.

### Can I leave the bike overnight and collect it later?

Only in a place that is open to you at the time, such as a hotel parking area that agrees. Ask the lot or hotel about hours and tickets first.

### What if a storm starts while I am out?

Stop in a safe place, preferably under a solid shelter, and wait. Do not shelter under a loose sign or temporary awning.
