For most visitors who have never ridden gears, the twist-and-go 50cc automatic scooter is the easier bike to start with in Hanoi, because it removes the foot shifter and the habit of changing gear at every junction. The Cub-style 50cc is the better choice if you already like a semi-automatic, want larger wheels, or are comfortable with a foot-shift pattern and a simple, tough design. Both are small, light and slow, and both are held to the same 40 km/h ceiling for mopeds in Vietnam, so the choice is about how the bike feels in stop-and-go traffic, not about speed. This guide compares the two in the situations a city rider actually meets: pulling away, parking, carrying a bag, wet roads and learning. It is general information as of October 2026, and the exact bike you receive is confirmed when you book.

## Two bikes that look alike on paper and ride differently

Nguyen Ha's 50cc group includes the Cub 50cc and 50cc scooters, and both are priced the same, so price will not decide this for you. What decides it is the control layout.

A Cub-style bike has a step-through frame, a foot lever on the left side that selects gears, and no clutch lever on the handlebar. You pull away in first, tap the lever up to second and third as speed builds, and tap down as you slow. Honda's well-known Super Cub 50 sold in Japan is the reference design: Honda's own specification lists a 49 cc air-cooled four-stroke engine producing 2.7 kW, a four-speed gearbox, a kerb weight of 96 kg, a seat height of 735 mm and a 4.3 litre fuel tank. Rental Cubs in Hanoi may be older or different models from that one, so treat these figures as a guide to the type, not a promise about a particular bike.

A 50cc automatic scooter has a twist grip for throttle, two brake levers and nothing else to operate. The engine drives the rear wheel through a belt and an automatic centrifugal clutch, so you open the throttle and the bike moves. It has a platform for your feet and usually a small compartment under the seat, though the size of that space varies from model to model.

## Pulling away and stop-and-go riding

In Hanoi's inner districts most riding is short bursts of 20 to 30 km/h between junctions, crossings and parked vehicles. This is where the two designs feel most different.

On the automatic, a stop is just a stop. You squeeze both brakes, put a foot down and, when the traffic moves, open the throttle. There is nothing to forget, and a nervous rider can give full attention to the road. The cost is that the throttle response is less direct than a geared bike, and on a very small engine the first metre can feel unhurried.

On the Cub, each stop adds a small task: click down to first before you pull away, or you will struggle. Riders who have driven a semi-automatic manage this without thinking, and some prefer it because they choose the gear and can feel the engine more directly. A new rider can stall, lurch or select neutral by accident, which is rarely dangerous at 20 km/h but can be unsettling in the middle of a junction. Our guide to [how to handle stop-and-go traffic on a semi-automatic bike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-handle-stop-and-go-traffic-on-a-semi-automatic-bike/) explains the routine, and the comparison of [when a semi-automatic bike is easier than an automatic scooter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/when-a-semi-automatic-bike-is-easier-than-an-automatic-scooter/) covers the reverse case.

## Side by side for city use

| Question | Cub-style 50cc | 50cc automatic scooter |
|---|---|---|
| Controls | Foot shifter, throttle, two brakes, no clutch lever | Throttle and two brake levers |
| First ten minutes | More to learn if you have never ridden gears | Easiest for a first-time rider |
| Stop-and-go | Needs a gear change after most stops | Nothing to select |
| Wheels | Typically larger, around 17 inches on the Honda Super Cub 50 | Typically smaller wheels on most scooters |
| Rough patches and manhole covers | Larger wheels roll over them more calmly | Small wheels need more care |
| Storage | Usually a rack or hook rather than a compartment | Often an under-seat compartment |
| Footing | Feet on pegs or a narrow floor | Flat platform, more relaxed |
| Starting | Electric and kick starter on many Cubs | Electric starter on most |
| Weight | Light, easy to push | Often a little heavier at the front and middle |

The wheel-size row is a general observation, not a rule: the Honda Super Cub 50 uses 17-inch tyres according to Honda's specification, and many automatic scooters run 10 to 12 inch wheels. Larger wheels roll over small holes and kerbs more easily, which matters on Hanoi streets patched many times over. Smaller wheels make a bike feel nimble and light in a tight turn but less forgiving of an unexpected edge.

## Parking, pushing and carrying a bag

Hanoi parking is a daily test. You will move your bike backwards out of rows of other bikes, up and down kerbs and onto a stand. A Cub, with its light frame and low seat, is easy to wheel and to balance by hand, and the step-through design suits any height. An automatic scooter of the same size is just as easy for most riders, though the body panels on some models make the bike a little wider at the knee.

Where the two differ is what you can do with your hands and your belongings:

- **Automatic scooter:** The flat floor and compartment make it practical for a daypack, a small shopping bag or a rain jacket. Check the compartment size before you assume your belongings fit.
- **Cub:** Cubs are often fitted with a rear rack or a hook on the leg shield, but this depends on the bike. Ask what is on the bike you are given.

For either bike, do not hang weight from the handlebars. Honda's guidance on loading says that carrying extra weight affects handling, braking and stability, and that luggage should be tied securely, evenly balanced and close to the centre of the vehicle. ## Rain, slippery roads and brakes

Both bikes have drum brakes in the typical 50cc class, and Honda's specification for the Super Cub 50 lists mechanical leading and trailing drum brakes, a reminder that braking distances on these bikes are longer than on a larger scooter. On wet roads, painted lines, manhole covers and metal road plates can become slippery.

The twist-and-go bike has the advantage of simplicity: you control speed with throttle and two brakes, and you need not worry about a gear change while braking. The Cub rider has the benefit of engine braking in a lower gear, which helps on a slope, but must remember to shift down smoothly. Neither bike should be pushed in heavy rain; our guide to [riding in Hanoi's rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) explains the season and the streets that flood. Raincoats are not included for short rentals, so bring your own.

## Speed, hills and the 40 km/h limit

Under Vietnam's Circular 38/2024/TT-BGTVT, in force since 1 January 2025, mopeds have a maximum operating speed of 40 km/h. A 49 cc engine with a power output around 2.7 kW, as in Honda's Super Cub 50, is not built for more. In Hanoi's centre the traffic flow is often at or below that speed, and the 50cc bike keeps up. On open roads, dyke roads and bridge approaches, the flow moves faster and your bike will be the slowest on the road, so stay to the right and let others pass.

A Cub has a small advantage on an incline because you can drop into a lower gear and keep the engine in its working range. An automatic does this by itself, usually without fuss on a short ramp, but both bikes will slow noticeably when climbing with a passenger and a bag. Rural routes and the hills around Ba Vi or Tam Dao are a poor fit for either bike.

## Who should choose which

**Choose the 50cc automatic if:**

- You have never ridden a gear bike.
- You want the least to think about while you learn Hanoi traffic.
- You plan to carry a small bag or daypack on short trips.
- You are riding mostly on flat inner-city streets.

**Choose the Cub-style 50cc if:**

- You already ride a semi-automatic and like the feel of choosing a gear.
- You value larger wheels on rough surfaces.
- You want a bike light enough to manoeuvre by hand in narrow alleys.
- You are happy with fewer storage options.

If you are undecided, ask for a few minutes in the shop's lane or a quiet side street before you leave. An automatic is more forgiving of a bad first minute.

## FAQ: Cub or automatic 50cc

### Which 50cc bike is easier for a complete beginner?

The automatic scooter, because there is no gear lever to operate. You need only the throttle and the two brakes.

### Is a Cub faster than a 50cc automatic?

Not in any way that matters. Both are limited by the same engine size, and Vietnamese rules cap mopeds at 40 km/h.

### Which handles potholes better?

Larger wheels usually cope better with rough surfaces. The Honda Super Cub 50 has 17-inch wheels, while most automatic scooters have smaller ones. This is a general observation and varies by model.
