Bac Ninh is one of the easiest culture days out of Hanoi, but it works best if you choose one cluster of stops instead of trying to see the whole province. Published guides put the area roughly 30 to 40 kilometres from central Hanoi, about an hour by road in good conditions, and the sights are old pagodas, communal houses, temples and craft villages that you walk around once you have parked. This guide splits the province into a river cluster around Thuan Thanh and a northern cluster around Tu Son and Tien Du, explains what each stop is, suggests a day plan, and covers the road decisions that matter on an ordinary rental scooter: which highway to take, what to avoid, when festival crowds make a stop a bad idea and what to check before leaving the city.

## Why Bac Ninh suits a day ride

Vietnam's Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism describes Bac Ninh as the heartland of the old Kinh Bac region, with almost 1,600 heritage sites, five national special relics and four UNESCO-recognised intangible heritages. For a visitor, that translates into short hops between places that carry real history. You do not need to go far to see something that is hundreds of years old.

The ride is also gentle compared with mountain trips. The terrain is flat river plain, the stops are close together, and a rental scooter handles the distances comfortably. If you want a hill ride instead, our guides to [Tam Dao](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-to-tam-dao-by-motorbike/) and [Ba Vi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-to-ba-vi-by-motorbike/) cover the climbs.

One practical warning on names. Provincial boundaries and local names have changed in recent years, so older guides refer to places such as Thuan Thanh district, while a 2026 VietnamNet article places the Dong Ho painting village in Thuan Thanh Ward, Bac Ninh Province. If a map and a blog disagree on the administrative label, trust the pin on the map.

## Pick one cluster

The two groups below are each a comfortable day. Doing both in a single day means a lot of riding for little time at each place.

### The river cluster around Thuan Thanh

This group sits near the Duong River dike east of Hanoi.

**Dau Pagoda.** The Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism describes Dau Pagoda, also called Dien Ung, in Thuan Thanh as the oldest pagoda in Vietnam, built in the third century. VnExpress adds that it was restored by the scholar Mac Dinh Chi in the fourteenth century, who built its tower. The Dau Pagoda Festival is held on the eighth day of the fourth lunar month, which falls around spring.

**But Thap Pagoda.** VietnamPlus describes But Thap, or Ninh Phuc Tu, as built in the fourteenth century and home to four groups of national treasures. They include a thousand-hands and thousand-eyes Avalokitesvara statue and a nine-storey lotus tower about 7.8 metres tall, and the pagoda was listed as a special national relic in 2013. Its festival is on the 23rd and 24th of the third lunar month.

**Dong Ho painting village.** VietnamNet describes Dong Ho as a folk art village of more than 400 years, about 30 kilometres from Hanoi on the southern bank of the Duong River, where visitors can watch woodblock printing by hand. Artisans in the village welcome visitors, but opening depends on each household, so do not assume every workshop is open.

Travel guides commonly combine But Thap and Dong Ho into a single day because they are close together along the river dike.

### The northern cluster around Tu Son and Tien Du

**Dinh Bang communal house and Do Temple.** VietnamPlus calls Dinh Bang, in Tu Son town, one of the three most beautiful communal houses of the old Kinh Bac region, about 20 kilometres north of Hanoi, a massive timber structure with traditional carved decoration. VnExpress describes the nearby Do Temple as the place that honours the Ly kings, with a festival in the third lunar month.

**Lim Hill and quan ho.** Tuoi Tre describes the Lim Festival in Tien Du district, about 30 kilometres from downtown Hanoi, held on the 13th day of the first lunar month, with quan ho love duets at its heart. UNESCO inscribed Quan Ho Bac Ninh folk songs on its list of intangible cultural heritage in 2009. The UNESCO file explains that the songs are sung in alternating verses between women from one village and men from another.

Quan ho is a living tradition, not a daily show. Do not count on hearing it outside festival periods unless a venue or club confirms a performance.

## How far, how long

Treat these figures as rough, since each guide measures from a different start point.

| Stop | Reported distance from Hanoi centre | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Dinh Bang and Do Temple | About 20 km | Tu Son town |
| Lim Hill | About 30 km | Tien Du, festival site |
| Dau Pagoda | About 30 to 35 km | Thuan Thanh, about an hour from the Old Quarter by one guide |
| Dong Ho village | About 30 to 35 km | Duong River dike |
| Bac Ninh city | About 40 km | VnExpress: about an hour |

From the centre of Hanoi, allow at least an hour and a half each way in ordinary traffic, then add your time on foot. Leaving the city at 8 a.m., you could be at the first stop by mid-morning, spend two to three hours across two or three places, have a long lunch and start back by mid-afternoon. We think that is more pleasant than racing the light, but the timing depends on your route and traffic.

## Choosing the road

The published route descriptions for the river cluster are not identical. One guide for Dong Ho says to take National Highway 5, toward Hai Phong, then turn toward Thuan Thanh and the Duong River dike. Another mentions turning onto National Highway 38 near the Ho bridge. Because of that, do not trust a single written route. Use a map app, look at the road numbers and compare them with the places named above.

Three rules keep the plan realistic.

**Stay off expressways.** Article 25 of Law 36/2024/QH15, in force since 1 January 2025, says that motorcycles, mopeds and similar vehicles may not use expressways. A navigation app may still suggest a fast corridor. Our guide to [checking whether a navigation app suggests an expressway](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-check-if-a-navigation-app-suggests-an-expressway/) and the summary of [Hanoi's ring roads and highways](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-ring-roads-highways-motorbikes-allowed/) explain how to avoid them.

**Expect lorries on national highways.** Heavy goods vehicles are normal on the main roads out of Hanoi. Keep a longer gap, avoid riding beside a truck's rear wheels, and do not overtake on a bend.

**Know the limits.** Circular 38/2024/TT-BGTVT sets maximum speeds by road type, and in built-up areas the general limit for most vehicles on a two-way road is 50 km/h. Signs override the general figure, and mopeds, as a class, have their own limit. Our guide to [motorbike speed limits](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/speed-limits-motorbikes-vietnam/) explains it in more detail.

## Timing around festivals

Bac Ninh is known for its festival calendar. VnExpress notes that the Lim Festival, the Dau Pagoda Festival and the Do Temple Festival all fall in the cooler months from January to March, while it considers summer and autumn better for natural sights and craft villages. The Dau and But Thap dates quoted above fall in spring on the lunar calendar.

For a rider, a festival means three things: heavy crowds, parked vehicles along the road verges, and slow traffic for several kilometres around the site. If you want the atmosphere, go on the main day with a flexible plan and park early. If you want to see the buildings in peace, choose an ordinary weekday outside the festival weeks.

Lunar dates move against the calendar every year, so look up the actual dates for the year of your trip.

## Etiquette at pagodas and temples

These are places of worship, and many are working religious sites.

- Dress modestly, with shoulders and knees covered, and take off your helmet and sunglasses.
- Follow the signs about shoes and photography, and do not step on thresholds.
- Keep voices low, and do not walk through ceremonies.
- Park where attendants or signs direct, and do not block the entrance for processions.

We cannot confirm opening hours or any entrance charges for these sites, since guides give different figures and they change. Check on the day, or ask a local.

## A sample day: the river cluster

This is a suggestion, not a timetable.

- **8:00.** Leave Hanoi on the main road out toward Thuan Thanh.
- **9:30 to 11:00.** Walk around But Thap Pagoda.
- **11:00 to 12:30.** Ride a few kilometres to Dong Ho and see a printing demonstration if a workshop is open, then eat locally.
- **13:30 to 14:30.** Visit Dau Pagoda if energy allows.
- **15:00.** Start the ride back, with a fuel stop on the way.

For the northern cluster, swap in Dinh Bang and Do Temple for the morning and use the afternoon for Bac Ninh city or an early return.

## FAQ: Hanoi to Bac Ninh by motorbike

### How far is Bac Ninh from Hanoi by motorbike?

Published guides put the main stops 20 to 40 kilometres from central Hanoi, roughly an hour by road in good conditions. Allow more in traffic.

### Can I do Bac Ninh in one day?

Yes, if you choose one cluster and keep the plan to two or three stops.

### Are motorbikes allowed on the expressway?

No. Law 36/2024/QH15 keeps motorcycles off expressways. Use ordinary national highways and check your map app.

### When are the big festivals?

Lim, Dau Pagoda, Do Temple and But Thap festivals fall on lunar dates between the first and fourth lunar months. Look up the dates for your year.

### Will I hear quan ho singing?

At festivals, yes. Outside festivals, only if a club or venue confirms a performance.

### Do I need a different bike for this ride?

An ordinary scooter or semi-automatic bike is fine on these flat roads. Ask the shop which bike is available.
