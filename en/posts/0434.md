To compare two rental quotes for the same class of motorbike fairly, first make them describe the same thing: the same bike or model group, the same dates and pickup time and the same place. Then compare the whole cost and the whole set of conditions, not just the daily rate: how a rental day is counted, the size and form of the deposit and when it comes back, the fuel rule, the late-return charge, what extras are included, who pays for delivery, how damage and theft are handled and how you pay.

A quote that is cheaper by a small amount per day can be dearer once a larger deposit, a stricter late charge or an extra helmet is counted, and a quote that looks high can include things the other leaves out. This guide shows how to make quotes comparable, gives a worksheet, lists the published terms of Nguyen Ha as a reference point, works through an illustrative example without inventing prices, explains red flags and shows how to ask for a clean quote. It is general information, not a recommendation of any shop.

## Step 1: make the quotes comparable

Two quotes are only comparable if they answer the same question. Before you compare, check that both describe:

- **The same class of bike.** For manual and semi-automatic bikes, Nguyen Ha's published group covers the Honda Wave, Yamaha Sirius and Honda Blade. Automatic scooters, 50cc bikes and electric bikes are separate groups with their own ranges. Our guide to [how to confirm the exact model you will receive](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-confirm-the-exact-model-you-will-receive/) explains why the model name matters.

- **The same dates and times.** A day is a block of hours, not a calendar date, so pickup and return times matter.

- **The same pickup and return place,** and whether delivery is included.

- **The same period,** whether by the day, the week or the month. Packages are priced on their own, not as a daily rate multiplied up, so compare like with like.

- **The same riders and luggage,** because a second helmet or a larger bike changes the price.

If a quote does not say these things, ask for them before you compare. A quote that names only a price is not yet a quote.

## An illustrative example, with invented shops

These shops and figures are invented to show how to think. They are not real quotes.

**Shop A** quotes VND 150,000 per day for a manual bike, a cash deposit of VND 5,000,000, a late-return charge of VND 30,000 per hour and a basic helmet included.

**Shop B** quotes VND 200,000 per day for a similar bike, a cash deposit of VND 2,000,000, a late-return charge of VND 30,000 per hour and a basic helmet included.

At first glance Shop A is cheaper by the day. But look at the whole picture.

- **Cash tied up.** Shop A holds a deposit that is a good deal larger. Over a short rental, that money is simply out of your hands, and if there is a dispute, more is at risk.

- **Deposit return terms.** If Shop A returns the deposit slowly or deducts for vague reasons, the saving on the day rate can disappear.

- **Day counting.** If one shop counts a calendar day and the other 24 hours, the difference in real days could change the result.

- **Condition and support.** If Shop B's bike is newer and its hours are longer, that may be worth the higher rate.

The point is not that one is better. It is that the lower number on the price board is only one input. ## Weighing what matters to you

Different renters value different things. Decide your priorities before you decide.

- **Short rental, tight budget:** the day rate, the deposit and how a day is counted matter most.

- **Longer rental:** the weekly or monthly package, servicing, and how an extension works matter most. Our guide to [manual motorbike rental: daily versus monthly use cases](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/manual-motorbike-rental-daily-versus-monthly-use-cases/) explains the trade-offs.

- **A long trip:** breakdown help, condition and any area limits matter most. Our guide to [how to check whether a rental allows an out-of-city trip](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-check-whether-a-rental-allows-an-out-of-city-trip/) explains what to ask.

- **First-time rider:** helmet quality, bike condition and the shop's willingness to explain the controls matter most.

- **Cash flow:** the deposit size and how it comes back matter most.

Score each quote against your own priorities, and decide on the whole picture.

## Red flags in a quote

- **A price far below every other quote.** A very low price usually means something is left out or changed later.

- **No model named,** or "a Honda" with no further detail.

- **"From" prices** with no way to learn the actual price for your dates.

- **No written quote,** only a verbal figure.

- **A passport as the deposit.** Our guide to [how a motorbike rental deposit works in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbike-rental-deposit-hanoi/) explains why this is risky.

- **Pressure to pay at once,** or a deadline that expires within the hour.

- **An unclear deposit return,** with no statement of when or how.

- **Conflicting addresses, hours or phone numbers** across the listings. Our guide to [how to book a Hanoi motorbike rental on WhatsApp](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-book-a-hanoi-motorbike-rental-on-whatsapp/) explains how to spot a fake contact.

- **No answer about damage and theft.**

## How to ask for a clean quote

Send each shop the same message so the answers line up.

"Hello. I would like to rent a [bike model or class] from [date and time] to [date and time], for [number] rider(s), picking up at your shop. Could you please send a written quote showing: the price for these dates, how a rental day is counted, the deposit amount and form, when and how the deposit is returned, the late-return and fuel charges, what is included (helmets, accessories), any delivery charge, how damage and theft are handled and how I can pay? Thank you."

Ask for replies in writing. Our guide to [what to send the rental shop before pickup](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-to-send-the-rental-shop-before-pickup/) explains what to send, and our guide to [reading a motorbike rental agreement in English](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/reading-a-motorbike-rental-agreement-in-english/) explains how to read the contract that follows.

## What the law says about price

Vietnam's Civil Code (Law No. 91/2015/QH13), in force since 1 January 2017, says that the rent in a lease is set by agreement, and that where there is no agreement or it is unclear, it is the market price at the place and time the lease was made (Article 473). We read the lease articles in the text published by the Tuyen Quang Department of Justice. As of October 2026 that means the agreed figure is what counts, so make sure the agreed figure, and the conditions that go with it, are written down before you pay. This is general information, not legal advice.

## FAQ: comparing rental quotes

### What should I compare besides the daily rate?

How a day is counted, the deposit and its return, fuel and late-return terms, included extras, delivery, damage and theft terms and payment.

### Is the cheapest quote the best?

Not necessarily. A low day rate can come with a larger deposit, a stricter late charge or fewer inclusions.

### How do I get comparable quotes?

Send each shop the same message with the same bike, dates and place, and ask for a written reply.

### Are Nguyen Ha's prices fixed?

They are reference ranges. The exact price is confirmed on the actual bike, its condition and your dates before you sign.

### Should I pay before I compare?

No. Compare first, then pay once the quote is confirmed in writing.

### What if a quote leaves things out?

Ask. A gap in a quote is something to fill before you sign.
