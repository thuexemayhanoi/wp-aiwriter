A 50cc bike may struggle on an open road because it is designed and regulated as a slow, light vehicle. In Vietnam a moped has a design speed of no more than 50 km/h and, under the speed circular in force since 1 January 2025, a maximum operating speed of 40 km/h on roads other than expressways, which it may not enter at all. The engine is small, so it accelerates slowly, loses speed on gradients and into headwinds, and has little reserve for overtaking. On a road shared with lorries, buses and 110cc to 150cc scooters travelling faster, that makes the moped the slowest vehicle in the flow. This guide explains the causes, what the struggle feels like, and which journeys suit a 50cc bike and which do not.

## What "open road" means here

By open road we mean roads outside the dense core of Hanoi: national highways, provincial roads, dyke roads, bridges and ring-road sections where traffic flows faster and the road is not hemmed in by shopfronts. It does not mean expressways, which a moped cannot use. Law 36/2024/QH15 says that mopeds, motorbikes and similar vehicles are not allowed on an expressway, and our guide to [Hanoi's ring roads and highways](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-ring-roads-highways-motorbikes-allowed/) explains which roads that affects.

In the middle of the city traffic crawls, and a 50cc bike keeps up easily. TomTom's 2025 traffic data put the average speed in Hanoi's rush hours at around 14 km/h for vehicles generally. Once the road clears, the picture changes: other vehicles speed up and the moped does not.

## Reason 1: the legal ceiling is low

A moped cannot go as fast as the traffic around it, and the law says so in two layers. Law 36/2024/QH15 Article 34 defines a moped as a two- or three-wheeler with a design speed of no more than 50 km/h (and, for a petrol engine, a displacement of no more than 50 cc). Circular 38/2024/TT-BGTVT then sets the maximum operating speed for mopeds at 40 km/h on roads other than expressways.

So a rider on a 50cc moped should not exceed 40 km/h, even where the sign says 60 or 80. Other vehicles are allowed higher limits depending on road type, so on a national road you will be passed constantly. Riding at the cap does not bring you into trouble, but riding faster does. Our [speed limits guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/speed-limits-motorbikes-vietnam/) sets out the full table, and the [50cc rules guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/renting-a-50cc-scooter-in-hanoi/) covers who may ride one.

## Reason 2: very little power in reserve

A larger engine leaves a margin for acceleration, hills and wind; a 50cc engine does not. To put this in proportion, Honda's Japanese-market figures give the Super Cub 50 a maximum output of 2.7 kW and the Super Cub 110 a maximum output of 5.9 kW, at weights of 96 kg and 99 kg. These are Japanese models, not the bikes that Nguyen Ha rents, so treat them as an illustration of the class: the 110 cc version has more than twice the power in a frame of almost the same weight.

A rental 50cc Cub or scooter may differ in detail, but the pattern holds. On a flat road at a steady speed a 50cc engine copes. Where the demand goes up, it has little to give. This is also why the moped feels fine in a city stop-start flow, where short bursts are all that is asked.

## Reason 3: hills, wind and loads take a bigger bite

Anything that adds resistance costs a small engine proportionally more:

- **Gradients.** A climb that a 110cc bike takes without slowing may pull a 50cc bike down to a crawl. Even a long overpass or a dyke ramp can be enough.
- **Headwinds.** On open ground, wind slows you down, and on a light bike it also pushes the front wheel around.
- **Two riders and a bag.** Weight eats into the small power margin. Our guide to [carrying luggage on a scooter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/scooter-for-two-riders-and-luggage-hanoi/) discusses a larger bike, but the same effect is stronger on a 50cc one.
- **Heat.** Sustained full throttle in hot weather puts more load on an air-cooled engine. This is general mechanical advice, not a manufacturer rule, so ease off and take breaks.

The sign of struggle is a bike that is at full throttle and still losing speed. That is the cue to move to the right, slow and let traffic pass, not to push harder.

## Reason 4: overtaking and being overtaken

A moped has little ability to overtake, and the Law restricts overtaking in several places: on a narrow single-lane bridge, on bends with limited visibility and near the crest of a hill on a two-way road. Law 36/2024/QH15 Article 13 also says vehicles moving at a slower speed must stay on the right of their direction of travel, and a moped is usually the slowest vehicle on the road.

The practical result is that other vehicles overtake you. Hold a steady line near the right edge of your lane, do not swerve when someone passes and do not match their speed. Check your mirror before any move to the left, and use the horn only to warn of danger or to signal overtaking, as the Law describes. A moped that tries to keep pace with faster traffic ends up using more of the road than it should.

## Reason 5: the road itself

Open roads around Hanoi can be rougher than city streets. Trucks leave ruts, surfaces break at bridge joints and verges are soft or littered with sand and gravel. A 50cc bike has small wheels, which drop into holes harder than the larger wheels on a scooter. Heavy vehicles throw up a pressure wave and wind as they pass, and on a light bike you feel it.

Add the lack of a shoulder in many places and the long gaps between safe stopping spots, and an open-road ride becomes tiring in a way a city loop never is. Our guide to [planning breaks on a long ride](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-plan-breaks-on-a-long-semi-automatic-bike-ride/) helps if you ride further than usual.

## What the struggle looks like in practice

| Situation | What you notice | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Steady road at 35 to 40 km/h | Fine on flat ground, though the traffic passes you | Stay right and keep the line |
| A climb or an overpass | Speed falls even at full throttle | Ease off, stay right and let others pass |
| A headwind on an open stretch | Slow progress and a twitchy front end | Slow down, relax your grip and tuck in |
| Heavy lorry passing | Buffeting and noise | Hold your line and avoid sudden steering |
| Rough surface | Small wheels thump and the bike skips | Slow down and choose the smoothest strip |
| Two people and luggage | The bike feels sluggish | Cut the plans, or choose a bigger bike if your licence allows |

## If you do ride a 50cc bike on an open road

- Plan a short route and know where you can stop.
- Ride at a steady pace at or below 40 km/h and keep right.
- Leave extra room between you and anything ahead; the Law requires a safe distance from the vehicle in front.
- Check the weather, as wind and rain make a light bike harder to control.
- Make sure you have enough fuel and time to get back before the shop closes at 21:00.
- Do not take a second rider and a heavy bag unless you must.
- Stop and rest when the engine or you are tired.

## FAQ: 50cc bikes on open roads

### How fast can a 50cc moped go in Vietnam?

The design speed is up to 50 km/h and the legal operating limit is 40 km/h on roads other than expressways.

### Can a 50cc bike use an expressway?

No. Mopeds and motorbikes are not allowed on expressways under Law 36/2024/QH15.

### Will a 50cc bike climb hills?

It can manage gentle slopes but will slow on steeper or longer ones, especially with a passenger or a headwind.

### Is it dangerous to be the slowest vehicle?

It raises risk if you try to keep up. Staying right at a steady pace and letting traffic pass is safer.

### Is a 110cc scooter better for distance?

For most journeys, yes, if you are legally able to ride one. Check licence rules first.

### Should I take a 50cc bike on a day trip out of Hanoi?

Usually no. The distance, the lorries and the slow speed make it a tiring day.
