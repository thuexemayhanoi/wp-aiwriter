No: if heavy rain has flooded a road and you cannot see the road surface, do not ride into the water. Turn around, wait somewhere safe and dry, or take another route. The United States National Weather Service's "Turn Around, Don't Drown" guidance says never to drive through flooded roadways, because you do not know the condition of the road under the water, that six inches of moving water can make a person fall, that six inches of water reaches the bottom of most passenger cars and can cause loss of control or stalling, that a foot of water will float many vehicles and that two feet of rushing water can carry away most vehicles. A scooter is lighter and lower than a car, so those figures are not safe depths for you.

Law 36/2024/QH15 also requires operators to keep their speed suited to road and weather conditions and to slow down or stop in rain, on slippery surfaces and where there are warning signs or barriers (Article 12).

This guide explains why floodwater is more dangerous than it looks, a simple go or no-go test, what to do instead, how to cope if you are already in water, what to check after, how electric bikes differ and what to tell the shop. Our guides to [riding a manual motorbike through standing water](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-a-manual-motorbike-through-standing-water/) and [what to do if an electric vehicle gets wet in heavy rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-to-do-if-an-electric-vehicle-gets-wet-in-heavy-rain/) cover the technical side. This guide is general information based on the law and published flood-safety guidance as of October 2026, not an engineering opinion or legal advice.

## Why a flooded road is more dangerous than it looks

- **You cannot see the surface.** Murky water hides potholes, open drains, missing manhole covers, kerbs, debris and washed-out sections. The National Weather Service warns that road beds may be washed out beneath floodwater. Our guide to [dealing with potholes on a rental motorbike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/dealing-with-potholes-on-a-rental-motorbike/) explains how a hidden hole can throw a small wheel.

- **You cannot judge the depth.** National Weather Service flood-safety material says it is impossible to know how deep the water is just by looking at it.

- **Moving water pushes.** Six inches of fast-moving water can knock a person over, and a foot can carry away a small car, according to National Weather Service material. A scooter, with two small wheels, is easier to push.

- **Traction disappears.** Wet tyres, oil, mud and floating debris leave little grip, and brakes may not work well once wet.

- **The bike can stall.** Water can enter the air intake or exhaust, or reach electrical parts. Our guide to riding a manual motorbike through standing water explains the risk.

- **Other vehicles make waves** that push water onto your bike and hide the road.

- **Floodwater is dirty.** Public-health guidance warns that floodwater can contain sharp objects, debris, oil, fuel and sewage.

- **Night makes it worse.** The National Weather Service says to be especially cautious at night, when flood dangers are harder to recognise.

- **Other hazards come with it:** lightning, falling branches and live electrical equipment. Our guide to [what to do during a sudden Hanoi thunderstorm](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-to-do-during-a-sudden-hanoi-thunderstorm/) explains where to shelter.

More than half of flood deaths in the United States are vehicle-related, according to the National Weather Service. Hanoi's conditions differ, but the physics of water does not.

## A simple go or no-go test

Ask these questions in order. If the answer to any of them is "no" or "I do not know", do not go.

| Question | If the answer is no or unsure |
|---|---|
| Can I see the road surface under the water, all the way across? | Turn around |
| Is the water clearly shallow, well below the bottom of my wheels and the footboard? | Turn around |
| Is the water still, not flowing? | Turn around |
| Do I know this road, and is it free of holes, drains and debris? | Turn around |
| Is there a barrier, a sign or an officer telling me to stop? | Obey it and turn around |
| Is it daylight and is visibility good? | Turn around |
| Can I cross without stopping, and is the far side clear? | Turn around |
| Do I have to cross now, with no other way? | Wait, or take another route |

The National Weather Service adds a rule for barriers: never drive around barricades into floodwaters. Under Article 11 of the Law, temporary signs and officers' commands outrank permanent signs and markings, so a barrier or an officer's instruction at a flooded road is binding, and moving a barrier is also risky because Article 9 bans damaging traffic control facilities.

The test is deliberately strict. A short puddle you can see through is one thing, covered by our guide to riding through standing water, and a flooded road is another.

## What to do instead

1. **Stop in a safe, dry place.** Choose a covered, solid, well-lit place off the carriageway, away from trees and tall objects if there is lightning. Do not stop under a bridge where you block a lane, and do not stop in a low spot that could flood. Our guide to [riding a semi-automatic bike in heavy Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-a-semi-automatic-bike-in-heavy-hanoi-rain/) explains when to wait.

2. **Wait.** Many Hanoi downpours pass within an hour or so, although low streets may take longer to drain. Check the forecast. Our guide to [rainy-season routes from Hanoi: when to postpone](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rainy-season-routes-from-hanoi-when-to-postpone/) helps you decide.

3. **Choose a higher route.** If your map shows an alternative on higher ground, take it, but check that it is open to motorbikes. Our guide to [how to check which roads restrict motorbikes](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-check-which-roads-restrict-motorbikes/) explains how.

4. **Avoid underpasses, dips and low junctions.** They fill first. Treat an underpass full of water as a no-go.

5. **Use a taxi, a ride-hail car or a bus,** and leave the bike parked on high ground.

6. **Call the shop.** Nguyen Ha can be reached on 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969), open 09:00 to 21:00 daily. Ask what it advises, and whether the bike can wait. Our guide to [Hanoi rainy-day sightseeing: when to leave the bike parked](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-rainy-day-sightseeing-when-to-leave-the-bike-parked/) explains how to spend the day without it.

7. **Park above the flood line.** If you leave the bike, choose a place that will not flood. Our guide to [parking a motorbike in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-motorbike-in-hanoi/) explains how guarded lots work. Do not leave it in a basement car park or a low yard if heavy rain is forecast.

If the rain is heavy and the road ahead looks risky, stopping early and calling the shop is always a sound decision. A late return has a cost, as the shop's published terms explain, but it is small compared with a crash or a flooded engine. Our guide to [how to avoid missing your agreed return time](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-avoid-missing-your-agreed-return-time/) explains the options.

## If you are already in the water

1. **Stay calm and stop riding forward.** Do not accelerate or turn sharply.

2. **Look for the nearest higher ground,** and assess whether the water is rising or moving.

3. **Put your feet down carefully,** feeling for the bottom, and ease the bike out of the water by walking it with the engine off, only if the water is shallow and still, and you can see where you are going.

4. **If the water is rising or moving, leave the bike and get yourself to safety.** The National Weather Service advises that if floodwaters rise around a vehicle, you should abandon it and move to higher ground if you can do so safely, since the vehicle and you can be swept away quickly. A scooter can be replaced, and you cannot.

5. **Do not touch electrical equipment** such as fallen cables or street-lighting boxes, and stay out of water near them.

6. **Do not keep restarting a stalled engine.** Ask the shop what to do, since repeated attempts may damage it. Our guide to riding a manual motorbike through standing water covers what to do if the engine dies.

7. **Call for help** if anyone is in danger. Call 115 for an ambulance or 112, and see our guide to [emergency numbers and accident reporting in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/emergency-numbers-and-accident-reporting-in-vietnam/). Article 18 also says that a vehicle stopped by a technical fault on part of the carriageway should show an emergency light signal or a warning sign behind it.

8. **Do not wade into moving water to rescue the bike.**

## After you have been through water

Even if you crossed safely, the bike has been through water.

- **Check the brakes.** A Honda owner's manual for a car advises applying the brakes moderately after driving through deep water to see whether they feel normal, and notes that a longer distance is needed to stop with wet brakes. The principle applies to a scooter: test the brakes gently, at low speed, in a safe place, and allow extra distance. Our guide to [what to check if a scooter brake squeals](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-to-check-if-a-scooter-brake-squeals/) explains what to look for.

- **Check the lights and indicators.** Our guide to [checking motorbike brake lights and indicators daily](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/checking-motorbike-brake-lights-and-indicators-daily/) shows how.

- **Listen and watch** for odd noises, smoke, a rough engine or warning lights. If you notice any, stop and call the shop.

- **Check the tyres** for cuts and pressure. Our guide to [what to do if your tyre loses air during a ride](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-to-do-if-your-tyre-loses-air-during-a-ride/) explains.

- **Do not wash electrics or force anything.** Leave cleaning to the shop.

- **Photograph the bike,** the place and the water level, with the time.

## A quick checklist

1. **If you cannot see the road under the water, turn around.**

2. **Do not drive around barricades, and obey officers.**

3. **Avoid underpasses, dips and low junctions.**

4. **Wait in a safe, dry place, or take a higher route or a taxi.**

5. **Never wade into moving water for the bike.**

6. **If the water rises, leave the bike and move to higher ground.**

7. **Afterwards, test the brakes gently, check the lights, and photograph the bike.**

8. **Call the shop, and call 115 or 112 if anyone is in danger.**

## FAQ: riding when a road floods

### Is it ever safe to ride through floodwater?

Only if you can see the road surface all the way across, the water is clearly shallow, still and well below your wheels, and you know the road. If there is any doubt, do not. Official flood guidance says never to drive through flooded roadways.

### How deep is too deep for a scooter?

There is no safe depth to rely on. National Weather Service figures for cars and people, such as six inches of moving water knocking a person over, are upper limits for much heavier vehicles. A scooter is lighter and lower.

### What if I have to get home?

Wait for the water to drop, take a higher route, or take a taxi or a ride-hail car. Leave the bike in a safe place and tell the shop.

### Can I ride around a barrier?

No. Never drive around barricades into floodwater. Under Article 11 of Law 36/2024/QH15, an officer's command or a temporary sign outranks permanent signs, and a barrier or an officer's instruction at a flooded road is binding.

### What should I do if my engine stalls in water?

Get yourself to safety first. Do not keep restarting it, and call the shop.

### Who pays if the bike is water-damaged?

The shop's published terms do not describe flood damage specifically. Ask before you rent in the rainy season, and report any incident at once.
