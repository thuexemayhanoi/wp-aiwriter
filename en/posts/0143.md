Check a scooter's horn and turn signals before you leave the shop by switching the ignition on, flicking the signal switch left and then right while someone watches all four lamps, listening for a steady click at an even rate, pressing the switch to cancel, and then giving the horn one short tap. Each signal should flash at the front and rear on its own side, the dashboard indicator should blink with it and the horn should sound clear and stop the moment you release the button. The check takes about two minutes and is part of Honda's own pre-ride inspection. This guide gives the steps, explains what faults look like, summarises the traffic rules on signalling and horn use as of October 2026 and tells you what to ask the shop.

## The two-minute check in order

Do this with a staff member beside you, the scooter on its stand or standing upright and the ignition on. If your scooter has a smart key, keep the key fob with you.

- **Find the controls.** On most scooters the turn signal switch and the horn button are on the left grip, but ask the shop to point them out and try them before you move off.
- **Switch on the left signal.** Ask the helper to confirm that the front left and rear left lamps both flash, and that the dashboard arrow blinks.
- **Cancel it.** Honda's manuals describe pressing the switch to turn the signal off. Confirm that it stops cleanly and does not stick.
- **Repeat on the right.**
- **Listen to the rhythm.** A healthy signal flashes at a steady, moderate rate. A much faster blink usually means a lamp has failed. A signal that does not blink at all points to a switch, flasher or fuse problem.
- **Switch on the brake light.** Squeeze each brake lever and ask the helper to confirm the rear lamp lights. Honda's riding guide lists indicators, the brake light, high beam and low beam together with the horn as the lighting and horn checks.
- **Tap the horn once.** Press for less than a second.
- **Switch everything off,** then film a few seconds of the signals flashing so that the condition at handover is recorded.

## Turn signals in detail

### Look at all four lamps

A scooter has two signals at the front and two at the back. The Honda Air Blade manual lists 12 V, 10 W bulbs in each of the four positions. Walk around the bike while the helper works the switch, or watch the glow on a shop window. Look for these faults:

- **A lamp that stays dark.** It may be a blown bulb or a loose connection.
- **A lamp that is dim or flickers.** Often a poor contact or a weak battery.
- **A cracked or missing lens,** or a lamp hanging on its wire.
- **The wrong colour.** The Air Blade manual says to use only amber bulbs, so a signal that does not show amber suggests an incorrect replacement.
- **A lamp that points oddly,** which suggests a drop.

In bright sun a signal is hard to see. If the helper cannot see it clearly from about five metres away, a rider behind will not either.

### The dashboard indicator

The indicator tells you that the signal is working and that you have left it on. Many scooters also give a different blink if a lamp has failed. If the indicator does not blink, or blinks very fast, report it.

### Cancelling

Honda's manuals describe pressing the switch to turn the signal off. Make sure it works smoothly and does not stick. A signal that stays on after you turn tells following riders that you are about to turn again, which is why checking cancellation matters as much as checking the flash.

### Bulbs are consumables

Honda's maintenance page says the bulb is an expendable part and should be replaced at a dealer when it fails. A renter should not change one. If a bulb is out, ask the shop to replace it or swap the bike.

### Smart key flashes are not faults

On scooters with a smart key, such as the Vision and Air Blade, the signals flash once and a buzzer sounds when the bike is locked, and the signals can flash during the anti-theft alarm. The manuals describe both. Do not mistake that single flash for a malfunction.

## Checking the horn

Press the button once, briefly. A healthy horn gives a firm, clear tone at once and stops the moment you release. Be alert to these problems:

- **No sound.** Check that the ignition is on, then report it.
- **A weak, buzzing or broken tone,** which a rider in front may not hear.
- **A horn that sticks or sounds continuously.** Release the button and report it at once.
- **A horn that works only sometimes,** suggesting a loose wire.

Do not hold the horn down to test it. Besides irritating people nearby, continuous horn use is itself discouraged by the traffic rules below. If you are in a quiet street or near a hospital, ask the helper to test it somewhere suitable, or tap it very briefly.

## What the traffic rules say, as of October 2026

Vietnam's Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety No. 36/2024/QH15, effective 1 January 2025, deals with both signals and the horn. This is a plain-language summary of the parts that affect a scooter rider, not legal advice.

- **Before turning (Article 15).** A rider must look, keep a safe distance from the vehicle behind, slow down and signal the direction of the turn, then move gradually into the lane nearest the turn. The signal must be used continuously throughout the turn.
- **Overtaking (Article 14).** The rider who overtakes signals the manoeuvre, and a request to pass is made by flashing the headlight or by horn. In towns and built-up areas between 22:00 and 05:00 the request may be made only by light.
- **Horn use (Article 21).** The horn may be used only to warn road users of a situation that could be unsafe, or to signal that you are about to overtake. It must not be used continuously, with an incorrect volume, or between 22:00 and 05:00 in residential areas and near medical facilities, except by priority vehicles.

Decree 168/2024/ND-CP sets the administrative penalties for breaches, and this guide does not list amounts. Our guide to [traffic penalties that catch foreign riders out](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/traffic-fines-vietnam-foreign-riders/) explains the wider picture. A scooter whose signals or horn do not work also makes it harder for you to follow these rules, which is another reason to have it fixed before you ride.

## Using signals and horn well in Hanoi

Honda's riding guide describes the sequence for turning at an intersection: decide early, check behind in the mirror and over the shoulder, switch on the signal, slow down and move toward the correct lane, then give way and turn. The signal comes before the movement, not during it. The same guide advises signalling before joining a larger road and before and after overtaking.

On the horn, Honda's guide recommends a short beep on bends so that oncoming traffic knows you are there, and a signal during an overtake. A short tap that says "I am here" is far more useful than a long blast. Our guide to [crossing Hanoi intersections](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/crossing-hanoi-intersections/) describes how traffic negotiates, and our night-riding guide explains why a working lamp set matters after dark.

After every turn, glance at the dashboard to make sure the signal has stopped. Our explanation of [dashboard lights](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/semi-automatic-motorbike-dashboard-lights-explained/) helps you recognise the indicators.

## Faults and what to do

| What you find | What it may mean | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| One lamp does not flash | Blown bulb or loose connection | Ask the shop to fix it or swap the bike |
| Signals blink much faster | A lamp has failed | Ask the shop to check all four |
| No blink at all | Switch, flasher or fuse | Do not ride; ask for another bike |
| Signal will not cancel | Switch fault | Report it before riding |
| Brake light does not light | Bulb or switch fault | Do not ride until fixed |
| Horn silent or weak | Wiring, button or horn fault | Ask the shop to fix it |
| Horn sounds continuously | Stuck button or wiring | Stop and call the shop |

Honda's riding guide says never to ride at night if the lights are faulty. ## FAQ: horn and turn signals

### Where is the horn on a scooter?

Usually a button on the left grip, near the signal switch. Ask the shop to show you before you ride.

### Why do my signals blink very fast?

A fast blink usually means one lamp has failed. Ask the shop to check all four.

### Do the signals cancel by themselves?

Honda's manuals describe pressing the switch to turn the signal off, so check after every turn and cancel it yourself.

### Why did the signals flash when I locked the scooter?

On smart key models it is a lock confirmation or alarm signal, not a fault.

### Can I use the horn at night?

In Vietnam the horn may not be used between 22:00 and 05:00 in residential areas or near medical facilities, as of October 2026. Use lights to signal an overtake.

### Can I replace a bulb myself?

Honda says to have bulbs replaced at a dealer. Report the fault to the shop instead.
