Yes. An electric bicycle is the one powered two-wheeler in Vietnam that needs no driving licence, no number plate and, in the text of the law, no minimum age, so a traveller who has never ridden a motorbike can legally rent one. You still have to wear a helmet, obey the traffic rules and cope with Hanoi's traffic, and that is the real question. This guide explains what counts as an electric bicycle under Law 36/2024/QH15, what it asks of you, who the vehicle suits, which routes are gentle enough for a first ride, what to test at handover and what to do if you decide a bicycle is still too much. The rules are as of October 2026, and the shop confirms the bike actually available when you book.

## What counts as an electric bicycle

Vietnam's Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety, which took effect on 1 January 2025, defines the motor-assisted bicycle, which includes the electric bicycle, as a bicycle with power from a motor that is cut when the rider stops pedalling or when the bicycle reaches 25 km/h. Traffic police in Ho Chi Minh City explain the practical signs on their official site: it has pedals like an ordinary bicycle, a small motor and a role that is mainly to help your pedalling.

That definition matters because many vehicles sold as "electric bikes" are really mopeds. A machine with a flat floor, a seat box, a throttle and no real pedals is not an electric bicycle, however slow it looks, and it falls into a different class with different rules. Our guide to [electric bikes in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/electric-bikes-in-hanoi-rules/) explains how to tell them apart. If you want the bicycle class, ask the shop whether the bike has working pedals and an assist that stops at 25 km/h.

## What the rules ask of you

The electric bicycle is classed with bicycles as a non-motorised vehicle, so it is not registered and has no plate. The Khanh Hoa provincial traffic police, quoted by Lao Dong newspaper in October 2025, stated that the law sets no age for riding one, that it has no plate or registration certificate, and that the rider must wear a standard helmet. Children under seven must be accompanied by an adult on the road.

Other points come from the law's rules for bicycles, as summarised by the Thu Vien Phap Luat portal.

- **Helmet.** Wear a helmet that meets the standard. Do not treat a slow electric bicycle as an exception.
- **Passengers.** The law limits how many people a bicycle may carry. As a first-time visitor, ride alone.
- **Single file.** Ride in a line and on the part of the road meant for slow vehicles where there is one.
- **Lights.** Between 18:00 and 06:00 a front and rear light or signal is required.
- **Phones and umbrellas.** No phone in your hand and no umbrella while riding.
- **Traffic signals.** The same red lights, one-way streets and signs apply as for everyone else.

The rule that matters for your peace of mind is that none of this involves a driving licence. If you have never ridden a motorbike, you are not breaking any licence rule by renting an electric bicycle. Our guide to [helmet rules in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/helmet-rules-in-vietnam/) explains what helmet to wear.

## Who an electric bicycle suits

An electric bicycle is a good fit if you:

- can ride an ordinary bicycle confidently, including braking, signalling and looking over your shoulder;
- want a vehicle that tops out near cycling speeds and is light enough to handle;
- plan short, daylight trips on quiet roads rather than long, fast ones;
- like the idea of exercise with some help on hills, bridges and headwinds;
- are travelling alone or with other riders who are on similar bikes.

It is a poor fit if you have not ridden a bicycle in years, dislike busy junctions or want to cover long distances at traffic speed. In that case a different plan, covered below, will serve you better than a bike you do not enjoy.

## What Hanoi traffic is really like for a cyclist

Hanoi's central traffic is dense but slow. One tour operator's safety guide says traffic in the centre rarely exceeds 30 to 40 km/h, and that this is what makes cycling there tolerable. Treat that as a general impression from a cycling-tour company, not a measurement. The useful habits are the same ones locals use:

- **Be predictable.** Hold a steady line and speed. Other riders flow around you, and sudden stops or swerves cause problems.
- **Look, then go.** Watch the flow at junctions and merge gradually. Our guide to [crossing Hanoi intersections](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/crossing-hanoi-intersections/) explains how the traffic really moves.
- **Keep to the right-hand edge** on streets where traffic is heavy, and give parked vehicles room for opening doors.
- **Use the bell and your voice,** briefly, and expect others to do the same.
- **Avoid the Old Quarter at peak times.** The lanes are narrow and the pedestrians many. Our guide to [riding in the Old Quarter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-old-quarter/) explains when to walk instead.

## Gentle first routes

Choose routes with wide, calm roads for the first day.

**West Lake.** The loop around West Lake is about 17 km, with the shore length quoted at nearly 18 km in a Vietnamese news report. A travel blogger who rode it in a counter-clockwise direction took about three hours with frequent stops and found that the loop sticks almost entirely to small, less-frequented roads, with a couple of spots that need care. Our guide to [Tay Ho by scooter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/tay-ho-west-lake-by-scooter/) describes the lakeside streets and cafes.

**The Red River side.** Long Bien and the riverbank make an easy first ride out of the centre, as our guide to [Long Bien and the Red River](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/long-bien-red-river-first-ride/) explains. The shop itself is in Long Bien District, on the east side of the river.

**The east bank villages.** Our guide to [Gia Lam and the east bank](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/gia-lam-east-bank-bat-trang-by-scooter/) describes quiet roads and craft villages.

**Hoan Kiem Lake on weekends.** The area around the lake has a weekend walking street, and the tourism board describes motorbikes and cars as diverted from it. Rules for bicycles there are not stated in the source I read, so follow the signs and staff.

Avoid expressways, ring-road stretches built for fast traffic and the roads out to the mountains. Our guide to [ring roads and highways](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-ring-roads-highways-motorbikes-allowed/) explains which roads are for motorbikes.

## A handover test for a first-time rider

Take ten minutes at the shop, with staff beside you.

1. **Confirm the class.** Do the pedals work, and does the assist stop at about 25 km/h?
2. **Check the brakes.** Squeeze both levers, and roll the bike a few metres to feel that both wheels bite.
3. **Test the lights and bell.** You will want them if you ride past dusk.
4. **Adjust the saddle** so your foot reaches the ground at a stop.
5. **Learn the assist levels.** Start on the lowest and move up gradually.
6. **Ride a short loop** in the quiet lane or yard before you join a road.
7. **Check the battery level** and agree what level it should come back with.
8. **Ask about locking.** The shop does not provide an extra lock, so ask how to lock the bike and where to park.

## If you decide a bicycle is still too much

Not riding is a legitimate choice. Options for a traveller who prefers not to ride:

- **Walk,** which is the best way to see the Old Quarter and the lakeside.
- **Ride-hailing and taxis** for longer hops.
- **Public buses.** A VOV report on the low-emission zone launch says the city will offer free public buses inside Ring Road 1 through 30 June 2027, with four transfer hubs.
- **A cyclo** for a slow ride around the old streets.
- **A guided cycling tour,** where a leader sets the pace and route.

Compare these before you commit to a week-long rental you may not use. The [electric scooters page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/electric-scooters/) describes the other vehicles in the electric group, and you can ask Nguyen Ha on 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969), open 09:00 to 21:00 daily.

## FAQ: electric bicycles for non-riders

### Do I need a licence to ride an electric bicycle in Vietnam?

No. A motor-assisted bicycle that cuts out at 25 km/h is treated with bicycles, without a licence or plate.

### Do I need a helmet?

Yes. The Khanh Hoa traffic police say the rider must wear a standard helmet.

### Is there a minimum age?

The law sets none for an electric bicycle, but children under seven must be accompanied by an adult on the road. A shop may set its own conditions.

### Can I ride one if I have never ridden a motorbike?

Legally yes. Practically, you should be comfortable on an ordinary bicycle and start on quiet roads.

### Is an electric scooter the same thing?

No. A scooter without working pedals is a moped or motorcycle and has different rules. Ask the shop which class the bike is.
