On a 50cc bike, a large Hanoi intersection is a place to slow down early, stop behind the line, start gently, keep to the right and never try to beat a yellow light. A 50cc moped has the weakest acceleration in the traffic stream and is held to a maximum operating speed of 40 km/h, so the margin that a larger scooter uses to scramble out of a bad position is not available to you. What you do have is time: if you arrive at a wide junction slowly, with a plan, you can let the faster traffic pass, choose your gap and cross without hurry. This guide covers the specific problems a small-engine rider faces at big intersections, from starting at the red light to turns, signals and positioning. It reflects Vietnamese traffic rules as of October 2026, and for the general flow of traffic our guide to [crossing Hanoi's intersections](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/crossing-hanoi-intersections/) is the companion read.

## Why a 50cc is different at a big junction

A large intersection has a lot of traffic, more lanes and more distance to cross. Three features of a 50cc bike make that harder.

First, acceleration. Honda's published figures for its Japanese-market Super Cub 50 give a power output of 2.7 kW, about 3.7 PS. That is enough to move a light bike at city pace, but not to launch it ahead of cars and larger scooters when the lights go green.

Second, speed. Under Circular 38/2024/TT-BGTVT, mopeds and similar vehicles have a maximum operating speed of 40 km/h. On a wide junction, a larger scooter may cross at 40 or 50 km/h; you cannot, and the crossing takes longer.

Third, braking. A small bike with simple brakes needs a longer distance to stop than most riders expect, especially in the wet. Our guide to [riding in Hanoi's rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) explains how wet roads change that.

The right response is not to be afraid, but to ride in a way that does not need acceleration or sudden braking: early, smooth and predictable.

## Approaching: look at the whole junction early

Start reading the junction from 100 m or more away.

- **Check the light phase.** Many Hanoi signals have countdown timers, which the law allows. If the green has just started, you can plan to stop for the next red. If the green has been running for some time, slow down now, because you cannot expect to cross before it changes.
- **Look at the lanes.** Which lane suits your direction? On a wide junction, lanes may be marked for straight, left or right movements, and a slow bike should be in the correct lane well before the junction.
- **Note what is around you.** Buses, trucks and fast scooters will pass you. Let them.
- **Choose your lane position.** A 50cc is normally best kept to the right of the traffic stream, in line with the rule that slower vehicles keep to the right. Article 13 of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (Law 36/2024/QH15) says vehicles moving at a slower speed must stay on the right in their direction of travel.

Do not accelerate to catch a green light you cannot reach. Ease off and let the next cycle come.

## The red light: where to stop and how to start

When the light is red, you must stop. The Law's traffic-light rules say a red light means stop, and a yellow light means stop before the stop line unless you are already on or past the line when it changes. Stopping at the line also protects you: it keeps you clear of crossing traffic and visible to the vehicles behind you.

How to stop:

1. **Slow gradually** with both brakes, keeping the bike upright.
2. **Stop behind the line,** not on the zebra crossing. Pedestrians need it.
3. **Stay to the right** of the lane if you are slower than the others, so the faster bikes can fill the space to your left.
4. **Put a foot down and keep the bike in a ready position.** On a Cub, select first gear; on an automatic, hold the brake until the light changes.
5. **Watch the cross traffic and the light on the other side,** not only your own.

How to start:

- **Wait for the green and check.** Look left and right for vehicles still crossing. Many riders enter on the last second of the previous phase.
- **Pull away smoothly.** Do not twist the throttle hard. On a small engine, that only makes the bike hesitate.
- **Expect to be passed.** That is normal. Hold your line and let others go by.
- **Do not stop in the middle of the junction.** If you stall, get to the side as soon as you can and restart.

## Yellow lights: do not race them

A 50cc bike cannot outrun a yellow, and this is where small-engine riders get into trouble. The Law's rule is that a yellow light means stop before the stop line, with an allowance if you are already on or past the line. If you are close to the line at moderate speed, you may be unable to stop in time; if you are far away, stop. The aim is to avoid being in the middle of the junction when the light changes, because the cross traffic will start at full speed.

If you are in the junction when the light turns red, keep going steadily and clear the intersection, rather than stopping in the middle. This is the least risky choice at that moment, but it is the result of a poor decision earlier. The cure is to slow down sooner.

Running a red light carries a heavy fine, and licence points for those who hold a licence, under Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, in force since 1 January 2025, and traffic police and cameras both monitor major Hanoi junctions. VietnamPlus reported in January 2025 that Hanoi Traffic Police said camera-based fines come with video evidence of the violation. A moped needs no licence, but the fine applies all the same.

## Right turns, including on red

Turning right at a large junction is usually easier than turning left, but not always simpler. Article 15 of the Law says you must observe, keep a safe distance, slow down, signal, move towards the nearest lane in the turning direction, and give way to pedestrians and cyclists. At wide junctions, pedestrians may be crossing the side street you turn into.

A right turn on red is not generally permitted. Vietnam's Traffic Police Department explained in January 2025, as reported by Viet Nam News, that when the light is red drivers must not proceed, except where a green arrow light or a supplementary sign allows a right turn on red, or where traffic police on duty direct it. Even then, they advise giving way to pedestrians. A moped is not exempt. If there is no sign and no arrow, wait for the green. Our companion article on [crossing Hanoi's intersections](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/crossing-hanoi-intersections/) explains the order in which signals and officers are obeyed.

Some junctions have a separate right-turn lane or an island. If you are in a lane reserved for right turns and the light is red, check the markings and any signs before you go.

## Left turns across a wide junction

Left turns are harder for a slow bike because you cross the path of oncoming traffic and your acceleration is poor. Choose a method that does not need speed.

- **Use the signal phase if there is one.** A green left-turn arrow or a protected phase makes the turn simple.
- **At a normal green, wait for a gap.** Move to the left side of the lane well ahead of the junction, signal, and enter only when the oncoming stream has a clear gap. A slow bike needs a bigger gap than a fast one.
- **Do not cut the corner.** Keep to your own side of the road through the turn.
- **If the junction is very large and busy, consider turning later.** Go straight through, find a safer place to stop, turn around legally, and cross at a place where the lights give you a clear phase. Do not turn illegally to save time.
- **Know when to walk.** Pushing the bike across a very wide crossing with the pedestrian signal is slower but safe if you are uncomfortable.

Article 22 of the Law covers giving way at intersections: a vehicle entering from a non-priority road gives way to vehicles on the priority road, and where there is no roundabout sign, vehicles give way to those entering from the right. Where a sign indicates a roundabout, vehicles give way to those entering from the left.

## Where not to stop, and the 5 m rule

Do not stop or park near an intersection. Article 18 of the Law forbids stopping or parking at an intersection and within 5 m of its edge, among other places. This matters for errands as well as for emergencies. If you need to stop to check your phone, to wait for someone or to let a passenger off, go beyond the junction to a legal spot. ## Visibility, horns and signals

At a big intersection, you are easy to miss. A 50cc bike is small and often partly hidden behind larger vehicles.

- **Signal early** for every turn, and keep the signal on throughout.
- **Use your lights.** Daylight use of the low beam helps other riders notice you.
- **Wear bright clothing** if you can.
- **Stay out of blind spots.** Do not ride alongside the rear wheels of a bus or truck, whose drivers cannot see you.
- **Keep the horn for warning.** The Law permits it only to warn others of a danger or to signal an overtake, and not repeatedly.

## Planning a route to avoid the worst junctions

You do not have to cross the largest junctions on a 50cc. A route with smaller streets and fewer big crossings is easier and often just as fast at a 40 km/h limit.

- **Look at the route before you go.** On a map, count the big crossings.
- **Use quiet parallel streets** where you can.
- **Avoid rush hours,** typically the morning and late afternoon.
- **Choose a bridge or ramp route with care.** Slow bikes struggle on steep ramps.

## A short checklist for each big junction

1. Read the light and the lanes from far away.
2. Slow down early and stay to the right.
3. Stop behind the line, never on the crossing.
4. Wait for green, check for late traffic, and pull away smoothly.
5. Do not race a yellow.
6. Turn right on red only if a sign, arrow or officer allows it.
7. For left turns, use the signal phase or wait for a bigger gap.
8. Do not stop within 5 m of the junction.
9. Signal early and be visible.
10. If in doubt, take a different route.

## FAQ: 50cc bikes and big intersections

### Is a 50cc bike too slow for Hanoi's big intersections?

It is slow, but manageable if you ride early and calmly. Do not try to beat lights and let faster vehicles pass.

### Can I turn right on a red light on a 50cc?

Only where a sign, a green arrow or a traffic officer allows it. A moped has no exemption.

### What should I do if the light turns yellow as I approach?

Stop before the line if you can do so safely. A 50cc cannot accelerate through, so do not race the light.

### Where should I stop at a red light?

Behind the stop line, not on the pedestrian crossing, and to the right of the lane if you are slower than the traffic around you.

### Do I need a licence for a 50cc?

A true moped, 50 cc or less with a design speed of 50 km/h or less, needs no licence, but the traffic rules still apply. Our guide to [renting a 50cc scooter in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/renting-a-50cc-scooter-in-hanoi/) explains who can ride one.
