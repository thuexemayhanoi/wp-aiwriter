Hanoi is turning electric faster than most visitors realise. VinFast scooters hum past on every street, the city's ride-hailing drivers increasingly ride them, and electric bicycles carry half the secondary-school population to class. Renting one is possible and, for the right rider and the right trip, a pleasure: no fuel stops, no noise, no clutch and no gears. It also raises questions that petrol scooters do not: how far a charge really goes in traffic, where and how you charge in a hotel room, which models need a licence and which do not, and whether an electric bike is a sensible choice for a day out of the city. This guide answers them with the law in force since 1 January 2025 and the terms Nguyen Ha publishes as of October 2026.

## Three kinds of electric two-wheeler

Vietnamese law and the rental market both sort electric two-wheelers into three groups, and the group decides the licence you need.

- **Electric bicycles** have pedals, a small motor and a low top speed. They are the slowest and lightest option, fine for a few kilometres around a neighbourhood, and they need no licence.
- **Electric mopeds** are scooters with a design speed of no more than 50 km/h and a motor of no more than 4 kW. Under Article 34 of Law 36/2024/QH15 they are mopeds like a petrol 50cc bike: anyone aged 16 or over may ride one, and no licence is required. Models limited to 49 km/h, such as VinFast's Evo200 Lite, were built to sit in this class.
- **Electric motorcycles** are anything faster or more powerful, such as the VinFast Evo200 with its 70 km/h top speed or the Feliz S at about 80 km/h. They are motorcycles in law and need a class A1 licence, or a foreign licence with an International Driving Permit that covers motorcycles, just like a petrol Honda Vision.

Nguyen Ha's electric group spans all three: electric bicycles, 50cc-equivalent electric scooters and some VinFast electric motorbikes. Because the licence situation changes from one model to the next, the shop explains each bike individually when you book; tell them whether you hold a motorcycle licence so that they reserve a model you may legally ride.

## Range: the headline figure and the real one

Manufacturers quote range under gentle conditions. VinFast's figure for the Evo200 is about 205 km on a full charge, measured at a steady 30 km/h with a 65 kg rider; the Feliz S is quoted at about 198 km under similar conditions. Those numbers are honest for what they measure, and they are not what you will see in Hanoi. Stop-start traffic, a passenger, a hot afternoon, a headwind on the Red River bridges and a heavy throttle hand all cut into them, and a battery that has been through two or three years of charging holds less than a new one.

Plan on well under the headline figure for a day of normal city riding with a passenger, and treat the battery gauge the way you would treat a fuel gauge on a bike with a small tank: look at it before you set off, know where you will charge, and turn back with a margin. For the way most visitors use a rental, twenty or thirty kilometres of errands and sightseeing in a day, even a conservative real-world range leaves plenty in hand. For a hundred-kilometre day trip it does not, which is why the Ninh Binh ride in our [route guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-to-ninh-binh-by-motorbike/) is a petrol-scooter trip.

## Charging: where, how long, what to ask

Electric scooters charge from an ordinary 220-volt wall socket through a charger the size of a laptop power brick. With the standard charger, a VinFast scooter takes roughly eight hours to go from a fifth full to full and about ten hours from empty, so the practical routine is to charge overnight, every night, wherever you sleep. Electric bicycles and smaller scooters charge faster, usually in a few hours.

Ask three questions at handover: where the charger is kept, how the battery is removed if it is removable (many VinFast batteries are, so that you can carry the battery to a socket rather than the bike), and what the shop expects the charge level to be when you return the bike. Nguyen Ha confirms the battery level and the charging arrangement at handover for every electric model, in the same way that fuel levels are noted for petrol bikes, so there is no surprise at the return.

Then ask your hotel. Most guesthouses in Hanoi are used to electric scooters and will point you to a socket in the lobby, the courtyard or the parking area; a few allow charging in the room. Charge where the staff tell you to, keep the charger and cable off beds and soft furnishings, and do not run a cable across a corridor. Public charging points exist but are not yet something to plan a day around.

## How an electric scooter rides

The first surprise is the silence. Pedestrians stepping off the kerb do not hear you coming, so use the horn the way local riders do, a short tap to announce yourself. The second is the instant pull from a standstill: an electric motor gives all its torque at once, which is useful at a green light and startling for the first few metres if you are heavy-handed. After that an electric scooter is an automatic scooter: twist-and-go, a brake on each side, a flat floor and a box under the seat. Weight is similar to a petrol scooter, handling is the same, and the weather is not a problem; Hanoi's electric scooters ride through the rainy season like everything else.

Hanoi's rules apply unchanged. A helmet is compulsory for rider and passenger on every electric two-wheeler with a motor, including electric bicycles. The speed limit for electric mopeds is 40 km/h; electric motorcycles follow the limits for motorcycles. The alcohol limit is zero for every rider. For the fines behind these rules, read our guides to [helmet rules](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/helmet-rules-in-vietnam/) and [licence rules for foreigners](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbike-licence-rules-for-foreigners-in-vietnam/).

## Prices at Nguyen Ha

Electric bikes are quoted individually by the day, because an electric bicycle, a 49 km/h scooter and a VinFast electric motorbike are not the same product. Weekly rentals are VND 600,000–800,000 and monthly rentals VND 1,500,000–1,800,000, which makes a month on an electric scooter cheaper than a month on most automatic petrol scooters, before you count the fuel you will not buy. The deposit, the 24-hour rental day, the delivery rules for weekly and monthly rentals and the late-return charges are the same as for petrol bikes and are listed on the [price guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/prices/).

## Who should rent electric, and who should not

Rent electric if your riding is inside the city and your days are short: a month of commuting across Long Bien, two weeks of sightseeing around Hoan Kiem and West Lake, a quiet bike for a nervous rider who wants no gears, no clutch and no fuel stops. Rent electric if you have no motorcycle licence and the shop can offer a 49 km/h model, which gives you a legal, modern scooter where a petrol 50cc bike is the only alternative.

Rent petrol if you plan day trips, if you will ride two-up all day, if your hotel cannot offer a socket, or if you simply do not want to think about charging on holiday. A Honda Vision refuels in two minutes anywhere in the country; an electric scooter needs a night. ## FAQ

### Do I need a licence to ride an electric scooter in Vietnam?

It depends on the model. An electric moped (design speed up to 50 km/h and a motor up to 4 kW) needs no licence and may be ridden from 16; an electric motorcycle above those limits needs class A1 or a foreign licence with an International Driving Permit covering motorcycles. Law 36/2024/QH15, effective 1 January 2025, draws the line in Article 34.

### How far does an electric scooter go on a charge in Hanoi?

Manufacturers quote around 200 km for the larger VinFast models under gentle test conditions. In city traffic with a passenger, plan on well under that, and charge every night.

### Can I charge at my hotel?

Usually yes, from a normal 220-volt socket in the lobby, courtyard or parking area. Ask the staff where, and allow about eight to ten hours for a full charge on a VinFast scooter.

### Do I need a helmet on an electric bicycle?

Yes. Vietnam's helmet rule covers every motorised two-wheeler, electric bicycles included, for the rider and the passenger.

### Is an electric scooter cheaper to rent?

By the month, yes: VND 1,500,000–1,800,000 at Nguyen Ha against VND 1,200,000–2,000,000 for petrol automatics, with no fuel to buy. Daily rates are quoted per model.
