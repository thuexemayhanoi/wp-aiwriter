Under Vietnamese law a tourist does not need an International Driving Permit to ride a genuine 50cc moped. As of October 2026, Law 36/2024/QH15 (in force since 1 January 2025) exempts moped riders from the licence requirement and asks only that the rider is at least 16, understands the traffic rules and can control the vehicle. The catch is the word "genuine": a bike that looks like a 50cc scooter but has a larger engine is a motorcycle, and for that an IDP and your home licence are required. The shop's own conditions, your travel insurance and the exact engine size on the registration papers also matter more than most visitors expect. This guide separates the legal answer from the practical one, walks through the situations travellers actually find themselves in and lists what to ask before you pay.

## The legal answer in plain terms

Vietnam's traffic law defines a moped, in Vietnamese xe gan may, as a two- or three-wheeled vehicle with a design speed of no more than 50 km/h and a petrol engine of no more than 50 cm3, or an electric motor of no more than 4 kW (Article 34). Article 56 then says that a driver must hold a licence valid for the vehicle being driven, except for moped riders, who must instead understand the traffic rules, have the skill to control the vehicle and meet the age and health conditions. Article 59 sets the minimum age for riding a moped at 16.

The licence classes in Article 57 start with A1, which covers motorcycles up to 125 cm3. There is no class for mopeds, because none is required. That is why published guidance for visitors says that no licence of any kind is needed for a moped: not a Vietnamese licence, not an IDP and not a home-country licence. This applies to foreign visitors in the same way as to Vietnamese riders.

Everything else in the traffic rules still applies. You must wear a helmet, obey signals and signs and stay under the moped speed ceiling of 40 km/h, which our guide to [speed limits for motorbikes in Vietnam, including mopeds](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/speed-limits-motorbikes-vietnam/) explains. Drinking and riding is prohibited whatever the size of the engine.

## Why the answer is not just "yes"

The law answers whether you may ride. Whether a particular shop will rent to you is a separate question, and several things sit between the two.

- **The shop's own policy.** A rental business can ask for more than the minimum the law requires, such as a passport, a deposit and, in many shops, a licence for any bike. Nguyen Ha's published practice is that identity documents and the licence are checked and photographed for the contract, and the originals are not kept. Whether the licence check changes for a genuine moped is for the shop to say, so ask when you book.
- **The bike itself.** The exemption covers only a vehicle that meets the moped definition. A badge on the fairing is not proof. The engine size on the registration papers is.
- **Your insurance.** Many travel policies attach their own conditions to motorbike riding, including a licence condition that does not follow Vietnamese law. Our guide to [travel insurance and motorbike rentals](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/travel-insurance-motorbike-rental-vietnam/) explains what is usually excluded.
- **Local practice.** Some commentary on the web calls the 50cc exemption a grey zone and warns that enforcement is tightening. We did not find an official source that removes the exemption, but it is a reason to carry your papers and to ask the shop how it handles the question.

## Four tourist situations

| Your situation | Can you ride a genuine 50cc moped? | Can you ride a 110 or 125 cc scooter? |
|---|---|---|
| No motorcycle licence at all | Yes, from age 16, if the shop agrees | No |
| A car licence only, with or without an IDP | Yes for a genuine moped, as a licence is not required | No, a car class does not cover a motorcycle |
| Home motorcycle licence plus a 1968 Vienna Convention IDP showing the motorcycle category | Yes | Generally yes, for classes up to 125 cm3, if the shop agrees |
| Home licence plus an IDP from a country that only issues the 1949 Geneva Convention type | Yes for a genuine moped | Not recognised for petrol bikes above 50cc, according to published guidance |

The last row matters because many visitors assume that any IDP will do. Vietnam's law recognises international permits issued under the 1968 Vienna Convention, which must be carried together with the national licence for the class being ridden (Article 57, paragraphs 6 and 8). UNECE explains that the 1968 permit is valid only alongside a domestic licence, while the 1949 model is a standalone document, and travellers holding a 1949-type permit are commonly told it is not accepted in Vietnam. Check which type you hold before you travel. Our guide to [licence rules for foreigners](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbike-licence-rules-for-foreigners-in-vietnam/) goes through the permits in detail.

## What "without an IDP" does not mean

A few misunderstandings are worth clearing up.

1. **It does not mean "without any documents".** You still need identity documents for the rental contract, and the bike should have its own papers with it.
2. **It does not mean any small scooter.** A 110 cc scooter described as "automatic and easy" is a motorcycle. An IDP and a licence are needed for it.
3. **It does not mean unlimited use.** A moped is slow, capped at 40 km/h and unsuited to expressway-style roads, which are closed to all motorbikes anyway.
4. **It does not settle the insurance question.** Read your policy's wording.
5. **It does not mean the age rule is relaxed.** The minimum is 16, and a rental shop may ask for a parent or guardian to sign for a renter under 18.

## What the shop is weighing up

A shop has its own reasons for asking about documents, and understanding them makes the conversation easier. The owner of a motorbike carries responsibility for who rides it. Decree 168/2024/ND-CP provides a heavy penalty for an owner who hands a motorbike to a person who is not entitled to ride it, which is one reason shops check licences and ages carefully, and why a shop that rents a 110 cc scooter to an unlicensed visitor is the party most exposed. Renting a genuine moped to a visitor who is 16 or older removes the licence issue, but not the questions of identity, deposit and who is responsible for damage.

That is also why the answer you get may differ from shop to shop. One may happily rent a moped on a passport and a deposit. Another may prefer to see a licence for every bike it hires out, as a matter of policy. Neither is acting unreasonably, and the sensible response is to ask early, ideally before you travel, instead of discovering the condition at the counter.

## FAQ: 50cc rental and the IDP

### Do I need an IDP to rent a 50cc scooter in Hanoi?

Under Law 36/2024/QH15 no licence or IDP is required to ride a moped, which is a vehicle of up to 50 cm3 and a design speed of up to 50 km/h. The shop may still have its own conditions, so ask before you book.

### Is a 125 cc scooter covered by the same rule?

No. A 125 cc scooter is a motorcycle. You need a licence valid for the class, and as a visitor that means a motorcycle licence carried with a 1968 Vienna Convention IDP, or a Vietnamese licence.

### Can I use a car IDP to ride a motorbike?

No. An IDP reflects the categories on your home licence, so a car-only permit does not cover a motorcycle. It can still be carried alongside a genuine moped rental, but it is not needed for the moped itself.

### Does a 50cc rental need a minimum age?

The law's minimum for a moped is 16. A shop can set a higher age or ask a parent or guardian to sign for a renter under 18, so check the shop's terms.

### Is it safe to rely on the 50cc exemption?

It is the law's wording, and we found no official source that has withdrawn it, but practice and insurance wording can differ. Carry your passport, keep the bike's papers with it and read your insurance policy before you ride.
