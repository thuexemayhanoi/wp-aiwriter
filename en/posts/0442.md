If new damage appears on a rented motorbike during your rental, photograph it as it is, note when and where you found it and tell the shop promptly with plain facts, whether you caused it, someone else did or you do not know how it happened. Do not repair it yourself, do not cover it up and do not guess about the cause. Nguyen Ha's published pages say to contact the shop if there is a mechanical issue, puncture, accident or unexpected damage, and not to assume the cost or the repair approach without checking the agreement, and they say that damage from a collision, an accident or misuse is priced by its cause, the actual condition of the bike and the contract, with the renter possibly bearing a cost in proportion to the real damage.

Reporting early keeps the facts clear and usually keeps the cost down. This guide covers the main situations, how to describe each, a message template, what to photograph, what the shop may do and how the cost is likely to be judged. It is general information, not legal advice.

## The situations

New damage can come from several sources, and the right description depends on which.

| Situation | Example | How to describe it |
|---|---|---|
| You caused it | A drop at a stop, a scrape on a wall while parking | "I dropped the bike at [place] at [time]; here is the damage" |
| A third party caused it | Another vehicle knocked the parked bike | "I came back to the bike at [time] and found this; it was parked at [place]" |
| Cause unknown | A scratch or crack you did not see before | "I noticed this at [time]; I do not know how it happened" |
| Vandalism or theft attempt | A forced lock, a cut seat, a broken mirror | "The bike was parked at [place]; I found this at [time]" |
| Gradual damage | A crack that grows, a leak that starts | "I noticed this on [date]; it is getting worse" |
| A mechanical or wear fault | A bent lever from age, a worn part | "This part has stopped working as it should" |

Do not label the cause unless you know it. "I do not know" is a good answer, and an honest one. Our guide to [how to report a mechanical problem to the rental shop](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-report-a-mechanical-problem-to-the-rental-shop/) covers faults that are not damage.

## Why prompt reporting matters

Damage reported at once is damage whose time and place are known. Damage found at return, with no earlier report, is damage that anyone can dispute. A prompt report does four things.

1. It gives the shop a chance to say whether the bike is safe to ride.

2. It records when you first noticed the damage.

3. It makes it easier to separate damage you caused from damage you did not.

4. It shows good faith, which matters if the cost has to be discussed.

The shop sees these bikes daily, and new marks are obvious to staff at return. Our guide to [what to do after dropping a rented semi-automatic bike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-to-do-after-dropping-a-rented-semi-automatic-bike/) explains why honesty at once is better than a surprise later.

## Step 1: check safety first

Before you report, check whether the bike is safe to ride. Look at the brakes, steering, wheels, tyres, lights and any fuel or oil leak. If any of these is affected, stop and do not ride. If anyone is hurt, deal with that first. Our guide to [what to do after a minor collision on a rental scooter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-to-do-after-a-minor-collision-on-a-rental-scooter/) covers a collision.

## Step 2: photograph and note

Take the same kind of photographs you took at pickup.

- **The whole bike,** with the plate number, so it is clear which bike it is.

- **The part that is damaged,** at medium range.

- **A close-up of the damage,** with a finger or coin for scale.

- **The surroundings,** especially if a third party caused it: the parking spot, any sign, any other vehicle.

- **Anything relevant,** such as paint on a wall, glass on the ground or a mark that matches another vehicle.

Note the time, the place, and what you were doing. Compare with your pickup photographs, and note whether the damage was there before. Our guide to [how to document existing scratches on a rental bike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-document-existing-scratches-on-a-rental-bike/) explains the three-photo method.

## Step 3: if a third party may be involved

If another person or vehicle caused the damage, or you think so, collect what you can.

- **Ask nearby people** and the car-park attendant whether they saw it.

- **Ask about cameras,** at the car park, shops or cafes nearby, and tell the shop and the police if footage exists. Do not take the footage yourself without permission.

- **Note the other vehicle's plate number,** if known.

- **Do not confront anyone.**

- **Ask the shop** whether it wants a police report. If the damage looks like vandalism or an attempted theft, a report may be sensible. The Ministry of Public Security's guidance says a report may be made orally or in writing, in person at the commune or ward police or at a police station, and that the person who reports has the right to be told the result. Our guide to [what to do if your rental motorbike is stolen](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-to-do-if-your-rental-motorbike-is-stolen/) explains how to report.

If the bike was in a guarded car park, tell the attendant and ask for their name and a note of what they saw. Our guide to [parking a motorbike in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-motorbike-in-hanoi/) explains guarded lots.

## Step 4: tell the shop

Call or message Nguyen Ha on 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969), open 09:00 to 21:00 daily. For a record, send a message with photographs even if you also call. Say:

- your name and the plate number;

- what you found, where and when;

- how it happened, if you know, or that you do not know;

- whether the bike is safe to ride;

- what you have done so far;

- what you would like to happen next.

A template: "Hello, this is [name], renting the Honda Wave, plate [number]. At [time] today I found [describe the damage] on the [part]. The bike had been parked at [place]. I do not know how it happened [or: I dropped it at X]. I have attached photographs. The bike [is / is not] safe to ride. Could you advise?"

Keep it factual and brief. Our guide to [how to keep receipts for repairs on a rental motorbike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-keep-receipts-for-repairs-on-a-rental-motorbike/) explains how to keep a record of what follows.

## Step 5: do not repair it yourself

Do not fix, repaint, glue or replace a damaged part yourself or through a stranger, unless the shop has agreed.

- **A poor repair can make the damage worse** or hide it, which can look like concealment.

- **The contract usually requires** you to use the bike as agreed and not to alter it.

- **The Civil Code** says a lessee may repair at reasonable cost only in specific cases, such as after notifying a lessor who does not act on a defect (Article 477), and the lessee must tell the lessor.

If you think a repair is needed urgently, tell the shop, ask for its agreement and keep every receipt.

## What the shop may do

Depending on the damage, the shop may:

- ask you to bring the bike in, or collect it;

- assess the damage on return, against the handover record;

- repair it itself or through a workshop;

- agree a cost with you, or deduct it from the deposit;

- ask for more information, such as the police report or witness details.

The shop's own pages say that damage from a collision, an accident or misuse is priced by its cause, the actual condition of the bike and the contract, and that the renter may bear a cost in proportion to the real damage. That wording points to an assessment based on facts, not a flat charge, which is another reason to keep clear records.

## Who pays: the general picture

Vietnam's Civil Code (Law No. 91/2015/QH13), in force since 1 January 2017, says the lessee must preserve and maintain the leased property and compensate if it is lost or damaged, and is not responsible for natural wear (Article 479). It says the lessee must return the property in the condition received, apart from natural wear (Article 482). We read the lease articles in the text published by the Tuyen Quang Department of Justice. As of October 2026, the general position is:

- **Damage you caused** is normally yours to bear, in proportion to the real damage, as the contract provides.

- **Damage a third party caused** is a matter between you, the shop and the third party. Ask the shop how it handles it, and whether a police report or the other party's details are needed. Do not assume you are excused.

- **Damage of unknown cause** is the hardest. A prompt report, a comparison with the pickup photographs and the circumstances, such as a guarded car park, help.

- **Wear or a defect** is not the renter's cost. Our guide to [what counts as normal wear on a rented Honda Wave](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-counts-as-normal-wear-on-a-rented-honda-wave/) explains the difference.

The contract decides the detail, and our explanation of [what the clauses in a Hanoi rental contract mean](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-motorbike-rental-contract-explained/) covers the damage clause. Our guide to [what to ask about damage liability before renting](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-to-ask-about-damage-liability-before-renting/) lists the questions to ask before you sign. This is general information, not legal advice.

## Insurance

The compulsory insurance on the bike covers third parties, not damage to the rental bike. Your travel insurance rarely covers hired property. So the cost of damage to the bike is normally dealt with through the contract and the deposit. ## A quick checklist

1. **Check safety first.**

2. **Photograph the whole bike, the damage and the surroundings.**

3. **Note the time, place and what happened, or that you do not know.**

4. **Report to the shop promptly, with photographs.**

5. **Collect witness and camera details if a third party may be involved.**

6. **Do not repair or hide the damage.**

7. **Keep every message and receipt.**

8. **Mention it again at return.**

## FAQ: reporting damage during a rental

### What if I do not know how the damage happened?

Say so. Report it promptly with photographs and the circumstances, such as where the bike was parked.

### Should I report small damage?

Yes. Small damage is obvious to staff at return, and a prompt report is better than a surprise.

### Who pays for damage caused by someone else?

It depends on the contract and the facts. Ask the shop how it handles third-party damage, and provide any witness or camera details.

### Should I call the police?

If it looks like vandalism, an attempted theft or a collision, ask the shop and consider a report. Ask for a record or reference.

### Can I get it repaired myself?

Only with the shop's agreement. Do not alter the bike or hide the damage.

### Will the cost come out of my deposit?

The deposit is held against damage, loss and unpaid charges, and is reconciled on return according to the recorded condition. Ask for any deduction to be itemised.
