Before you sign a monthly electric bike rental, get answers to six things: what state the battery is in today, where and how you will charge it, who pays for electricity and for a lost or damaged charger, what happens if the bike or battery fails mid-month, how the deposit and late return work, and what exactly the return inspection will check. A month is long enough for a tired battery, a frayed charging cable or a charging problem at your accommodation to become your daily problem, so it is worth settling these points on day one rather than on day twenty.

This guide gives you the questions in a sensible order, explains why each one matters, and ends with a one-page list you can take to the shop. It is general information, not a contract template, and the shop's own written terms always come first.

## Why a monthly electric rental needs more questions than a petrol one

A petrol scooter fails in familiar ways, and a petrol station is never far away. An electric bike or scooter adds a battery whose condition you cannot see, a charger you must keep safe, and a daily routine of finding a socket. Over a week you can often work around that. Over a month you cannot, because every weakness repeats thirty times.

## Questions about the bike and its battery

The battery is the part that ages, so begin there.

- **Which model and which battery is this?** Ask for the model name and whether the battery is removable or fixed in the bike. It changes where and how you can charge it.

- **How old is the battery, and has it been replaced?** A shop may not know the exact age, but a straight answer tells you something. Lithium batteries lose capacity gradually with use. VinFast's own FAQ for its e-scooters, written about its Klara S, Impes and Ludo models rather than the shop's rental bikes, says a tested battery kept about 70 percent of its capacity after 1,000 charge and discharge cycles.

- **What does the display show when the battery is full, and how far does the shop expect it to go?** A shop's honest estimate is more useful than a brochure figure. Our guide to [charging an electric scooter in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/charging-an-electric-scooter-in-hanoi/) explains why real-world distance depends on speed, load and traffic.

- **What was the battery level when the bike was handed over?** The shop confirms the battery level and charging arrangements at handover for electric bikes. Ask for that level to be written on the handover sheet and take a photograph of the display.

- **Does the bike have any known faults?** Warning lights, a rattling battery cover, a stiff throttle or weak brakes should be noted before you ride away, not discovered in week three.

## Questions about charging

Charging is the biggest practical difference between a petrol and an electric month.

1. **Which charger comes with the bike, and is it the original one?** Fire services and battery guidance agree on one rule: use the charger supplied with the battery or one the manufacturer approves. The GOV.UK battery safety guidance for e-scooter users says to use only the charger supplied or an authorised replacement.

2. **Where will you charge?** Ask your hotel, hostel or landlord first. Some Hanoi apartment buildings and boarding houses have restricted electric vehicle charging, and VnExpress reported in December 2025 that without clear national rules, buildings set their own. Never assume you can plug in at your accommodation.

3. **Can the battery be taken indoors, or must the bike stay at a charging point?** Some batteries lift out, others do not.

4. **How long is a full charge, and what should you do about night-time?** Fire services such as London Fire Brigade advise never leaving a battery charging while you sleep, and that has real consequences for your routine, so ask the shop directly what it recommends for your bike.

5. **Are there charging points or swap stations near where you live?** Our [charging guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/charging-an-electric-scooter-in-hanoi/) covers the main options.

## Questions about faults and replacement

A month gives problems time to appear. Ask in advance:

| Situation | What to ask |
|---|---|
| The bike will not power on | Who do you call, and how quickly can the shop help or swap the bike? |
| The battery drains much faster than at pickup | Will the shop test it or replace it, and is a replacement included? |
| A puncture, brake problem or warning light | Who pays and who repairs it? |
| The charger stops working | Is a spare available, and what does it cost? |
| Rain or flooding | What does the shop say about water and charging afterwards? |

## Before you sign: a short checklist

- Ask for the model, battery type and removable or fixed status.
- Photograph the display at full charge and the whole bike, including the charger and cable.
- Record the battery level on the handover sheet.
- Confirm the monthly amount, the deposit and when each is due.
- Ask who pays for electricity and for a lost or broken charger.
- Ask what to do and whom to call if the bike or battery fails.
- Confirm the return date, return time and late return rule.
- Read the contract clauses on batteries and damage. Our guide to [Hanoi rental contract clauses](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-motorbike-rental-contract-explained/) explains the usual wording.
- Ask where you may charge at your accommodation, and get permission if you need it.

## Red flags that justify walking away

Be cautious if the shop cannot say what charger belongs to the bike, offers a loose or mismatched charger, will not let you photograph the display, avoids saying what a replacement battery or charger costs, or cannot tell you where you may charge. None of these proves a problem, but together they suggest nobody has thought about the month ahead. It is better to say no politely on day one than to renegotiate on day ten.

## At the end of the month

Before returning the bike, charge it to the level agreed, wipe it down, collect the charger and cable, and photograph the bike and display again. Our guide to [returning your rental motorbike in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/returning-your-rental-motorbike-hanoi/) lists what the return check normally covers. For an electric bike, also ask how the shop will treat normal battery wear, because a battery that holds slightly less than at pickup after a month of use is ordinary ageing, not damage.

## FAQ: electric bike monthly rental

### Is a monthly electric rental cheaper than a petrol one?

The shop's electric monthly range is VND 1,500,000 to 1,800,000, while petrol manual bikes are listed at VND 1,000,000 to 1,200,000 and automatics at VND 1,200,000 to 2,000,000. Electricity costs and charging convenience belong in the comparison.

### Can I charge the bike at my hotel or apartment?

Only if the property allows it. Ask before you rent, because some buildings restrict electric vehicle charging.

### Who pays if the battery wears out during the month?

Ask the shop and get the answer in writing. Normal ageing over a month is usually not the renter's fault, but the contract decides.

### Do I need to charge it every night?

That depends on distance, the bike and the battery. Ask the shop what it advises, and follow fire-service advice not to leave a battery charging while you sleep.

### Can the shop deliver an electric bike to me?

Delivery is for weekly and monthly agreements within 10 km of Hoan Kiem Lake, at VND 50,000 per trip or VND 100,000 both ways, with beyond 10 km agreed in advance. There is no airport service. Confirm availability with the shop.
