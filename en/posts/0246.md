Before you ride away on a rental electric bicycle, spend ten minutes at the counter checking six things in this order: the paperwork and photographs, the battery and charger, the display and assistance, the brakes, the tyres and wheels, and the frame, lights and fittings. Then take a slow test ride. An electric bike is an ordinary bicycle with a motor, a battery and a computer added, so you inspect the bicycle part exactly as you would any bike and then add the electrical checks. Anything that fails should be fixed or swapped before you leave, and anything minor should be written down and photographed so that the condition at handover is on record. This checklist is general guidance from bicycle-safety and e-bike sources, not a mechanical inspection or a substitute for the shop's own instructions.

## Before you touch the bike: set up the record

The handover is the best moment to protect yourself, because the shop and you are both looking at the same bike at the same time.

- **Ask for the contract and read the damage and breakdown clauses.** Our explanation of [what the clauses in a Hanoi rental contract mean](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-motorbike-rental-contract-explained/) shows what to look for.
- **Photograph the whole bike** from both sides, front and back, with the frame number or any identifying mark if visible.
- **Photograph existing marks:** scratches, dents, scuffed pedals, a cracked mudguard, a worn saddle.
- **Photograph the display** once it is switched on, showing the battery level.
- **Note the time and date** in the photo or in a message to the shop.

The shop's helmet is one basic helmet per rider, free of charge, so also try it on and adjust it now. Our guide to [choosing a helmet that fits](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/choosing-a-helmet-that-fits-for-a-manual-motorbike-journey/) applies to any rider.

## Step 1: battery and charger

For electric rentals the shop confirms battery level and charging at handover, so ask for exactly that: how full is it, how long does a charge last on the way you plan to ride, and where do you charge.

| Check | What good looks like | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Battery seated and locked | Sits firmly, does not rattle or shift when you press it | Mounting wear and a loose lock can cause cut-outs on bumps |
| Battery casing | No cracks, swelling, dents, burn marks or damaged terminals | Damage to a lithium battery is a safety issue, not a cosmetic one |
| Charge level | Shown on the battery or display and matches what the shop tells you | A full battery is the only way to judge the range |
| Charger | Present if you will charge, with an undamaged cable and plug | A missing or damaged charger is a problem to raise at handover |
| Key, if there is one | You are given the battery key or told it is not needed | Without it you may not be able to remove or lock the battery |

Bosch, a major e-bike drive maker, advises checking a battery for visible damage before charging, using only compatible chargers and not charging near combustible material or on flammable surfaces, and not leaving a battery unattended while it charges. Those are the same rules a renter should follow with any charger the shop supplies. Our guide to [charging an electric scooter in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/charging-an-electric-scooter-in-hanoi/) covers the practical side in a hotel.

Never open a battery, and never use a charger that is not meant for the bike.

## Step 2: display, assistance and lights

Switch the system on with staff beside you.

1. **Does the display light up cleanly?** It should show the charge level and assistance level without error messages.
2. **Do the buttons work first time?** Change the assistance level up and down.
3. **Does the display stay on and steady** when you press the handlebar or tap the frame?
4. **Do the front and rear lights work,** if the bike has them?
5. **Does the bell or horn work?**
6. **If there is a throttle,** test it only while seated and in control, at low speed. The mokwheel maintenance guide recommends testing assistance and any throttle only during a slow check.
7. **Note any warning icons.** A bike that shows an error before you have ridden it should be exchanged.

Do not rely on lights in daylight. Check them in the shaded part of the shop. For night riding, see our guide to [riding at night in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-at-night-in-hanoi/).

## Step 3: brakes

This step matters more on an electric bike because the bike is heavier than a normal bicycle and the motor adds speed.

- **Squeeze each lever separately** with the bike stationary. The brake should bite before the lever travels all the way to the handlebar, according to a safety guide from the Naval Postgraduate School.
- **Roll the bike forward and backward** while squeezing, to confirm each brake stops the wheel.
- **Spin each wheel** and listen for a rotor or pad rubbing on every turn. Constant rubbing is worth reporting.
- **Look at the pads** and, on disc brakes, at the rotor, for contamination, deep grooves or bends.
- **Feel for softness.** A spongy lever, one that touches the grip or one that feels different from the other is a reason to ask for a mechanic or another bike.
- **Check that cables or hoses** are not frayed, kinked or caught.

Some e-bikes cut the motor when a brake lever is squeezed, so you may hear or feel the assistance stop during this test. That is normal; ask the shop to confirm.

The shop's own brake advice for petrol bikes is in [what to test before leaving the rental shop](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/manual-bike-brakes-what-to-test-before-leaving-the-rental-shop/), and while that is a different type of bike, the idea of testing each control separately is the same.

## Step 4: tyres and wheels

The US National Highway Traffic Safety Administration's "ABC" check for bicycles begins with air: tyres should have the right pressure, which is usually printed on the sidewall, with no cuts or nicks and tread that is not worn down. E-bikes are heavier than normal bicycles, and the Upway checklist notes that low pressure makes the bike feel slower, reduces range, wears tyres faster and increases the risk of flats.

- **Squeeze both tyres** and compare them with the pressure printed on the sidewall. A gauge is better than your thumb, so ask whether the shop has a pump with a gauge.
- **Look at the tread and sidewalls** for cuts, cracks, bulges or any visible fabric.
- **Remove embedded debris** and ask for a tyre with a cut or bulge to be replaced.
- **Check the wheels are firmly attached.** Quick-release levers or axle nuts should be tight.
- **Lift each wheel and spin it.** It should run true, without a wobble or knocking.
- **Hold the wheel and push the rim side to side.** Play at the hub can mean worn bearings.

Our guide to [reading a Honda Wave tyre size and pressure label](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-read-a-honda-wave-tyre-size-and-pressure-label/) shows how to read the pressure label on a motorbike tyre, a useful habit even though a bicycle tyre prints its own figures on the sidewall.

## Step 5: frame, handlebar, saddle and fittings

- **Look along the frame and fork** for cracks, dents, bent tubes or repair welds.
- **Twist the handlebar** while holding the front wheel between your knees. The bar should not move independently of the wheel, according to the Naval Postgraduate School guidance.
- **Turn the bars fully in both directions** and make sure no cable or wire becomes tight or pinched.
- **Check the saddle height and clamp.** Raise the saddle to a comfortable height, and make sure it does not slip.
- **Check the pedals and cranks** are tight.
- **Lift the bike an inch or two and drop it.** A new rattle can mean something is loose.
- **Check the kickstand** holds the heavy bike steadily, or ask about the parking routine.
- **Look at the chain, if it has one,** for rust or a dry, noisy run.

Do not tighten bolts yourself unless the shop tells you to. Overtightening or wrong torque on a safety-critical bolt can cause damage.

## Step 6: a slow test ride

Ask permission, then ride a short loop near the shop on a quiet stretch.

1. Start on the lowest assistance level.
2. Brake gently first, then more firmly.
3. Listen for grinding, clicking or a whine that changes with the assistance level.
4. Check that the bike tracks straight with your hands loose for a moment (if you are comfortable doing so) and not pulling to one side.
5. Try each assistance level and the throttle, if it has one.
6. Come back and tell staff about anything odd.

## When to say no

Ask for another bike, or a mechanic's check, if you find:

- Brakes that are soft, rubbing or do not hold the wheel.
- A tyre with a cut, bulge or low pressure that will not hold.
- A damaged, loose or swollen battery, or a damaged charger.
- A display with errors or one that will not stay on.
- A frame crack, a loose handlebar or a wobbling wheel.
- A shop that does not want you to photograph the bike.

If anything fails after you have left, stop riding and call 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969), open 09:00 to 21:00 daily. The steps in [what to do with a rental bike breakdown](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-bike-breakdown-hanoi-what-to-do/) apply here too.

## FAQ: electric bike pickup checks

### How long should the handover check take?

About ten minutes for a calm check, plus a short ride. If staff hurry you, take the time anyway.

### Should I ask for a full battery?

Ask what the battery level is and how it matches your plans. The shop confirms battery level and charging at handover, so ask where you can charge.

### Do I need to know about bike mechanics?

No. You are checking for obvious problems and recording the condition. Leave adjustments to the shop.

### Should I wear a helmet on an electric bicycle?

Yes. The US Consumer Product Safety Commission advises wearing a bicycle helmet on micromobility devices, and the shop provides one basic helmet per rider.

### Can I carry a passenger?

Only if the bike is designed for it. Ask the shop; many e-bikes are single-rider.
