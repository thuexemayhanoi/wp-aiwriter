Anything that dangles, flaps or swings on a motorbike can catch on something that moves, block something you need to see or pull the bike off line. A long scarf can wrap around a wheel or a lever, a bag strap can snag on a mirror, and a heavy backpack can shift your balance in a turn. Law 36/2024/QH15 limits what a motorcycle or moped may carry: Article 33 bans carrying bulky objects and loads beyond set limits, namely 0.3 metre beyond each side of the cargo rack, 0.5 metre behind it and a total height of 2 metres from the road. The safest rule is simple: secure everything, shorten everything and carry only what fits in the seat storage or on your body without loose ends.

This guide explains what can go wrong, where loose items catch on a scooter or a manual bike, how to carry bags safely, what to wear on your neck, legs and feet, what the law says about loads, what to do if something catches and what to ask the shop. It is general information based on the law and published rider guidance as of October 2026, not legal advice.

## Why loose items matter

A motorbike has several moving parts within reach of a rider's clothing and belongings: the rear wheel, the front wheel, the brake levers, the throttle, the kickstand and, on a manual bike, the chain and sprocket. A loose item can catch on any of these, and the consequences are sudden.

- **Entanglement.** A rider-training and gear commentary warns that a scarf's ends trail behind you, and a long one can catch on something and yank you back. A passenger-safety guide advises riders and passengers not to wear anything loose and floppy, such as a long scarf or wide-bottomed trousers, that could be caught in the rear wheel, sprockets, chain, belt or any other moving part.

- **Distraction.** A scarf that comes undone can flip over your face or fly into another driver's windscreen, and a flapping shirt or poncho pulls your attention away from the road.

- **Loss of control.** A bag swinging from the handlebars changes the steering, and a heavy load that shifts in a corner can pull the bike off its line.

- **Obstruction.** A bag on the footboard, a scarf over the mirrors or a strap across the dashboard hides the things you need to see and reach.

A news report from India in 2025 described a woman rider whose scarf was drawn into the rear sprocket of her motorcycle and tightened around her neck, causing a minor injury and a frightening few seconds, and noted that loose ends should be shortened and kept above knee level. Many scooters have an enclosed drive, which lowers one risk, but wheels, brake levers and mirrors remain, and other riders in Hanoi's dense traffic pass very close.

## Where things catch

| Part | How a loose item catches | Where it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Rear wheel and axle | A long scarf end, a hem or a trailing strap falls into the wheel | Manual and semi-automatic bikes and scooters alike |
| Chain and sprocket | A hanging end is drawn in | Manual and semi-automatic bikes with an exposed chain |
| Front wheel and brake | A flapping cape or bag strap reaches the spokes or the disc | Any bike |
| Brake lever, throttle and handlebar | A strap or a sleeve cord snags | Any bike, especially when you reach to brake |
| Mirrors | A bag strap or a scarf hooks on a mirror | Any bike, and on other riders' bikes as you pass |
| Footboard and kickstand | A bag or a hem catches your foot or the stand | Scooters |
| Other vehicles | A swinging bag brushes a passing bike or a bus | Dense city traffic |

## Scarves and neckwear

- **Avoid long scarves and shawls.** Gear commentary says that regular scarves should be avoided on a motorcycle, and that if you want one, it should be a short neck tube or a scarf with its ends sewn together.

- **Tuck any neck covering inside your jacket or collar,** with no free ends.

- **Use a snug neck gaiter or face cover** that stays in place, and make sure it does not cover your eyes or your helmet strap.

- **Shorten lanyards, ID cords and drawstrings,** and tuck them in. A hood string can blow forward over your eyes.

- **Tie long hair back** so that it does not whip your face or catch in the helmet's strap.

- **Choose a helmet visor or sunglasses** for glare, not a scarf over the eyes.

## Bags: what to avoid and what to do

| Way of carrying | Problem | Safer choice |
|---|---|---|
| Heavy backpack on your back | Moves in turns and under braking; strains your back; heavy items hit you in a fall | Put heavy items in the seat storage; keep the backpack light and snug |
| Bag hung on the handlebar hook | Swings, changes steering and can hide the instruments or the light | Use only for a light bag; check the owner's manual for the hook's limit |
| Bag on the footboard | Blocks your feet; can slide under your shoe | Keep the footboard clear unless the bag fits securely and does not touch your feet |
| Bag on the mirror or the lever | Pulls the mirror out of place and may block the view | Never hang anything on mirrors or levers |
| Long strap across your body | Can slide forward or catch | Shorten it and tuck in the loose end |
| Loose shopping bags in hand | You cannot hold the bars properly | Do not ride with anything in your hand |
| Helmet hung by its strap | Swings and can be damaged | Our guide to [how to transport a helmet on a scooter while parked](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-transport-a-helmet-on-a-scooter-while-parked/) explains the options |

Gear commentary adds that a heavy backpack is uncomfortable, affects your posture and can add to injury in a crash, depending on what is in it. ## What the law says about loads

Article 33 of Law 36/2024/QH15 is the main provision for motorcycles and mopeds.

- **No bulky objects.** Drivers may not carry bulky objects, and passengers on two-wheeled motorcycles and mopeds may not carry them either.

- **Load limits.** Cargo may not extend more than 0.3 metre beyond each side of the cargo rack, or 0.5 metre behind the rack according to the manufacturer's design, and the height of the load from the road must not exceed 2 metres.

- **Both hands on the handlebars.** Drivers may not take both hands off the handlebars, and may not tow or push other vehicles or objects.

- **Acts affecting safety.** The Law bans other acts that affect road traffic order and safety.

Penalties are set in Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, so read the current text for the figure that applies to your case, and see our guide to [how to verify the current fine for a traffic offence](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-verify-the-current-fine-for-a-traffic-offence/). A rental scooter is not a delivery bike, so keep luggage small.

## Clothing and footwear

- **Choose fitted trousers or tuck the hems** into your socks or shoes. A wide hem can catch on the footboard, the kickstand or the pedal.

- **Avoid long skirts, sarongs and loose robes,** or wear them with leggings and tie them.

- **Wear closed shoes with grip.** A loose sandal can slip off and catch under a pedal.

- **Keep sleeves and jacket cords snug,** with no loose ends near the levers.

- **Remove dangling jewellery,** keys and charms that swing near the throttle.

- **Keep your phone secure,** in a mount or a zipped pocket, not in your hand. Our guide to [scooter phone mounts: safer navigation without distraction](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/scooter-phone-mounts-safer-navigation-without-distraction/) explains how.

## A pre-ride shake test

Before you move off, do a quick test.

1. **Stand beside the bike and look at yourself.** Is anything long, loose or loose-ended?

2. **Check the bags.** Is each one inside the seat storage, or held firmly?

3. **Turn the handlebars fully both ways.** Does anything catch or swing into the wheel?

4. **Pull each brake lever.** Is there a strap near it?

5. **Check the mirrors.** Is anything hanging on them?

6. **Sit and shake your upper body.** Does a scarf or a strap move around?

7. **Ask the shop for a second opinion** if you are unsure.

## If something catches

1. **Do not yank it.** A sudden pull can twist the bars or pull you over.

2. **Ease off the throttle and brake smoothly** with both brakes, and keep the bike upright.

3. **Stop in a safe place,** put the stand down and switch off the engine.

4. **Free the item,** and check the wheel, the brake and the chain for damage.

5. **If anyone is hurt,** call 112. Our guide to [emergency numbers and accident reporting in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/emergency-numbers-and-accident-reporting-in-vietnam/) explains what to say.

## A quick checklist

1. **No long scarves, loose ends, dangling cords or flapping layers.**

2. **Tuck hems, sleeves and straps.**

3. **Heavy items go in the seat storage, not on your back or the hook.**

4. **Nothing hangs on mirrors, levers or the handlebars.**

5. **Never ride with anything in your hand.**

6. **Do a shake test before you move off.**

7. **If something catches, brake smoothly, stop and free it.**

## FAQ: scarves, straps and bags

### Can I wear a scarf on a motorbike?

It is safer not to, unless it is a short neck tube or has its ends sewn together and is tucked inside your jacket. Long, loose scarves can catch and pull.

### Can I hang a bag on the scooter's hook?

A light bag, yes, if the hook is made for it and the bag does not swing or block the light or the instruments. Check the owner's manual and ask the shop about the limit.

### Is it legal to carry a large backpack on a scooter?

Article 33 of Law 36/2024/QH15 bans bulky objects and sets load limits, so keep it modest. Heavy loads are also a safety risk.

### Is a loose hem dangerous on a scooter?

It can be. A hem can catch on the footboard, the pedal or the stand, so tuck it in.

### What should I do with my phone?

Use a secure mount or a zipped pocket, and do not hold it while riding.

### What if my scarf is caught?

Brake smoothly, stop in a safe place and free it. Do not pull hard while moving.
