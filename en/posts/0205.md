The safest way to carry a backpack on a Cub 50cc is to keep it small, keep it low and keep it secure. A daypack of a few kilograms can be worn on your back. A bigger pack belongs on the rear rack, strapped down so it cannot shift, with the taillight and number plate still visible and nothing hanging from the handlebars. Vietnam's traffic law also limits how far a load may stick out: no more than 0.3 m past each side of the rack, 0.5 m behind it and 2 m in height from the road. A 50cc moped is light and slow, so every extra kilogram changes how it accelerates, brakes and steers. This guide explains the options, the legal limits, a step-by-step way to strap a pack on and the point at which you should choose a different bike.

## Three ways to carry a pack, compared

A Cub-style 50cc is a step-through semi-automatic with a foot gear shift and no clutch lever. Most have a small rear rack, but there is usually no lockable storage under the seat, so a bag goes on your back, on the rack or nowhere. The table sets out the three realistic choices.

| Option | Best for | Main risk |
|---|---|---|
| Worn on your back | Small daypack with a camera, water, a light jacket | Pulls your shoulders back and tires you on long rides |
| Strapped on the rear rack | A medium pack or a soft bag, short to medium rides | Shifts or slips if strapped badly; can hide the light or plate |
| Held on the floor or hung on the handlebars | Nothing: avoid it | Blocks your feet, upsets steering, can snag a wheel or the throttle |

If your bag is bigger than a school-sized daypack, expect to use the rack and plan your day around it.

## What Vietnamese law says about the load

The Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (Law 36/2024/QH15), in force since 1 January 2025, has a specific article for motorcycles and mopeds. Article 33 says that a rider must not carry bulky items or load goods beyond the permitted limits, and it gives the measurements: the load may not extend more than 0.3 m beyond the width of the rack on each side, more than 0.5 m behind the rack beyond the manufacturer's design, and may not rise more than 2 m above the road surface.

Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, the penalty decree, goes further in its Article 36. As summarised by thuvienphapluat.vn, a rider can be penalised for carrying luggage beyond the vehicle's permitted weight or dimensions, for a load that blocks the rider's view or covers the lights or number plate, and for letting cargo fall onto the road.

In practice, a backpack on a rack seldom breaks the width rule, because most packs are narrower than the rack. The more likely problems are a pack that covers the taillight, a loose strap that trails towards the wheel and a bag that is so heavy the bike is not safe to ride. Treat these as the things a traffic officer, and a rental shop, would notice first.

## What the manufacturer's guidance suggests

Honda publishes loading advice for its Super Cub family. The owner's manual for the Super Cub 125 warns that overloading or improper loading affects handling, braking and stability, and the manual for the same family says luggage weight should not exceed 5 kg. Honda's accessory guide for a rear carrier on a Super Cub C125 repeats a 5 kg limit for cargo on the carrier.

Those figures belong to the larger 125 cc machines, not to a 50cc bike, so they are not a specification for the bike you will rent. Cub-style 50cc bikes also come from more than one maker, and a rental fleet may include copies of the original design. I did not find a published carrier limit for those, so the sensible reading is this: if a bigger Honda Cub is advised to keep luggage near 5 kg, a smaller 50cc bike has no spare capacity to waste. Ask the shop for the limit that applies to your bike, and carry less than that.

## Wearing a daypack well

A pack on your back is simple and fine for a short ride, provided you set it up properly.

- **Keep it light.** A few kilograms is plenty. Put the heaviest items against your back and the soft ones outward.
- **Tighten the straps.** A pack that swings behind you makes the bike twitch when you lean or brake.
- **Use the chest or waist strap** if the pack has one, so it cannot slide across your shoulders when you look over your shoulder.
- **Keep it below the helmet.** A tall pack can push your helmet forward when you look back, so check you can turn your head freely.
- **Do not wear it in front.** A bag worn on your chest blocks your view of the handlebars and the instruments.
- **Take it off at stops.** Resting it on the seat or the rack helps your back more than you might expect over a day.

Remember that you also have to wear a helmet in Vietnam, on a moped as on any motorbike. The shop provides one basic helmet per rider, as noted on the [rental guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-guide/).

## Strapping a pack to the rear rack, step by step

A rack carries weight better than your shoulders, but only if the load is tied down properly. Use two elastic cords with hooks, or one cargo net, and follow this order.

1. **Park on the stand on level ground** with the engine off and look at the rack. Check for bent or loose parts and tell the shop if you find any.
2. **Put the heaviest, flattest item on the rack first,** with the pack's back against the seat side, so its weight sits as low and as far forward as possible.
3. **Centre the load** so it hangs the same distance past each side of the rack, if at all.
4. **Hook the first cord across the top,** then a second at a different angle, so that the pack cannot move forward, backward or sideways.
5. **Tuck loose ends and straps away,** since a dangling strap can wrap around the rear wheel or the chain.
6. **Walk behind the bike.** The taillight, indicators and number plate must be clear, and the pack should stay far below the 2 m height limit.
7. **Shake it.** Push and pull the pack hard. If it shifts, re-tie it before you ride.

If you cannot meet steps 5 and 6 with the bag you have, switch to a smaller bag or a different arrangement.

## What changes on a loaded 50cc

A 50cc moped has little engine to spare. With a pack on the rack it will pull away from a green light a little more slowly than it did unloaded, and it will take more distance to stop. Several effects are general to any light motorbike:

- **The rear gets heavier.** A load behind the seat reduces weight on the front wheel and can make steering feel lighter, especially when braking or going uphill.
- **Parking needs more care.** A heavy rack load makes the bike harder to hold on a stand, especially on a slope.
- **Braking distances grow.** Leave more space than you normally would and use both brakes together.
- **Wind affects a tall pack.** A large bag catches crosswinds on bridges and exposed embankment roads.

Vietnam also caps mopeds at a lower speed than other motorbikes, which the [guide to renting a 50cc scooter in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/renting-a-50cc-scooter-in-hanoi/) explains. That cap works in your favour: at the pace of a loaded moped, you have time to react.

## Keeping the contents dry and safe

Hanoi rain arrives quickly, and a daypack is not waterproof. Put electronics, documents and a spare layer in a dry bag or a plastic bag inside the pack. The rental shop does not include a raincoat for short rentals, so a rain cover for the bag is a cheap addition, and our guide to [riding in Hanoi in the rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) covers the rest.

Never leave a bag on the bike. The shop does not supply an extra lock, and a bag tied to a rack is easy to take. When you park, take the pack with you, or leave it at your hotel before you set off, and use a guarded place as described in [parking a motorbike in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-motorbike-in-hanoi/).

## A one-minute check before every ride

1. Is the pack tied down so that it does not move when you shake it?
2. Are the taillight, indicators and number plate visible from behind?
3. Is nothing hanging from the handlebars or touching the throttle or brake levers?
4. Can your feet rest on the footboard without touching the bag?
5. Do the loose ends of straps stay clear of the wheel and chain?
6. Does the bike feel stable in a slow figure-eight in a quiet place?
7. Are you carrying less than the shop's stated limit?

## When a Cub 50cc is the wrong bike for your bag

A 50cc moped is well suited to a light visitor with a daypack. It is the wrong tool when you carry a full travel pack or a bulky bag, or when you want to ride long distances with luggage. In that case consider a bigger bike. ## FAQ: carrying a backpack on a 50cc bike

### Can I wear a backpack while riding a Cub 50cc?

Yes, a small, light daypack is fine. Keep it tight against your back, free of dangling straps, and do not let it push your helmet forward.

### Is there a legal limit for luggage on a moped in Vietnam?

Law 36/2024/QH15, Article 33, limits the load to 0.3 m past each side of the rack, 0.5 m behind it and 2 m in height from the road, and it bans bulky loads and loads above the permitted limit.

### How much weight can a Cub 50cc carry?

It depends on the bike and its maker. Honda's guidance for the larger Super Cub 125 suggests about 5 kg of luggage on the carrier, so a 50cc has less room to spare. Ask the shop for the figure that applies to the bike you rent.

### Can I hang a bag from the handlebars?

Avoid it. The weight upsets steering, the bag can catch the throttle or brake levers and it may swing into the wheel.

### Will a rental shop store my big backpack?

Do not assume it. Ask when you book, since arrangements vary. Our [contact page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/contact/) shows how to reach the shop.
