Hai Duong is worth a scooter day trip if you want food, craft and history without crowds, and it is not worth it if you want dramatic scenery or a quick, easy ride. The city is about 60 km east of Hanoi by a figure in Vietnamese state media, and the road there is a busy national highway, so the ride is something to endure on the way out and back, not the highlight. What you get at the end is a provincial city known for its mung bean cakes, a very large Confucian temple, an ancient pottery village and a mountain-and-cave heritage site. This guide gives a verdict first, then the reasoning: what there is to do, how the road behaves, which kind of traveller should go, and what a realistic day looks like.

## The short verdict

| If you are... | Worth it? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Interested in food and local specialities | Yes | Hai Duong's mung bean cakes are a regional signature |
| Curious about Vietnamese history and Confucian tradition | Yes | A major temple of literature and a famous pottery site are nearby |
| Looking for scenery and photography | Partly | Heritage sites have charm, but the route is mostly flat farmland and towns |
| A nervous or first-time rider | Probably not | National Highway 5 is heavy with trucks |
| Short on time (under a full day) | Probably not | You will spend much of it riding |

## What Hai Duong is now

Until mid-2025 Hai Duong was a province in its own right. Since 1 July 2025 it has been merged into an enlarged Hai Phong city, according to a World Bank document and the Hai Phong government's own news. For a visitor, almost nothing changes: roads, towns and signs keep their names, and people still say "Hai Duong". The practical point is for research. Newer official pages are under Hai Phong, and older ones under Hai Duong.

## Why a scooter makes sense, and where it does not

The distance, about 60 km to the city, suits a modest scooter. The delta landscape is largely flat, so an automatic scooter of the Honda Vision or Air Blade type is a comfortable fit. The sights are spread over a wider area than the city itself, which is where having your own wheels helps: the temple of literature, the pottery village and the Kinh Mon caves are in different directions. Public transport options are mostly aimed at the city itself, so the outlying sights are harder to reach without your own vehicle.

Where a scooter does not help: the road out is dominated by trucks and buses. A World Bank project document describes National Highway 5, the main route toward Hai Phong, as a corridor with a large volume of heavy trucks and a major cause of fatal crashes. That is the context in which to judge a scooter trip. Choose a day with good light, keep to the right-hand side, and leave extra distance in front of trucks. Our guide to [crossing Hanoi intersections](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/crossing-hanoi-intersections/) is useful practice for the first stretch.

## The food reason: mung bean cake

The Vietnam National Authority of Tourism calls Hai Duong's green bean cake the province's most famous speciality, and says there are more than 50 brands. A Vietnamnet feature explains that the cake is made from mung beans, sugar, pork fat and pomelo flower essence, and is traditionally eaten with green tea. Other local products named in the same sources include Thanh Ha lychee, Ke Sat dried pancake and a black glutinous cake called banh gai.

The practical advice: buy cakes at the end of the day, not the start. They are dry and a little fragile, so carry them in a small bag in the seat compartment or between your feet, not in a loose bag on the handlebar. Do not rely on any particular brand without checking the packaging and the seller yourself. Food-safety standards differ between sellers, so choose busy, established shops.

## The history reasons: Mao Dien and Chu Dau

**Mao Dien Temple of Literature.** According to VietnamPlus, Mao Dien in Cam Giang is the second-largest temple of literature in Vietnam after Hanoi's, dating in origin to the 15th century and moved to its present site under the Tay Son. It honours Confucius and some 600 Confucian doctors from the province. In December 2018 it was recognised as a special national relic. It makes a natural companion to a visit to Hanoi's own Temple of Literature. Check opening hours and any entry arrangements on the day.

**Chu Dau pottery.** Chu Dau village, in Nam Sach, was a high-class ceramic centre from the late 14th to the 17th century, according to the National Museum of History. Its archaeological site was the first in the country recognised as a national relic, in 1992, and Nhan Dan newspaper places the village about 80 km east of Hanoi. Today there is a modern ceramic company and a visitor-oriented village. If you like craft shopping, this is the best stop; if you do not, skip it.

**Kinh Mon.** A VIR report describes the An Phu - Kinh Chu - Nham Duong area in Kinh Mon as a heritage and scenic complex with a temple to Tran Lieu, a pagoda, a 13 m statue of Tran Hung Dao and the Kinh Chu cave. UNESCO's nomination file lists Nham Duong pagoda and Kinh Chu cave as component parts of the Yen Tu complex. It sounds the most scenic of the three, but it lies further east beyond the city, so treat it as a stop for a long day or an overnight stay.

## Choose one or two, not all

Hai Duong rewards focus. A sensible shape is one main stop and one smaller one:

1. **Food and temple day:** Hai Duong city, the mung bean cake shops, then Mao Dien in Cam Giang.
2. **Craft day:** Chu Dau, then a loop through the surrounding villages.
3. **Heritage day:** Kinh Mon, with a note that it adds distance. For a different heritage-focused day in the same region, see our [Hanoi to Ninh Binh ride](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-to-ninh-binh-by-motorbike/) for comparison.

Trying to cover all three is how a pleasant day becomes a tiring one.

## A realistic time budget

These are planning allowances, not measured times. About 60 km on a busy highway through towns can take well over an hour and a half each way once you include lights and slow trucks. Add a meal, a stop at a temple, and a shopping stop, and you will use most of a day. Start by mid-morning, aim to be on the way back before late afternoon, and do not plan to ride in the dark; [riding at night in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-at-night-in-hanoi/) explains why.

## The road rules to remember

The fast Hanoi to Hai Phong expressway runs through Hai Duong, but motorcycles and mopeds are not allowed on expressways under Law 36/2024/QH15 (Article 25). ## Alternatives to riding

If the road puts you off, a travel guide notes that trains to Hai Phong stop at Hai Duong, Phu Thai and Cam Giang stations, departing from Long Bien station, so you can go by rail and walk or take a local taxi to the city sights. You would lose the freedom to reach Chu Dau or Kinh Mon, but gain a relaxed day. For a ride that avoids heavy trucks, a shorter option such as [Gia Lam and Bat Trang](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/gia-lam-east-bank-bat-trang-by-scooter/) offers a craft experience much closer to the shop.

## FAQ: Hanoi to Hai Duong by scooter

### How far is Hai Duong from Hanoi?

About 60 km to the city, according to Vietnamese state media. Chu Dau and Kinh Mon are further.

### Is the road safe on a scooter?

It is a busy national highway with many trucks, which a World Bank document identifies as a safety problem. It suits an experienced rider on a clear day, not a beginner.

### Is Hai Duong the same as Hai Phong now?

Administratively, Hai Duong has been part of Hai Phong city since 1 July 2025. Signs and conversation still use the name Hai Duong.

### What should I buy?

Mung bean cake is the signature product. Buy it near the end of your day and carry it carefully.

### Can I ride on the expressway?

No. Motorcycles and mopeds are not allowed on expressways.

### Should I go for one day or stay overnight?

One day suits the city, Mao Dien and one other stop. An overnight stay suits Kinh Mon and a slower pace.
