Hanoi Botanical Garden, known locally as Bach Thao Park, is an easy ride from the Old Quarter and has two gates on different streets, which is the whole secret to getting in and parking without fuss. The main gate is at 3 Hoang Hoa Tham Street and a second gate is at 2 Ngoc Ha Street, both in Ngoc Ha Ward, Ba Dinh, just behind the Ho Chi Minh Mausoleum complex. Opening hours are commonly given as about 5:00 to 22:00 and entry is free or a token charge, depending on the source, with a small fee for leaving a motorbike. This guide explains the approach, how to choose between parking at the gate, parking nearby and not riding at all, what condition the garden is in, when to go and what a possible low-emission zone means for rental scooters in Ba Dinh.

## What the garden is, and how big

The garden was created by the French colonial government in 1890, according to guides to the park, on land that had been part of the old Thang Long citadel area. It was originally much larger, with accounts giving a figure of about 33 hectares and a setting among French villas. Part of the land was later used for the Ba Dinh Square historic complex, and animals kept in the garden were moved to other zoos. The name Bach Thao means roughly "hundreds of plants".

Its present size depends on who you ask. Travel guides say about 10 hectares, one says about 17 and a VOV report from February 2025 says about 20, so treat any single figure with caution. What matters on the day is that it is a mid-sized city garden, with lawns, a lake, a small raised mound known as Nung Mountain and mature trees. Visitor accounts consistently describe the trees as the main attraction, and the shade is the main reason local people use it, especially in the early morning when many come to exercise.

## The two gates

Which gate you choose changes the ride and the parking.

| Gate | Address | Useful when |
|---|---|---|
| Main gate | 3 Hoang Hoa Tham Street | You are coming from the Old Quarter or West Lake along Hoang Hoa Tham |
| Second gate | 2 Ngoc Ha Street | You are coming along Ngoc Ha or from the Ba Dinh Square area |

Both addresses are given consistently by travel guides including VinWonders and Vinpearl, and by a Vietnamese-language Vinpearl review. Check the gate on a map and look at street names, because the two entrances are on different roads and the walk between them inside the garden is longer than it looks.

## Approaching from the Old Quarter

VinWonders places the garden about 3.5 km from the Old Quarter, under 10 minutes by motorbike, car or taxi. Vietnam Online suggests a route for drivers along Hang Dao and Hang Ngang, then left at Hang Dau Street, on along Quy Thanh Street and left onto Hoang Hoa Tham until the garden appears on the left. Treat that as one possible route and not as the only one: a navigation app will choose among several, and Ba Dinh's streets include one-way sections. Our guide to [riding in Ba Dinh](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/ba-dinh-by-motorbike/) explains the one-way grid, the Mausoleum area and the parking habits of the district, and our page on [Tay Ho and West Lake](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/tay-ho-west-lake-by-scooter/) describes the lakeside approach.

One practical point: this is a government district with ceremonial roads. If a road is closed with barriers or staff, follow the signs, do not argue and choose another route.

## Parking choices

There are three realistic ways to handle the bike.

### Option 1: the garden's own parking

Several guides say that visitors who bring a vehicle pay a small parking fee, with different rates for motorbikes and cars, and some say there is a small entrance charge as well. Because figures vary by source and may have changed, take small change and read the posted board at the gate. Ask the attendant where to put the bike and keep the ticket.

### Option 2: parking nearby on the street

Road-traffic Law 36/2024/QH15, in force since 1 January 2025, restricts where vehicles may stop and park. As of October 2026, Article 18 of the published text says drivers must not stop or park at a junction or within 5 metres of it, in front of an organisation's gate or within 5 metres either side where vehicles enter and leave, on a pedestrian crossing, at a passenger pick-up point or where the road is only wide enough for one motor-vehicle lane. On the street, park beside the kerb or pavement on the right in your direction of travel, with the nearest wheel no more than 0.25 metres from it.

Two gates and a pair of busy roads mean those rules bite. Do not leave the scooter in front of a gate, a driveway or the corner of Hoang Hoa Tham and a side street, even for a few minutes.

### Option 3: park elsewhere and walk

If the garden's lot is full, or if you are combining it with other Ba Dinh sights, use a supervised lot in the wider area and walk the last few hundred metres. Our guide to [parking a motorbike in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-motorbike-in-hanoi/) describes guarded lots, tickets and towing. Because the shop does not supply an extra lock, choose supervised places and lock the steering.

If you are unsure, the simplest rule is to park inside the garden's own bay or in a staffed lot, and not at the kerb.

## Hours, entry and what to expect on arrival

Most guides give opening hours from around 5:00 or 5:30 to 22:00 daily. Tripadvisor's page for the garden lists 5:00 to 21:00 on the Thursday it was viewed, and one site gives much shorter hours, so there is variation. Entry is described as free by some sources and as a token charge by others, which suggests the charge, if any, depends on how you arrive and has changed over time. Prices should be confirmed at the gate.

Early morning from about 6 to 8 is when local people jog, stretch and practise tai chi, according to a travel site, and it is a pleasant time for a visitor too. By mid-day the paths are quieter, and in the evening there are families and couples.

## Condition of the garden

Visitors should arrive with realistic expectations. A VOV report in February 2025 said that many items in the park had been run down for years: uneven and subsiding paving around the lake, a rotten wooden bridge that management had closed to the public, damaged exercise equipment and play structures, and only one public toilet for a park of about 20 hectares. The park's managers said a renovation was planned and handled by the Ba Dinh authorities, but it had not yet started at the time of the report. Visitor reviews on Tripadvisor between 2024 and 2026 are mixed, with several describing the garden as run down and some criticising the conditions of caged animals.

This does not mean you should skip it. For many people the old trees, the lake and the quiet are enough. But it is not a manicured botanical collection, and it is better treated as a green stop within a Ba Dinh morning than as a destination on its own. Wear flat, closed shoes, because a travel site notes that the paved paths can be uneven with roots pushing through, and bring water.

## A Ba Dinh morning combining the garden with other stops

Travel guides place the garden close to the Ho Chi Minh Mausoleum complex, Ba Dinh Square, the Presidential Palace area and the One Pillar Pagoda. A sensible sequence for a scooter is:

1. Ride from the Old Quarter in the early morning, before the day's traffic builds.
2. Park once, at the garden or in a staffed lot, and walk for the rest of the morning.
3. Visit the garden first while it is cool, then walk to the nearby sights if their opening hours allow.
4. Return to the scooter by mid-morning, ride out before the lunchtime peak.

Opening times and closures at the Mausoleum area vary, so check them separately before you plan the walk.

## A note on Hanoi's low-emission zone

Hanoi has been phasing in a low-emission zone inside Ring Road 1. VOV World reported that Phase One began on 1 July 2026 in part of Hoan Kiem Ward, including the lakeside pedestrian area and the Old Quarter, with all motorbikes and cars banned from 7 pm to midnight on Fridays, Saturdays and Sundays in Zone 1. A VnEconomy report from April 2026 described a three-phase plan that would extend the zone to Hoan Kiem and Cua Nam in 2027 and to nine wards inside Ring Road 1, including Ba Dinh and Ngoc Ha, from 2028 to 2029. Hanoi officials also made clear that there would be no sudden blanket ban on petrol motorbikes in Ring Road 1.

For a visitor, that means the garden is outside the Phase One zone as reported, but the rules may change. If you plan an evening visit around the Old Quarter on a Friday, Saturday or Sunday, check the current arrangement, and read our guide to [parking in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-motorbike-in-hanoi/) for options such as parking outside the zone and walking in.

## FAQ about the Botanical Garden by scooter

### Which gate is the main one?

The main gate is at 3 Hoang Hoa Tham Street, and a second gate is at 2 Ngoc Ha Street.

### Is the garden free?

Sources disagree: some say free, others mention a small charge. A parking fee for vehicles is widely reported. Confirm at the gate.

### What are the opening hours?

Commonly 5:00 or 5:30 to 22:00, though listings vary. Check before a special trip.

### Can I park on Hoang Hoa Tham Street?

Only where parking is permitted and not within 5 metres of junctions or in front of gates. The safest choice is the garden's own parking or a staffed lot.

### Is the garden worth a visit?

For shade, quiet and local life, yes. For a tidy botanical collection, expect less, as reports and reviews describe worn facilities.
