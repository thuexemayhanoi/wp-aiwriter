For most museum days in central Hanoi, walking plus the occasional taxi or ride-hailing car beats a scooter, because the main museums cluster close together and parking at each stop adds friction. A rented scooter earns its place when your list includes the museums on the western side of the city, when you want to string together four or five stops over a full day, or when you simply enjoy riding. The deciding factors are the opening hours and closing days, which differ by museum, plus the weather and how many stops you really mean to make. Details below were checked on official museum pages as of October 2026.

## Check the opening days first

A museum day fails more often because of a closed door than because of the transport. Several of Hanoi's best-known museums close on Mondays, and one closes on the first Monday of each month, so settle the day before you settle the vehicle. The table uses official museum pages.

| Museum | Hours given by the museum | Closed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vietnam Museum of Ethnology (Cau Giay) | 8:30 to 17:30 | Mondays and New Year holiday |
| Vietnam National Fine Arts Museum, 66 Nguyen Thai Hoc | 8:30 to 17:00 | Mondays |
| Vietnam National Museum of History, 1 Trang Tien | 8:00 to 12:00 and 13:30 to 17:00 | First Monday of each month |
| Vietnam Women's Museum, 36 Ly Thuong Kiet | 8:00 to 17:00 | Open Monday to Sunday |
| Hoa Lo Prison Relic, 1 Hoa Lo | 8:00 to 17:00, including holidays | Open every day |

Two further notes. The History Museum's closing between noon and 13:30 means you can plan lunch around it. And the Military History Museum, which visitor reports say moved to a new site at Xuan Phuong in Nam Tu Liem in 2024, is described by visitors as closed on Mondays and Fridays and roughly 45 minutes from the Old Quarter, which makes it a different kind of trip. Because that detail comes from visitor accounts rather than the museum, confirm it before you commit.

If your free day is a Monday, the Women's Museum and Hoa Lo are the safe choices. Ticket prices change, so check each museum's page instead of relying on a figure here.

## Two clusters, two answers

Hanoi's museums fall roughly into two groups, and the transport answer follows the geography.

**The central cluster.** The History Museum on Trang Tien, the Women's Museum on Ly Thuong Kiet and Hoa Lo Prison are all in the Hoan Kiem area near the lake. The Women's Museum's own visit page lists Hoa Lo Prison, the cathedral and Hoan Kiem Lake among nearby sights, so a day here can be done almost entirely on foot. The art museum is slightly farther in Ba Dinh near the Temple of Literature.

**The western and outer sites.** The Museum of Ethnology is on the western side of the city, and a travel guide describes it as about a twenty-minute drive from the Old Quarter. The relocated Military History Museum is farther again. These are the stops where a scooter or a car makes sense.

## Scooter or taxi, stop by stop

### Why a taxi often wins in the centre

Short central hops are where a scooter helps least. You need to find proper parking at each stop, pay for it, remember where you left the bike and carry your helmet around the galleries. Hoa Lo's visitor rules tell guests that handbags must be put in a designated place and that parking is at the designated lot only, which gives you an idea of how little informal flexibility there is. Our guide to [where to park a scooter near Hoan Kiem Lake](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/where-to-park-a-scooter-near-hoan-kiem-lake/) shows how tight central parking can be.

A ride-hailing car solves each of those problems. Apps such as Grab, Be and XanhSM are commonly used by visitors, and you see a quoted fare before you book. For two or three stops in the central area, compare the quoted fares with a day's rental rather than assuming the scooter is cheaper.

### Why a scooter can win across the city

If your plan is the Ethnology Museum in the morning, the national art museum after lunch and a lakeside evening, the distances and the waiting time for cars add up. A scooter lets you move on your own schedule between the west side and the centre. It also helps if you want to stop at a cafe or two on the way.

The shop's published hours are 09:00 to 21:00 daily. If you collect the bike at opening time, you are unlikely to reach a museum before about 10:00, which suits places that open at 8:30 or 9:00 but means a late start. A one-day rental is 24 hours, and shorter use is charged as a full day. The [prices page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/prices/) shows current rates, and the shop's day rate for a manual or automatic bike is in the range of VND 150,000 to VND 200,000, so a single-museum half-day is rarely the economical use.

### A quick rule of thumb

- Fewer than three central stops: taxi or walk.
- A full day that includes the western museums: scooter, if you are comfortable in traffic.
- Rain, heat, or tired legs: taxi, and leave the bike parked.
- A group bigger than two: a taxi keeps everyone together.

## A central-cluster day mostly on foot

This is a template, not a timetable, and each museum's hours should be rechecked.

1. Start at the National Museum of History when it opens at 8:00, and finish before it closes for the midday break at 12:00.
2. Walk or take a short ride to the Women's Museum, which stays open from 8:00 to 17:00, and have lunch nearby.
3. Visit Hoa Lo Prison in the afternoon.
4. Finish at the lake. Evenings are pleasant but note that since 1 July 2026 Hoan Kiem's first low-emission zone has banned motorbikes, mopeds and cars from 7 pm to midnight on Fridays, Saturdays and Sundays, per VOV and Lao Dong. The museums close before then, but a scooter left in the area for dinner is something to plan around.

This day suits a taxi for any longer hop and does not need a rental at all.

## A west-side day where a scooter helps

1. Collect the bike when the shop opens and ride across the river in daylight, avoiding the morning rush if you can.
2. Reach the Ethnology Museum in the morning. It opens at 8:30 and closes at 17:30, which gives you a generous window even with a 09:00 bike pick-up.
3. Ride to the art museum, which closes at 17:00, and pair it with the nearby Temple of Literature if you have time.

## Practical details that save the day

- **Bags and lockers.** The Women's Museum lists free lockers and wifi, and Hoa Lo asks visitors to leave handbags in a designated place. A small day bag is easier than a large pack.
- **Helmet.** One basic helmet per rider is provided with the rental. Ask where it can be stored inside; do not leave it hanging on the bike.
- **Shoes and weather.** Galleries mean a lot of standing. Closed shoes are safer than sandals on a scooter, and Hanoi's heat and rain both change the plan.
- **Fuel.** Petrol bikes are handed over with a small amount of fuel, so check the gauge before a long day. Our guide to [fuel in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/fuel-in-hanoi-petrol-stations-rental-scooter/) covers stations and filling up.
- **Pacing.** Museums can be tiring. Do not ride home when you are worn out; take a taxi and collect the bike on the next day within your rental period if you need to.

## Decision checklist

- Which day of the week is it, and which museums are closed?
- How many stops are in the central cluster, and how many are outside it?
- Will you be riding in daylight, heat or rain?
- Are you confident in Hanoi traffic after a day of standing in galleries?
- Is parking at each stop a known quantity?
- Have you compared the quoted ride-hailing fares with a day's rental?

If most answers point towards the centre, a taxi is the better tool. If they point west, rent the scooter.

## FAQ

### Which Hanoi museums are closed on Monday?

The Museum of Ethnology and the national art museum are closed on Mondays. The History Museum closes on the first Monday of each month. The Women's Museum and Hoa Lo Prison are open daily, according to their own pages.

### Can I reach all the central museums on foot?

Several of them, yes. The History, Women's and Hoa Lo sites are in the Hoan Kiem area, and the Women's Museum lists Hoa Lo and the lake as nearby. The art museum and Ethnology are farther away.

### Is a scooter cheaper than taxis for a museum day?

It depends on the number of stops. Compare the quoted ride-hailing fares with the rental rate on the prices page, and add parking.

### Where do I park at Hoa Lo?

Hoa Lo's visitor rules say to park at the designated lot only. Ask the staff at the entrance where that is.

### Should a beginner ride between the museums?

Only if you are comfortable with Hanoi traffic. A beginner is better off with a taxi, or a short, quiet route in daylight. See our [rental guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-guide/).
