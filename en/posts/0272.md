If the display on a rental electric scooter is dead, stop in a safe place and treat the bike as not ready to ride until the shop says otherwise. The screen is where the bike shows speed, battery level, riding mode and fault warnings, so a blank one leaves you without the information you need to ride safely. A few gentle checks are reasonable, such as switching the key off and on again, making sure the charger is unplugged and, only if the shop has shown you where it is, looking at the battery switch under the seat. Beyond that, call the shop rather than experimenting. This guide explains what a dead display can mean, what the screen normally does, a calm sequence of steps, whether to keep riding and exactly what to say on the phone. It is general information based on one manufacturer's manual, not a repair guide.

## What "dead display" can mean

People use the phrase for several different situations, and sorting out which one you have is the first step:

- **Nothing lights at all.** The key is on, but the screen stays black and the bike does not respond. This may be a power or battery problem, not a screen problem.
- **The bike seems alive, but the screen is blank.** You hear a click when the key turns, the lights work, yet the display shows nothing. Here the link between the display and the rest of the bike may be at fault.
- **The screen lights but is partly wrong.** It flickers, freezes, shows odd fragments or omits the battery indicator.
- **The screen lights and then goes off while riding.** This is the most serious case, because you may be moving at speed with no information.

The focus here is the display itself and whether it is safe to carry on, not a full fault-finding routine.

## What the display normally does

VinFast's owner's manual for the Evo 200, one model of electric motorbike, describes the instrument cluster, which it calls the HMI. It shows the speed, the total and trip distance, the clock, the Parking-mode indicator, the riding mode, the high-beam and turn-signal indicators, the battery temperature, the lithium-ion battery information including remaining charge and charging status, a charging icon and a symbol for a system fault. The battery bar starts flashing when the state of charge falls below 20 per cent, and the battery icon flashes below 10 per cent. If the self-diagnosis finds a fault, a warning symbol appears with a code. The manual's fault table includes a code for a lost signal to the display, and it says simple faults can be read through the phone app.

When the screen lights up after the key is turned on, the manual says the cluster comes on and the bike enters Parking mode. It also says the battery makes a clicking sound when it switches on, and that the bike goes back into Parking mode if it has not moved for two minutes. Knowing this tells you what "normal" looks like at the start of a ride, which is what you are comparing against.

Your bike may be a different model with a different layout, so ask the shop to show you the start-up sequence at pickup.

## Step 1: stop somewhere safe

If the display fails while you are riding, slow down gently, signal and pull in away from traffic. Use both brakes smoothly, as the manual advises. On a quiet electric bike, other riders may not hear you stop, so use the horn if needed and keep clear of the lane. Do not open panels or touch cables. The manual warns that parts of the system run above 60 volts, which can injure a person, so leave electrical parts alone.

## Step 2: a short, safe sequence

Take each step in order. Stop when something works, and call the shop if nothing does.

1. **Wait a few seconds.** Turn the key off, wait about ten seconds, turn it on and watch. Listen for the click when the battery switches on.

2. **Check the stand and the mode.** The manual says a side-stand sensor cuts drive power when the stand is down, and that the bike enters Parking mode when stationary. These do not light a dead screen, but they explain a bike that will not move once the screen is back.

3. **Check that nothing is plugged in.** The manual says the throttle will not work and the motor will not run while the charger is connected, and that the charging icon appears on the display. If the charger is attached, unplug the wall end first and then the bike end, with the key off, as the manual describes.

4. **Ask the shop whether you may use the battery switch.** The manual's advice for a bike that gives no response is to turn the key off, switch the battery switch under the seat off and on, and try to start again. On a rental, do this only if the shop has told you where it is and that you may.

5. **Follow the 45-second rule if the shop agrees.** The manual says that if the screen does not light 45 seconds after the key is turned on, and this happens repeatedly, you should connect the charger, charge for at least one minute, unplug it and restart. VinFast's charging guide repeats this. Only use the charger supplied with the bike, and only in a safe, dry spot where you can stay beside it.

6. **Stop and call.** If the screen is still dead, do not continue to try things.

## Should you ride on without a display?

Whether to go on depends on what you have lost. Without the display you cannot see:

- your speed;
- how much battery is left, or the low-battery flash at 20 per cent and 10 per cent;
- whether the bike is in Parking mode or ready to drive;
- fault warnings, including battery temperature and protection alerts;
- the indicator that tells you charging is in progress.

Lights, horn and brakes are separate controls and may still work, so test them, but a rider with no speedometer and no battery reading is guessing. The safe answer is not to ride beyond what is necessary to get to a safe place or the shop, and only if the shop says that is acceptable. If the bike can still move, ask whether they would prefer to collect it. If you are low on battery and cannot see it, the risk of being stranded rises. ## Partial failures at a glance

| What you see | Possible meaning | Sensible response |
|---|---|---|
| Completely black, no click | No power to the bike | Check the charger is unplugged; call the shop |
| Black, but lights and horn work | Display or its link | Do not ride on; call the shop |
| Lights briefly, then off | Possible power dip or fault | Wait, restart once; call if repeated |
| Flickering or frozen | Display or wiring issue | Park, switch off, call |
| Fault symbol with a code | Self-diagnosis fault | Photograph the code; call |
| Display fine but battery bar flashing | Low charge | Plan to charge; call if far from help |
| Screen dim in bright sun | Normal glare | Shade it with your hand to check |
| Screen fogged or wet inside | Moisture | Stop riding; call the shop |

These are general explanations, not diagnoses. A mechanic must say what is wrong.

## Heat, rain and water

Weather can be part of the story. The Evo 200 manual says performance is reduced when the battery temperature is above 40 degrees Celsius, below minus 10 or when the charge is below 30 per cent, and that charging should wait until the battery has cooled after riding. It also warns that riding in storms or floods is dangerous and can damage the electrical system. If the screen went blank after heavy rain, a puddle crossing or a wash, do not keep trying. Park in the dry, switch off and call. Our guide to [riding in Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) covers wet-season planning.

## What to tell the shop

Speak clearly and give the facts in this order:

1. **Where you are,** with a map pin if possible.

2. **What the bike was doing** when the display failed: riding, stopped, just started or charging.

3. **What you see,** for example black, flickering, a code or a symbol.

4. **What you tried,** in the order you did it.

5. **Whether you hear a click,** and whether lights, horn and indicators work.

6. **The last battery reading you saw,** and the time.

## Prevention at pickup

A thirty-second check at handover can head off a surprise later:

- Watch the shop start the bike in front of you. The screen should come on fully, with speed, battery level and no fault symbol.
- Photograph the start-up screen, showing the battery level and the odometer.
- Ask what the normal start-up looks like and how long it takes.
- Ask which controls you may use, and which, such as the battery switch, you should leave alone.
- Ask what the shop wants you to do if the screen fails, and who to call after hours.

## What not to do

- Do not take the dashboard or any panel off.
- Do not borrow another charger to try to revive the bike.
- Do not keep riding at speed with no information on the screen.
- Do not leave the bike charging unattended while you wait; follow the supplied charger's rules and stay nearby.
- Do not ignore a fault symbol because the bike still moves.
- Do not assume the display fault is your responsibility. Report it and let the contract and the facts decide.

## FAQ: a dead display on a rental electric scooter

### Can the scooter still run if the display is dead?

Sometimes the bike moves, but you lose speed, battery and fault information, so do not carry on beyond getting safe.

### Is a dead display always a dead battery?

No. It can be a flat battery, a switch, a connection, the display itself or a protection fault. Only the shop can tell.

### Should I charge the bike to bring the screen back?

Only if the shop agrees. The manual describes a short charge and restart if the screen does not light, but use the supplied charger and stay with the bike.

### What if the display dies in the rain?

Park in the dry, switch off, and call the shop. Do not keep switching it on.

### Can I read the fault code myself?

If the screen works, a code may be shown. The manual says simple faults can be read through the phone app. Photograph what you see and tell the shop.

### Who pays if the display fails?

It depends on the contract and the cause. Report promptly, keep photographs and ask the shop before you agree to anything.
