When rain cuts visibility, the safe response is to slow down to a speed at which you can stop within the distance you can see, switch on your headlight, keep a larger gap, signal earlier than usual and stop in a safe place if you cannot see well enough to ride. Law 36/2024/QH15 backs each step. Article 20 requires front lights to be on from 18:00 to 06:00 and whenever fog, smoke, dust, rain or bad weather limits visibility. Article 12 requires operators to slow down in rainy conditions and when visibility is limited, and Circular 38/2024/TT-BGTVT requires a larger distance from the vehicle ahead in rain and on slippery surfaces. Article 33 also bans riders of motorcycles and mopeds from using umbrellas while riding.

This guide covers what changes when you cannot see, how to prepare your helmet, visor and bike, how to ride when the view is poor, how to be seen by others, how to deal with fogged visors and glare, where to stop and what to check on a rental scooter. It is general information based on the law and published riding guidance as of October 2026, not legal advice.

## What goes wrong when visibility drops

Several things happen at once in heavy rain.

- **Water on the visor or glasses** scatters light and blurs shapes.

- **Fog on the inside of the visor** builds up when you are warm and moving slowly.

- **Spray from vehicles ahead** hides brake lights and the road.

- **Glare** from headlights, streetlights and wet roads becomes harder to judge, especially after dark.

- **Wet mirrors** are less useful.

- **Pedestrians in ponchos or under umbrellas** have their faces hidden and their view reduced.

- **Other road users see you less well,** because windscreens, wipers and spray hide small vehicles.

Published riding guidance from a rider-gear company makes the same point: rain demands a different margin for error, and your goal is to maximise grip, protect your vision and give yourself more time to react. Our guide to [riding in Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) covers the season and the streets that flood, and this guide concentrates on seeing.

## What the law says

Four provisions of Law 36/2024/QH15 apply.

- **Article 20(1):** drivers and operators must turn on front lights between 18:00 and 06:00, or when there is fog, smoke, dust, rain or bad weather that limits visibility. In the daytime, that means a headlight on in the rain.

- **Article 12(3):** operators must observe the surroundings and slow down or stop for safety when they suffer limited visibility, in rainy or windy conditions, and in fog, smoke or dust, or on slippery and muddy road surfaces.

- **Article 20(2):** operators must switch from high beam to low beam when they meet pedestrians crossing, in lit populous areas, when they meet oncoming traffic and when they change direction at intersections.

- **Article 33(3):** riders of two-wheeled motorcycles and mopeds may not use umbrellas while riding, and passengers may not use them either (Article 33(4)).

Circular 38/2024/TT-BGTVT adds that in rain, fog and on slippery surfaces, you must keep a larger distance from the vehicle ahead than the standard values. ## Before you ride: helmet, visor and view

- **Choose a helmet and visor that suit the rain.** A clear visor is best. A tinted visor or dark sunglasses cut light and make things worse after dark and in heavy rain. Our guide to [choosing a helmet that fits for a manual motorbike journey](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/choosing-a-helmet-that-fits-for-a-manual-motorbike-journey/) explains fit, and the helmet must be fastened, as Article 33 requires.

- **Clean the visor** with a soft cloth and a visor-safe cleaner, not a dry wipe that drags grit across it. Published guidance warns that dry-wiping grit can scratch the surface, and scratches scatter light from headlights at night.

- **Manage fogging.** Use an anti-fog treatment or insert where available, open the helmet's vents and avoid sealing yourself into warm, humid air. If the visor fogs every time you stop, solve that before riding in heavy traffic.

- **Check your lights and mirrors.** Our guides to [how to check a scooter's horn and turn signals](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-check-a-scooters-horn-and-turn-signals/) and [scooter headlight checks for evening rentals](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/scooter-headlight-checks-for-evening-rentals/) explain how.

- **Wipe the mirrors,** and adjust them from the riding position.

- **Wear a raincoat that does not cover your lights, your view or your hands.** The shop's published terms say a raincoat is not part of short rentals but may be provided on a monthly rental, so bring or buy your own for a short rental. ## Riding when you cannot see well

### Speed: ride within your sight distance

The key principle is to be able to stop within the distance you can see. If the visible road ahead is 20 metres, your stopping distance, including your reaction time and the longer wet-road braking distance, must fit within it. A rule of thumb from rider guidance is that wet roads need roughly double the dry stopping distance, and poor visibility gives you less warning. So the answer to poor visibility is almost always a lower speed.

### Gap: more space, and out of the spray

- **Leave a larger gap** than in the dry, at least double the dry time gap.

- **Do not follow so closely that spray hides the road.** If you cannot see past the vehicle ahead, drop back.

- **Stay slightly to one side** of a large vehicle's wake, where the road allows.

- **Keep clear of the edge of the road,** where water collects.

### Lights: switch on, and use low beam

- **Switch on your headlight,** in daytime as well as at night, if rain limits visibility.

- **Use low beam,** not high beam. High beam in rain reflects off the water and spray and can dazzle others.

- **Keep your brake light working.** Tap the brake early, so that the rider behind sees you slowing.

### Eyes: look through, not at

- **Look ahead,** to where you want to go, and scan from side to side.

- **Look through the gaps** between vehicles to the brake lights further ahead.

- **Do not stare at oncoming headlights.** Look to the right edge of your lane if you are dazzled, and slow down.

- **Check your mirrors** more often, because following vehicles are harder to see.

### Hands and controls: stay smooth

Cold, wet hands lose feel. Keep a relaxed grip, make smooth inputs and brake early, with both brakes, while upright. Our guide to [braking safely on wet road markings](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/braking-safely-on-wet-road-markings/) explains why painted markings and metal covers are slippery.

## Do not use an umbrella

Article 33 of the Law says that drivers of two-wheeled motorcycles, three-wheeled motorcycles and mopeds may not use umbrellas, and that passengers on them may not use umbrellas either. An umbrella catches the wind, blocks your view and takes a hand off the bars. Use a raincoat or poncho instead, and make sure it is the right length and fastened. Our guide to [how to carry a raincoat and helmet on a small manual bike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-carry-a-raincoat-and-helmet-on-a-small-manual-bike/) explains how to stow one.

## Being seen: help others see you

Rain makes every rider less visible.

- **Keep your headlight and tail light on and clean.**

- **Signal earlier than usual.** Published guidance says brake lights and indicators are harder to pick out through rain and dirty windows, so give drivers more time to process your intention.

- **Assume you have not been seen** until another driver proves otherwise, especially at junctions. Our guide to [crossing Hanoi intersections](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/crossing-hanoi-intersections/) explains how to cross.

- **Wear a bright or light-coloured raincoat,** and reflective strips if you have them.

- **Keep your place in the lane,** and avoid weaving.

- **Do not stop in the middle of the road.**

## Fogged visors and glare: practical fixes

- **At a stop,** open the visor briefly to clear the fog, and close it again when moving. Cracking the visor open helps, but it also admits rain, so it is a compromise.

- **Do not wipe with a gloved hand** while riding, because it smears the water and takes a hand off the bars.

- **At night,** wet roads reflect lights into your eyes. Slow down, and look at the edge of the road, not at the lights.

- **If your view is compromised,** reduce speed at once and create space. Published guidance says that if your view is compromised, you should reduce speed immediately and create space.

## When to stop, and where

There is a point at which the right decision is to stop.

- **Stop if you cannot see the road** or the vehicle ahead, if your visor is fogged beyond repair or if the rain is so heavy that your speed has fallen to a crawl.

- **Choose a safe place:** a petrol station, a covered parking area, a shop front with room or any safe place away from moving traffic. Article 18 requires that stopping and parking be done close to the right roadside, without causing obstruction or danger, and with a signal when entering or leaving a stopping place.

- **Do not shelter under an overpass or on a bridge** where stopped vehicles and low visibility can create another hazard. Article 18 bans stopping on bridges and under overpasses except in places where it is allowed.

- **Do not park on the carriageway,** and do not block a footpath or a driveway.

- **If you have to stop** because of a fault, switch on your hazard lights and, if the bike is on part of the carriageway, place a warning sign behind it if it is safe.

- **If lightning is close,** follow the guidance in our guide to [what to do during a sudden Hanoi thunderstorm](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-to-do-during-a-sudden-hanoi-thunderstorm/).

## A quick checklist

1. **Slow down to a speed at which you can stop within the distance you can see.**

2. **Switch on the headlight, low beam.**

3. **Double your following gap, and keep out of the spray.**

4. **Clean and defog your visor, and use a clear one.**

5. **Signal early, and tap the brake early.**

6. **Never use an umbrella.**

7. **Stop in a safe place if you cannot see.**

## FAQ: riding in rain when visibility is poor

### Do I need my headlight on in the rain?

Yes. Article 20 of Law 36/2024/QH15 requires front lights whenever rain, fog, smoke, dust or bad weather limits visibility, and from 18:00 to 06:00.

### Can I use an umbrella on a scooter?

No. Article 33 bans riders and passengers on motorcycles and mopeds from using umbrellas.

### How fast should I ride?

Slowly enough to stop within the distance you can see. Wet roads roughly double the dry stopping distance as a rule of thumb.

### How do I stop my visor fogging?

Use an anti-fog treatment or insert, open the vents and clean the visor properly. At a stop, open the visor briefly to clear it.

### Where should I stop if I cannot see?

In a safe place away from traffic, such as a petrol station or covered parking, and not on a bridge, under an overpass or on the carriageway.

### Should I use high beam in heavy rain?

No. Use low beam. High beam reflects off rain and spray and can dazzle others.
