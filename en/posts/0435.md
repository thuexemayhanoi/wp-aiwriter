A low advertised rental price may not be the final price because an advertised figure is usually a starting point for a particular bike, particular dates and particular conditions, and the real cost depends on the exact bike you receive, how many 24-hour days are counted, which extras you take, what charges apply if you are late or return the bike with an empty tank and how the deposit is handled. Nguyen Ha's own published rates are reference ranges, and the shop says the exact price is confirmed on the actual bike, its condition and your dates before you sign, which is how a careful shop should work.

The danger is not a range. It is a price that stays low until you are at the counter, with the bike changed, a fee added or a condition revealed that was not in the advertisement. This guide explains the ways a price can move, shows a table of the usual causes, lists the published charges at Nguyen Ha as a reference, explains how to spot a bait-and-switch and sets out how to get one honest total before you pay. It is general information, not a judgement on any shop.

## What an advertised price really is

An advertisement has to be short. A price on a board, a map listing or a social-media post is a headline, and a headline leaves things out. It may describe the cheapest bike in a range, the lowest rate for the longest period, a promotional rate or a rate that has not been updated. It may not say how a day is counted, what the deposit is or what is excluded.

Nguyen Ha's published figures show the pattern. For the manual group, which covers the Honda Wave, Yamaha Sirius and Honda Blade, the reference rates are VND 150,000–200,000 per day, VND 700,000–800,000 per week and VND 1,000,000–1,200,000 per month. They are ranges because bikes differ in age, model and condition. The shop's pages say that a model mentioned online may not be available for your dates and that the exact price is confirmed on the actual bike, its condition and your dates before you sign. That is honest. A listing that gives one low number and no range, no conditions and no way to confirm the bike deserves more questions.

## The usual ways a price changes

| Cause | What happens | How to guard against it |
|---|---|---|
| A different bike | The advertised model is not available, and another costs more | Ask for the exact model and price in writing |
| Day counting | A rental day is 24 hours, and shorter use is charged as a full day | Ask how a day is counted, and set your pickup time |
| Period | Daily, weekly and monthly packages are priced separately | Ask for the price for your exact dates |
| Late return | A charge per hour, then a full extra day | Know the rule, and leave a margin |
| Fuel | A fee for an empty tank | Check the fuel rule at handover |
| Delivery | A fee, and only for some rentals | Ask whether it applies and what it costs |
| Extras | A second helmet, a phone holder, a raincoat | Ask what is included and what costs extra |
| Deposit | A larger deposit than you expected | Ask the amount before you travel |
| Damage | Charges after return | Record the bike's condition at handover |
| Payment | A fee for cards or a different currency | Ask how you can pay, and at what cost |

Each of these can be reasonable. The problem is when you learn about them only after you have committed.

## Day counting changes the real number of days

A rental day is a block of hours, not a calendar date. At Nguyen Ha it runs from the moment you take the bike to the same clock time the next day, not to midnight. That matters in two directions. If you pick up at 17:00 and return at 09:00 the next day, you have used 16 hours but are charged one full day. If you pick up at 10:00 and return at 17:00 the next day, you are 7 hours over 24 hours, so the late rule applies: one extra day from 6 hours late. Our guide to [how a 24-hour rental day is counted in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-a-24-hour-rental-day-is-counted-in-hanoi/) works through the examples. A shop that counts differently can make a low daily rate cost more or less in practice, so ask.

## When the bike you are shown is not the bike advertised

This is the oldest way for a price to move. The advertisement shows a newer or better bike, and what is available is older, or smaller, or in poorer condition, or a different class at a different price. Ask before you pay:

- **Which exact bike will I get,** the model, the engine size and the year?

- **Is it the one in the picture?**

- **What is its condition,** and when was it last serviced?

- **Will the price change if the model changes?**

Nguyen Ha's pages advise asking the shop to confirm the exact vehicle, engine capacity, accessories and condition before paying. ## The deposit is not part of the price, but it affects your cash

A cash deposit of VND 2,000,000–5,000,000 is separate from the rent and is meant to be returned, but it is money you must have on the day, and it is released only after the bike is checked. If a shop's headline rate is low and the deposit is high, or if the deposit return is vague, the real cost of the rental to you includes the time your money is held and the risk that deductions are made. Ask for the deposit amount, what it covers and when it comes back, as our guide to [how to check deposit return conditions before signing](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-check-deposit-return-conditions-before-signing/) explains. Our guide to [understanding the difference between rent and deposit](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/understanding-the-difference-between-rent-and-deposit/) separates the two.

## Charges that appear after the rental

Some costs only appear at return. They are fair if they are in the agreement and tied to what happened, and unfair if they are new. Examples include:

- a late-return charge you were not told about;

- a fee for an empty tank, or a fuel top-up at a rate you did not agree;

- a charge for cleaning, or for ordinary wear;

- a damage charge you cannot check against a record.

The shop's own pages say that damage from a collision, accident or misuse is priced by its cause, the actual condition of the bike and the contract, and that a renter may bear a cost in proportion to the real damage. The best protection is a handover record, with photographs, so that you and the shop can compare the bike at return. ## Signs of a bait-and-switch

- **A very low price** that no other shop comes near.

- **No exact model,** only "a scooter" or "a Honda".

- **"From" prices** with no statement of the real price.

- **The price changes at the counter,** with no clear reason.

- **A new fee** is added when you are ready to pay.

- **A pressure to decide quickly,** or "this price ends today".

- **A refusal to put the price in writing.**

- **A request for a passport** as the deposit, instead of cash. Our guide to [how a motorbike rental deposit works in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbike-rental-deposit-hanoi/) explains why you should not agree.

If you see several, walk away. There are other shops.

## What the law says about price

Vietnam's Civil Code (Law No. 91/2015/QH13), in force since 1 January 2017, says that the rent in a lease is set by agreement or by a third party at the parties' request, and that where there is no agreement or it is unclear, the rent is the market price at the place and time the contract was made (Article 473). We read the lease articles in the text published by the Tuyen Quang Department of Justice. As of October 2026, that points to the same practical advice: the agreed price, in writing, is what counts. The Code also says the lessor must hand over the property in the agreed quality and condition (Article 476), so a bike that differs from what was agreed is something you can raise. This is general information, not legal advice.

## How to get one honest total

1. **Name the exact bike and the exact dates and times.**

2. **Ask for a written quote** that states the price, how a day is counted, the deposit and its return, the late and fuel charges, what is included and any delivery charge.

3. **Ask what could change the price,** and ask for the answer in writing.

4. **Ask how you can pay,** and whether any fee applies.

5. **Confirm the price on the actual bike** before you sign, as Nguyen Ha says it does.

6. **Compare it with your written quote.** If anything differs, ask why before you pay.

7. **Photograph the price board** and keep the messages.

## FAQ: advertised and final rental prices

### Why is the price on the shop's page a range?

Because bikes differ in model, age and condition. Nguyen Ha says the exact price is confirmed on the actual bike, its condition and your dates before you sign.

### Can the bike I see online be unavailable?

Yes. The shop's pages say a model mentioned online may not be available for your dates. Ask before you pay.

### What extras should I ask about?

Delivery, helmets, a raincoat, fuel, late return and any damage or cleaning charges.

### Is the deposit part of the price?

No. It is separate and meant to be returned, but it is cash you need on the day, and it is released after the bike is checked.

### What if the price changes at the counter?

Ask why, compare it with your written quote and decline if it does not make sense.

### How do I avoid surprises?

Get a written quote for the exact bike and dates, record the bike's condition at handover and keep every message.
