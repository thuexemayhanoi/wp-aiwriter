In Vietnam a moped may not be ridden faster than 40 km/h on roads other than expressways, and motorbikes of any size are kept off expressways altogether. As of October 2026, Circular 38/2024/TT-BGTVT, in force since 1 January 2025, sets 40 km/h as the maximum operating speed for mopeds, special-purpose machines and similar vehicles, while the bike itself is defined as having a design speed of no more than 50 km/h. That makes the moped limit the lowest of any ordinary two-wheeler on the road, and lower than the 50 to 70 km/h ceilings that apply to larger motorcycles. This guide explains what counts as a moped, where the 40 km/h figure comes from, how it relates to speed signs, what exceeding it can cost and how a rider on a rented 50cc bike stays comfortably inside it.

## Which vehicles count as mopeds

The limit applies by vehicle class, so the first question is whether the bike you are on is a moped. Law 36/2024/QH15 (Article 34) defines a moped, in Vietnamese xe gan may, as a two- or three-wheeled motor vehicle with a design speed of no more than 50 km/h. If it has a petrol engine, its displacement must be no more than 50 cm3. If it has an electric motor, the motor must be rated at no more than 4 kW. A motorcycle is anything with two or three wheels that is not a moped.

In practice this covers the classic Cub-style 50cc bike, small 50cc automatic scooters and low-powered electric scooters that meet the 4 kW and 50 km/h tests. It does not cover the ordinary rental scooters and semi-automatics of 100 cc and above, which are motorcycles and follow the higher limits. Our guide to [speed limits for motorbikes in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/speed-limits-motorbikes-vietnam/) explains those higher figures road by road.

The class is decided by the bike's specification, not by how it looks. A scooter with a 50cc badge that has been modified to go faster is no longer what the paperwork says it is. If you are unsure, ask the shop to show you the engine size on the registration document before you rent.

## Where the 40 km/h figure comes from

Circular 38/2024/TT-BGTVT replaced the earlier speed circular from the start of 2025. It sets general maximum speeds for each vehicle class, and for special-purpose machines, mopeds and similar vehicles the wording published by Thu Vien Phap Luat is that the maximum operating speed is 40 km/h, outside expressways. Secondary sources cite the provision under slightly different article numbers, so check the official text on vbpl.vn if you need to quote it exactly.

Two points follow from the way it is written:

- **It is a ceiling for the vehicle type.** It does not depend on whether the road is divided or whether you are in a built-up area, unlike the figures for larger motorcycles.
- **It sits below the design speed.** A moped may be built to reach 50 km/h, but the rider is only permitted to use 40.

## Mopeds compared with other two-wheelers

| Vehicle | Typical class | General ceiling inside residential areas |
|---|---|---|
| 50cc Cub or 50cc scooter | Moped | 40 km/h |
| Electric scooter up to 4 kW and 50 km/h design speed | Moped | 40 km/h |
| Honda Wave, Vision, Air Blade and similar | Motorcycle | 50 or 60 km/h depending on the road |

The motorcycle figures are summarised from the same circular and are explained in our wider speed-limit guide. The point of the table is the contrast: the moped is held to one low number, and the motorcycle to a range that depends on the road type.

## How speed signs interact with the moped limit

A red-circled speed sign is the figure that governs a stretch of road for the vehicles it applies to. Because mopeds already have a lower general ceiling, the safe reading is as follows:

- **A sign lower than 40 km/h.** Obey the sign. Signs near schools, bridges, tunnels, junctions and some Hanoi avenues carry lower numbers than the general rule.
- **A sign higher than 40 km/h.** Do not treat it as permission to go faster. The 40 km/h ceiling is the maximum for a moped, and a number set for motorcycles or cars does not raise it. This is a practical reading of the rule, so if you ever need certainty for a particular road, ask the shop or a local.
- **No sign.** The general ceiling for your vehicle type applies, which for a moped is 40 km/h.
- **A plate below the sign.** Read it. Some signs apply to particular vehicle types or lanes, and the number may not be meant for you.

Look out also for the signs that tell you which lane a motorbike should use, because the speed shown for a lane applies to the vehicles allowed in it. ## Expressways and fast roads

Vietnamese expressways are closed to motorbikes of every class, so there is no expressway speed for a moped to learn. The rule is broader than the speed circular and sits in the traffic law itself, which keeps expressways for cars, buses and lorries. A moped will also find several of Hanoi's busiest ring roads and elevated sections off limits or restricted to the lower road. ## What exceeding the limit can cost

Speeding penalties are set in Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, in force from 1 January 2025. Article 7 covers riders of motorcycles and mopeds, and published summaries give three bands:

- **5 km/h to under 10 km/h over the limit:** a fine of VND 400,000 to 600,000.
- **10 km/h to 20 km/h over:** a fine of VND 800,000 to 1,000,000.
- **More than 20 km/h over:** a fine of VND 6,000,000 to 8,000,000, with points deducted from a driving licence in the most serious band.

For a moped with a 40 km/h ceiling, those bands begin at 45 km/h. That is a speed a 50cc bike can reach downhill or with a tailwind, so the ceiling is easier to exceed than it sounds. Decree 238/2026/ND-CP, in force from 15 August 2026, amended parts of Decree 168. Published guidance on it describes mainly wording changes to match the new traffic law, and we found no report of a change to the speeding bands, but check the current text before relying on any figure. ## Why a moped rider should treat 40 km/h as a ceiling, not a target

The law sets a maximum, and a small bike in dense traffic rarely needs it. Hanoi's average rush-hour speed was 14.3 km/h in 2025 according to TomTom's traffic index, so most of a city ride is spent well below 40 km/h. A few conditions are worth keeping in mind:

- **Downhill and tailwinds.** A light bike picks up speed without any extra throttle on a flyover descent. Ease off and use the brakes early.
- **Wet roads.** Small, narrow tyres lose grip sooner than wide ones, and braking distances grow in rain.
- **Junctions and school gates.** The traffic law separately expects every rider to slow down where conditions require it, whatever the posted figure.
- **Night.** A 50cc bike's headlight is modest, so the speed at which you can still stop within the lit area is lower than the daytime limit.

## Speedometers, tolerance and cameras

Small scooters rarely have precise speedometers, and the instrument on a rental bike may read high or low. Two habits are more reliable than relying on the dial. Ride at the pace of the traffic around you in the city, which is almost always below 40 km/h. And on open roads, check your speed against a navigation app's reading, which is based on satellite positioning, while remembering that the figure the authorities use is their own.

Fixed and mobile cameras and roadside checks exist in Hanoi and on the highways. A fine detected by a camera is addressed to the registered owner of the bike, which for a rental is the shop, and the rental contract records who had the bike. Keep your contract and ask at handover how later notices are handled.

## A simple routine for a 50cc rental

1. Confirm with the shop that the bike is a moped and note the engine size on the papers.
2. Take a few minutes on a quiet road to learn what 30 and 40 km/h feel like on this particular bike.
3. Use the right-hand side of the road, as faster motorcycles will pass you on the left.
4. Ease off on descents and approaches to junctions.
5. If a route would make you ride faster than 40 km/h for any distance, choose a different route or a different bike. ## FAQ: moped speed limits

### What is the maximum speed for a moped in Vietnam?

40 km/h on roads other than expressways, under Circular 38/2024/TT-BGTVT in force since 1 January 2025. A moped's design speed may be up to 50 km/h, but that is a design figure, not a permission to ride at 50.

### Does a higher speed sign let a moped go faster?

The safe reading is no. The 40 km/h ceiling belongs to the vehicle class, so a higher number posted for other traffic does not lift it. Where a sign shows a lower number, obey it.

### Is a 50cc scooter always a moped?

Not always by name alone. The legal test is a design speed of no more than 50 km/h and an engine of no more than 50 cm3, or an electric motor of no more than 4 kW. Ask the shop for the engine size and class.

### Can a moped use the expressway?

No. Motorcycles and mopeds are banned from expressways, so there is no expressway speed limit for them.

### What happens if a moped exceeds the limit?

The rider can be fined under Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, with the bands starting at 5 km/h over the limit, and the amount rises with the margin. Check current guidance for the latest figures.

### Do speed limits for mopeds apply to electric scooters?

They apply to electric bikes that meet the moped definition, a motor of no more than 4 kW and a design speed of no more than 50 km/h. Our guide to [electric bikes in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/electric-bikes-in-hanoi-rules/) covers the rules for electric models in more detail.
