You can combine Co Loa Citadel and Bat Trang in a day on a rented scooter, but only if you treat the day as a budget of daylight and not a list of sights. The two places are not neighbours: Co Loa is in Dong Anh, north of Hanoi, and Bat Trang is in Gia Lam, south-east of the centre, so the ride between them is a real third leg. Add about two hours on foot at each site, a proper lunch, fuel and a safety margin before dusk, and a winter day from a 09:00 shop opening is already full. Tour operators sell the pair as a seven or eight hour day with transport included, which is a useful warning. This guide shows how to do the sum before you commit, which stop to put first, three pacing rules that keep the day calm, and a split plan for a relaxed pace over two half-days.

## Why these two are not an easy pair

Co Loa Citadel lies in Dong Anh district, roughly 17 kilometres north of central Hanoi by Wikipedia's description, in the plain north of the Red River. Vietnam's national tourism administration describes a conservation area of about 830 hectares and some 60 relics, among them the An Duong Vuong Temple, the Cao Lo Temple and the Trong Thuy and My Chau well. Bat Trang is a ceramics village in Gia Lam district on the left bank of the Red River, about 13 kilometres south-east of the centre in a Vietnam Airlines guide, though other guides give 10 to 20 kilometres depending on the starting point.

Notice what that means. From the centre the two lie in different directions, one to the north and one to the south-east. Each is a reasonable ride from the shop in Bo De Ward, Long Bien, on its own, but going from one to the other is a separate journey that you cannot read off a brochure. I could not find a published distance between them that I trust, so do not take anyone's number on trust, including mine. Ask a map app for the shop-to-first-stop, first-to-second and second-to-shop times at the hour you plan to ride, and write down the longest figure for each.

## What tour operators tell us

Commercial listings show how the pair is usually sold. One booking platform lists full-day Vespa, jeep and bicycle tours of Bat Trang and Co Loa at about seven to eight hours with pickup, and another operator's programme pairs the two as a morning or afternoon trip with hotel transfers. Those day lengths include guides who know the road, vehicles that do not need parking, and sometimes a meal.

A self-guided rider has more freedom and less support, so a similar day with no pickup and a rented scooter should be planned at least as generously. If a guided group needs seven hours, you should not expect to fit the same sights into five.

## The daylight sum

This is the heart of the plan. Use your own figures from the map, and treat the allowances below as planning guesses rather than measured times.

| Block | Planning allowance |
|---|---|
| Collect and inspect the bike | 30 minutes |
| Co Loa on foot | 1.5 to 2 hours |
| Bat Trang on foot, with market | 1.5 to 2 hours |
| One proper lunch | 1 hour |
| Fuel, parking and short breaks | 45 minutes |
| Safety margin before dusk | 45 to 60 minutes |
| **Total without riding** | **about 6 to 7 hours** |

Sunset in Hanoi from late October to January is about 17:15 to 17:30, according to published sun tables, so counting back a margin the useful day runs from about 09:30 to about 16:45. That is roughly seven hours. If the non-riding blocks take six or seven of those hours, only an hour or less is left for the three legs of riding. Unless your map shows very short legs, the full combination does not fit in a winter day.

The sum is easier in other months, but hot afternoons and sudden storms eat time too. Vietnam Airlines suggests spring and autumn for Bat Trang, and cooler months are generally kinder for a long day on foot.

## A rule for deciding: add the three legs

Before you leave the shop, add the three riding times from your map.

- **Under about two hours in total,** the one-day combination is workable with trimmed on-site times.
- **Between two and three hours,** choose one site as the main event and the other as a short look, or split the day.
- **Over three hours,** split it into two separate half-days, one for each place.

These thresholds are my planning rule of thumb, not figures from an official source. Their purpose is to make you look at the real map numbers before you start.

## Which one first?

There is a case for each, and your day decides.

**Co Loa first** suits a morning start. It involves more walking outdoors across a village site with temples, a lake and rampart sections, so cooler hours help. The scooter spends the early part of the day on roads that are busiest with commuters, so leave only after the shop opens and the worst of the rush is past. By early afternoon you move to Bat Trang, where shops, the market and the museum are set up for visitors until later in the day.

**Bat Trang first** suits a rider who wants an easy start. The village is open, according to a Vietnam Airlines guide, from 08:00 to 17:00, with its museum open to 17:30 on weekdays and 18:00 at weekends, so you have flexibility there. Co Loa is reported to open from 08:00 to 17:00, so doing it second leaves a tight window. If you choose Bat Trang first, make sure you reach Co Loa with at least two hours of light.

My recommendation for a winter day is Co Loa first and Bat Trang second, because the more time-sensitive and weather-sensitive outdoor site comes in the cooler morning, and the second stop has the more forgiving hours. Reverse the order if the forecast makes Co Loa's open paths a poor choice in the morning, or if your map shows Bat Trang closer to the shop and you want the shorter ride at the end.

## Three pacing rules that stop the rush

1. **Fix lunch first.** Pick a time, around the middle of the day, and treat it as immovable. A day that has a fixed meal has a fixed hinge, and you can see at once whether you are early or late.

2. **Set an alarm for leaving each site,** not for arriving. Time at a temple or a market stretches without anyone noticing. Decide the leaving time before you go in, and leave when it rings, even if there is one more lane to see.

3. **Protect the last ninety minutes.** Whatever happens, start for the shop by your turnaround time. Our guide to [riding at night in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-at-night-in-hanoi/) explains why dusk riding is best avoided, and the simple way to work this out is to count back from sunset.

A few smaller habits help. Eat before you are hungry, drink water at each stop, and turn the phone's navigation volume up so you can listen instead of staring at the screen. Our guide on [planning breaks](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-plan-breaks-on-a-long-semi-automatic-bike-ride/) explains rhythm, and [when to refuel on a day trip](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/when-to-refuel-a-manual-motorbike-during-a-day-trip/) explains topping up.

## Three ways to arrange the days

| Plan | When it suits | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| One full day, both sites | Short legs on your map, an early start, a confident rider | Co Loa 09:45 to 11:45, lunch, Bat Trang 13:15 to 15:15, return by 16:45 |
| Two half-days | Most visitors, most months | One site per half-day, with a relaxed lunch and a margin |
| One site plus a quiet loop | Limited time, rain, a passenger with luggage | Pick the site that interests you most and use the spare time for the area around it |

## What to do at each place

You do not need a full guide to each site here, just a plan that suits a combined day.

**At Co Loa,** you are in a living village with temples, a lake, a well and earth ramparts. A travel-style visit of two to three stops is enough: for example the An Duong Vuong Temple, the well and a short stretch of rampart. The main local festival is on the sixth day of the first lunar month, and a Vietnam Tourism article says it draws many visitors, so avoid that date if you want a calm combined day. The site's management board is part of the Thang Long-Hanoi Heritage Conservation Centre and charges entrance fees under city regulations, so ask at the entrance what applies on the day. Dress modestly for the temples and ride slowly in the village.

**At Bat Trang,** you are in a working craft village with a market, workshops and a museum. Park at the first guarded space, walk the lanes and keep a rough limit on the museum or workshop if you add one, since both can swallow an hour. Entry to the village is reported as free, but the museum and workshops charge, so ask before joining.

## Carrying pottery home after a long day

Put Bat Trang last so you do not carry fragile goods through Co Loa and the rest of the ride. Buy small, wrap each piece well and ask the seller about packing or shipping. Remember that the scooter has limited storage: Honda's manuals give small limits for the under-seat box and the rear rail, and the law bars carrying bulky loads and goods that overhang the carrier. Our guide to [how much luggage a Honda Wave can carry](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-much-luggage-can-you-safely-carry-on-a-honda-wave/) explains the numbers, and [parking a motorbike in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-motorbike-in-hanoi/) explains how to leave a loaded bike safely.

## Choosing the route between the sites

Use a map app to compare two or three ways, and check that none of them puts a motorbike on a road where it is not allowed. Our guides to [which ring roads and highways allow motorbikes](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-ring-roads-highways-motorbikes-allowed/) and [how to check if a navigation app suggests an expressway](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-check-if-a-navigation-app-suggests-an-expressway/) show how. Prefer the main road, avoid village shortcuts when you are tired and read signs on dyke roads. ## If the weather or the clock turns

- **Rain:** drop the second site, not the margin. See [riding in Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/).
- **Heat:** do the ramparts and open paths early, and keep the market and museum for the warmest hours.
- **Running late:** skip the workshop or museum, shorten lunch last, and never skip the turnaround.

## FAQ: combining Co Loa and Bat Trang

### Can I see Co Loa and Bat Trang in one day?

Yes, but it is a full day. Tour operators schedule seven to eight hours for both, and a winter day has only about seven useful hours of light.

### Which should I do first?

Co Loa in the morning, because it is the more outdoor and weather-sensitive stop, and Bat Trang in the afternoon. Reverse it if the map or the forecast suggests otherwise.

### How far apart are they?

I could not find a trustworthy published figure. Both are about 13 to 20 kilometres from the centre in different directions, so ask a map app for the actual leg.

### Are there entrance fees?

Bat Trang village is reported as free, with charges for the museum and workshops. Co Loa has entrance fees set by the heritage board, so ask on the day.

### Is it better to split it over two days?

For a first visit, yes. Two half-days give each place enough time.
