A Vietnamese prohibitory sign tells you what you may not do, and when it shows a vehicle symbol, it bans that type of vehicle. The sign for motorcycles and the sign for mopeds look similar but are different, so the first thing to know is which of the two your rental is. Under Article 34 of Law 36/2024/QH15, a moped is a two- or three-wheeled motor vehicle with a design speed of no more than 50 km/h and, if it has a petrol engine, a capacity of no more than 50 cm³ or, if electric, a motor of no more than 4 kW. A motorcycle is a two- or three-wheeled motor vehicle that is not a moped. So a 50cc rental is a moped, and a 110cc Honda Wave or Vision is a motorcycle. If a sign bans the class your bike belongs to, you may not enter.

This guide explains the basic look of a prohibition sign, how the main vehicle symbols differ, how to tell the moped sign from the motorcycle sign, how the law's classes apply to petrol and electric rentals, what supplementary panels and end signs do, what to do when you meet one and what the fine is. It is general information based on the law and published guidance as of October 2026, not legal advice.

## What a prohibition sign looks like

Article 11 of the Law says prohibitory signs are to indicate prohibitions. The usual design, described in the national technical regulation and in Vietnamese guidance, is a circle with a red border and a white background, with a black picture inside. A red diagonal bar across the picture is common. The picture shows what is banned.

Because the national regulation on road signs has been reissued over the years as QCVN 41:2024/BGTVT, and older books and websites use earlier code numbers, this guide describes signs by their pictures and meaning and not by code numbers. Where a sign is unclear, treat it as applying to you until you have checked.

## The main vehicle symbols

| What the picture shows | What it bans, in general | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| A car | Cars | Does not by itself ban motorbikes |
| A lorry | Goods lorries, sometimes by weight shown on the sign | A weight on the sign limits which lorries |
| A bus | Buses | |
| A bicycle | Bicycles | May not cover people pushing them |
| A pedestrian | Pedestrians | |
| A motorcycle with a rider on it | Motorcycles | Vietnamese guidance says people pushing a motorcycle are not covered |
| A small motorbike with no rider | Mopeds | Vietnamese guidance says bicycles are not covered |
| Two overlapping pictures | Several classes at once | Read each picture |
| An empty red circle | All vehicles | Except priority vehicles |
| A red circle with a white bar | No entry from this direction | For example, the wrong end of a one-way street |

These are the common patterns. Always read the actual picture, and read any panel below it. ## Motorcycle or moped: the sign that matters most for riders

Vietnamese guides, including a legal-information site and a newspaper article from 2026, make the same point about the two signs.

- **The moped sign** shows a small motorbike without a rider, and it bans mopeds. Mopeds are bikes up to 50 cm³ or electric up to 4 kW. The guidance notes that it does not apply to bicycles.

- **The motorcycle sign** shows a motorcycle with a rider on it, and it bans motorcycles, the bigger bikes. The guidance notes that it does not ban people who walk and push their motorcycles.

An important consequence follows. If a road has only the moped sign, a larger motorcycle is not banned by that sign, unless another sign also applies. If a road has the motorcycle sign, it bans motorcycles. Whether a 50cc moped is also covered depends on the signs actually posted, so ask before entering with a 50cc bike. Do not guess from the picture alone, and when two signs are present, both apply.

## Which sign applies to your rental

The class of your bike decides, and the class depends on engine capacity and power.

| Rental type | Legal class (Article 34) | Typical sign that applies |
|---|---|---|
| 50cc petrol bike | Moped | Moped sign |
| Electric scooter with a motor up to 4 kW and design speed up to 50 km/h | Moped | Moped sign |
| 110cc to 160cc petrol scooter or manual bike | Motorcycle | Motorcycle sign |
| Faster electric scooter above 4 kW | Motorcycle | Motorcycle sign |
| Pedal-assist electric bicycle that cuts power at 25 km/h | Motorised bicycle, a rudimentary vehicle | Check; may fall under the bicycle sign |

If you are not sure, ask the shop before you ride which legal class your bike belongs to. ## Supplementary panels and end signs

A panel under a sign can limit it.

- **A time panel:** the ban applies at the times shown, and not at others.

- **A distance panel:** the ban applies for that distance.

- **A direction or lane panel:** the ban applies in one direction or on one lane.

- **A text panel:** read it, or take a photograph and check it.

The sign has force from where it stands. Some prohibitions have an end sign. Others have no end sign and apply until the next junction, or until another sign changes the rule, so do not assume it has ended because you cannot see another sign. Article 11(12) says that if a fixed sign and a temporary sign stand in the same place and mean different things, you obey the temporary one.

## What to do when you meet one

1. **Look at the picture and any panel,** from a distance, and slow down.

2. **Decide whether it applies to your bike,** using your bike's class.

3. **If it applies, do not enter.** Stop in a safe place, and turn back where it is lawful to do so, or find another route.

4. **If you are unsure, treat it as applying,** and check before going on.

5. **If you realise too late,** do not make a U-turn where the law bans one, and leave at the next legal place. Article 15 bans U-turns on bridges, on expressways and in several other places.

6. **Do not push the bike through** unless the guidance for that sign clearly allows it, and be aware that a guard or a local rule may differ.

## Fines

Entering a road where a sign bans your vehicle type is an offence. Vietnamese legal publications, citing Article 7 of Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, report a fine of VND 2,000,000 to 3,000,000 for a rider who enters a road or area that a sign prohibits for the type of vehicle being ridden, with 2 licence points deducted, and a fine of VND 10,000,000 to 14,000,000 with 10 points if it causes an accident. Check the current text of the decree and its amendments if a figure matters to you. A camera notice can reach the registered owner, so ask the shop how it handles notices. ## FAQ: prohibited vehicle symbols

### What is the difference between a moped and a motorcycle in Vietnam?

Under Article 34 of Law 36/2024/QH15, a moped has a design speed of no more than 50 km/h and, if petrol, a capacity of no more than 50 cm³ or, if electric, a motor of no more than 4 kW. A motorcycle is a two- or three-wheeled motor vehicle that is not a moped.

### How do I tell the two signs apart?

Vietnamese guidance says the motorcycle sign shows a rider on the bike, and the moped sign shows a small bike with no rider.

### Does a sign banning mopeds ban bicycles?

The guidance says no. A separate bicycle sign applies to bicycles.

### Does a sign banning motorcycles apply if I push my bike?

Vietnamese guidance says people who push a motorcycle are not covered, but follow any local instruction.

### Is my electric rental a moped?

If its motor is up to 4 kW and its design speed is up to 50 km/h, yes. Faster, stronger electric scooters are motorcycles. Ask the shop.

### What is the fine for entering a banned road?

Legal publications report VND 2,000,000 to 3,000,000 and 2 points under Decree 168/2024/ND-CP. Check the current text.
