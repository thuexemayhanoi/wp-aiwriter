The best short scenic ride in central Hanoi links two very different pieces of water: the broad calm of West Lake and the brown width of the Red River. It runs along the Thanh Nien causeway between West Lake and Truc Bach Lake, down Yen Phu towards the river and across the steel spans of Long Bien Bridge, where motorbikes have their own narrow lane beside the railway. It is short on the map, which is the point: you ride it slowly, stop for the view and finish on the east bank in Long Bien, where our shop is based. This guide gives the route in stages, explains what is special about each one, warns about the bridge's repair closures and Hanoi's new weekend rules in the Old Quarter and tells you when not to go.

## The route at a glance

| Stage | What you ride | What to look for |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | West Lake's south-east shore and the Thanh Nien causeway | Lake on both sides, pagoda on the water |
| 2 | Yen Phu and the streets leading to the river | The change from lakeside to dyke and traffic |
| 3 | The approach and Long Bien Bridge | Steel spans, railway, river views |
| 4 | Arrival on the Long Bien side | Bo De, the riverbank and the market area |

I have not verified turn-by-turn directions for every junction, and Hanoi's one-way streets and temporary diversions change. Use a map app set to motorbike, read the signs at each junction and treat the stages below as the outline, not a script.

## Stage 1: The causeway between two lakes

Thanh Nien Road is only about 1 km long, according to the Vietnamese Wikipedia entry on it, which calls it the shortest street in Hanoi. It runs from the Yen Phu slope at its north-eastern end to the Quan Thanh and Thuy Khue crossroads at the south-western end, and it separates West Lake from the smaller Truc Bach Lake. The causeway began as a small embankment that local fishing families built from the seventeenth century. Around 1959 and 1960 Hanoi's young people widened it, and it was renamed Thanh Nien, meaning "youth".

Today it is one of the best places in the city to slow down. West Lake is the city's largest lake, about 500 hectares with a shore of roughly 17 km, according to Vietnam Airlines' travel guide, and Truc Bach lies on the other side of the road. The guide also places Tran Quoc Pagoda, on a small island near the lake's south-eastern shore. Our guides to [Tay Ho and West Lake by scooter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/tay-ho-west-lake-by-scooter/) and [Ba Dinh by motorbike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/ba-dinh-by-motorbike/) cover the surrounding streets in more detail.

Ride the causeway, do not park on it. Traffic here is steady and the pavement is narrow, so if you want a photograph, find a proper place to stop first.

## Stage 2: Yen Phu and the way to the river

At the north-eastern end of Thanh Nien you reach the Yen Phu slope, and Yen Phu street takes you towards the Red River dyke. This is where the setting shifts from lakeside to ordinary city streets and the road gets busier. A Vietnam News report about the bridge's 2026 repair diversions shows how the streets connect. It says that vehicles coming from Yen Phu and Hang Dau were redirected to Tran Nhat Duat Street, then to the roundabout at the approach to Chuong Duong Bridge, which tells you these streets feed the bridge area.

This stretch can be slower than it looks. A Lao Dong report on the 2026 repair closure said that while the bridge was shut, roads around the river crossings, including Nguyen Van Cu, Tran Nhat Duat, Tran Quang Khai and Minh Khai, were frequently congested, especially at peak hours. The bridge is open again, but the report shows how busy these streets get when traffic is squeezed. Our guide to [crossing Hanoi intersections](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/crossing-hanoi-intersections/) explains how to merge, and the best answer is often to leave earlier or later.

## Does Hanoi's low-emission zone affect this route?

Since 1 July 2026 Hanoi has been running a pilot low-emission zone in the Old Quarter area of Hoan Kiem ward. According to the government's English news portal, the pilot has two zones. In Zone 1, which covers streets such as Trang Tien, Hang Khay, Hang Dao, Hang Ngang, Hang Bac and Ma May, all road vehicles including motorcycles, mopeds and cars are prohibited from 7 p.m. to midnight on Fridays, Saturdays and Sundays. Zone 2 surrounds it and includes Hang Dau, Tran Nhat Duat, Tran Quang Khai, Trang Tien and Hang Khay. VnExpress reports that the restriction there applies to fossil-fuel passenger vehicles with more than 16 seats, not to scooters.

For this route, the practical conclusion is simple: it does not need to enter Zone 1, but it does skirt the edge of the pilot area, so keep to the main streets, follow signs and avoid cutting into the Old Quarter on a Friday, Saturday or Sunday evening. Later phases are planned to widen the zone, with the area inside Ring Road 1 proposed for 2028 onwards, and that would include streets near this route, so check the current position when you ride. Our guide to [riding in the Old Quarter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-old-quarter/) has more detail.

## Stage 3: Crossing Long Bien Bridge

Long Bien Bridge was built between 1898 and 1902 under French rule. The tourism authority describes it as carrying a single railway track down the middle, with a motorbike and bicycle lane about 2.6 metres wide on each side and a narrow pedestrian path. Cars are banned. It was damaged by bombing in the 1960s and rebuilt, and it remains part of daily life.

Practical points for riders:

- **The lane is narrow.** Keep a steady line, do not weave and do not overtake aggressively.
- **Expect pedestrians, bicycles and people taking photographs.** Go slowly, and never stop in the lane.
- **Traffic keeps to the left on this bridge.** The national tourism authority notes that direction on the bridge is left-hand traffic, unlike other bridges, so follow the lane markings and the signs and do not assume.
- **Check the surface yourself.** Repairs have resurfaced the motorbike lane, but wet weather makes steel and concrete slick, so slow down in the rain.
- **Trains share the structure.** You will feel it and hear it. Do not park or dawdle near the rails.

The bridge is not always open to motorbikes. Hanoi suspended road traffic on it for 60 days, from 28 March to 27 May 2026, for urgent repairs to the walkway and the lanes for motorbikes and non-motorised vehicles. During that closure, riders were diverted through Long Bien streets and Long Bien to Xuan Quan road to Chuong Duong Bridge, or via Tran Nhat Duat on the other side. Reports published on 28 May 2026 said it had reopened to motorbikes, bicycles and pedestrians, after reinforcement of the steel beams, new slabs on the walkway and resurfacing of the motorbike lane. Closures of this sort can recur, so check current news before building a day around it.

If it is closed or you do not like the look of it, Chuong Duong Bridge next to it is the alternative, with a mixed traffic lane. Our guide to [Hanoi's ring roads and highways](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-ring-roads-highways-motorbikes-allowed/) explains how the Red River crossings differ.

## When to ride it

The ride is short, so timing matters more than distance.

- **Late morning on a weekday** is the calmest option, once the rush has eased.
- **Late afternoon** gives soft light over the water, but traffic builds from about the time offices close, and the road near the bridges becomes slower.
- **Weekend evenings** are the busiest, and the Old Quarter restrictions apply from 7 p.m. to midnight from Friday to Sunday.
- **Rain** makes the bridge and the lakeside road slippery, and our guide to [riding in Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) explains when to wait.
- **After dark,** lighting is uneven and the bridge is not a place to learn. Read [riding at night in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-at-night-in-hanoi/) before you plan an evening ride.

## Parking for a walk by the lake or river

Many riders will want to stop at the lake or the bridge on foot. Vietnam Airlines' walking-street guide lists motorbike parking on Tran Vu and Thanh Nien streets and near Truc Bach Park. I could not confirm the current fees or hours, and I have not quoted any, so check at the lot. Choose a guarded place, lock the steering and take your valuables with you. ## FAQ: West Lake to Long Bien

### How long does the ride take?

It is short, but allow an hour or more with stops, because the junctions near the bridges can be slow and you will want to pause for the views.

### Can I ride a motorbike across Long Bien Bridge?

Yes, in the motorbike lane, when the bridge is open. Cars are not allowed. The bridge was closed to motorbikes from 28 March to 27 May 2026 for repairs, so check for current closures.

### Is the Old Quarter restriction a problem?

Not for this route if you avoid the Old Quarter streets in Zone 1 on Friday to Sunday evenings. Check the current notices.

### Which way should I ride, east to west or west to east?

Either works. Starting at the lake and finishing on the Long Bien side means you cross the bridge towards the shop.

### What if the bridge is closed?

Use Chuong Duong Bridge, or reroute using your map app.
