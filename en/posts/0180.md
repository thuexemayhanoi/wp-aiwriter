The most reliable way to check the engine capacity of a rented 50cc bike is to ask to see the vehicle registration card and read the line marked "Dung tich", which gives the displacement in cubic centimetres. A genuine moped reads 49 or 50. Then check that the engine and frame numbers on the card match the numbers stamped on the bike, and photograph the card before you ride away. Badges, plate letters and the word "50cc" on a rental list are weaker evidence, because shops and sellers sometimes use "50cc" loosely for any small automatic scooter. This guide explains why the number matters, walks through the card step by step, shows what the plate and the bodywork can and cannot tell you and describes what to do if the figures do not fit. It is general information, not a legal opinion.

## Why the capacity on the paper matters

Vietnam's Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (Law 36/2024/QH15, in force since 1 January 2025) defines a moped, in Vietnamese xe gan may, by several conditions that must all be met. The vehicle must have two or three wheels, a design speed of no more than 50 km/h and, if it has a petrol engine, a working displacement of no more than 50 cubic centimetres. An electric moped must have a motor of no more than 4 kW. A bike that misses any one of these conditions is a motorcycle, and the rules for motorcycles apply to it. Our guide to [renting a 50cc scooter in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/renting-a-50cc-scooter-in-hanoi/) explains what that means for riders who do not hold a motorcycle licence.

So the displacement figure is not a technicality. If you chose a 50cc rental because it suits your situation, the paper figure is what supports that choice. A scooter described as "50cc" that is really a 110cc or 125cc machine would put you on the wrong side of the line without you noticing.

## Step one: ask for the registration card

Every registered bike has a registration certificate, which riders call the ca vet. It is a small card, and its colour and layout vary with the year of issue. Ask the shop to show it to you before you sign anything. The shop is open 09:00 to 21:00 daily.

What to look for on the card:

- **The displacement line.** In Vietnamese this reads "Dung tich" and the unit is cm3. A petrol moped shows 49 or 50.
- **The make and model.** They should fit the bike in front of you.
- **The plate number.** It should match the plate fitted to the bike.
- **The engine and frame numbers.** Compare them with the stamped numbers on the bike, as the next section explains.

If the card shows a displacement such as 108, 110, 124 or anything above 50, the bike is a motorcycle even if it is small, automatic and marketed as a scooter. Ask for a different bike.

## Step two: match the numbers on the card to the bike

The card lists a frame number and an engine number. Both are stamped into the metal of the bike itself. The frame number is usually found near the steering head or under the seat, and the engine number on the engine casing, although the exact place varies by model. Ask a staff member to point to them, then compare them character by character with the card.

This check matters for two reasons. First, if the numbers do not match, you cannot be sure the card describes this bike, so the displacement on it proves nothing about the engine you are about to ride. Second, Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, the road traffic penalty decree in force since 1 January 2025, lists riding a vehicle whose registration certificate does not match its frame and engine numbers among the offences. Our wording here is a summary, not legal advice. The practical point is simple: a mismatch is a reason to decline the bike and ask for another.

Photograph the card and the stamped numbers together, with the date visible on your phone if possible. The photos become part of your handover record and take less than a minute.

## What the licence plate can and cannot tell you

Some guides tell renters to identify a 50cc bike by its plate. Under the older rules in Circular 15/2014/TT-BCA, as explained by [VinFast](https://vinfastauto.com/vn_vi/cach-nhan-biet-xe-50cc-va-100cc), a 50cc moped carried a series of two letters, such as AA or HA, while a bike over 50cc carried a letter followed by a digit, such as M1 or P1. That is why you may still hear "two letters means 50cc".

It is no longer a safe test. Vietnamese press reported in December 2024 that, under the rules from 2025, the series on white motorbike plates has two letters whatever the engine size. Bikes registered earlier keep their old plates, so the plate now tells you roughly when and how the bike was registered, not its engine capacity. Treat the plate as a clue to cross-check against the card and never as proof.

## What the badge and the bodywork tell you

A real factory 50cc scooter usually carries a model name and sometimes a capacity mark on the side panel. This is weak evidence, because a badge can be changed and some bikes have none. On the other hand, a badge that contradicts the card is a warning sign.

The shape of the bike gives clues of a different sort. A classic Cub-style 50cc bike is a small, light underbone with a footshift and no clutch lever. A 50cc automatic scooter is typically smaller, with a short seat and a small storage box. A Honda Vision, a Yamaha Janus or an Air Blade is not a 50cc bike, even if a rental list calls it one, because those models have engines of about 110cc or more. If the bike looks like a mainstream scooter, check the card even more closely.

You cannot judge displacement by listening to the engine or by feeling how it pulls away. A 50cc engine is gentle and quiet and a 110cc engine in a light bike can feel similar in the first ten metres. Do not rely on ear or feel.

## A quick comparison of evidence

| Evidence | How reliable | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Displacement line on the registration card | High, if the numbers match the bike | Read it, photograph it |
| Frame and engine numbers matching the card | High | Compare character by character |
| Plate letters | Low since 2025 | Use only as a cross-check |
| Model badge on the panel | Low to medium | Check it agrees with the card |
| The word "50cc" on a rental list | Low | Ask which bike is meant |
| How the bike feels | Very low | Ignore it for this purpose |

## Electric and "50cc-equivalent" bikes

Electric bikes have no cubic centimetres. On an electric moped the card shows the motor power in kilowatts, and the moped limit is 4 kW. ## What to do if the figures do not fit

If the card shows more than 50 cm3, if it is missing, or if the numbers do not match the bike, do not argue about it at the kerb. Take these steps:

1. Say politely that you need a bike that is genuinely in the moped class.
2. Ask the shop to show you a different bike and its card.
3. If you hold a licence that covers motorcycles, you may choose a bigger bike on purpose. Our [motorbikes page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbikes/) shows what the shop lists.
4. Do not sign a contract that describes a different bike from the one you take.
5. Keep your photos in case you need to show what you were given.

## FAQ: checking engine capacity

### Where is the engine capacity written?

On the vehicle registration card, in the line marked "Dung tich", in cubic centimetres. A moped shows 49 or 50.

### Can I tell a 50cc bike from its number plate?

Not reliably any more. Under the 2025 plate rules, white motorbike plates carry two letters whatever the engine size, so the plate is only a clue.

### Does a "50cc" label on the rental list prove anything?

No. The term is sometimes used loosely for any small automatic. Check the registration card.

### What if the engine and frame numbers do not match the card?

Decline that bike. The card cannot be trusted to describe it, and the mismatch is a recognised offence under the traffic penalty decree.

### Is an electric scooter measured in cc?

No. It is rated in kilowatts, and the moped limit is 4 kW.

### Should I photograph the card?

Yes. Photograph the card and the stamped numbers before you leave the shop.
