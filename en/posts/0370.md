To plan a motorbike route around Hanoi's restricted roads, sort every restriction into one of four layers before you ride: roads closed to motorbikes at all times, roads or lanes assigned to certain vehicles, roads closed only at certain hours and roads closed temporarily for works or floods. The first layer is stable and written into law, the second is shown on signs and lane markings, the third changed in July 2026 when a pilot low-emission zone opened in the centre, and the fourth changes without notice. This guide gives you a method and a checklist rather than a list of streets, because the list moves faster than any article. It is general planning information as of October 2026, and the signs and officers on the day always take priority.

## Why a method beats a map of banned streets

A map of banned roads is out of date as soon as it is published. Hanoi's Long Bien Bridge, for example, was closed to road traffic for repairs in late 2025, reopened to motorbikes, bicycles and pedestrians on 1 January 2026, closed again from 28 March to 27 May 2026 and reopened on 28 May. A route that was perfect in February was impossible in April and fine again in June.

What stays useful is the way restrictions are organised. Once you know the four layers, you know what to look up, how often it changes and how much margin to leave. That is what this guide teaches. Our separate guide to [Hanoi's ring roads and highways](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-ring-roads-highways-motorbikes-allowed/) lists the main fast roads by name; this one is about planning around whatever the current list is.

## Layer 1: closed to motorbikes at all times

The firmest rule comes from national law. Law 36/2024/QH15 on road traffic order and safety, effective 1 January 2025, says in Article 25 that motorcycles, mopeds and similar vehicles may not use expressways, apart from people and vehicles serving the maintenance of the road. Engine size does not matter.

Related rules shape how you behave when a restriction is signed:

- **Dedicated lanes (Article 13).** Where a lane is reserved for one type of vehicle or group of types, drivers of other types may not enter it. This covers lanes marked for buses or for motorbikes.
- **Order of priority (Article 11).** The law ranks road signals from the instructions of a traffic officer, then traffic lights, then signs, then road markings and then physical markers such as bollards and barriers. If an officer waves you away from a road, that instruction beats the sign.
- **Bridges (Articles 14, 15 and 18).** You may not overtake on a narrow single-lane bridge, make a U-turn on a bridge or its approaches, or stop or park on a bridge unless traffic organisation allows it.

For a route planner the lesson is simple: treat any road with a motorway-style sign or a motorbike prohibition sign as closed, and draw the route on roads that have no such sign. Elevated ring-road decks and the central lanes of Thang Long Avenue are closed to motorbikes by Hanoi's own traffic organisation rather than by their being formal expressways, as our ring-road guide explains.

## Layer 2: roads and lanes assigned to vehicle types

Many Hanoi roads are open to motorbikes but only in a particular lane. Bridges are the clearest case. Reports describe Nhat Tan Bridge being reorganised in September 2025 from four mixed lanes into three car lanes and one motorbike lane, divided by steel barriers, with enforcement of lane violations planned from 1 October. A 2022 report on Thanh Tri Bridge described three car lanes and one motorbike lane, 2.9 metres wide, separated by a divider. Long Bien Bridge is different again: cars are banned, and motorbikes and bicycles share narrow side lanes on either side of the railway.

For planning, two points follow:

1. **Find the motorbike lane before you reach the bridge.** The lane is usually on the right, and the approach is where you choose it.
2. **Assume the layout may have changed since the last article you read.** Lane layouts have been reorganised repeatedly, so look at the signs and the barriers.

## Layer 3: closed only at certain hours

This is the layer that changed most recently. Hanoi's central pedestrian zone around Hoan Kiem Lake has existed since 2016, when the city opened it from 19:00 on Friday to 24:00 on Sunday. On 1 July 2026 the city began the first phase of a pilot low-emission zone in Hoan Kiem Ward, which runs until 31 December 2026.

According to VnExpress and VOV reports on the launch, in Zone 1, which includes the Hoan Kiem Lake pedestrian area, the Old Quarter and the weekend night market, motorcycles, motorbikes and all cars (apart from defence and public-security vehicles) may not enter from 7 p.m. to midnight on Fridays, Saturdays and Sundays. Zone 2 covers the wider Old Quarter and restricts large fossil-fuel passenger vehicles during the morning and evening peaks. Reports during the planning stage named 11 core streets: Trang Tien, Hang Khay, Le Thai To, Hang Dao, Hang Ngang, Hang Buom, Ma May, Hang Bac, Hang Mam, Nguyen Huu Huan and Ly Thai To.

Earlier drafts described a longer window for petrol motorbikes, beginning at 6 p.m. on Fridays and at 6 a.m. on Saturdays and Sundays, and news reports in spring 2026 disagreed about the details until the scheme was finalised. That is a good example of why you should check the current signs. Draft plans reported during 2026 would widen the area later, with 2027 for Cua Nam Ward and 2028 onward for the whole area inside Ring Road 1; treat those dates as plans, not guarantees.

What this means for a rental rider:

- **Friday to Sunday evenings:** do not plan to ride through the Old Quarter core between 7 p.m. and midnight. Park outside and walk. Our guides to [riding in the Old Quarter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-old-quarter/) and [parking near Hoan Kiem Lake](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/where-to-park-a-scooter-near-hoan-kiem-lake/) help.
- **Other times:** the core streets are still narrow, one-way in places and crowded, so a route that avoids them is usually faster anyway.
- **Parking:** the city reported reviewing 220 parking facilities and offering free public buses inside Ring Road 1 until 30 June 2027, so leaving the bike and travelling the last stretch is realistic.

## Layer 4: temporary closures

Temporary restrictions are the hardest to predict, and they come from four main causes.

- **Repairs.** During the 2026 closure of Long Bien Bridge, the Department of Construction diverted motorbikes to Chuong Duong Bridge, using the Long Bien to Xuan Quan road, Tran Nhat Duat and Tran Quang Khai depending on direction. Traffic on the diversions was heavier for weeks.
- **Floods.** In September 2024, after Typhoon Yagi raised the Red River, Hanoi shut Long Bien Bridge from 3 p.m. on 10 September until further notice, and banned coaches, large tourist cars and trucks from Chuong Duong Bridge.
- **Events and holidays.** The city regularly issues traffic plans around public holidays, naming alternative routes.
- **Construction on service roads.** Works beside fast roads can narrow a route that is normally open to motorbikes.

The practical defence is a margin. For any trip that depends on one bridge or one corridor, know a second one, and add time for the diversion. Our guide to [Long Bien and the Red River](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/long-bien-red-river-first-ride/) is a good starting point for the crossing nearest the shop.

## A step-by-step planning worksheet

Run through these steps before any trip that crosses the river or enters the centre.

1. **Write down your start, destination and day of the week.** Friday to Sunday evenings matter in the centre.
2. **Draw the route in a map app set to a two-wheel mode,** then zoom out and look for motorway-style roads and long straight stretches without junctions. Our guide to [checking a navigation app for expressways](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-check-if-a-navigation-app-suggests-an-expressway/) explains the check.
3. **Name your river crossing** and a backup crossing.
4. **Check current notices.** Search the Hanoi Department of Construction announcements, reputable news and the city portal for works or closures on your crossing and corridor.
5. **Check the time rules.** If the route enters the Old Quarter core, confirm whether the evening closure applies.
6. **Choose your parking before you arrive,** especially in the centre.
7. **Add margin,** such as 15 to 30 minutes for a diversion.
8. **Save the shop's address and phone** so you can ask for help.

Print or screenshot the route. If your phone dies, a map of the whole route is still in your pocket.

## What to do when you meet a closure

Even a good plan meets a barrier sometimes. The right reaction is calm and slow:

- **Slow down early** when you see barriers, cones or a queue that is not moving.
- **Follow officers and temporary signs,** which outrank permanent ones under the order of priority described above.
- **Do not squeeze through a gap or ride on a pavement.** Barriers exist for a reason.
- **Do not reverse or turn on a bridge or approach.** The law bans U-turns there. Continue to the first safe, lawful place.
- **Stop in a permitted place,** open the map and choose the backup route.

## Keeping your plan current

Information goes stale, so build a habit of checking, not a habit of trusting.

- Check on the day of the ride, not the day you booked.
- Prefer official or established news sources for closures.
- Treat travel blogs as background, since some still describe earlier drafts of rules.
- If two sources disagree, assume the stricter one applies until a sign says otherwise.

## FAQ: planning around restricted roads

### Are all of Hanoi's expressways closed to motorbikes?

Yes. Law 36/2024/QH15 says motorcycles and mopeds may not use expressways. Use national highways and local roads.

### Can I ride in the Old Quarter on a Friday night?

Reports on the pilot zone say motorbikes and cars are barred from the core area from 7 p.m. to midnight on Fridays, Saturdays and Sundays. Park outside and walk, and check the signs.

### Does the low-emission zone ban rental scooters everywhere in Hanoi?

No. The first phase covers part of Hoan Kiem Ward only, and the restriction on motorbikes applies to the evening window in the core zone.

### What should I do if my usual bridge is closed?

Use your backup crossing. During the 2026 repairs, motorbikes were diverted to Chuong Duong Bridge.

### Who decides which road is restricted?

National law covers expressways, and Hanoi's authorities decide local arrangements such as lanes, decks and time-based zones.

### Where can I check current closures?

Look at the Hanoi city portal, Department of Construction announcements and reputable news, and read the signs at the road itself.
