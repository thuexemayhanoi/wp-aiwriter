Fifty-cubic-centimetre bikes occupy a special place in Vietnamese traffic law and in Hanoi's rental market. They are the one kind of motorbike that can be ridden legally without a driving licence, which is why they fill the school gates every afternoon and why visitors who never got round to a motorcycle licence ask about them. They are also slow, light and limited in ways a 110 cc scooter is not, so they suit some riders and some trips very well and others not at all. This guide explains what the law says about mopeds in force since 1 January 2025, what a 50cc bike is like to ride in Hanoi, who should choose one, and what Nguyen Ha charges, with the rules as of October 2026.

## What the law calls a moped

Vietnam's Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (Law 36/2024/QH15, effective 1 January 2025) draws a line between a motorcycle and a moped in Article 34. A moped, in Vietnamese xe gan may, is a two- or three-wheeler with a design speed of no more than 50 km/h and, if it has a petrol engine, a displacement of no more than 50 cubic centimetres, or, if it has an electric motor, a motor of no more than 4 kW. Anything faster or larger is a motorcycle. The classic Honda Cub 50 and its many copies, the small 50cc scooters sold to students, and electric scooters limited to 49 km/h all fall on the moped side of the line.

The distinction matters because of two other articles. Article 59 sets the minimum ages: a person of 16 or over may ride a moped, while class A1 and class A motorcycle licences are issued from 18. And the licence classes in Article 57 start at 50 cc: class A1 covers motorcycles up to 125 cc, class A covers larger ones, and no class exists for mopeds, because none is required. A 16-year-old Vietnamese student, or a 40-year-old visitor who has never passed a motorcycle test, may legally ride a 50cc bike, provided they obey every other rule of the road.

## No licence does not mean no rules

Riding without a licence is where the exemption ends. The helmet rule in Article 33 applies to mopeds exactly as to motorcycles, for the rider and for the passenger, with the fines of Decree 168/2024/ND-CP (VND 400,000–600,000 for the rider, as our [helmet rules guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/helmet-rules-in-vietnam/) explains). The alcohol rule applies: any alcohol in your blood or breath is an offence on a moped as on anything else. Traffic lights, one-way streets, lane markings and the ban on riding on pavements apply. And a moped is held to a lower speed limit than a motorcycle: under the speed regulation in force since 1 January 2025 (Circular 38/2024/TT-BGTVT), mopeds may not exceed 40 km/h on any road, whatever the posted limit for other traffic.

Age is enforced in the other direction too. Decree 168/2024/ND-CP fines a rider aged 16 to under 18 who rides a motorcycle of 50 cc or more, that is, anything bigger than a moped, VND 400,000–600,000, and fines the owner who hands a motorbike to a person not entitled to ride it VND 8–10 million for an individual. A rental shop that gives a 110 cc scooter to a 17-year-old, or to an adult without a licence, is the party in trouble, which is why shops ask for the licence and why the 50cc class exists as a legal alternative.

## What a 50cc bike is like to ride

A 50cc bike is a small motorbike in every sense. The engine is roughly half the size of a Honda Vision's, the bike is lighter, and it accelerates gently: pulling away from a green light with the stream of 110 cc and 125 cc scooters around you, you will be the one being passed. On the flat inner-city streets of Hanoi that is no problem, since traffic rarely exceeds 30 km/h anyway. On the dyke roads and the bridges across the Red River, where the flow runs at 50 or 60 km/h, a moped at its 40 km/h limit keeps to the right and lets the rest go by.

Two kinds are common. The Cub-style 50cc bike is a small semi-automatic with a foot shift and no clutch lever, light enough for a slight rider to push and park; the 50cc scooter is a twist-and-go automatic with a small under-seat box. Both carry one adult comfortably and two adults slowly. Neither suits a day trip to Ninh Binh or Ha Long: a hundred kilometres each way at 40 km/h is a long day, the national roads are no place for the slowest vehicle on them, and motorbikes of every size are banned from the expressways in any case.

## Who a 50cc rental suits

A 50cc bike is the right choice when the licence is the obstacle. If you hold no motorcycle licence, or your licence and International Driving Permit do not cover motorcycles, a moped is the only motorbike you can ride legally in Vietnam, and it will carry you around the Old Quarter, along West Lake and across to Long Bien perfectly well. It also suits nervous riders who want the lightest, slowest machine available while they get used to the traffic, and parents renting for a 16- or 17-year-old on a family trip.

It is the wrong choice when you have a licence that covers a 110 cc scooter and plan to ride any distance. The Vision or Wave costs the same or less per day at Nguyen Ha, keeps up with traffic, carries two people and a bag without strain, and is the bike every mechanic in the country knows. Riders with a licence rarely rent a moped twice.

## Travel insurance and the licence question

Most travel insurance policies pay for a motorbike accident only if you were riding legally, and many add a condition that you hold a motorcycle licence. On a moped the law requires no licence, so the first condition is satisfied, but the second is a matter of your policy's wording, not Vietnamese law. Read the motorbike clause before you travel; if it demands a licence regardless of engine size, a 50cc bike does not solve your insurance problem even though it solves the legal one. ## FAQ

### Can I ride a 50cc scooter in Vietnam without a licence?

Yes. Under Law 36/2024/QH15, in force since 1 January 2025, no licence class exists for mopeds (design speed up to 50 km/h and up to 50 cc, or an electric motor up to 4 kW), and anyone aged 16 or over may ride one. Every other rule of the road applies.

### How fast can a 50cc bike go legally?

No faster than 40 km/h on any road, under the speed regulation in force since 1 January 2025. The bike's own design speed is capped at 50 km/h.

### Do I need a helmet on a 50cc bike?

Yes, and so does your passenger. The helmet rule and its fines apply to mopeds exactly as to motorcycles.

### Is a 50cc bike cheaper to rent than a 110 cc scooter?

No. At Nguyen Ha a 50cc bike is VND 200,000 per day, the top of the range for automatic scooters and semi-automatics. Its advantage is legal, not financial.

### Can two adults ride a 50cc scooter?

Legally yes, with a helmet each; in practice it is slow, and a 110 cc scooter is far more comfortable for two if your licence allows one.
