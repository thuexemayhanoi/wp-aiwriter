The range printed on an electric bike's brochure is a best-case figure measured under fixed conditions, so a normal day of riding in Hanoi should be planned around a lower number. For the VinFast Evo200, VinFast publishes four range figures for the same bike and battery: 203 km at a steady 30 km/h with a 65 kg rider, 146 km at 45 km/h, 129 km at 50 km/h and 145 km in a mixed test. The headline number is the first and the sensible planning number is closer to the last. This guide explains how to read a range claim, shows why one battery produces several numbers, and gives a simple method to turn the brochure figure into a safe distance for your own day, using the example model but not claiming to describe any particular rental bike.

## What a range claim measures

A range figure answers a narrow question: how far does this bike go on a full battery under the maker's test conditions? Those conditions are chosen to be repeatable, not typical. For the Evo200, VinFast's own battery page states the main one: a constant speed of 30 km/h with one rider weighing 65 kg. Constant speed means no traffic lights, no queuing, no braking and no accelerating from a standstill, which are exactly the things that fill a Hanoi street.

None of this means the number is dishonest. It means the number answers a different question from the one you are asking. The useful habit is to find the test condition printed beside the figure and ask how closely your day resembles it.

## One bike, four numbers

VinFast's published figures for the Evo200, which has a 3.5 kWh battery, show how much the conditions matter.

| Test condition (VinFast) | Rider weight | Range | Share of the headline |
|---|---|---|---|
| Constant 30 km/h | 65 kg | 203 km | 100% |
| Constant 45 km/h | 70 kg | 146 km | about 72% |
| Constant 50 km/h | 70 kg | 129 km | about 64% |
| Mixed conditions | not stated | 145 km | about 71% |

Two things stand out. First, a modest rise in speed costs a lot of distance, because the energy needed to push through air grows quickly with speed. Second, even the mixed figure, the most realistic of the four, is nearly 30 per cent below the headline. If you remember one number from the brochure, make it the lowest honest one, not the highest.

The figures are for one model on its own terms. A different model, an electric bicycle or a bike with a smaller battery will have its own table, and some brands publish only a single figure. ## Why a rental bike may do worse than the table

Brochure figures describe a new bike with a healthy battery. A rental is different in three ways.

**The battery has aged.** VinFast says its LFP batteries keep about 70 per cent of their capacity after roughly 2,000 charge cycles, so a bike that has been rented daily for a year or two may not match a new one. That is normal, not a fault, but it means you should plan on less than the brochure.

**The rider is not the test rider.** Test riders weigh 65 or 70 kg. Heavier riders, a passenger or a bag all use more energy per kilometre.

**The route is not flat and steady.** Bridges, flyovers, headwinds, uneven surfaces and repeated stops cost more than a steady cruise. If you will carry a passenger, our guide to [two riders and luggage on a scooter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/scooter-for-two-riders-and-luggage-hanoi/) explains what the extra load does to a small bike, although it is written about petrol models.

## Reading the real Hanoi day

Hanoi riding is not one condition. It is a mixture, and the mixture changes by area.

- **Central streets.** Speeds are low and stops are frequent. You rarely approach the speeds at which range collapses, but you accelerate often. This is the closest to the mixed test, and the numbers are usually acceptable.
- **Dyke roads, bridges and ring-road stretches.** Traffic moves faster, and a bike held at 45 or 50 km/h sits on the lower rows of the table. Our guide to [Hanoi's ring roads and highways](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-ring-roads-highways-motorbikes-allowed/) explains which of these roads motorbikes may use.
- **Rain and heat.** Wet roads and a hot battery are both reasons to expect a little less. Our guide to [riding in Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) explains the season and the streets that flood.

I have not found an independent Hanoi test that measures how much each of these costs, so the table above is the only quantified evidence here. Treat any claim of an exact percentage loss for rain or heat with caution, including from sellers.

## Turn the brochure into a safe distance

A simple method works for any bike.

1. **Choose the lowest credible figure.** Use the mixed-condition number if the maker gives one, or about two thirds of the headline if it does not.
2. **Keep a reserve.** VinFast advises charging when the battery reaches about 20 per cent, so treat the last fifth as unavailable.
3. **Subtract for your weight and load.** If you are well above the test weight, or carrying a passenger, subtract a further margin.
4. **Plan the distance to the nearest safe charge, not to the end of the trip.** The point is to arrive with enough left to charge, not to arrive empty.

With the Evo200 numbers, a usable range of about 80 per cent of the 145 km mixed figure comes to roughly 116 km. For a rider who is heavier than the test rider, or carries luggage, a sensible plan is lower still. These are arithmetic examples on one model, not a promise about any bike at the shop.

## A yardstick you can use on day one

Instead of trusting a number, measure your bike. On the first day:

1. Note the battery percentage and the trip odometer before a ride of known length, for example a 17 km lake loop.
2. Ride normally, without trying to save energy.
3. Note the percentage afterwards and divide. If a 17 km loop used 15 per cent, the full battery is worth roughly 113 km at your style and weight.

Repeat on a different kind of route, such as a stretch of bridge and dyke road. After two rides you have a number that belongs to you, the bike and the battery you are actually riding. ## If the battery is lower than you planned

Check the display at every stop, not only at the start. If the percentage is falling faster than the loop yardstick predicts, the sensible response is to shorten the plan before the battery decides for you.

- **Ease the speed.** The table shows that range rises as speed falls, so holding the bike near 30 km/h on open stretches buys more distance than any other single change.
- **Use the gentler mode if the bike has one.** Several VinFast models offer ECO and SPORT riding modes, and ECO is the one to choose when the margin is thin.
- **Accelerate smoothly** from junctions instead of launching hard.
- **Head toward a charging point early,** while there is still a reserve, rather than waiting for a warning.
- **Call the shop** if the display shows a low-battery alert far from anywhere you can charge.

## Electric bicycles and range

For electric bicycles, range is even more dependent on you. The rider's pedalling adds to the motor, and makers often quote range by assist level. General guidance, not a sourced figure: the highest assist level on a hilly route drains the battery faster than a low level on a flat one, and a brochure figure without a stated assist level is hard to compare. Ask which level the figure refers to, and use the same yardstick ride above.

## FAQ: range and real riding

### Why is the brochure range higher than what I get?

It is measured at a steady speed with a light rider, with no stops. Real riding adds acceleration, weight, hills and heat.

### Which number should I trust?

The lowest number the maker publishes for the same bike, such as a mixed-condition figure, then reduce it for your load and keep a reserve.

### Does a faster speed shorten the range?

Yes. For the Evo200, VinFast's figures fall from 203 km at 30 km/h to 129 km at 50 km/h.

### Will the rental bike match the table?

Not necessarily. The table describes one model when new. A rental battery may have aged, and the shop confirms the actual bike at booking.

### How do I check range on the first day?

Ride a loop of known length and compare the battery percentage before and after.
