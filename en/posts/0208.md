For most new riders who want to keep up with Hanoi traffic, a 50cc moped is the more practical choice, while a pedal-assist electric bicycle suits people who already cycle and want a slower, quieter ride on short trips. Under Vietnam's Law 36/2024/QH15 an electric bicycle is a non-motorised bicycle whose motor cuts out when you stop pedalling or reach 25 km/h, whereas a 50cc bike is a moped with a 40 km/h limit on the road. Both require a helmet and neither needs a motorcycle licence. They differ in speed, effort, range, where you can ride and what you must do before and after the ride. This guide sets them side by side so a beginner can choose with the facts in hand.

## The two vehicles in legal terms

Vietnamese law sorts two-wheelers by numbers on the specification sheet, not by what the seller calls them. The table summarises what Law 36/2024/QH15, in force since 1 January 2025, says about the two vehicles.

| Point | Electric bicycle | 50cc moped |
|---|---|---|
| Legal class | Motor-assisted bicycle, a non-motorised vehicle (Article 34) | Moped, a motor vehicle (Article 34) |
| Defining limit | Motor assist stops when you stop pedalling or at 25 km/h | Design speed up to 50 km/h and up to 50 cc (petrol) |
| Motorcycle licence | Not needed | Not needed |
| Minimum age | None found in the law's age article (Article 59) | 16 (Article 59) |
| Helmet | Yes (Article 31) | Yes (Article 33) |
| People on board | One, plus one child under 7 | One rider; a second person only in the exceptions listed |
| Road speed limit | Built to cut out at 25 km/h | 40 km/h under Circular 38/2024/TT-BGTVT |

The practical message is that an electric bicycle is not a loophole around helmets, and a moped is not a licensing hurdle. If a machine has a twist throttle and no working pedals, it is not an electric bicycle in law, whatever the sign says. ## Speed and keeping up with traffic

Speed is the biggest difference for a beginner. An electric bicycle assists up to 25 km/h, and past that you pedal on your own. In central Hanoi the stream of scooters often moves at 20 to 30 km/h, so an electric bicycle can sit near the slow edge of that stream, but on wider roads and bridges it will be overtaken by nearly everything.

A 50cc moped may go up to 40 km/h on the road. It still accelerates gently, as our guide to [renting a 50cc scooter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/renting-a-50cc-scooter-in-hanoi/) explains, but it can match the pace of ordinary city traffic far more of the time. Being able to move with the flow is a safety advantage for a beginner, because the riders around you do not have to swerve past you.

## What each one asks of you physically and mentally

An electric bicycle asks for effort. The motor assists only while you pedal, so you pedal throughout, and a long trip in the heat is tiring. It asks for balance at low speed and some road sense, but anyone who has ridden a bicycle already has the basic skill.

A 50cc moped asks for none of the pedalling and a little more of the mind. There are two common kinds:

- **The 50cc scooter** has twist-and-go acceleration and no gears. It is the simplest motorised bike to learn.
- **The Cub-style 50cc** is a semi-automatic with a foot-operated gear shift and no clutch lever. It is light and easy to push, but you must learn to shift gears.

If you have never ridden a motorbike, the scooter kind is easier. If you have never ridden anything, the electric bicycle's low speed may be more forgiving, provided you are comfortable in traffic on a bicycle.

## Weight, braking and handling

A motorised moped is heavier than a bicycle, and weight matters when you park, push the bike or catch a wobble. The Honda Super Cub 50, for instance, has a kerb weight of about 96 kg. A bicycle with a motor and battery is usually lighter, though the exact weight depends on the model, so ask the shop.

Brakes differ as well. On most scooters the right-hand lever brakes the front wheel and the left-hand lever the rear, while on a Cub-style bike the rear brake is usually a foot pedal. Practise braking in a quiet place before heading into traffic, because the first emergency stop is not the time to find out which control does what. On an electric bicycle, ask the shop how the brakes and the motor assist work together on the bike you are given.

## Range, charging and rain

A petrol moped needs a litre or two of fuel, and the shop hands bikes over with about 0.5 to 2 litres and expects them back with about 0.5 to 1 litre. An electric bicycle needs a charged battery instead. At handover for electric bikes the battery level and charging arrangements are confirmed with the shop. Ask how far a full charge will take you with the assist level you plan to use, and how the battery is charged, since hotel charging rules differ. Our guide to [charging an electric scooter in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/charging-an-electric-scooter-in-hanoi/) covers the practical side of plugging in, much of which applies to bicycle batteries too.

Rain affects both. Wet roads lengthen braking distances on any two-wheeler, and a pedal-assist bicycle gives you less cover than a scooter's floorboard. Our guide to [riding in Hanoi in the rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) has the details. The shop does not include a raincoat for short rentals.

## Where each can go

Both can be used on city streets, riverside roads and quiet lanes. The differences show at the edges:

- **Lanes.** Where a road has separate lanes, motor-assisted bicycles are expected to use the lanes for slow and non-motorised vehicles, while mopeds ride with the motorbike stream. Read the signs rather than following the bike in front.
- **Bridges and dyke roads.** The faster flow on the roads along the Red River suits the moped better. On an electric bicycle, keep to the right and let faster traffic pass.
- **Old Quarter at weekends.** Hanoi's pilot low-emission zone bans motorbikes, motorcycles and cars from the Hoan Kiem Lake and Old Quarter core from 7 p.m. to midnight on Fridays, Saturdays and Sundays, according to VOV. A pedal bicycle is not a motorbike in law, but ask local advice before relying on that, and walk if in doubt. Our guide to [riding in the Old Quarter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-old-quarter/) explains the streets.
- **Pavements.** Neither may use them as a riding surface.

## Which one should you choose?

| You are | Better choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A confident cyclist who wants a short, quiet outing | Electric bicycle | Familiar skills, low speed, light controls |
| A complete beginner who wants to keep up with traffic | 50cc scooter | No pedalling, steady speed, simple controls |
| Aged 16 or 17 | 50cc moped, after asking the shop | The legal minimum age for a moped is 16, and the shop sets its own rental conditions |
| Planning a day with bridges and longer stretches | 50cc moped | Better able to match traffic, longer range per fill |
| Staying in one neighbourhood for a few hours | Either | Distance is short, so comfort decides |
| Carrying a daypack or a purchase | 50cc moped | More stable under load |

## A ten-minute test before you commit

Ask to try the bike in a quiet spot near the shop before you head out. In ten minutes you can learn most of what matters:

1. Start, stop and pull away smoothly three times.
2. Ride a slow figure-eight to feel the steering.
3. Brake gently, then firmly, with both brakes.
4. Check the horn, lights, indicators and mirrors.
5. Confirm the fuel or battery level and ask what the assist or speed modes do.
6. Ask how the bike is locked and where the key stays.

If you do not feel steady after that, say so and ask for the other bike. A good shop would rather swap the bike than see you hurt.

## FAQ: choosing between a 50cc bike and an electric bicycle

### Do I need a licence for an electric bicycle?

No motorcycle licence is required for a pedal-assist bicycle that meets the legal definition, but you must wear a helmet. Check that the bike you are given has working pedals and cuts assistance at 25 km/h.

### Do I need a licence for a 50cc moped?

Under Law 36/2024/QH15, a moped needs no motorcycle licence, and the minimum age is 16. Other rules of the road apply.

### Is an electric bicycle safer for a beginner?

It is slower and lighter, which helps in some ways, but it is also slower than the traffic around you, so safety depends on the route. The best choice is the bike you can control calmly.

### Can I carry a passenger?

An electric bicycle may carry one person, or two if the second is a child under 7. A moped carries one person, with a second allowed only in the cases the law lists, such as a child under 12.

### Which has the longer range?

It depends on the bike. A moped's range follows its tank, and an electric bicycle's follows its battery and how much you pedal. Ask the shop for figures for the actual bike.
