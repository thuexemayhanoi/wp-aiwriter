A helmet that fits is one you can wear for hours without it moving, pinching or fogging, and for a longer journey on a manual motorbike that matters as much as the law's requirement to wear one. The legal side is short: every rider and passenger must wear a helmet that meets the national standard, with the chin strap fastened correctly, and a rider without one is fined VND 400,000–600,000 as of October 2026. The fit side is where most visitors go wrong, because the free helmet that comes with a rental is a one-size-fits-many item and a long ride exposes every problem with it. This guide uses Honda Vietnam's training advice on choosing and wearing a helmet, explains the fit tests, what to inspect on the shell, liner and strap, how visors, heat and rain change the choice and when to buy your own. For the rules and the national standard, see our guide to [helmet rules in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/helmet-rules-in-vietnam/).

## What the law requires, briefly

Article 33 of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (Law No. 36/2024/QH15), in force since 1 January 2025, requires the rider and any passenger on a two-wheeled motorbike to wear a helmet that meets the national technical standard and to fasten the strap correctly. Under Article 7 of Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, which we read on the Government portal, a rider without a helmet, or with one whose strap is not fastened properly, is fined VND 400,000–600,000, and carrying a passenger without a proper helmet carries the same fine, with narrow exceptions, including a child under six. Decree 238/2026/ND-CP, which amends the decree from 15 August 2026, does not change Article 7. Our helmet guide explains the standard and the exceptions in detail. The rest of this article is about getting a good fit.

## Honda's guidance on choosing a helmet

Honda Vietnam's safe-riding training document, dated August 2026, has a section on choosing and wearing a helmet correctly. It lists these checks:

- **Size.** Turn the helmet back and forth on your head to make sure it fits.
- **Standard marks.** The quality-inspection labels should be complete and in the correct form.
- **Strap.** It should be strong and hold up under a pull.
- **The two-finger rule.** Slide two fingers between your chin and the strap. They should just fit. If the strap is too tight or too loose, adjust it.
- **The shell.** It should be smooth, thick, hard and difficult to break on impact. Rivets on the shell should not be metal and should not stand out more than 2.5 millimetres.
- **The liner.** The foam lining should be thick and should not sink when pressed.
- **Condition.** Do not use a helmet that has been broken or has taken a heavy blow.

These are simple tests that need no tools, and you can do all of them in the shop in a couple of minutes.

## The fit test, step by step

1. **Put the helmet on without fastening it.** It should go on with slight resistance, not slide on loosely.

2. **Check the position.** The front edge should sit about a finger's width above your eyebrows, level, not tilted back or forward.

3. **Shake and twist.** Nod your head and shake it, then hold the helmet and try to rotate it on your head. Your skin should move with the helmet. If the helmet moves independently, it is too big.

4. **Fasten the strap** and use the two-finger rule: two fingers between chin and strap, no more and no less.

5. **Try to roll the helmet forward off your head** by pushing up at the back of the rim. It should not roll off.

6. **Check pressure points.** After a few minutes, there should be no sore spot on the forehead or the cheeks. A pressure point that is slight in the shop becomes a headache in an hour.

7. **Check your vision.** You should be able to see to both sides and above and below with the helmet on, and glasses should fit comfortably if you wear them.

8. **Check hearing.** A helmet that is very noisy can become tiring. You do not want so much wind noise that you cannot hear traffic.

If the helmet fails any of these, ask for another size or another helmet.

## Inspecting the shell, liner and strap

A rental helmet has been worn by many people, so inspect it. Honda's checks give you the framework.

**The shell.** Look for cracks, deep scratches and dents. A shell that is thin, flexible or easy to dent is not what Honda describes as thick and hard. Check the rivets. They should not be metal and should not protrude more than 2.5 millimetres.

**The liner.** Press the foam with your thumb. It should feel firm and spring back, not sink or crumble. A liner that is flattened, torn or dusty from age is not doing its job.

**The strap and buckle.** The strap should show no fraying, and the buckle should click firmly and hold when pulled. Honda says the strap should be strong and take a pull well.

**History.** Honda says not to use a helmet that has been broken or has suffered a heavy impact. You cannot always tell by looking, so ask the shop whether the helmet has ever been dropped hard or involved in a fall. A helmet that has been in a crash should be replaced even if it looks fine.

**Hygiene.** A shared helmet can be unpleasant. A clean liner, or a disposable liner or a thin cap that you bring yourself, helps.

## Journey-specific points

A longer ride adds issues that a ten-minute trip does not.

**Weight and neck strain.** A heavy helmet tires your neck over hours. Try the helmet on and tilt your head up and down. If it feels heavy now, it will feel worse later.

**Heat and ventilation.** Hanoi's summers are hot and humid. A well-ventilated helmet helps. A closed full-face helmet is warmer but offers more protection. Take breaks and drink water.

**Visor.** A clear, scratch-free visor is useful for wind, dust and rain, and a dirty or scratched one is a hazard, especially at night. Honda's night-riding advice is to keep the lights in good order and avoid looking directly at oncoming headlights, which a scratched visor makes worse. If you wear glasses, check that the helmet fits over them without pressing.

**Rain.** In a downpour, a visor with an anti-fog treatment or a pin-lock insert helps, and a helmet that lets water pour down your neck is unpleasant. A wet liner can slip. Our guide to [riding a semi-automatic bike in heavy Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-a-semi-automatic-bike-in-heavy-hanoi-rain/) covers wet-weather riding.

**Noise.** Wind noise is a fatigue factor on longer rides. A helmet with a better seal and a lower profile is quieter. Earplugs can help but should not block traffic sounds.

**Strap comfort.** A strap that digs in under the chin is a nuisance. Check the padding, and make sure the strap sits on the chin and not on your throat.

## Wearing it correctly every time

Even a well-fitting helmet protects only if it is worn properly. Honda's training document lists the helmet check among the basics. Before each ride:

- **Fasten the strap,** and check it with two fingers.
- **Check the visor is clean** and moves freely.
- **Make sure the helmet sits level.**
- **Do not carry the helmet on the bike's handlebar,** where it can be knocked off or scratched.
- **Do not leave it on the seat in the sun,** which can heat the liner.
- **Replace it** after a heavy impact, even if it looks undamaged.

## FAQ: choosing a helmet for a journey

### How tight should a helmet be?

Snug, with no movement against your head and no pressure points. When you turn your head the skin should move with the helmet.

### What is the two-finger rule?

With the strap fastened, two fingers should fit between your chin and the strap. Honda uses it as a quick check of strap tension.

### Can I use a helmet that has been dropped?

Honda says not to use a helmet that has been broken or has taken a heavy blow. If in doubt, replace it.

### Is the free rental helmet enough?

It meets the basic requirement if it fits and is in good condition. For a longer trip, check the fit and consider a better one.

### Do passengers need a helmet?

Yes. The rule applies to everyone on the bike, and the fine for a passenger without one is VND 400,000–600,000 with narrow exceptions.

### Can I bring my own helmet?

Yes, if it meets Vietnam's standard and fits. Check with our helmet guide.
