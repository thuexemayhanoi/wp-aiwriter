If a traffic officer signals you to stop, pull over safely at once, switch off the engine, stay calm and polite, and show the documents the law says you must carry. Law 36/2024/QH15 says traffic police may stop vehicles for inspection when they detect a violation or have grounds to identify one, when they are carrying out an authorised patrol plan, to protect security or to follow up reports (Article 66). It requires operators to obey orders to stop and requests for inspection (Article 72), and it gives them rights too: to be told the grounds for the stop, the content and results of the inspection and any violations and handling measures, and to explain, complain or denounce through the lawful channels. Refusing to stop or fleeing is the worst option, because officers may pursue and handle it as a separate violation (Article 73).

This guide explains why you may be stopped, what to do in the first minute, which documents to carry, what rights and duties apply, what the record and the penalty decision are, how fines are paid, what to do if your bike is held and what to tell the rental shop. It is general information based on the law as of October 2026, not legal advice.

## Why traffic police may stop you

Article 66 of the Law lists four grounds on which traffic police may stop a vehicle on the road for inspection and control.

1. **They detect a violation,** or have grounds to identify a violation of road traffic law or another law.

2. **They are following orders and carrying out patrol and control plans** to detect violations that need a stop for checking.

3. **They are protecting national security,** social order and safety, or preventing crime, disasters, fires and epidemics.

4. **They have received reports, denunciations or proposals** about crimes or other violations.

So a stop does not always mean that you have done something wrong. It may be a routine check at a checkpoint. Article 65 adds that traffic police may inspect and control road users and vehicles, handle violations and use cameras and other equipment, and Article 67 lists the measures for detecting violations, including cameras, recorded data and observation by officers on duty. ## In the first minute

1. **Signal and slow down** when you see the signal to stop. Officers may signal with a baton, a hand or a whistle, and the law ranks the commands of a traffic guard above signs, lights and markings (Article 11).

2. **Stop in a safe place** close to the right edge, away from a bend or junction if you can, and where the officer indicated. Do not stop abruptly in the lane.

3. **Switch off the engine,** put the stand down and stay on or beside the bike as directed.

4. **Take off your sunglasses,** and your helmet only if asked.

5. **Keep your hands visible,** and do not reach into bags or pockets without saying what you are doing.

6. **Greet the officer politely,** and say that you are a visitor and that the bike is rented.

7. **Do not argue, do not run and do not offer money.**

Article 73 provides that if an operator refuses signals and orders to stop and flees, officers may pursue. It also provides for measures against people who obstruct or resist officers on duty, so keep the exchange calm. Article 9 also lists, among prohibited acts, insulting, threatening or failing to obey the commands or inspection requests of officers on duty.

## Documents to carry

Article 56 of the Law says that a driver of a motor vehicle must carry these papers while operating on the road.

- **The vehicle registration certificate,** or a certified copy (with a mortgage certification where applicable).

- **The driver's licence,** suitable for the type of vehicle.

- **The vehicle's technical safety and environmental inspection certificate,** for motor vehicles that require one.

- **The owner's compulsory civil liability insurance certificate.**

Where a driver's or operator's papers are integrated into an electronic identification account, they may be presented through that account (Article 56(3)). Moped drivers are in a separate category under Article 56(4), with conditions on age and health instead of a licence, which is why the class of your bike matters. ## Your rights and duties

Article 72 of the Law sets them out.

**Rights.** Operators may drive on the road in accordance with the law. They may be told the grounds for stopping their vehicle, the content and results of the examination and control, and any violations and the measures taken. They may make explanations, denounce and initiate lawsuits against administrative decisions or acts related to their lawful rights. They may also denounce acts of law violation in the patrol and control work, and provide information on violations.

**Duties.** Operators must abide by the law on road traffic order and safety, obey orders to stop and requests for inspection and control, and support and cooperate with the forces assuring road traffic order and safety.

In practice, it is reasonable to ask politely why you have been stopped, and what the officer has found. If you do not speak Vietnamese, say so, and ask for a translator or whether you may call the shop to help. ## If the officer finds an offence

If an offence is found, officers handle it according to the law on administrative violations.

1. **They explain the violation,** and may write a record. Read it, as far as you can.

2. **You may add a note** to the record, for example that you do not understand Vietnamese or that you disagree. Keep a copy.

3. **A penalty decision** states the fine and how to pay it. Check the plate number, the name, the offence and the amount.

4. **Payment is made as the decision states,** normally through the State Treasury, by bank transfer to the account on the decision, or online through the National Public Service Portal, and you should keep the receipt.

5. **Do not pay cash at the roadside** unless the paperwork is complete and the procedure is clearly official. If anyone asks for money with no written decision, ask politely for the record and the decision. Offering or paying a bribe is a crime.

Officers may hold documents or the vehicle in some cases. Under Article 83, when investigating an accident they may temporarily seize vehicles, documents and licences. If the bike is held, ask for a record of what is held and where, and tell the shop at once, because the bike belongs to it.

## Alcohol and other checks

Article 9 of the Law bans operating vehicles with alcohol in the blood or breath, and with narcotics or stimulants. Officers can require tests, and refusing a test or an inspection request is itself an offence. ## Camera notices and later contact

Not every offence is caught at the roadside. Cameras and other devices can record violations, and under Decree 168/2024/ND-CP the authority can send a notice asking the vehicle owner and related persons to come and settle the case. For a rental, the notice may reach the shop first. Ask the shop how it handles notices before you rent. ## What not to do

- **Do not run, or ignore the signal.**

- **Do not argue loudly or become aggressive.**

- **Do not offer money,** or agree to pay cash with no paper.

- **Do not sign a document you cannot read** without a translation, or without writing that you do not understand it.

- **Do not hand over the original of your passport** unless you understand why, and tell the shop. The shop's published terms say it does not keep an original passport instead of the deposit, and an officer's request is a separate matter, so ask for a record.

- **Do not lie about the bike or its owner.**

## A quick checklist

1. **Stop safely and switch off the engine.**

2. **Be calm and polite, and say you are a visitor with a rental.**

3. **Show registration, insurance and licence, and a copy of your passport.**

4. **Ask why you were stopped, and what the offence is.**

5. **Read and note the record, and keep a copy.**

6. **Pay only as the written decision says.**

7. **Call the shop if anything is held or unclear.**

## FAQ: being stopped by traffic police

### Why can traffic police stop me?

When they detect a violation or have grounds to suspect one, when carrying out an authorised patrol plan, to protect security and order, or to follow reports (Article 66 of Law 36/2024/QH15).

### Must I stop?

Yes. Article 72 requires operators to obey orders to stop and requests for inspection. Fleeing can lead to pursuit under Article 73.

### What documents must I carry?

The vehicle registration certificate or a certified copy, your driver's licence, the technical inspection certificate where required and the owner's compulsory insurance certificate (Article 56), plus a copy of your passport.

### Can I ask why I was stopped?

Yes. Article 72 gives you the right to be told the grounds, the content and results of the inspection and any violations and handling measures.

### How do I pay a fine?

As the written penalty decision says: normally through the State Treasury, by transfer to the account named on it, or online through the National Public Service Portal. Keep the receipt.

### What if the bike is held?

Ask for a record, and tell the shop at once.
