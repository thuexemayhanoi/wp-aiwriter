Yes, a child may ride as a passenger on a motorbike in Vietnam, and a child under 12 is the main reason the law allows a rider to carry two passengers instead of one. But the child must wear a proper motorcycle helmet, a child aged six or older must not sit in front of the rider, and no motorbike may carry more than two passengers in any case. Those are the rules in force since 1 January 2025 under the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (Law 36/2024/QH15) and the penalty decree that goes with it (Decree 168/2024/ND-CP), as of October 2026. This guide is written for visiting parents: what the rules actually say, where the well-known "under six" exemption begins and ends, and when a car or a walk is simply the better choice for your family.

## What the law allows in one paragraph

Article 33 of Law 36/2024/QH15 says that a rider of a two-wheeled motorcycle or a moped may carry only one person. It then lists the cases in which a rider may carry at most two: taking a sick person for emergency treatment, escorting a person who has broken the law, carrying a child under 12, and carrying an elderly, frail or disabled person. The same article requires everyone on the bike, rider and passengers alike, to wear a helmet that meets the national standard, with the chin strap properly fastened. Nothing in the article sets a minimum age for a passenger, so a toddler may legally ride, but nothing in it excuses a child from the helmet either.

## Counting heads: the under-12 exception

The under-12 rule is the one that makes family travel by scooter legal at all. A parent riding with one adult passenger and one child under 12 is within the law. So is a parent carrying two children under 12. What the exception does not do is raise the ceiling: two passengers is the maximum whatever their ages, so a parent with three small children, or two adults plus a child, is over the limit.

The age threshold was lowered by the 2024 law. Under the old Road Traffic Law of 2008 the exception covered children under 14; since 1 January 2025 it covers children under 12. A tall 12- or 13-year-old now counts as a full passenger, which means a rider carrying an adult and a 13-year-old is carrying one person too many.

Decree 168/2024/ND-CP fines the rider VND 400,000–600,000 for carrying two passengers outside the listed cases. Carrying three or more passengers falls into a higher fine bracket, and causing a crash while breaking any of these rules raises the penalty again and costs licence points. Decree 238/2026/ND-CP, which amended Decree 168 with effect from 15 August 2026, does not appear from published summaries to have changed these motorbike passenger fines.

## Helmets for children: the rule and the exemption

The helmet rule in Article 33 applies to every person carried, with no age exception. The confusion comes from the penalty decree. Decree 168/2024/ND-CP fines a rider VND 400,000–600,000 for carrying a passenger without a helmet or with the strap undone, but it excludes three cases from that particular fine: a patient being taken for emergency care, a person being escorted after an offence, and a child under six.

That exclusion is about the fine, not about safety and not about the law's basic duty. It exists largely because properly fitting helmets for infants and toddlers are hard to find and some very young children cannot hold the weight of one. From a child's sixth birthday there is no exclusion at all: a bare-headed seven-year-old on the back of your scooter means a fine for you, and police in Hanoi do stop riders for exactly this.

For the general helmet standard, the CR conformity mark and the separate fine for adult passengers, see our [helmet rules guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/helmet-rules-in-vietnam/). The point that matters for families is simpler. If your child is old enough to sit up steadily and wear a small helmet comfortably, give them one, whatever the age written in the decree.

## Where a child may sit on the bike

Three seating positions are common on Vietnamese streets, and they are not equal in law.

**Behind the rider.** This is the standard passenger position and the only one that is clearly appropriate for a child of six or more. The child sits astride the seat, feet on the passenger footrests, holding the rider's waist or the grab rail.

**Between two adults.** A small child sandwiched between the rider and an adult passenger is the arrangement many local families use, and it is legal within the two-passenger limit when the child is under 12. The adult behind keeps the child from sliding off.

**In front of the rider.** You will see toddlers standing on the floorboard of a scooter or sitting on the seat in front of the parent. Decree 168/2024/ND-CP penalises a rider who sits behind another person and reaches around them to steer, and the only exception it makes is for a child under six seated in front. Legal commentators, including luatvietnam.vn, read this as meaning that putting a child of six or older in front is an offence, carrying a fine of VND 8–10 million and suspension of the licence, with heavier penalties if a crash results. Even with a younger child, a front position puts the child between you and anything you hit, and it limits your steering. We do not recommend it.

## Seat belts and child seats: encouraged, not mandatory

During the drafting of the 2024 law, a proposal would have required a seat belt or a dedicated child seat whenever a child under six rode on a motorbike without an adult beside them. That requirement did not survive. According to reporting on the final text, the law as passed only encourages the use of a safety strap or child seat, or an adult sitting behind the child, and family members are asked to remind one another to follow these practices.

So there is no fine for riding without a child harness, but the idea behind the draft is sound. A child who falls asleep on the back of a scooter can slump sideways in seconds, and a sleeping child is common after a long day out. Harness straps that clip the child to the rider are sold in Vietnam and are worth considering for any ride longer than a few minutes, though they are no substitute for an adult behind the child or for keeping trips short.

## Children cannot ride the rental themselves

The passenger rules above are the only way a child can legally be on a motorbike. Under Article 59 of Law 36/2024/QH15, a person must be at least 16 to ride a moped (the 50cc class, which needs no licence) and at least 18 to hold a class A1 or A motorcycle licence. A 14-year-old cannot take the scooter round the block, and the person who hands a motorbike to someone not entitled to ride it can be fined heavily. ## What a single stop can cost a family

Fines under Decree 168/2024/ND-CP stack. Imagine a parent stopped with an adult passenger, a seven-year-old and a nine-year-old, and only two helmets between the four of them. The bike is carrying three passengers, which is over the limit. The children without helmets are both over six, so there is no exemption. The adult passenger, if bare-headed, is fined separately. A simple trip to dinner can end with several penalties written up at once, and the officer will also ask for the rider's licence and the bike's registration.

Fines are paid through official channels such as the State Treasury or the police's online services, not in cash at the roadside. ## Fitting a child's helmet properly

A child's helmet is only protective if it fits. Choose one made for motorcycle use that carries the CR mark of the Vietnamese standard; children's sizes are sold in the same shops as adult helmets. The shell should sit level, about a finger's width above the eyebrows, and should not rock forwards or backwards when the child shakes their head. Fasten the strap so that only one or two fingers fit between it and the chin. An adult helmet on a child's head, even with the strap pulled tight, will slide off in a fall.

Cartoon-printed "fashion" shells without the CR mark are sold cheaply at markets. They are not motorcycle helmets and police treat them as no helmet at all, so check for the mark and the manufacturer's label before buying.

## Is a scooter right for your family?

The law sets the minimum; judgement does the rest. A few questions help decide:

- **How old and how steady is the child?** A school-age child who can sit still, hold on and keep their feet on the footrests is a very different passenger from a toddler who wriggles and sleeps.
- **How experienced is the rider?** Carrying a child adds weight high on the bike and changes how it brakes. If you have not ridden in Vietnamese traffic before, do not learn with a child on board.
- **Where and when?** A short, slow hop along quiet lanes near your hotel in daylight is a different risk from a ring road at rush hour, a trip after dark or any ride in heavy rain.
- **How far?** Children tire, and tired passengers let go. Short legs between stops are safer than one long ride.

If the honest answer to any of these is uncertain, choose another way to travel for that trip.

## Safer choices than three on a scooter

For many families the best plan is a mix. Keep the scooter for the adult, or for one parent and one older child on short daytime trips, and move the whole family by car for longer journeys. Taxis and ride-hailing cars are easy to find in Hanoi.

Cars have their own child rule now. Article 10 of Law 36/2024/QH15 says a child under 10 and shorter than 1.35 m must not sit in the same row as the driver in a car with more than one row of seats, and that the driver must use suitable child safety equipment. The provision was originally due to start on 1 January 2026; an amending law moved it to 1 July 2026, and reports indicate that commercial passenger vehicles were exempted. In practice that means a taxi or ride-hailing car is unlikely to have a child seat ready, so families who care about one should bring a portable booster or book a private car that can supply one. Seat a child in the back either way.

For short distances in the centre, walking is often both safer and quicker. Our guide to [riding in the Old Quarter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-old-quarter/) explains where a scooter is more trouble than it is worth.

## A checklist before you ride with a child

- Count passengers: never more than two, and a second passenger only when a child is under 12 or another listed case applies.
- A motorcycle helmet with the CR mark on every head, strap fastened, including on children under six whenever a fitting helmet is available.
- A child aged six or older sits behind the rider, never in front.
- Small children ride between two adults or are strapped to the rider for anything longer than a few minutes.
- Daylight, dry roads and short distances; no ring roads or rush hour for a first ride together.
- Closed shoes and long trousers for the child, and feet kept well away from the exhaust, which burns many young passengers.
- Rider sober, licensed for the bike, and practised in Hanoi traffic before carrying anyone.

## FAQ: children on motorbikes in Vietnam

### Can I carry my child and my partner on one scooter?

Yes, if the child is under 12. Article 33 of Law 36/2024/QH15 allows a rider to carry two passengers when one of them is a child under 12. With a child of 12 or older, only one passenger is allowed.

### Does a child under six have to wear a helmet?

The law requires every passenger to wear one. Decree 168/2024/ND-CP only spares the rider the fine when the child is under six. For safety, use a fitting child's helmet whenever one is available.

### At what age can a child sit in front of the rider?

Only a child under six may sit in front without breaking the rule in Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, and even then it is not a safe position. Children aged six and older must ride behind.

### Is a child seat or harness required on a motorbike?

No. A mandatory seat or belt for children under six was proposed in the draft law but dropped; the final law only encourages one. A harness strap is still a sensible choice for small children.

### Can my 15-year-old ride a rental scooter?

No. The minimum age for a moped is 16, and a motorcycle licence is issued from 18. A 15-year-old may only travel as a passenger.

### Do taxis in Hanoi provide child seats?

Do not count on it. The car child-seat rule that took effect on 1 July 2026 reportedly exempts commercial passenger transport, so bring a portable booster or arrange a private car that can supply one.
