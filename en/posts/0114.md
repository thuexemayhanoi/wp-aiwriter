An automatic scooter is the right choice for a first-time visitor to Hanoi because there is no clutch or gear lever to manage: you twist the right grip to go, squeeze the levers to stop and spend your attention on the traffic. Start with a light, familiar model such as a Honda Vision, ask the shop to show you the controls before you leave, take your first ride on a quiet road rather than the Old Quarter, and keep your first day short. The rest of this guide explains what to ask when you collect the bike, how an automatic scooter behaves, what a gentle first hour looks like and which small habits prevent the most common first-week mistakes.

## Why an automatic suits a first rental

A first visit to Hanoi already asks a lot of you: unfamiliar streets, dense motorbike traffic and signs in another language. Removing the gearbox leaves one less thing to get wrong. An automatic scooter has a belt-driven transmission that adjusts itself, so pulling away at a junction is simply a matter of opening the throttle smoothly.

That is not the same as having no skills to learn. You still need balance at low speed, good braking habits and an eye for what the bikes around you are about to do. Our comparison of [manual, semi-automatic and automatic bikes](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/manual-semi-automatic-or-automatic-motorbike-hanoi/) explains why most first-timers end up on an automatic, and the guide to [when a semi-automatic is easier](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/when-a-semi-automatic-bike-is-easier-than-an-automatic-scooter/) covers the few cases where it is not.

## Choosing a model as a beginner

Nguyen Ha lists several automatic scooters, including the Honda Vision, Air Blade, Click, Lead and the Yamaha Mio. The bike you receive is confirmed at booking, so state that you are a new rider and ask for the easiest option. Based on Honda Vietnam's published figures, this is a reasonable ranking for a beginner:

| Model | Weight (Honda Vietnam) | Seat height | Why it suits a beginner |
|---|---|---|---|
| Honda Vision | 93 to 97 kg | 761 mm (some versions 785 mm) | lightest, easy to hold upright and park |
| Honda Lead | 114 kg | 760 mm | low seat, flat floor, roomy; heavier to push |
| Honda Air Blade | 110 to 113 kg | 775 mm | stable and sporty; taller and heavier |
| Honda Click family (Vario 125) | 113 kg | 769 mm | steady feel; a little more bike than a Vision |

If you are short or have never ridden, the Vision is the usual recommendation, because the weight is what you feel when you stop and when you park. If you will carry a passenger or heavy shopping, the Lead may suit you better. Our [Vision versus Air Blade guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/honda-vision-vs-air-blade-which-scooter-to-rent-in-hanoi/) goes through the trade-offs in more detail.

Skip bigger engines on a first visit. A 160 cc Air Blade or any larger bike is more power than you need, and the engine size also affects which licence class applies; check our [licence rules for foreigners](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbike-licence-rules-for-foreigners-in-vietnam/) before you choose.

## At the counter: what to ask and what to bring

Come with your documents ready. The shop checks and photographs your identity papers and licence for the contract, keeps no originals, and takes a cash deposit that depends on the bike, between VND 2,000,000 and 5,000,000. Our guide to [what documents to carry when riding in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/documents-to-carry-riding-in-vietnam/) lists what to keep on you, and the page on [how the rental deposit works](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbike-rental-deposit-hanoi/) explains how it comes back.

Then ask these questions:

1. What is the exact model, and is it a 110 or 125 cc?
2. Does the bike use a key or a Smart Key fob, and how do I lock the steering?
3. How much fuel is in the tank, and what is expected at return?
4. Where is the fuel cap, and how do I open the seat?
5. Which lever works which brake?
6. What is the total price for my dates, and what is the deposit? The [prices page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/prices/) shows the ranges, and the final price is confirmed on the actual bike before you sign.
7. Who do I call if something goes wrong? The shop's number is 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969), open 09:00 to 21:00 daily.

One basic helmet per rider is included at no charge, and there is no extra lock. Check that the helmet fits and the strap fastens.

## How an automatic scooter works

The controls are few, and it pays to learn them while the bike is stationary on its stand:

- **Right grip:** the throttle. Twist it towards you to accelerate and release it to slow.
- **Right lever:** usually the front brake.
- **Left lever:** usually the rear brake. On many Honda scooters with a combined braking system, it can also apply some front brake. Ask the staff to confirm how your bike behaves.
- **Starting:** you switch on with the key or Smart Key, then press the start button. Many scooters require a brake lever to be squeezed before they will start.
- **Stand:** put the side stand fully up before you pull away, and fully down when you stop.
- **Indicators and horn:** on the left grip area. Find them before you ride.
- **Fuel gauge:** check it each time you set off.

Honda Vietnam describes Smart Key on its current scooters, which lets you unlock and start the bike with a fob, and a seat that opens from the ignition position. If your bike has it, learn the sequence from the staff and keep the fob somewhere secure.

Two habits help immediately. First, open the throttle gently: a scooter lurches forward if you twist hard, and that is the classic first-hour surprise. Second, use both brakes together, smoothly, and start braking earlier than you think you need to.

## Your first hour: a gentle plan

Do not start with a rush-hour ride into the Old Quarter. Pick a quiet place and give yourself time.

**Minutes 0 to 10: the car park.** Without leaving the shop's street, practise pulling away slowly, stopping, putting your feet down and making a wide turn. Switch the indicators on and off. Walk the bike backwards a few metres.

**Minutes 10 to 40: a quiet road.** Look for calmer streets away from the main arteries; our guide to [an easy first ride out of central Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/long-bien-red-river-first-ride/) suggests a route. Ride at walking pace and then at a steady 20 to 30 km/h, and practise turning, braking and looking over your shoulder.

**Minutes 40 to 60: a short loop.** Make a loop that returns you to a safe place. Stop once on purpose, check the fuel gauge and take a deep breath.

If at any point you feel out of your depth, stop and park. There is no prize for riding further on day one.

## How Hanoi traffic really works

New riders are often surprised that Hanoi traffic is chaotic but not random. Vehicles flow like water, and the rule is to be predictable: ride at a steady speed, hold your line, and let others go around you. Vietnam drives on the right.

Some habits matter more than any rule:

- Look ahead and keep a gap in front of you, because the bike ahead can stop without warning.
- Do not stop suddenly in the middle of a flow; slow gradually.
- At junctions, move steadily and avoid hesitating halfway across; our guide to [crossing Hanoi intersections](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/crossing-hanoi-intersections/) explains how to merge.
- Use the horn lightly to signal your presence, not to express annoyance.
- Watch for bikes that pull out from side alleys and for pedestrians stepping off the kerb.

## Weather, night and fatigue

Rain makes the road surface slippery, especially painted lines and manhole covers. Slow down, brake earlier, and consider stopping for a short downpour. Our guide to [riding in Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) explains the season and the streets that flood. After dark, visibility drops and you will be tired sooner, so read [riding at night in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-at-night-in-hanoi/) before you plan an evening out.

Do not ride after drinking. Our guide to [drink-driving rules](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/drink-driving-rules-motorbike-vietnam/) explains why this is both dangerous and strictly enforced.

## Common first-week mistakes

- **Twisting the throttle too hard** when pulling away or at a junction.
- **Grabbing only the front brake** in a panic; practise both together.
- **Forgetting the side stand** before moving off.
- **Parking on the pavement** or in a no-parking spot; use a guarded lot, as our [parking guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-motorbike-in-hanoi/) recommends.
- **Letting the fuel run low.** Petrol bikes come with about 0.5 to 2 litres and should return with about 0.5 to 1 litre; an empty tank at return carries a VND 20,000 fee.
- **Following a navigation app onto the wrong road,** because some routes use roads that do not allow motorbikes.
- **Riding too far, too soon.** Save the day trips for when you feel settled.

## FAQ: automatic scooter rental for first-timers

### Is an automatic scooter easy to ride?

Yes, there is no clutch or gear shifting. You still need balance, smooth throttle and good braking habits, so spend the first hour somewhere quiet.

### Which scooter is best for a complete beginner?

A light model such as the Honda Vision is the usual suggestion. The bike available is confirmed when you book, so tell the shop you are new.

### Should I take a 125 cc or 110 cc scooter?

Either is plenty in the city. Avoid anything above 125 cc, and confirm the engine size before you accept the bike.

### Do I need to know how to ride before I rent?

Yes. Renting is not a place to learn from scratch in traffic. If you have never ridden a scooter, practise in a quiet area first or consider a different way of getting around.

### How long should my first ride be?

An hour is enough for a first outing. Build up from there.
