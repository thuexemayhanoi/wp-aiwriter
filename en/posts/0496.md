A sudden U-turn is risky because it puts a slow, wide, unstable scooter across the path of traffic that is not expecting it, usually with a blind spot behind you and oncoming riders in front. Article 15 of Law 36/2024/QH15 bans U-turns at pedestrian crossings, on bridges and bridge ends, under overpasses, on underground roads, at road-railway intersections, on narrow roads, on slopes, on bends with obscured visibility, on expressways, in road tunnels and on one-way roads, unless a traffic guard or a temporary sign directs otherwise.

Vietnamese legal publications report a fine of VND 400,000 to 600,000 under Decree 168/2024/ND-CP for a motorbike U-turn where it is not allowed, a fine of VND 800,000 to 1,000,000 in a road tunnel, and a fine of VND 10,000,000 to 14,000,000 with 10 licence points if the turn causes an accident. The better habit is to ride on to a lawful place, or to reverse direction with right turns.

This guide explains why U-turns are dangerous for a scooter, where they are banned, how to make a lawful one, what to do instead when you have missed a turn, why this matters on Hanoi streets and what the fines are. It is general information based on the law and published guidance as of October 2026, not legal advice.

## Why a sudden U-turn is dangerous

A U-turn is one of the hardest manoeuvres you can make on a two-wheeler, and one of the least predictable for others.

- **You slow down in a fast lane.** To turn tightly you must nearly stop, and vehicles behind you may not expect it.

- **The turning circle is wide for a scooter** with a passenger, luggage or a long wheelbase, so you may swing across the whole carriageway.

- **Low speed makes the bike unstable.** Balance at walking pace takes skill, and a wobble in the middle of a road is dangerous.

- **You cut across oncoming traffic,** which has right of way and may be moving fast.

- **Blind spots hide vehicles** that are overtaking you on the side you are turning towards.

- **Others cannot read your intention.** A sudden turn with no signal looks like a swerve.

- **You may hit the kerb or a median** while trying to complete the turn, and the front wheel can catch on it.

- **Surface hazards bite harder** in a turn: gravel, oil, wet markings and potholes. Our guide to [braking safely on wet road markings](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/braking-safely-on-wet-road-markings/) explains why painted lines are slippery in rain.

- **You are often flustered.** Most sudden U-turns come from panic over a missed turn, and a panicked decision is the worst kind.

The World Health Organization notes that more than half of all road traffic deaths are among pedestrians, cyclists and motorcyclists. A scooter that is struck while turning across a lane has no protection.

## Where the law bans a U-turn

Article 15(4) of Law 36/2024/QH15 sets out the places where a U-turn is forbidden.

| Place | Why it is dangerous |
|---|---|
| Pedestrian crossings | People on foot cannot see or avoid you |
| Bridges and bridge ends | Narrow, fast and with little room |
| Under overpasses and on underground roads | Limited visibility and space |
| Road-railway intersections | Risk of being stuck on the tracks. See our guide to [motorbike safety at railway crossings in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbike-safety-at-railway-crossings-in-hanoi/) |
| Narrow roads | There is no room to turn |
| Slopes | The bike may roll or stall |
| Bends with obscured visibility | Others cannot see you in time |
| Expressways | Motorcycles and mopeds are not allowed on them at all |
| Road tunnels | Narrow, fast and poorly lit |
| One-way roads | You would go against the flow |

The ban does not apply when a traffic guard commands it or a temporary traffic sign directs it. A "no U-turn" sign bans it where it stands. Our guide to [understanding common motorbike road signs in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/understanding-common-motorbike-road-signs-in-vietnam/) lists the sign groups, and our guide to [how to read motorbike lane markings in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-read-motorbike-lane-markings-in-hanoi/) explains solid centre lines, which should not be crossed.

## How to make a lawful U-turn

If a U-turn is allowed, for example at a marked median opening or a junction where the signs permit it, Article 15 describes the procedure for any change of direction.

1. **Observe the surroundings** and make sure you have a safe distance from the vehicle behind.

2. **Slow down** well before the turning point.

3. **Signal,** using the indicator or a hand signal, and keep the signal on throughout.

4. **Move gradually to the lane nearest the direction of the turn.** For a U-turn to the left, that means the left of your carriageway.

5. **Give way** to pedestrians, rudimentary vehicles and oncoming traffic.

6. **Turn only when it is safe,** and when it is not likely to obstruct or endanger anyone.

7. **Do not stop in the middle,** unless forced. Roll through in a smooth arc, with your eyes on where you want to go, not on the kerb or the traffic.

8. **Settle into the new lane,** and cancel the signal.

## Safer ways to reverse direction

The best U-turn is the one you do not make. When you have missed a turn or the map says to go back, use one of these.

1. **Go on to the next lawful turning point.** A roundabout, a junction with a permitted turn or a marked median gap is better than a roadside swing. Our guide to [riding through a Hanoi roundabout](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-ride-through-a-hanoi-roundabout/) explains how to use one.

2. **Reverse direction with right turns.** In a country where traffic keeps to the right, two right turns, first into a side street and then into the next street that runs parallel to your original road, put you on a parallel road heading the opposite way, with every turn made with the flow. It is easier and safer than one left-hand U-turn across traffic. Check each street, since some are one-way, and keep to your map. Our guide to [safe right turns at busy Hanoi intersections](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/safe-right-turns-at-busy-hanoi-intersections/) explains the technique.

3. **Stop lawfully and let the map re-route.** Pull in at a place where you may stop, away from crossings, junctions and bridges, and wait for a new route. Our guide to [when to stop and check a map instead of looking down](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/when-to-stop-and-check-a-map-instead-of-looking-down/) lists where.

4. **Use a petrol station or a car park** to turn, entering and leaving properly. Our guide to [motorbike safety at petrol station entrances](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbike-safety-at-petrol-station-entrances/) explains how to enter and leave.

5. **Walk the bike** if you are in a place where you cannot ride a lawful way back, such as a pedestrian area. Do not push it into traffic.

6. **Accept the longer route.** A few extra minutes is a small price.

## What to do if you are about to turn without thinking

If you catch yourself braking hard to turn back, stop and check:

- **Is there a sign or a solid line?** If so, do not.

- **Is the road narrow, a bridge, a slope or a bend?** If so, do not.

- **Is there traffic behind you or oncoming?** If so, wait or go on.

- **Can you see clearly in both directions?** If not, go on.

- **Can you signal and slow down for several seconds first?** If not, go on.

## Hanoi-specific reasons to be careful

- **Dense, fast-changing flow.** A bike behind you may be closer than you think, and may not slow.

- **Median barriers and long stretches without gaps.** Planning your route to use a sanctioned turn saves time.

- **Narrow streets and alleys.** A U-turn may block the street entirely.

- **Cameras.** Fines can arrive later, and the notice may reach the shop first.

- **Large vehicles.** Buses and lorries need space and cannot stop quickly. Our guide to [sharing the road with buses while riding a scooter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/sharing-the-road-with-buses-while-riding-a-scooter/) explains.

## A quick checklist

1. **Do not U-turn on impulse. Ride on.**

2. **Never U-turn at a crossing, on a bridge, under an overpass, at a rail crossing, on a narrow road, a slope, a bend, a tunnel or a one-way road.**

3. **Obey no-U-turn signs and solid lines.**

4. **If it is allowed: observe, slow down, signal, move to the lane, give way, turn when safe.**

5. **Prefer a roundabout, a permitted junction or a pair of right turns.**

6. **Stop lawfully and let the map re-route if you need to think.**

7. **Remember the reported fines: VND 400,000 to 600,000 for a banned U-turn and VND 10,000,000 to 14,000,000 if it causes an accident.**

## FAQ: U-turns

### Is a U-turn legal in Vietnam?

Yes, where it is not banned. Article 15 of Law 36/2024/QH15 lists the places where it is forbidden, unless a traffic guard or a temporary sign allows it.

### What is the fine for an illegal U-turn on a motorbike?

Vietnamese legal publications report a fine of VND 400,000 to 600,000 under Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, a fine of VND 800,000 to 1,000,000 in a road tunnel, and a fine of VND 10,000,000 to 14,000,000 with 10 licence points if it causes an accident.

### Can I make a U-turn at a red light?

A U-turn usually means crossing the stop line or the signal, so do not. Wait for the light or a lawful turning point.

### Can I U-turn across a solid centre line?

No. Do not cross a solid line.

### What if the map tells me to turn around?

Ride on to a lawful place, or reverse direction with right turns. Do not turn on the spot.

### Is a U-turn safer at a median opening?

If it is a sanctioned opening and you have observed, slowed and signalled, it is safer than a roadside swing, but give way to oncoming traffic.
