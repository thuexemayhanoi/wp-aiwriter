To check a 50cc bike's headlight for night riding, start the engine, switch the beam to low and then to high, and confirm that the lamp lights each time, the high-beam indicator on the dashboard follows the switch, the light stays steady when you rev the engine and squeeze the brake, and the beam on a wall or kerb a few metres ahead looks even and points down the road, not into the sky. Then check the tail light, brake light and indicators, because they decide whether other riders can see you. Do all of it before you leave the shop, in shade or after dusk, and refuse a bike whose lights fail. This guide gives the full routine, a table for reading what you see, and notes on rules, fogging and what a renter should leave to the mechanic, as of October 2026.

## The 60-second version

Do these in order with the bike on its stand and a staff member beside you:

1. **Switch on and start the engine.** Some small bikes have a headlight that comes on with the ignition, others have a switch. Find out which yours is.
2. **Low beam on.** The lamp should light at full, steady brightness.
3. **High beam on.** The high-beam indicator on the instrument panel should come on, and the beam should visibly reach further.
4. **Rev the engine gently.** The light should not flicker, dim or pulse.
5. **Squeeze each brake lever.** The brake light at the rear should light, and the headlight should not dim.
6. **Check the indicators and horn.** Left and right, front and rear.
7. **Look at the lens.** No cracks, no cloudy haze, no pool of water inside, and no loose housing.

If any step fails, ask for a repair or another bike. Honda's safe-riding guide for customers says plainly not to ride at all if the lights are faulty, and lists headlight, indicators, brake light and horn among the checks before every trip.

## What the rules say about lights

Vietnam's Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (Law 36/2024/QH15), effective 1 January 2025, deals with lights in Article 20. As of October 2026 its main points are these:

- Drivers must switch on the front lights from 18:00 in the evening to 06:00 the next morning, and also in fog, smoke, dust, rain or any bad weather that limits visibility.
- Drivers must dip to low beam when meeting a pedestrian crossing the road, when driving through populated areas with working street lighting, when meeting oncoming vehicles (except where an anti-glare central barrier exists) and when turning at a junction.
- Article 26 says motor vehicles must use low beam inside road tunnels.

For a rider, this means two things. The headlight must work, since you are required to use it for twelve hours out of every twenty-four, and it must be usable on low beam, because most Hanoi riding at night is through lit streets with pedestrians and oncoming traffic. The shop's contract and your travel cover may add their own conditions about night riding, so ask. ## Step by step: the headlight itself

### The switch and the indicator

Find the beam switch on the handlebar. Honda's owner's manuals for its scooters show a high/low beam switch and a dashboard indicator for high beam. Those manuals are for larger Honda models, not a 50cc bike, so treat them as a guide to what you are looking for rather than a description of your bike. Flip the switch and watch both the beam and the dashboard. A switch that sticks, or a high-beam light that does not come on when the beam changes, is worth reporting.

### Low beam and high beam

Low beam is the one you will use most. It should be bright enough to light the road a few metres ahead without dazzling anyone. High beam is for dark, empty roads, and it should be noticeably brighter and longer. If you see only one beam, or the high beam is the same as the low, the lamp or the wiring may have a fault.

### The wall test

Park the bike on its stand, about three metres from a wall or a shuttered shop front, in shade or after dusk. Switch on the low beam and look at the lit patch.

- **An even patch, brighter in the lower half,** is normal. Many low beams have a sharp upper edge, which keeps light from rising into drivers' eyes.
- **A patch that is far up the wall** suggests the lamp is aimed too high and may dazzle oncoming riders. This is common when the lamp has been knocked.
- **A patch that is very low and close,** where it would light only the road in front of the front wheel, suggests it is aimed too low, or that the bike's rear is loaded.
- **A patch that is tilted, lopsided or split,** or that has dark gaps, may mean a loose lamp unit or a damaged reflector.

Aiming is a job for a mechanic. Do not try to adjust it yourself on a rental. If the patch looks wrong, say so.

### Flicker and dimming

A headlight that flickers when you rev the engine, hit a bump or squeeze the brake can have a loose connection, a weak battery or a failing bulb. You are not expected to find the cause. Film it for a few seconds and show the shop. A light that flickers at night is a reason to swap the bike.

## Reading what you see

| What you notice | A likely cause | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| No light on low beam | Failed bulb or connection | Ask for repair or another bike |
| Low beam works, high beam does not (or the reverse) | Bulb with a failed element, or switch fault | Report it; do not accept the bike for a night ride |
| Light flickers when revving or braking | Loose connection or weak battery | Film it and report it |
| Dim, yellow light | Old bulb, dirty lens or low charge | Ask the shop to check |
| Cloudy or yellowed lens | Age and sun exposure | Ask whether it reduces the beam; compare with another bike |
| Water drops or mist inside the lamp | Condensation or a cracked seal | See the section on condensation |
| Beam points high | Lamp aimed too high | Ask for aiming; do not ride at night until corrected |
| Beam points very low | Lamp aimed too low | Ask the shop to check |
| Loose lamp that wobbles | Fixings loose | Ask a mechanic to tighten it |
| Cracked lens | Damage | Photograph it and report it before riding |

## Condensation and fogging

A foggy or misty lamp is not always a fault. Honda's Vision owner's manual says the inside of the headlight lens can fog temporarily after washing the bike or riding in the rain, that this does not affect the headlight's function, and that the moisture disappears within a few minutes after you start the engine and switch on the headlight. If there is a large amount of water inside, the manual says to take the bike to an authorised Honda dealer. Again, that guidance is for a larger Honda scooter, but the logic carries over: a bit of mist that clears is normal, and standing water is a seal problem for the shop.

This also explains a common rainy-season trap. If the bike was washed that morning, the lamp may look foggy at pickup and clear within minutes. Switch it on for a short time before judging it, and ask whether it was washed.

## Bulbs, LEDs and what is not your job

Honda's owner's manual for the Wave RSX FI, a 110 cc semi-automatic, describes bulb replacement as a procedure for the owner with care, and gives one rule that applies to any halogen bulb: do not touch the glass with bare fingers, and if you do, wipe it with an alcohol-soaked cloth. That is an instruction for the owner of a specific model, not for a renter.

On a rented bike, do not open the headlight housing, swap a bulb or adjust the beam yourself. Tell the shop, and let a mechanic do it. This protects you from disputes about damage as well as from wiring faults.

## The other lights that matter at night

The headlight lets you see, but the other lights let others see you.

- **Tail light.** It should be on whenever the headlight is on, steady and red.
- **Brake light.** It should light when you squeeze either brake lever, front or rear. Ask a staff member to watch.
- **Indicators.** Four lamps, flashing at a steady pace, with a clicking or a dashboard flash. A very fast flash often means one bulb has failed.
- **Plate light and reflectors.** These make the bike visible from behind and show the plate.
- **Horn.** Press it. At night it is as useful as the light.

The 50cc category is small and often ridden slowly, so a working rear light matters even more, since faster traffic may close on you before noticing.

## Test it after dark, not only in daylight

A headlight that looks fine at noon can disappoint in the dark. If you pick up the bike late in the day, ask whether you can switch the lights on again after sunset. The shop is open 09:00 to 21:00 daily. If you collect in the morning, a short test ride at dusk on your first evening, on a lit and quiet street, lets you judge the beam before you rely on it.

When you ride at night, Honda's safe-riding guide gives a few simple rules:

- Check that the lights work before you set off.
- Wear bright or reflective clothing.
- Ride at a slow pace, which gives you time to react.
- Keep a safe distance from vehicles ahead; in poor light or at night, the guide suggests a four-second gap, not two.
- Avoid looking straight at oncoming headlights, which dazzles you.
- Use high beam for a better view, and switch to low beam when vehicles or pedestrians approach or in urban areas.

A 50cc moped is limited to 40 km/h on the road, which is a sensible pace for a small headlight in any case. Do not push beyond what the beam lets you see.

## What to tell the shop

Be specific. "The headlight flickers when I brake" is more helpful than "the light is bad". Mention the beam (low or high), the symptom (no light, flicker, dim, mist), when it happens and what you did. Take a short video. ## If the light fails on the road

1. Slow down smoothly and pull over somewhere safe and lit, away from traffic.
2. Switch on the hazard lights if the bike has them, and keep your helmet on.
3. Do not continue in the dark on a bike with no headlight.

## FAQ: headlights on a 50cc bike

### Must I use the headlight in the daytime?

The road law requires lights from 18:00 to 06:00 and in poor visibility such as rain or fog. Some riders use a headlight all day for visibility, and some bikes have a headlight that is always on, so ask which applies.

### Is a dim headlight dangerous?

Yes, because it limits what you can see and reduces how well others see you. Ask for it to be checked, and do not ride at night until it is.

### Can I change the bulb myself?

Not on a rental. Report it and let the shop's mechanic replace it.

### Why is my headlight misty inside?

Often condensation after washing or rain, which clears within minutes once the light is on. A lot of water inside means a seal problem to report.

### When do I use high beam?

On dark roads with no oncoming vehicles or pedestrians. Switch to low beam for oncoming traffic, pedestrians, lit streets and junctions.

### Should I ride a 50cc bike at night at all?

It depends on your confidence, the route and the bike's lights. A lit, familiar street at a slow speed is very different from a dark road.
