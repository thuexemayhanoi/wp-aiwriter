An electric motorbike is usually a little heavier than a petrol scooter of the same size, and you notice it at walking pace, not at speed: when you push the bike off its stand, turn it in a tight parking space, creep through a crowded alley or put a foot down at a junction. Published figures for popular VinFast scooters run from about 92 kg to about 120 kg depending on model and battery, against 97 kg for the Honda Vision that Honda lists on its own site, so the gap is small on some bikes and large on others. The extra weight is manageable if you prepare for it. This guide shows what the numbers mean, why a heavy battery low in the frame helps, where weight causes trouble, and a ten-minute routine to try before you ride into traffic.

## What the published weights say

Weights come from different sources, measured in different ways, and none of them is the weight of a rental bike. Use them as a scale, not as a description of the scooter you will receive. The shop confirms which model you get when you book.

| Scooter | Published weight | What the figure includes |
|---|---|---|
| Honda Vision (petrol, 110 cc) | 97 kg | Honda's own specification for the version shown, kerb weight |
| VinFast Evo200 Lite | 97 kg | Including the battery, as reported by VnExpress |
| VinFast Vero X | 92 kg, or 110 kg with the optional second battery | Brochure figure for the vehicle and battery |
| VinFast Feliz Lite | 102 kg, or about 120 kg with the second battery | Dealer specification page |
| VinFast Klara S | 108 kg | Including two batteries, per VinFast's FAQ |
| VinFast Impes and Ludo | 75 kg and 68 kg | Including one battery, per VinFast's FAQ |

Three points come out of the table. First, a single-battery VinFast scooter in the Evo200 Lite class weighs about the same as a petrol Vision, so heaviness is not universal. Second, a second battery adds roughly 18 kg, which is the removable pack's published weight on the Feliz Lite and the Vero X, and that is the difference you feel when you wheel the bike. Third, the lightest electric bikes, such as the small Ludo and Impes, are well under 80 kg, but they are also the slowest and least suited to long rides.

Nguyen Ha's electric group includes electric bicycles, 50cc-equivalent electric scooters and some VinFast electric motorbikes, so the weight you meet may be far lower than the figures above, or similar to them. The right question for the shop is: "What does this bike weigh with its battery in, and is there a second battery in the boot?"

## Why weight matters at low speed

At speed, a scooter stays upright because it is moving, and a few extra kilograms barely register. At walking pace the situation reverses. A rider holds the bike up with legs and arms, and the bike's mass and its height of balance decide how much effort that takes.

Weight shows up in four everyday moments:

- **Pushing off the stand and putting it back.** A heavier bike needs a firmer pull and a steadier stance.
- **Parking in a tight row.** Turning the bike around by hand is harder when you must lift and swing the rear wheel.
- **Stopping on a camber or slope.** The bike leans toward the lower side and needs more strength to hold.
- **Recovering a wobble.** A small lean that you would catch on a light scooter can run away on a heavier one once it passes a certain angle.

A cycling-component maker's page about weight and electric scooters, published in 2026, makes the same general point: a heavier scooter needs more effort to lean, steer and balance, especially in tight spaces. That article is about Indian-market scooters and not Hanoi, so take it as a general principle, not a measurement.

## Where the weight sits: low battery, high boot

A scooter's handling depends on where the mass is, not only on how much there is. On the VinFast models described by dealers and by VinFast's brochures, the main battery sits low, under the footboard, and the second battery, where fitted, goes into the boot under the seat. A dealer's description of the Feliz Lite says this arrangement spreads the weight and keeps the centre of gravity steady.

The practical consequence is a rule of thumb, not a published figure: a battery under the floor makes the bike feel planted, while a second pack in the boot raises some of the mass and puts it behind you. If the bike you are given has a second battery fitted, expect it to feel a little more top-heavy when you are standing still and turning the bars to full lock. If the second pack is not needed for your day, ask the shop whether it can be left out, since that changes both the weight and the boot space.

## Electric-specific handling points

Weight is not the only difference at low speed. Four other things about electric drive affect how a bike behaves when it is slow:

1. **Instant response.** An electric motor can deliver torque the moment you open the throttle, with no clutch or revs to build. A heavy bike plus a sudden twist can make a jerky start. Roll on gently from a stop.
2. **Silence.** Pedestrians and other riders do not hear you. In a crowded alley, slow down, cover the brake and use the horn early.
3. **Regenerative braking.** Many electric scooters slow the bike when you close the throttle, and VinFast lists regenerative braking among the features of its newer models. The effect varies with the model and the riding mode, so find out how strongly your bike slows when you let go of the throttle, in a quiet place.
4. **Riding modes.** Some models offer Eco and Sport modes, and a calmer mode softens acceleration. Ask the shop which mode to use as a first-time rider, and whether the mode can be changed while riding.

None of this makes electric bikes harder to ride. It means the first few minutes on one deserve a quiet street and not a busy junction.

## A ten-minute low-speed routine

Do this before you leave the shop's street, ideally with staff nearby:

1. **Walk the bike** a few steps with the engine off and the stand up, to feel the weight.
2. **Park it and put it on the stand** twice, using your whole body and not only your arm.
3. **Do slow figure-eights** in an empty space, with your feet near the ground, to feel how the bars respond.
4. **Practise a stop and a start** on level ground, with a smooth throttle, three or four times.
5. **Test the brakes gently**, front and rear, at walking pace, then at a little more than walking pace.
6. **Try a U-turn** on a wide, quiet road and keep your eyes up, not on the front wheel.

If any step feels beyond you, say so before you ride away. A lighter bike or a different model is a better choice than struggling in traffic. Our guide to [what to check before you ride off](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-scooter-inspection-checklist/) covers the rest of the handover.

## Slow traffic, junctions and alleys

Hanoi's traffic often moves at walking pace. Weight and silence both matter there.

- **Keep your feet ready.** Keep both feet close to the ground and use the stand early when the traffic stops for more than a moment.
- **Leave a gap.** A heavier bike needs a little more room to stop and a little more room to be walked.
- **Look further ahead.** At low speed, look at where you want to go, not at the wheel in front of you.
- **Turn from the hips, not just the arms.** A smooth lean works better than a wrench of the bars.
- **Avoid sharp turns on a damp surface.** Our guide to [riding in Hanoi's rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) explains why slow turns on wet paint are risky.

For the wider flow of the city, see our article on [crossing Hanoi's intersections and merging with traffic](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/crossing-hanoi-intersections/).

## Parking and moving the bike by hand

Most low-speed falls happen when the bike is not moving at all. A few habits help:

- **Park facing out** where you can, so you can ride away without reversing.
- **Choose firm, flat ground**, and avoid soft edges and slopes where the stand can sink.
- **Straighten the bars before you walk the bike backward**, and do not try to lift it.
- **Ask for help** if the bike is stuck between others. A guarded lot's attendant moves bikes all day.

Our guide to [parking a motorbike in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-motorbike-in-hanoi/) covers guarded lots, tickets and towing, which affect how you place the bike. ## Load and passengers

VinFast lists a load capacity of 130 kg for several of its models, such as the Feliz Lite and the Vero X, and 150 kg for the Klara S. Those figures include the rider, any passenger and luggage. Near the limit, a heavy bike gets heavier to handle at low speed, and the battery runs down faster. Ask the shop about the capacity of your model, and be honest about the total weight you will carry. ## Questions to ask the shop

- What is this model's weight with the battery, and is a second battery fitted?
- How does it feel when you release the throttle: does it slow strongly?
- What is the load capacity?
- Is there a mode I should use while I get used to it?
- Can I practise on a quiet street before I leave?

## FAQ: electric motorbike weight and low-speed handling

### Are electric motorbikes always heavier than petrol scooters?

No. A single-battery VinFast Evo200 Lite is listed at 97 kg, the same as the Honda Vision figure above. Models with a second battery weigh about 18 kg more.

### Is a heavy electric scooter dangerous to ride?

Not by itself. It needs more care at walking pace, so practise slow turns and stops in a quiet place first.

### Can I ask for a lighter bike?

You can ask. The shop confirms the bike actually available when you book, and a smaller model may suit you better.

### Does the second battery change handling?

It adds weight and, where it sits in the boot, raises some of the mass. Ask whether it is needed for your day.

### What should I do if the bike tips over?

Switch off, step clear and get help to lift it if it is heavy. Check for damage, take photographs and call the shop.

### Does weight affect range?

Yes, a heavier load, including rider and luggage, generally uses more energy. Our article on [electric bikes in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/electric-bikes-in-hanoi-rules/) explains the main differences between electric bicycles and scooters.
