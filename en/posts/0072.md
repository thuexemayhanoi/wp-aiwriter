When you rent a manual motorbike, you do not choose the tyres, but you do choose the bike, and in wet weather the tyres are the part of that choice that matters most. A Honda Wave or Yamaha Sirius rolls on small 17-inch wheels with narrow, tubed tyres, and the grip you have in the rain depends on how much tread is left, whether the pressure is right, whether the rubber is cracked or old and whether anything is stuck in it. This guide shows what to look at on a rental bike, uses Honda's tyre guidance and the pressures in Honda's Wave RSX FI manual to explain what the numbers mean, lists the questions worth asking and says when to ask for a different bike. It is general information. The correct pressure and tyre size for the bike you are given are on the bike or in its manual, and the shop should confirm them.

## What tyres these bikes use

The semi-automatic bikes in most Hanoi rental fleets share the same tyre sizes. Honda lists 70/90-17 at the front and 80/90-17 at the rear for the Wave RSX FI and the Blade, and Yamaha lists the same sizes for the Sirius FI. The Wave Alpha uses the same 70/90-17 front tyre. Honda's Wave RSX FI manual describes the tyres as cross-ply with inner tubes. In plain terms, the tyre is a bias-ply construction and an inner tube holds the air, not a tubeless design. A bike this light does not ask much of its tyres, which is exactly why a worn or soft tyre is easy to overlook.

That is not a reason to avoid these bikes in the rain. It is a reason to take the tyres seriously and to ride within their limits.

## The four checks that matter most

**1. The wear indicator.** Honda's aftersales guidance is blunt: look for the wear indicator, commonly called a wear bar, and if you can see it on a tyre, replace that tyre immediately. Honda's Wave RSX FI manual says the same and marks the position of the indicator with a TWI symbol on the tyre's sidewall. Find the symbol and look at the groove beside it. If the tread has worn level with the bar, the tyre is finished. A tyre that is close to the bar will give little grip on a wet surface.

**2. Pressure.** Honda's manual for the Wave RSX FI gives these cold pressures:

| | Rider only | Rider and passenger |
|---|---|---|
| Front | 200 kPa (29 psi) | 200 kPa (29 psi) |
| Rear | 225 kPa (33 psi) | 280 kPa (41 psi) |

These are for that model, so ask the shop for the figures for your bike. Honda's guidance is to check pressure with a gauge at least once a month, because under-inflation is hard to see by eye, and to check when the tyre is cool, since driving warms the tyre and raises the pressure. A tyre that is too soft squirms in corners and under braking and can overheat. A tyre that is too hard has a smaller contact patch and less grip on a wet road.

**3. Damage and foreign objects.** Honda says to inspect the whole circumference of the tyre for cracks, cuts, nails, stones and other objects, and to inspect the tyres after contact with a kerb or after riding over large bumps. Run your hand carefully around the tread and look at the sidewalls. Bulges, deep cuts and cracks are a reason to refuse the bike.

**4. Age.** Honda notes that older tyres are more likely to burst at extreme road temperatures and to slip in the cold, and recommends periodic inspection for tyres that have been in use for more than five years. You can find a date code on the sidewall, a four-digit number showing the week and year of manufacture, but rental bikes often carry tyres that have been replaced one at a time, so look at each. Hard, shiny, cracked rubber is a sign of age even without a code.

## A two-minute check at the shop

With the shop's staff present, ask to look at the tyres in good light.

1. **Find the TWI mark** on each tyre and check that the tread is clearly above the wear bar.
2. **Look at the tread pattern.** The grooves should be distinct, not smooth or flattened in the centre.
3. **Look for cracks** in the sidewalls and where the tread meets the sidewall.
4. **Feel for embedded objects** by running a hand over the tread, carefully.
5. **Press the tyre** with your thumb. It should feel firm, not squashy. Ask staff to check the pressure with a gauge and compare it with the figure for your bike.
6. **Check the valve cap** and that the valve is straight, not leaning.
7. **Compare front and rear.** A rear tyre worn flat in the middle and a front tyre that looks new, or the other way round, is common, but both need to be sound.

If you are not sure what you are looking at, ask the shop to show you a worn tyre and a good one side by side.

## What changes in wet weather

Honda's manual states that wet road surfaces are very slippery and that wet brakes lose effectiveness. Tread helps by moving water out from under the tyre, and a tyre with little tread has less ability to do that. On a wet road, a worn tyre loses grip sooner and with less warning, especially at the rear under braking or at the front in a bend.

A few practical points follow:

- **A tyre with plenty of tread is worth more in the rain than a new-looking bike.** Choose on tyres, not on paint.
- **Pressure matters more when the road is wet.** A tyre that is noticeably soft squirms and slides sooner.
- **Check the rear especially.** It carries the drive and most of the braking load in a slide.
- **Reduce your speed to the tyres you have.** If they are mediocre, ride as if the road were slippery even on a light shower.

## Tubes, punctures and what to ask

Because these tyres use inner tubes, a puncture is usually repaired by patching or replacing the tube, a repair that most roadside workshops handle. Ask the shop what its policy is if you get a puncture on a trip: who pays, whom to call and whether you can use a local workshop. Our guide to [lost keys, flat tyres and breakdowns with a rental bike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-bike-breakdown-hanoi-what-to-do/) explains the usual steps.

Questions to ask before you ride:

- When were the tyres last replaced?
- What pressure should the front and rear be for me, with and without a passenger?
- Is there a spare tube or a repair arrangement for trips outside the city?
- Can I swap bikes if I am not happy with the tyres?
- What happens if I get a puncture, and who pays?

## Load and passengers change the numbers

A passenger and luggage need more rear pressure, as the table shows for the RSX FI: 280 kPa at the rear for rider and passenger against 225 kPa for the rider alone. Honda's manual also lists maximum loads of 115 kg or 155 kg depending on the version, and warns that overloading affects handling, braking and stability. If you add a passenger in the rain, you need both the right pressure and a lower speed. Our guide to [riding a manual motorbike with a passenger](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-a-manual-motorbike-with-a-passenger-in-hanoi/) explains what two people change, and our guide to [how much luggage you can carry on a Honda Wave](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-much-luggage-can-you-safely-carry-on-a-honda-wave/) explains the load.

## When to ask for a different bike

Ask for another bike, or choose another shop, if:

- the TWI mark is level with the tread or the grooves are smooth;
- a tyre has a bulge, a deep cut, a crack in the sidewall or a visible nail or screw;
- the rubber is hard, shiny and cracked with age;
- the front and rear tyres are visibly mismatched in condition;
- the shop cannot tell you the right pressure or will not check it;
- the tyres seem to lose pressure when you stop to look.

A few minutes spent now saves a slide later. If the shop has no other bike, a different transport choice is a reasonable response in a downpour. ## FAQ: tyres on a rental motorbike

### Can I choose the tyres on a rental?

Not normally, but you can choose which bike you take and refuse one with poor tyres.

### How do I know if the tread is worn out?

Find the TWI mark on the sidewall and check whether the tread has worn level with the wear bar. Honda says to replace the tyre immediately if you can see the bar.

### What pressure should the tyres be?

It depends on the model. For the Honda Wave RSX FI, Honda's manual gives 200 kPa at the front and 225 kPa at the rear for a lone rider, with 280 kPa at the rear for rider and passenger. Ask the shop for your bike's figures.

### Are these tyres tubeless?

On the Honda Wave RSX FI, no. Honda describes them as cross-ply tyres with inner tubes.

### How often should pressure be checked?

Honda advises at least once a month with a gauge, when the tyres are cool.

### Do old tyres matter?

Yes. Honda notes that older tyres are more likely to fail in extreme temperatures and recommends periodic inspection after five years of use.
