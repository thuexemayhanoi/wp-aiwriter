The best protection for a rented electric scooter in Hanoi is a routine, not a gadget: park where someone is paid to watch the bike, turn the handlebar to the full lock, take every key or fob with you, keep the battery and charger out of sight and check the bike again before you walk away. Electric models add some useful features, such as an alarm and a smart key on certain VinFast models, but nobody can promise that a feature exists on the bike you are given, so you ask the shop at handover. This guide explains what a rental renter can and cannot rely on, how to build a short locking habit, what to do about removable batteries and chargers, and the exact steps to follow if the scooter disappears. It is general information, not a guarantee against loss.

## Why this matters more on a rental

A stolen scooter is a problem for you and for the shop. The bike is not yours, the contract is, and an untraceable bike can turn a pleasant trip into a long conversation about who is responsible. Our guide to [what the clauses in a Hanoi rental contract mean](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-motorbike-rental-contract-explained/) shows where that conversation usually starts, so read it before you sign.

Thieves in Hanoi are not a myth. In August 2026 the Voice of Vietnam reported that the Ngoc Ha ward authorities had identified a group of young men who stole a Honda Wave from the mouth of an alley by breaking the steering lock and connecting the wiring to start it. That case involved a petrol bike, but the method it describes, defeating a mechanical lock and starting the bike without its key, is exactly why a steering lock alone is a weak defence on any scooter. A telecoms firm's advice page on smart locks for electric vehicles, published in September 2025, makes a related point: simple mechanical locks on electric bikes can be defeated in seconds, especially in unguarded spots.

Neither source tells you how often scooters are stolen, and this guide does not either. The practical lesson is smaller: make the bike a harder, less attractive target than the next one.

## Security features on some VinFast models

Nguyen Ha's electric group includes electric bicycles, 50cc-equivalent electric scooters and some VinFast electric motorbikes, and the model you receive is confirmed when you book. What follows is manufacturer information about VinFast scooters generally. It does not say that any rental bike has every feature.

VinFast's own guidance on anti-theft describes three layers on models that have them:

1. **An alarm.** When the anti-theft mode is on and the scooter is moved by a set distance or the lock is opened without authority, the scooter shakes and sounds its horn. On one model VinFast describes the wheel as locking as well.
2. **A smart key.** A newer model, the Theon S, is described as having a smart key that works within about one metre, with buttons to start the anti-theft function and to find the bike. VinFast says a bike that has not been activated by its fob cannot be started by pressing the power button.
3. **An app.** Through an eSIM and the VinFast E-Scooter app, an owner can switch the anti-theft function on and off, receive alerts on a phone and locate the scooter. VinFast's Evo200 brochure lists tracking, journey history and a safe-zone setting among the app-connected features, and these are optional.

Three cautions follow. First, an app belongs to an account. Ask the shop whether it uses the app on rental bikes and who receives the alerts, rather than assuming that your phone will. Second, a fob is a key: treat it like one, and never leave it on the bike or in the boot. Third, a fob that goes missing is not a quick fix on a rental, so keep it in a zipped pocket and not in a bag you put down.

## Where you park matters more than what you carry

Most of the protection comes from the place. A scooter in a guarded lot, with a ticket and an attendant, is exposed far less than one left at the end of a dark alley. Our guide to [parking a motorbike in Hanoi: guarded lots, tickets and towing](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-motorbike-in-hanoi/) explains how the guarded lots work, and the page on [where to park a scooter near Hoan Kiem Lake](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/where-to-park-a-scooter-near-hoan-kiem-lake/) covers the busiest area.

Use this order of preference:

- **Inside your hotel or homestay**, in a space staff watch, if they allow it and the scooter is not charging unattended.
- **A guarded lot with a ticket**, and keep the ticket safe, because it is how the attendant matches you to the bike.
- **A visible spot beside a busy shop** during the day, with the handlebar locked, if there is no guarded lot nearby.
- **Not an unlit alley or a quiet street overnight**, even for a short stay.

If the hotel asks to keep the key, say no unless the shop has agreed. A key held by someone you do not know is a key you have lost control of.

## A thirty-second locking routine

Do the same five things every time, until it is automatic:

1. **Stop on firm ground** and put the bike on its stand.
2. **Turn the handlebar fully to one side** and engage the steering lock. A bike with the bars straight is easier to wheel away.
3. **Switch off and remove the key**, or switch off and put the fob in a zipped pocket. If the model has an anti-theft mode, switch it on and wait for the confirming signal.
4. **Take the helmet and any valuables** with you, and keep the charger out of the boot if the bike will stay for long.
5. **Look back once** from a few steps away: is the bike where it should be, locked, upright and not blocking a doorway?

A second look takes five seconds and catches the common errors: the lock not engaged, the fob still in the ignition area, the scooter on a slope.

## Batteries, chargers and the boot

An electric scooter holds items that a petrol bike does not. Some models carry a second battery in the boot. The Feliz Lite, for example, is listed by a VinFast dealer with an 18 kg removable battery in the boot, and the Vero X brochure also mentions a second pack in the boot as an option. These figures come from VinFast models, not from a rental bike, so ask the shop what your scooter has.

The general advice is simple:

- **Do not leave a removable battery or charger in the boot of a parked scooter** unless the shop has said that is normal for that model.
- **Do not leave valuables in the boot or the underseat space.** A boot lock is a deterrent, not a safe.
- **Charge only where someone can watch.** A national fire-prevention and rescue unit advised in September 2026 against charging overnight or when nobody is there to monitor it, which is also sensible for theft. Our guide to [charging an electric scooter in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/charging-an-electric-scooter-in-hanoi/) explains the timing.
- **Never swap or lend a battery or charger** to anyone, and do not accept an offer from a stranger to "fix" the bike.

## Handover and return: the paper trail

Security begins with a record. At handover:

- Photograph the bike on all sides, the display, the keys or fobs and any damage.
- Count the keys or fobs and write the number on the contract if there is room.
- Confirm the battery level and charging arrangement, and photograph the indicator.

At return, do the same, and hand every key or fob to a staff member and not to a bystander. The photographs will help the shop and you to agree on what the bike looked like when it left and when it came back.

## Everyday habits that reduce risk

- **Keep the bike in sight when you can.** Ten minutes at a cafe window is better than an hour away from it.
- **Avoid leaving it in one spot overnight** on a quiet street, even locked.
- **Do not advertise.** A bag or helmet hanging from the handlebar is an invitation.
- **Never leave the engine ready to go.** A scooter that is switched on, or in ready mode, can be ridden away silently, because an electric motor makes no engine noise.
- **Return to the same guarded spot** each day, so the attendant knows you.

## If the scooter is missing

1. **Stay calm and check the obvious.** Did you park here? Could the attendant have moved it? Could a hotel worker have shifted it into the courtyard?
2. **Ask the attendant or staff** if the bike was moved, and ask whether there is camera footage.
3. **Call the shop at once** on 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969). Give your name, the time you parked and the exact location, and say whether the key or fob is with you.
4. **Follow the shop's instructions.** Ask whether it wants you to report the loss to the local police station and what paperwork it needs. Keep the contract, the parking ticket and your photographs ready.
5. **Do not chase anyone and do not negotiate** with someone who offers to return the bike for money. Let the shop and the authorities handle it.

How the loss is treated depends on the contract and the circumstances, so ask before you sign what happens if the bike is stolen, and whether the deposit is affected. ## FAQ: electric scooter theft prevention in Hanoi

### Is a steering lock enough?

Usually not on its own. Thieves in Hanoi have been reported to break steering locks quickly, so use it together with guarded parking and by taking the key or fob with you.

### Does every electric scooter have an alarm?

No. VinFast describes an alarm and app features on certain models. Ask the shop whether your scooter has one and how it works.

### Can I buy a lock to add?

The shop does not provide an extra lock. If you want to bring your own, tell the shop before you fit it, so that nothing blocks the wheel when staff need to move the bike.

### Should I leave the battery in the bike?

Follow the shop's advice for the model. Never leave a removable battery or charger in an unattended boot for long.

### What if the key or fob is lost?

Call the shop immediately and do not leave the bike unattended. Replacement is usually a shop matter and may be charged under the contract, so ask.

### Who decides who pays if the bike is stolen?

The rental contract and the circumstances decide. Read the relevant clause before signing and ask the shop to explain it.
