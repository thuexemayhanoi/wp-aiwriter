On a Vietnamese motorbike registration certificate, "under 50cc" means that the engine displacement printed on the card, in the line marked Dung tich and measured in cubic centimetres (cm3), is no larger than 50. That figure is one of three tests the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (Law 36/2024/QH15, effective 1 January 2025) applies to decide whether a two-wheeler is a moped, called xe gan may, or a motorcycle: displacement of no more than 50 cm3 for a petrol engine, or no more than 4 kW for an electric motor, and a design speed of no more than 50 km/h. The card gives you the first test directly and says nothing about the speed test. This guide explains where the number appears, how the law words the ceiling, why a bike sold as a 50cc is almost never exactly 50, what replaces the number on an electric bike, and what the card cannot tell you, as of October 2026.

## Where the number sits on the card

The registration certificate for motorcycles and mopeds, commonly called a ca vet, is a small card printed on security paper with a QR code. Its form, issued under Ministry of Public Security Circular 79/2024/TT-BCA, which took effect on 1 January 2025 and replaced Circular 24/2023/TT-BCA, has a block headed with the vehicle's characteristics. The lines in that block include:

- **Nhan hieu**, the brand.
- **So loai**, the model code.
- **Loai xe**, the vehicle type.
- **Dung tich**, the displacement, followed by the unit cm3.
- **Cong suat**, the power, followed by kW.
- **So may** and **So khung**, the engine and chassis numbers.

The QR code on the form contains the plate number, the plate colour and the engine and chassis numbers. A later circular amended the form templates from 1 March 2025, so the layout of a newer card can differ from the one in the 2024 circular, but the idea is the same: the displacement is a printed figure, not something you have to estimate.

## How the law words the 50 cm3 line

Article 34 of Law 36/2024/QH15 defines a moped as a two- or three-wheeled motor vehicle designed for roads, with a design speed of not more than 50 km/h, and, for a heat engine, a working or equivalent displacement of not more than 50 cm3, or, for an electric motor, a power of not more than 4 kW. Bicycles with a motor assist are excluded. Everything else that fits the motorcycle description is a motorcycle.

| Test | Moped | Motorcycle |
|---|---|---|
| Petrol engine displacement | Not more than 50 cm3 | More than 50 cm3 |
| Electric motor power | Not more than 4 kW | More than 4 kW |
| Design speed | Not more than 50 km/h | More than 50 km/h |

Failing any single test moves the vehicle into the motorcycle class. That matters for the rider: Article 59 allows a person of 16 or over to ride a moped, while class A1 and class A licences are issued from 18, and a moped needs no licence class at all.

## "Under 50" and "not more than 50" are not the same words

Older texts used a slightly different wording. Under the 2008 Road Traffic Law, which applied until 31 December 2024, a 16-year-old could ride a moped with a displacement "under 50 cm3" (duoi 50 cm3), and the age rule for motorcycles began at "50 cm3 or more". Law 36/2024/QH15 instead says "not more than 50 cm3" (khong lon hon 50 cm3), which includes exactly 50.

The penalty decree for the road traffic rules, Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, still describes the offence of a 16- or 17-year-old riding a motorcycle by the phrase "from 50 cm3 or more", or an electric motor "from 4 kW or more". Read literally, a vehicle at exactly 50 cm3 or exactly 4 kW sits in an awkward overlap between the two texts. In practice the overlap rarely matters, because manufacturers rate small engines just below the line, and a registration card that shows exactly 50, or a figure above it, is a reason to ask the shop for clarity rather than a reason to guess.

## Why a "50cc" bike is rarely exactly 50

The name on a bike is a marketing round number. The card, by contrast, records the actual displacement. Two examples from Honda illustrate the gap. Honda Global's history of the Super Cub describes the first Super Cub, the C100 of 1958, as having a 49cc four-stroke engine, and places the 50cc Super Cub within the 49cc to 125cc engine-size family. At the other end, Honda Vietnam lists its Lead 125 at 124.8 cm3, not 125.

So when you see "49" or "49.5" on a registration, expect it to be a 50cc bike, and when you see "108" or "110", expect a bike that is not a moped, whatever label it carries in a rental listing. A bike advertised as a 50cc but showing a figure well above 50 is a different vehicle from the one advertised.

## Electric bikes: kW replaces cm3

An electric bike has no displacement, so the test is power. Law 36/2024/QH15 uses 4 kW as the moped ceiling for an electric motor and 11 kW as the class A1 ceiling. On the registration card, the Cong suat line is where the power is recorded. An electric scooter marketed as the equivalent of a 50cc would be expected to show 4 kW or less, together with a design speed of no more than 50 km/h, but only the power appears on the card. ## What the card cannot tell you

The displacement line answers one question only. Three things stay outside it:

1. **Design speed.** The card does not show the design speed, and the moped definition requires it to be no more than 50 km/h. The manufacturer's data covers this.
2. **The bike's current state.** A card records the bike as it was registered. It cannot show whether an engine has since been enlarged or swapped.
3. **Whether the card belongs to the bike in your hands.** Check that the plate, and where possible the chassis and engine numbers, match what the card says.

## Why the number matters to a renter

The displacement line decides three practical things. First, it decides whether you need a licence: a rider with no motorcycle licence can only legally take out a bike that is a moped, so a bike that looks like a 50cc but is actually larger puts that rider outside the law without any warning. Second, it decides the minimum age, which is 16 for a moped and 18 for anything larger. Third, it affects the speed you may ride at, since mopeds are held to a lower operating limit than other motorcycles. The responsibility for the licence lies with the rider, which is why checking the number before you leave the shop is worth two minutes.

## Worked examples

| What the card shows | How to read it |
|---|---|
| Dung tich 49 cm3 | Inside the petrol moped limit, subject to the speed test |
| Dung tich 49.5 cm3 | Same reading, a typical 50cc rating |
| Dung tich 50 cm3 exactly | At the line; the law says not more than 50, but confirm with the shop |
| Dung tich 108 or 110 cm3 | A motorcycle; class A1 licence needed |
| Dung tich 124.8 cm3 | A motorcycle within class A1, as the Honda Lead 125 |
| Cong suat 3 kW, electric | Inside the 4 kW moped limit, subject to the speed test |
| Cong suat 5 kW, electric | Above 4 kW, so a motorcycle |

The table is a reading guide, not a ruling on any particular bike, and the final decision rests with the authorities and the manufacturer's data.

## FAQ about under-50cc on a registration

### Does under 50cc mean the bike is exactly 50cc?

No. The Law 36/2024/QH15 test is a displacement of not more than 50 cm3. Most 50cc bikes are rated slightly below, such as 49 cm3.

### Where on the card do I find the engine size?

On the line headed Dung tich, in cm3, in the block describing the vehicle's characteristics.

### Is a bike with 110 cm3 on the card a moped?

No. Above 50 cm3 it is a motorcycle and needs the matching licence class.

### What replaces cm3 on an electric bike?

The power in kilowatts. A moped has an electric motor of not more than 4 kW and a design speed of not more than 50 km/h.

### Does the card tell me how fast the bike can go?

No. The design speed is not on the card. The 40 km/h operating cap for mopeds comes from Circular 38/2024/TT-BGTVT, effective 1 January 2025.
