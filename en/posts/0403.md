Before renting a motorbike in Hanoi, ask the shop fourteen things about damage liability: what counts as damage and what is ordinary wear, who decides the repair cost and on what evidence, whether the deposit is also the limit, what happens after a fall, a theft, a breakdown or an accident involving another person, and what insurance does and does not cover. Get each answer in writing on the rental paper or in a message, because the contract and the Civil Code, not an assurance at the counter, decide who pays. This guide gives the questions in a sensible order, explains why each matters and describes what a clear answer sounds like. It is general information, not legal advice.

## The legal starting point in one minute

Under Vietnam's Civil Code 2015 (Law No. 91/2015/QH13), a motorbike rental is a lease of property, and three of its rules frame every damage question.

- **Care.** Article 479 says the lessee takes care of the property as if it were their own, carries out minor repairs and maintenance, and must compensate the lessor for any loss or damage they cause. It also says the lessee is not liable for normal wear and tear from using the property.
- **Return.** Article 482 requires the property to be returned in the same condition as received, normal wear and tear excepted, and lets the lessor claim compensation if its value has fallen.
- **Defects.** Article 477 says the lessor must keep the property in the agreed condition and suitable for its purpose throughout the lease, and must repair damage or defects, apart from minor damage the lessee repairs by custom.

So the starting position is balanced: you answer for damage you cause, not for wear or for the bike's own defects. The contract then fills in the detail, which is why the questions below are worth asking.

## Questions about what counts as damage

### 1. What is ordinary wear, and what is damage?

Ask the shop to give examples. A fair answer separates worn tread, a stretched chain or faded paint (wear) from a cracked fairing, a bent lever or a torn seat (damage). Our guide to [what counts as normal wear on a rented Honda Wave](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-counts-as-normal-wear-on-a-rented-honda-wave/) gives part-by-part examples you can quote back.

### 2. Which marks already exist, and where are they recorded?

The answer should be "on the handover sheet and in photographs". If the shop has no record, make one together. The photographs should be taken before you ride, and our [10-minute rental scooter inspection checklist](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-scooter-inspection-checklist/) shows how.

### 3. Are small marks charged, or only repairs?

Some shops ignore light scuffs; others charge for any new mark. A clear answer states the threshold, for example "marks smaller than a coin are not charged", written on the paper.

## Questions about money

### 4. Who decides the repair cost, and how?

Article 585 of the Civil Code says actual damage must be compensated in full and promptly, and that the parties may agree the amount and form of compensation. Article 585 also lets either side ask a court or other competent authority to change an amount that has become unrealistic. In practice, ask whether the cost will be the real cost of parts and labour, whether you can see the damaged part and the receipt, and whether a fixed price list exists.

### 5. Is the deposit also the limit?

This is the most important money question. At Nguyen Ha the cash deposit is VND 2–5 million depending on the bike and the contract, and it is separate from the rent. Ask whether a repair bill above the deposit can be claimed from you, and whether the shop can deduct from the deposit without telling you first. Our guide to [how a motorbike rental deposit works in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbike-rental-deposit-hanoi/) explains the usual pattern.

### 6. When will I be told, and how long do I have to respond?

Ask for notice of any deduction with the amount, the reason and the evidence, and ask how soon the deposit is returned if there is no dispute. A quick, written process protects both sides.

### 7. Can I repair the bike myself or choose the workshop?

Article 479 expects a lessee to do minor repairs and maintenance, but a shop may want its own mechanic for anything that affects safety. Ask which repairs you may do, which you must report and whether an outside bill will be accepted.

## Questions about specific events

### 8. What if the bike is dropped or falls over?

Report it at once. Article 362 of the Civil Code says the injured party must take necessary and reasonable steps to limit its own damage, which is another reason prompt reporting helps both sides. Our guide on [what to do after dropping a rented semi-automatic bike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-to-do-after-dropping-a-rented-semi-automatic-bike/) gives the steps.

### 9. What if it is stolen or the key is lost?

Ask who is responsible, what you must do (lock the steering, park in guarded places, report quickly) and which documents you must produce. Article 329 of the Civil Code states that where leased property can no longer be returned, the security collateral belongs to the lessor, so a theft can cost at least the deposit. Nguyen Ha does not provide an extra lock, and its terms advise locking the steering and parking in guarded places. Our guide to [parking a motorbike in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-motorbike-in-hanoi/) explains guarded lots.

### 10. What if it breaks down through no fault of mine?

Article 477 puts the duty to repair defects on the lessor. Ask whether you pay for a recovery, who pays for a repair that is not your fault and whether the rental period is extended for lost time. Our article on [lost keys, flat tyres and breakdowns with a rental bike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-bike-breakdown-hanoi-what-to-do/) explains the practical steps.

### 11. What about a puncture?

Punctures are common and are often treated separately from damage. Ask whether the shop repairs them, whether you pay for the repair and whether a puncture caused by a pothole counts as damage or as ordinary use.

## Questions about accidents and insurance

### 12. Does the shop's insurance cover damage to the rental bike?

Not necessarily, and probably not. Decree 67/2023/ND-CP on compulsory civil liability insurance for motor vehicle owners describes the insured object as the owner's civil liability for third parties and passengers, and its scope is damage to people and property outside the contract, caused by the vehicle in use. It is not a cover for the repair of the rented bike. The decree also lists exclusions, including some where the driver does not hold a licence valid for the vehicle. A later decree, 220/2026/ND-CP, amended parts of Decree 67/2023 from 1 July 2026, and its published summary mostly concerns construction insurance. Ask the shop or the insurer for the current policy terms. Our guide to [travel insurance and motorbike rentals](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/travel-insurance-motorbike-rental-vietnam/) explains what personal policies usually leave out.

### 13. If I hurt someone or damage another vehicle, who deals with it?

Ask what the shop expects you to do, whom to call and what the contract says about third-party claims. Article 584 of the Civil Code makes a person who harms another's property or health liable to compensate, unless damage results from force majeure or entirely from the injured person's fault. Contact the shop immediately and keep every document.

### 14. Does anything in the contract make me liable for fines or damage caused by someone else?

Ask whether other riders are allowed on the bike, what happens if one of them damages it and how traffic fines are handled after you leave. Our guide to [traffic fines in Vietnam that catch foreign riders out](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/traffic-fines-vietnam-foreign-riders/) covers the fines themselves.

## What a good answer sounds like

Compare the replies with this simple scale.

| Answer | What it suggests |
|---|---|
| Specific, with examples and written on the paper | The shop has thought about it and is open |
| Specific but verbal only | Ask to have it written or messaged |
| "Do not worry, it is normal" | No information; ask again with a concrete case |
| "It depends, we will see" | The rule may be set after the event; ask for the method |
| Refusal to explain | A signal to compare other shops |

A fair shop will accept that you ask these things. Questions asked before you pay are cheaper than disputes afterwards.

## How to put the answers on paper

You do not need a lawyer to make a short record. Write each answer in a line on your copy of the paper, or in a message to the shop's number, 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969), and ask for a reply that says "OK". A message thread with the date on it is easy to keep. If something is added to the printed form, both sides should initial it, since an appendix has the same effect as the contract itself under Article 403 of the Civil Code, provided it does not contradict it. For help reading the paper in English, see our guide to [Hanoi rental contract clauses](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-motorbike-rental-contract-explained/) and the [rental guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-guide/).

## If a dispute arises anyway

Stay calm, keep your photographs and messages, ask for the claim in writing with the evidence and respond in writing. The Civil Code allows the parties to agree the amount of compensation and says an unrealistic amount can be reviewed by a competent authority, so a calm written exchange often resolves it. For a large claim, get advice on the actual contract text, because this guide cannot assess your case.

## FAQ: damage liability on a rental motorbike

### Am I responsible for normal wear and tear?

No. Articles 479 and 482 of the Civil Code excuse normal wear and tear. Damage you cause is different.

### Does the deposit cap what I can be charged?

Only if the contract says so. Ask whether the shop can claim more than the deposit.

### Does compulsory insurance pay for the rental bike's damage?

Decree 67/2023/ND-CP concerns the owner's liability to third parties and passengers, so it is not designed to repair the rented bike. Check the policy.

### Who pays if the bike breaks down without my fault?

The Civil Code puts the duty to repair defects on the lessor, but the contract's wording matters. Ask in advance.

### Should I get answers in writing?

Yes. Write them on the paper or in a message with a date.
