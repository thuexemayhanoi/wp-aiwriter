To return a Cub 50cc cleanly, bring it back on time with a little fuel left in the tank (not empty, and not necessarily full), the same body and controls you were given, the key, the helmet and nothing of yours left on board. At Nguyen Ha the guide figure is about 0.5 to 1 litre in the tank at return, and an empty tank at return costs VND 20,000. The rest of the job is a five-minute walk-round with the shop's staff, set against the photographs you took at pickup. This checklist covers fuel, the parts of a Cub that matter at return, how timing works, what to say about any scuff or fall, and what to confirm before you walk away. It is general information, so follow the shop's own instructions for your bike.

## A five-minute plan for the day you return

Most return problems come from rushing. A few simple habits make the handover short and calm:

1. **Refuel the evening before or a few hours ahead,** not in the last ten minutes, so you are not hunting for a pump while the clock runs.
2. **Dig out your pickup photos** and have them ready on your phone, so you can compare like for like.
3. **Empty the bike of your things:** rack, hook, leg-shield pocket if there is one, and the helmet.
4. **Arrive inside opening hours.** The shop is open 09:00 to 21:00 daily, and a handover after 21:00 is hard to arrange.
5. **Walk round the bike with a staff member,** not alone and not after you have left the key on the desk.
6. **Ask what happens next** with the deposit and note the answer.

If you are not sure how a return usually runs, our general guide to [returning a rental motorbike in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/returning-your-rental-motorbike-hanoi/) sets out the order of events for any bike. This page adds the details that belong to a small Cub.

## Fuel: how much to leave and what to put in

The fuel rule at Nguyen Ha is simple. Petrol bikes are handed over with roughly 0.5 to 2 litres, and they are expected back with roughly 0.5 to 1 litre. An empty tank at return costs VND 20,000. In other words, the shop does not ask you to hand the bike back full, and it does not want it running on fumes.

| Moment | Guide figure | What it means for you |
|---|---|---|
| Handover to you | About 0.5 to 2 litres | Enough to reach a pump, not a full tank |
| Return to the shop | About 0.5 to 1 litre | A little in the tank, so the next renter can set off |
| Empty tank at return | VND 20,000 | Avoid it by topping up the day before |

A Cub's tank is small. Honda's specification for the Japan-market Super Cub 50, a newer fuel-injected model and not necessarily the one you rent, lists a 4.3 litre tank. Even a few extra litres is a big share of the capacity, so a "little top-up" is easy to overdo. If the bike has no fuel gauge, which is common on small bikes, ask the staff how they judge the level, and add fuel in small amounts rather than guessing.

### Which petrol, and how to fill

The simplest advice is to put in what the shop tells you to. Since 1 June 2026, Vietnam's retail network has been moving to E10 petrol, with E5 RON92 expected to remain on sale until the end of 2030, according to VietnamPlus. The Ministry of Industry and Trade reports that most gasoline vehicles in the country are compatible with E5 and E10, and the motorcycle makers' association said models from Honda, Yamaha, Piaggio and SYM need no modification for E10. A rental Cub could be older than the models those statements cover, so ask the shop rather than assume. Our guide to [fuel in Hanoi and filling up a rental scooter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/fuel-in-hanoi-petrol-stations-rental-scooter/) explains how stations work.

When you fill, switch the engine off, take the key out of the ignition, add fuel slowly, and stop when the level reaches the filler neck, not above it. Wipe any spill from the paint and the seat. Do not add additives, oil or anything "to help" the engine.

## The condition walk-round, part by part

A Cub is a step-through bike with plenty of exposed plastic and a light frame, so scuffs show up in predictable places. Work through the list below with the staff member and compare each item with your pickup photographs.

| Area | What to look at | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Leg shield and front fender | Scratches, cracks, loose fixings | Common spot for parking scuffs |
| Floor and side panels | Scrapes at the edges | Often from the stand or a kerb |
| Handlebar and levers | Bent lever, loose grip, scuffed end | A bent lever suggests a drop |
| Mirrors | Cracks, loose stems | Check both sides |
| Lights and indicators | Lens cracks, all lamps working | Test with the key on |
| Horn | Works | A short press is enough |
| Seat | Tears, working lock | Report a new tear |
| Rear rack | Straight, no new bends | Heavy loads can bend a small rack |
| Chain cover | Dents, secure | A loose cover can rattle |
| Foot controls | Gear pedal, brake pedal, kick lever | All present and straight |
| Tyres | Obvious cuts, bulges, low pressure | Compare with pickup photos |
| Stands | Centre and side stand both work | Check they spring back |

Honda's specification lists mechanical brakes of the leading and trailing type on the Japanese Super Cub 50, the layout usually called drum brakes. Drum brakes are tucked inside the wheel hub, so the part you can actually check is feel: both brake controls should work firmly, not stick, and not feel loose. Report anything unusual at return, not afterwards.

If you want to know what counts as ordinary ageing and what does not, our explanation of [what counts as normal wear on a rented Honda Wave](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-counts-as-normal-wear-on-a-rented-honda-wave/) uses a different model but the same logic: faint rubbing at touch points on an older bike is one thing, a fresh crack is another.

## Be upfront about scuffs and drops

If you dropped the bike, clipped a wall or left it on its side, say so at the start of the walk-round. Staff will see a bent lever or a scraped panel anyway, and a frank account is easier to settle than a surprise. Our guide on [what to do after dropping a rented bike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-to-do-after-dropping-a-rented-semi-automatic-bike/) explains the checks that matter and why a quick report is better than silence.

Photograph the damage from two angles in daylight and keep the pictures. If the shop proposes a charge, ask what is being repaired or replaced and ask for the figure to be confirmed in writing before you agree to it. Prices are not set here, so ask the shop.

## Timing: when "on time" ends

Rental time is counted in 24-hour days, so the return time is tied to the time you collected the bike. For petrol bikes at Nguyen Ha, the late return rule is:

- **1 to 5 hours late:** VND 30,000 per hour.
- **From 6 hours late:** one extra rental day.
- **Early return:** no refund for unused rent.

Two practical consequences follow. First, if you pick up at 15:00 and plan a morning return, you are returning "early" in rental terms, and the unused hours are not refunded. Second, if you are running late, call the shop before your time is up and tell them, so you can agree what happens next. The shop is open 09:00 to 21:00 daily, and you can ring 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969). You can see the day, week and month rates for 50cc bikes on the [prices page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/prices/), and the [50cc bikes page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/50cc-bikes/) describes what the shop offers in that class.

## What to hand back

- **The key,** including any spare you were given.
- **The helmet:** one basic helmet per rider is included free, and it comes back with the bike.
- **Any paperwork or card** the shop gave you.
- **Anything you added,** such as a phone holder, a bungee cord or a bag hook.
- **Your own things,** which are easy to forget under the seat or in a rack bag.

Park where the staff ask, switch off, lock the steering if the shop's routine includes it, and do not leave the bike in a spot that blocks the lane or the entrance.

## Before you leave: confirm the deposit

The cash deposit is separate from the rent. It is set by the bike and the contract, between VND 2,000,000 and VND 5,000,000, and it is not a no-deposit service. Before you leave, ask three things: whether the bike has been accepted as returned, how and when the deposit comes back, and whether any charge is being deducted. Write the answers on your phone. Our guide to [how a rental deposit works in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbike-rental-deposit-hanoi/) explains the usual pattern and how to protect yourself.

## FAQ: returning a Cub 50cc

### Do I have to fill the tank before returning a Cub 50cc?

No. The shop's guide is about 0.5 to 1 litre in the tank at return. What you want to avoid is an empty tank, which costs VND 20,000.

### What if I cannot tell how much fuel is left?

Ask the staff how they check on that bike and add a small amount at a pump. Small bikes often have no gauge, so guessing is risky.

### Can I return the bike outside opening hours?

The shop is open 09:00 to 21:00 daily. If you are going to be late, ring 0334 699 969 before your time ends so you can agree what to do.

### What happens if I scratched the bike?

Say so at the start of the walk-round, show your pickup photos, and ask what is being charged and why. Do not sign anything you do not understand.

### Is the deposit returned at once?

That depends on the contract and how the shop handles returns. Ask when you collect and again when you return.

### What if the bike will not start at return time?

Do not force it. Phone the shop and explain. Our guide to [what to do if a rental bike breaks down](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-bike-breakdown-hanoi-what-to-do/) covers the first steps.
