The reliable way to avoid night riding on a return trip is to plan backwards from the evening, not forwards from the morning. Look up the sunset time for your date, subtract the ride home as your map shows it, add a generous buffer for fuel, rain and slow traffic, and the result is a fixed turnaround time. When the clock reaches it you start for home, wherever you are and whatever you have not yet seen. 

In Hanoi from late October to January the sun sets at about 17:15 to 17:30, and on the road dusk is under way before that, so a trip that feels like it still has the afternoon left may in fact have less than an hour of good light. This guide shows how to find your turnaround time, what eats the buffer, how to choose a destination that fits a short day and how to cope if you are caught out. For lighting, visibility and city streets after dark, read [riding at night in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-at-night-in-hanoi/) separately.

## Why the return leg is the risky one

On the outward ride you are fresh, the light is rising and nothing is pressing. The return is different in four ways, and each pushes the same direction.

- **You are later than you planned.** Stops stretch: a second coffee, a longer walk, one more temple. Nobody arrives early at the end of a day.
- **You are more tired.** Fatigue is a recognised risk factor for riders, and the Motorcycle Safety Foundation's general rider guidance tells riders not to ride when tired.
- **The light is going.** Contrast drops well before full darkness, which makes potholes, parked carts and pedestrians in dark clothes harder to see.
- **Everyone else is going home too.** Traffic builds toward the evening peak, so your journey time is longer exactly when you can least afford it.

Put those together and the final hour of a day trip is the one with the least margin. The goal of planning is to make sure that hour happens in daylight.

The sources also show that dusk is not instant. In the timeanddate listing for a Hanoi location on 3 October, sunset was at 17:43 and civil twilight, when there is still enough light to be outdoors without artificial light, ended about 22 minutes later. So in winter the sky stays usable until roughly 17:40 to 17:50, and after that you depend on your headlight.

Summer is different, with later sunsets, but summer brings afternoon thunderstorms that can darken the sky early. Check the date you are riding, not a rule of thumb.

## The lighting rule that changes your plan

Vietnam's Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (Law No. 36/2024/QH15, in force since 1 January 2025) requires drivers to switch on the front lights from 18:00 to 06:00, and at any hour in fog, smoke, dust, rain or other bad weather that limits visibility. It also tells riders to dip the beam when meeting oncoming vehicles and on stretches through populated areas where street lighting is working. As of October 2026 that is the text on the Government policy portal.

Two practical consequences follow. First, in winter the sun has already gone by 18:00, so your headlight must be on for the whole of the dusk period anyway. Second, switching on the light does not make an unfamiliar road easy to read, because a small bike's beam shows you little beyond braking distance at city speeds. Use the light, but do not treat it as a reason to stay out late.

## Work backwards: the turnaround-time method

This is the whole technique, and it takes two minutes before you leave.

1. **Find the sunset time** for your date from a weather or sun-times app.
2. **Subtract 45 minutes** so that you are back before the sky has really dimmed. This is a planning allowance, not a measured figure.
3. **Subtract the ride home.** Ask your map app for the journey from the farthest point of the day to the shop, and use the longer end of any range it shows.
4. **Subtract a buffer** for fuel, a wrong turn, a shower or a closed road. A quarter of the ride time is a reasonable start, with more for rural roads.
5. **The result is your turnaround time.** Write it on your phone's lock screen or set an alarm.

A worked example with made-up numbers: sunset is 17:15, the map shows 1 hour 10 minutes for the way back, you allow 15 minutes of buffer. Counting back, 17:15 minus 45 minutes is 16:30, minus 1 hour 10 minutes is 15:20, minus 15 minutes is 15:05. The turnaround time is about 15:05. A rider who thought they had until sunset has in fact got until the middle of the afternoon.

The method is blunt on purpose. It does not ask how much you have left to see. It asks only whether the clock has reached the number.

## What eats the buffer

Plan for these, because they are the ordinary reasons the return runs late.

- **Fuel.** Running low pushes you to hunt for a station at the worst moment. Our guide on [when to refuel during a day trip](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/when-to-refuel-a-manual-motorbike-during-a-day-trip/) explains how to top up on the way out instead.

- **Breaks.** Short, regular stops make the ride safer but add up. See [how to plan breaks on a long ride](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-plan-breaks-on-a-long-semi-automatic-bike-ride/).

- **Photographs and shopping.** Each is a few minutes that becomes thirty.

- **Meals.** A relaxed lunch is part of the trip, but a late one pushes everything back.

- **Rain.** A downpour can make the afternoon dark and slow every vehicle. Our guide to [riding in Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) covers the season and gear.

- **River crossings.** Reports from spring 2026, when Long Bien Bridge was closed for repairs, described heavy congestion on Chuong Duong Bridge at the morning and afternoon peaks. The bridge reopened to motorbikes on 28 May 2026, but the lesson holds: a crossing can add time at rush hour, so check live traffic before you commit to it.

- **Breakdowns.** A puncture or a stalled engine at 16:30 is a very different problem from the same fault at 11:00. See [what to do with a rental bike breakdown](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-bike-breakdown-hanoi-what-to-do/).

## Choose a trip that fits a short winter day

The easiest way to avoid a night return is to pick a destination whose full round trip fits comfortably in daylight, and to start early enough to use it. Note that the rental shop opens at 09:00, so a morning pickup leaves roughly eight hours of usable light in November and December.

- **Stay close.** Half-day loops on the Long Bien and Gia Lam side, such as the ideas in [Gia Lam and the east bank by scooter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/gia-lam-east-bank-bat-trang-by-scooter/) or the [first ride around Long Bien and the Red River](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/long-bien-red-river-first-ride/), leave wide margins.

- **Treat long days as stretch goals.** A mountain such as [Tam Dao](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-to-tam-dao-by-motorbike/) or the one-day trip in [Hanoi to Ninh Binh and back](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-ninh-binh-one-day-motorbike-itinerary/) makes sense only if you set a turnaround time and keep to it. Read the daylight maths before choosing, not after.

- **Consider staying the night.** For longer destinations an overnight stay removes the return-in-the-dark problem altogether. Our [guide on taking a rental motorbike out of Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/take-rental-motorbike-out-of-hanoi/) explains what to ask the shop first, and the [prices page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/prices/) sets out the rental terms.

## Make the return the easy leg

A few choices at the start make the ride home calmer.

- **Do the far, demanding part first** and leave the closer, flatter stops for the afternoon.
- **Eat at the far point, not near the end,** so the last stretch is a ride and not a meal and a ride.
- **Fill the tank before the turnaround,** when you can do it without hurry.
- **Pick the main road home.** Saving a few minutes on an unfamiliar village lane is a poor trade at dusk. Our guide on [checking whether a navigation app suggests an expressway](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-check-if-a-navigation-app-suggests-an-expressway/) helps you check the route before you commit.
- **Charge your phone** and carry a power bank, since a dead map is a bigger problem at nightfall.

## If dusk catches you anyway

Sometimes the plan fails. A calm response beats a hurried one.

1. **Switch the headlight on early,** before you feel you need it, and check that the tail light works at your next stop.

2. **Slow down.** Speed that suits the day is too fast for dusk. The 2024 law (Article 12) asks riders to keep a speed suited to the bridge, road, traffic, terrain and weather, and a general road-safety guide from the US National Safety Council notes that even high beams show a limited distance, which leaves less time to react.

3. **Stay on main roads you can read,** and avoid unlit rural shortcuts and dyke tops.

4. **Wipe your visor** and take off sunglasses or tinted lenses.

5. **Stop if you are tired.** A ten-minute break at a lit cafe is better than pressing on.

6. **Tell someone.** If you will be late, call the shop on 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969), open 09:00 to 21:00 daily. Ask about the late-return terms, which the prices page also covers, and our guide to [returning your rental motorbike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/returning-your-rental-motorbike-hanoi/) explains the end-of-trip steps.

Do not use a phone in your hand while riding. Article 9 of Law 36/2024/QH15 bans holding and using a phone or other electronic device while operating a moving vehicle, so mount it or use audio directions.

## A one-minute check before you set off

- Sunset time for today, written down.
- Turnaround time worked out and set as an alarm.
- Return route chosen, with a main-road option.
- Fuel level checked; headlight and tail light tested.
- Phone charged, power bank packed, shop number saved.
- A plan B: the nearest stop you could shorten or skip.

## FAQ: avoiding night riding on a return trip

### What time does it get dark in Hanoi in winter?

Sunset is about 17:15 to 17:30 from late October to January, according to published sun tables, and the sky stays usable for roughly another 20 minutes. Check your date.

### Do I have to use my headlight at dusk?

Under Article 20 of Law 36/2024/QH15, the front lights must be on from 18:00 to 06:00 and in fog, rain, dust or other poor visibility. In practice you will want it on earlier than that.

### How early should I start back?

Use the turnaround method. Count back from sunset, subtract the ride home and a buffer, and leave when the clock reaches that time.

### Is riding at night in Hanoi forbidden?

No. The rule is about lights and conditions, not a ban. It is simply riskier, especially on unfamiliar roads, so it is sensible to avoid it on a first visit.

### What if I will be late returning the bike?

Call the shop before you are late, and ask about the late-return terms, which the prices page also covers.
