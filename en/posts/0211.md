A rider on a 50cc moped in Hanoi needs to recognise about a dozen road signs well: the red-ringed prohibition circles (above all P.111a, which bans mopeds, and P.104, which bans motorcycles), the speed sign P.127, the no-stopping and no-parking signs, the no-entry and turn bans, the blue lane signs that name motorbikes, and the warning signs for pedestrians, children and roadworks. Vietnam's signs follow a national standard, QCVN 41:2024/BGTVT, and a moped has two extra things to bear in mind: it is held to a lower speed cap than other motorbikes, and some roads that other motorbikes can use are closed to it. This guide explains the signs, what they mean for a slow, light bike and how the order of signals works.

## The five groups of signs

Vietnam's standard divides road signs into five basic groups: prohibition signs, mandatory (command) signs, danger and warning signs, information signs, and supplementary plates with text signs. The standard describes prohibition signs as mainly circular with a red border and white background, carrying a black symbol or number for what is banned. Warning signs are typically triangular, mandatory signs typically blue and round, and information signs typically blue and rectangular. Codes begin with a letter that tells you the group at a glance: P for prohibition, W for warning, R for mandatory signs and I for information.

You do not have to memorise the codes to ride safely. They are useful because they let you look up a sign you cannot identify, and they help when reading official sources or a navigation app that names a restriction. The practical habit is to read a sign by its colour and shape first, then the picture, then any supplementary plate below it, which can limit a ban to certain hours, vehicles or distances.

## Signs that apply to 50cc bikes in a special way

### P.111a and P.104: the moped ban and the motorcycle ban

Two prohibition signs look alike. P.111a means "no mopeds" (xe gan may); P.104 means "no motorcycles" (xe may). Both are red-ringed white circles with a diagonal red bar from left to right. According to the newspaper Thanh Nien, the motorcycle sign shows a rider seated on a bike, while the moped sign shows a vehicle without a rider and resembles a bicycle. Under Law 36/2024/QH15 a moped is a two- or three-wheeler with a design speed of up to 50 km/h, and a motorcycle is faster than that.

Thanh Nien adds that few roads ban only mopeds without also banning motorcycles, because both are classed as motorbikes. In practice, treat either sign as a "turn around here" instruction on a 50cc bike, unless a plate says otherwise. The standard notes that P.104 does not stop people wheeling a motorbike, and that P.111a does not apply to bicycles. Riding into a banned section is a traffic offence under Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, so do not argue the fine print with the sign. Our guide to [Hanoi's ring roads and highways](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-ring-roads-highways-motorbikes-allowed/) lists where motorbikes of all sizes are not allowed.

### P.127: speed signs and the moped cap

P.127 is the maximum speed sign, a red-ringed circle with a number. The standard says it bans motor vehicles from exceeding the figure, and that riders must also judge speed against weather, road surface, traffic and the vehicle. For a 50cc moped the number on the sign is rarely the one that binds you. Circular 38/2024/TT-BGTVT, in force since 1 January 2025, sets a maximum operating speed of 40 km/h for mopeds on roads other than expressways. A sign showing 50 or 60 therefore does not lift that cap. Our [speed limits guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/speed-limits-motorbikes-vietnam/) gives the full picture.

Two related signs are worth knowing: R.420, which begins a built-up area, and DP.134, which ends a speed limit. The standard notes that inside the zone shown by R.420, a P.127 sign still applies when you meet one.

## Signs that control where and how you stop

- **P.130, no stopping and parking.** You may not stop or park along that stretch.
- **P.131, no parking.** You may not park the bike there; do not leave it unattended. Our [parking guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-motorbike-in-hanoi/) explains guarded lots and where staff move bikes.
- **P.102, no entry.** Placed where a one-way street would have you heading into oncoming traffic. The standard says it bans all vehicles, motor and non-motor, though pedestrians may use the pavement or verge.
- **P.123 and P.124, turn bans.** These forbid left or right turns and U-turns, sometimes with a plate showing hours or vehicle types.
- **P.125, no overtaking.** Rarely a worry for a moped, but it appears on narrow or winding sections.
- **P.132, give way to oncoming vehicles on a narrow road.** This one matters in the alley-like streets of the Old Quarter.

A 50cc bike is easy to park and easy to turn, which tempts riders to stop quickly or squeeze through a gap. The signs are there to stop that.

## Blue signs: lanes and directions

Blue circular signs are mandatory instructions. R.301 shows the direction vehicles must take, for example straight only or right only. R.412d, a lane for motorbikes, is the one that helps a moped rider. According to the standard it marks a lane reserved for motorbikes and mopeds, and it is placed above the lane at the start of the road. Law 36/2024/QH15 Article 13 adds that where a lane is reserved for one type of vehicle, others may not enter it. A similar family, R.403e and R.403f, marks roads for motorbikes, or for motorbikes and bicycles. An end-of-lane sign marks the end of the reservation.

Lane rules for slow vehicles are simple. Article 13 says vehicles moving at a slower speed must stay on the right. That is where a 50cc moped belongs. Our [lane rules guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/lane-rules-motorbikes-vietnam/) goes deeper on multi-lane roads.

## Warning signs that matter at low speed

Warning signs for a slow bike are mainly about surfaces and people. The standard lists W.224, a pedestrian crossing ahead; W.225, children; W.227, roadworks; W.222a, slippery road; W.221a and W.221b, an uneven road and a speed hump; W.220, a steep climb; and W.247, a warning about a stopped vehicle taking part of the carriageway. On W.224 the standard says vehicles must slow down and give way to pedestrians, and proceed only when they do not endanger them. At W.227 you must reduce speed suitably.

For a 50cc bike with small wheels, the uneven-road and speed-hump signs deserve respect, since a small wheel drops into a pothole harder than a large one. Our guide to [riding over speed bumps](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-a-manual-motorbike-over-speed-bumps/) covers technique on a different bike, but the principle of slowing early is the same.

## P.128: the no-horn sign

P.128 is the no-horn sign and bans the horn on that stretch. Law 36/2024/QH15 Article 21 separately bans continuous honking and horn use between 22:00 and 05:00 in residential areas and near medical facilities. Use the horn sparingly and only to warn of danger or to signal an overtaking move, as the Law describes.

## Which signal wins: the order of precedence

Hanoi crossings often have a traffic light, a sign and a person directing traffic at once. QCVN 41:2024/BGTVT sets the order. The hand signals of the person directing traffic come first, then traffic lights, then road signs, then road markings and other devices. A temporary sign overrides a fixed sign that says something different, and a temporary sign usually carries a plate marked as temporary.

Traffic lights follow the rule you would expect: green means go, but pedestrians already in the road must be given way; red means stop behind the stop line; flashing yellow means proceed with caution, slow down or stop for pedestrians and others. Countdown timers, where fitted, show how long the current light will last. Our [guide to crossing Hanoi intersections](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/crossing-hanoi-intersections/) explains how the flow works in practice.

## FAQ: road signs for 50cc riders

### Is a 50cc moped affected by signs that ban motorcycles?

Usually yes in practice. The sign meant for mopeds is P.111a, but few roads ban only mopeds, so treat P.104 as a ban on you too unless a plate says otherwise.

### What is the speed limit for a 50cc moped?

The Circular in force since 1 January 2025 sets a maximum of 40 km/h for mopeds on non-expressway roads, whatever a higher sign says.

### What does P.131 mean?

No parking. You may stop briefly but must not leave the bike.

### Which sign shows the lane for motorbikes?

R.412d marks a lane for motorbikes and mopeds. Other vehicles must not enter it.

### Do signs override traffic lights?

No. The order is the person directing traffic, then lights, then signs, then markings, and temporary signs override fixed ones that conflict.

### Where can I check a sign I do not recognise?

The national standard QCVN 41:2024/BGTVT lists every sign by code. The link is in the Sources.
