Parking a scooter near one of Hanoi's large hospitals is manageable if you treat it as two separate tasks: dropping someone off, and leaving the bike for a long stay. Use the gate that the hospital reserves for motorbikes, park in an attended lot rather than on the pavement, keep clear of emergency routes, and keep your ticket. Avoid the early-morning and early-afternoon rush if you can. If you are unwell yourself, visiting overnight, or heading to an emergency department, a taxi or ride-hailing car is usually the better choice than a rental bike. This guide uses official hospital guidance and city enforcement reports, checked as of October 2026.

## Why hospital streets are harder than ordinary parking

A hospital draws patients, relatives, ambulances, delivery vans and staff into the same few streets, and most of the big public hospitals in Hanoi were built long before today's traffic. Bach Mai, Viet Duc, the K hospital sites and the central maternity and paediatric hospitals all sit on crowded roads where the legal parking supply is limited.

That pressure shows up in enforcement. In July 2026 the Vietnamese outlet Cong Ly reported a check around Thanh Nhan Hospital, Hanoi Oncology Hospital and nearby streets such as Tran Dai Nghia, Dai Co Viet, Ta Quang Buu and Bach Mai. Officials described a dense cluster of hospitals, schools and offices with limited parking, and said cars, taxis, trucks and motorcycles were being recorded for stopping at no-parking signs, on pedestrian areas, at junctions and against the flow of traffic. One morning alone produced ten recorded cases.

You do not need to be nervous about this, only deliberate. The people who run into trouble are usually those who stop "for two minutes" in front of the gate.

## Start with the hospital's own entrance rules

Hospitals publish their own traffic arrangements, and following them is the fastest way to avoid a wasted loop around the block. Bach Mai Hospital's Hanoi campus at 78 Giai Phong Street is a good example because it states the rules plainly.

In guidance dated March 2025, the hospital said Gate 1 on Giai Phong is for cars and has a pick-up and drop-off point. Gate 2 is for cars leaving during office hours. Gates 3 (Phuong Mai) and 4 (the on-demand examination building) are open to both cars and motorbikes. It also recommended buses and shuttle services, naming routes 25, 28 and 38 as stopping at Gate 1, and said the worst congestion near the hospital falls at about 7 to 9 am and 1 to 2 pm.

The same hospital also publishes grounds rules for vehicles: a maximum speed of 5 km/h, no horn, no parking at building entrances or on routes needed by ambulances and fire engines, and a warning that obstructive vehicles can be towed at the owner's cost. Check the hospital's current notice before you go, because gates and rules change.

## Separate drop-off from parking

The most useful habit is to ask what you actually need. Most mistakes come from mixing these up.

**A quick drop-off.** If you are bringing someone with limited mobility, stop only where the hospital allows it. Bach Mai's guidance limits car pick-ups at its main gate to about two minutes. For a motorbike, use the gate and lane the security staff direct you to, help the person out, then move on. Do not leave a bike unattended while you walk someone inside.

**A longer stay.** Go to a lot that issues tickets and follow the guards' directions to the designated yard. Bach Mai's guidance for cars says fees follow the city's published tariff, and the same principle is a sensible expectation for bikes. Do not settle the price in conversation; look for the listed rate and keep your paper ticket.

**An overnight stay.** Bach Mai says outright that it does not encourage leaving vehicles overnight, and advises families staying overnight to use public transport, taxis or ride-hailing instead. If you are supporting someone through a long admission, that advice is worth taking.

## The rules on the street outside

Outside the hospital gate, the general road rules apply. Under Law 36/2024/QH15, in force since 1 January 2025, a vehicle stopping or parking on a city road must stay along the kerb or pavement on its right-hand side, with the nearest wheel no more than 0.25 m away, without obstructing pedestrians or other vehicles. The Law also prohibits stopping or parking at pedestrian crossings and in several other places, including bridges and the area right by junctions unless traffic arrangements permit it.

If a vehicle obstructs traffic and its owner cannot be found, traffic police may relocate it under Article 69. The result for a visitor is not just a penalty notice. It can be a long search for where the bike has been taken, with a hospital appointment waiting.

Our [Hanoi parking guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-motorbike-in-hanoi/) explains the difference between guarded lots, pavement stalls and towing zones in the city. A pavement full of motorcycles near a hospital entrance does not mean that spot is lawful. Look for signage, an attendant in a uniform or a clear tariff board before you leave the bike.

## A practical checklist before you leave the bike

- Read the hospital or lot's rules at the entrance and follow the direction the guards give.
- Park in an attended, ticketed area rather than on the pavement or in front of a gate.
- Do not block ambulance lanes, loading bays, ramps, or doors, even briefly.
- Lock the steering and take the key with you.
- Keep the ticket in a pocket; do not leave it in the basket.
- Take your helmet with you if the attendant allows, or lock it securely to the bike.
- Photograph where you parked, since large hospital campuses have several yards.
- Check the closing time of the lot if you plan to leave late.
- Keep your phone charged in case you need to call a ride home.

## Choosing where to park around different hospitals

Each large hospital has its own pattern.

**Bach Mai (Dong Da side).** Use Gate 3 or Gate 4, as the hospital advises. Expect heavy queues at the start of office hours and after lunch. An afternoon arrival is easier on traffic, and the hospital itself recommends afternoons and weekends for non-urgent visits because they are less crowded.

**Viet Duc and the central maternity hospitals.** These sit in narrow streets near Hoan Kiem, where kerb space is scarce and street stops draw scrutiny. A parking-platform guide published in May 2026 lists on-street motorbike points along Phu Doan and Trieu Quoc Dat and larger pay lots at some distance from the hospital, though it is written for car drivers and has a commercial interest. Treat it as a starting list only and verify on site. For the lakeside area, our guide to [where to park a scooter near Hoan Kiem Lake](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/where-to-park-a-scooter-near-hoan-kiem-lake/) covers the general approach.

**Other large hospitals.** Look up the hospital's official visitor page or call its information line to ask which gate to use for motorbikes. Information changes and hospital websites are generally more current than travel blogs.

## When a scooter is not the right way to go

A rental bike is a convenience, not an obligation. Choose a different option when:

- You or the person you are with is acutely unwell. Do not ride yourself while in pain, dizzy or distressed. Ask your accommodation to call an ambulance or book a taxi.
- You are going to an emergency department. Ambulances and emergency routes must stay clear, so there is little room for improvised stops.
- It is late at night or raining hard. See [riding in the Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) for why wet streets and fatigue do not mix.
- You will be at the hospital all day. A long stay costs more in parking fees and worry than a few ride-hailing trips.
- You are carrying equipment, bags or a patient who cannot sit upright. A car is safer.

If you decide to ride, collect the bike when the shop is open (09:00 to 21:00 daily), check lights and brakes using our [10-minute inspection checklist](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-scooter-inspection-checklist/), and ask staff about the rental period. ## Paying and keeping receipts

Parking at hospitals is paid in cash or by whatever method the lot lists. Keep small notes. Our guide to [paying for fuel, parking and tolls as a visitor](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/paying-fuel-parking-tolls-hanoi-visitor/) explains what to expect. If you are asked for far more than a posted tariff, do not argue at the gate. Ask to see the price list, note the lot and speak to the hospital's security desk afterwards.

## FAQ

### Which Bach Mai gate is for motorbikes?

Bach Mai's March 2025 guidance says Gates 3 and 4 admit motorbikes, while Gate 1 on Giai Phong is for cars. Confirm on the hospital's website before you travel.

### Can I wait in front of the hospital gate for someone?

Not for long. Hospital guidance for pick-ups limits waiting at the drop-off point to a couple of minutes, and Law 36/2024/QH15 prohibits obstructive stopping on the road outside.

### Is it better to use a taxi than a scooter?

Often yes, especially for emergencies, late visits, overnight stays and rainy days. A scooter makes more sense for short, daytime visits when you are well and the hospital has a motorbike gate.

### What if the attendant says the yard is full?

Ask where else to go, and use another attended lot nearby. Do not move onto the pavement.

### Does the rental include parking?

No. Parking is paid separately wherever you leave the bike. See the [rental guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-guide/) for what a rental covers.
