The safest place for shopping on an automatic scooter is low and central: in the under-seat box first, then in the footwell area only if it is clear of your feet and the controls, and on the front hook only for something light. Honda Vietnam's Lead 125 owner's manual gives specific limits: 1.5 kg for the front hook and front glove box combined, 10 kg for the under-seat box and 3 kg for the rear grab handle. It also says luggage should be tied tightly, balanced and as close to the centre of the scooter as possible. This guide turns those printed limits into a practical routine for a market or supermarket trip in Hanoi: what to put where, what never to hang from the handlebars, how to tie a load, and how to ride differently once the bike is loaded.

## Why a loaded scooter rides differently

Honda's manual states the principle plainly. Carrying extra weight changes handling, braking and stability, so the rider should travel at a speed that suits the load. Overloading or loading badly can cause a crash.

On a scooter the effect is easy to feel. A bag swinging from the handlebar tugs the steering, a heavy load high at the back makes the front feel light, and anything hanging low on the left can brush the exhaust or the rear wheel. The aim is to keep the weight low, near the middle and fixed in place, so that the scooter steers and brakes as it did when it was empty.

## The load limits printed for a Honda Lead

The owner's manual for the Lead 125 is published in Vietnamese by Honda Vietnam. Its tyre and loading information gives these maximum loads:

| Storage point | Maximum load printed by Honda |
|---|---|
| Front hook and front glove box (together) | 1.5 kg |
| Under-seat storage box | 10 kg |
| Rear grab handle | 3 kg |

The same manual warns against hanging bulky items from the hook, because they can slip off or get in the way of your feet, and against putting valuables or fragile items in the front compartment. It also says the helmet hook under the seat is for use when parked only, and that you should not ride with a helmet hanging from it.

Other Honda scooters print similar numbers: Honda's published manuals for other scooter models list 1.5 kg for hooks and front boxes and about 10 kg for the centre compartment. The Air Blade is a different model, so ask the shop for the loading label or the manual for the bike you rent. Do not assume the limits are the same.

## Where each kind of shopping belongs

### Under the seat: heavy and compact items

The under-seat box takes the heaviest, most compact items, because the weight sits low and near the centre. Honda gives the Lead's storage as more than 37 litres and the Air Blade's as 23.2 litres. Good candidates:

- Rice, sugar and other dry goods in sealed bags.
- Tins and bottles, wedged so they cannot roll or knock together.
- A pack of eggs or fruit, in a hard container and not under heavier items.
- Your helmet, if it fits, when you are walking round the market.

Keep the 10 kg limit in mind, and do not store anything flammable or heat-sensitive there. The box sits near the engine, so frozen or chilled food, soft chocolate or anything that melts will suffer. Remember to check that you have the key, and not locked it inside, before closing the seat. Honda's manual specifically warns about leaving the key in the box.

### The front hook: one light bag

The hook is for a single light bag, such as bread, herbs or a small bag of fruit, and its limit of 1.5 kg is shared with the front compartment. A litre bottle of water on its own is close to that limit. If the bag is bulky or swings, it does not belong on the hook, because it can catch your knee on a turn or loosen the steering.

### The front glove box: small and dry

Keep a phone, a wallet pouch and keys in a small pouch in the front compartment, where it is easy to reach, but do not put valuables in a place that is easy to open when parked. Honda also warns against fragile items there. The same 1.5 kg total applies.

### The footwell: only if it is clear

Some riders put a bag on the floor between the feet. That can work for a light, soft bag if the floor is flat, the bag cannot slide back under the heel, and nothing blocks the brake lever or foot movement. It is a poor place for anything heavy, tall or round, and it cannot be used when carrying a passenger. If you are unsure, put it in the under-seat box.

### The rear grab handle: light and secure

Honda's manual gives 3 kg for the Lead's rear grab handle. It is a handle for the passenger or for lifting the bike, so a bag tied to it should be light, flat and tied tightly enough that it cannot swing or reach the rear light. Wide items that stick out past the bodywork are a hazard to people walking past.

## Packing before you leave the shop or market

Five minutes of packing makes the ride safer:

1. **Sort by weight.** Heavy and hard items go in the under-seat box. Soft and light items go on the hook or in a small bag.
2. **Use fewer, smaller bags.** One bag per hand-load is easier to control than six thin plastic ones.
3. **Double bag leaks.** Fish, meat and wet vegetables can drip. Put them in a second bag, in a hard container if possible.
4. **Separate fragile items.** Eggs, glass and ripe fruit belong in a hard box or on top.
5. **Keep sharp ends covered.** Spring onions, bamboo sticks and tools should not poke out where they could snag someone.
6. **Check the seat closes.** If the seat will not close without force, the load is too big. Honda's manual says to press the seat shut and lift it slightly to confirm it is latched.

Your own backpack worn on your back can carry soft items and keeps your hands free, but a heavy backpack changes your balance and heats your back. Keep it light, and keep your arms free to steer.

## What not to do with shopping

- **Do not hang heavy bags from the handlebars.** They affect steering and can catch the brake levers.
- **Do not hang bags from the mirrors or indicators.**
- **Do not place anything near the lights or the exhaust.** Honda's manual specifically warns against it.
- **Do not carry large, flat or wide items such as boards, trays or a case of drinks on the footboard.**
- **Do not hold a bag in your hand while riding.** You need both hands on the grips.
- **Do not carry a bag across your lap** or between your body and the handlebars.
- **Do not let a bag touch the rear wheel.** Straps and plastic can wind round the axle.

## Tying a load

If you carry something that is too big for the box, such as a bag of rice or a box, tie it where the maker meant luggage to be tied, and tie it as follows:

- Place it low, central and in line with the bike, not off to one side.
- Use straps, a luggage net or a strong cord. Ask the shop if one can be lent, because it is not listed among the items included with a rental.
- Make a tight, flat load. A loose load sways, and the sway is felt in the steering.
- Check that no strap or loose end can reach the wheel or the exhaust.
- Test it by pushing hard on the load before you move off. If it shifts, retie it.

Honda's manual says luggage should be tied securely, balanced and as close to the centre as possible. If the load is large enough that it needs more than this, take a taxi or a delivery service.

## Size limits set by Vietnamese road rules

Vietnam's Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (Law 36/2024/QH15), in force since 1 January 2025, sets limits on loads carried on motorbikes and mopeds. Article 33, paragraph 5 says that goods must not extend more than 0.3 m on each side of the width of the luggage rack as designed by the manufacturer, or more than 0.5 m beyond the rear of the rack, and that the height of the load measured from the road surface must not exceed 2 metres. A scooter has no conventional rack, so the practical reading is simply to keep the load within the body of the bike. Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, which sets the penalties, covers carrying bulky objects, with higher penalties if it causes a crash. As of October 2026 these texts are what the sources below show, but rules and penalty tables can change, so ask the shop if in doubt. A shopping trip should never come close to the limits.

## Riding with a load

A loaded scooter calls for a gentler style:

- **Pull away slowly.** Open the throttle gradually and feel whether the front is light.
- **Brake earlier.** Honda says to use both brake levers together and to close the throttle fully first. The extra weight lengthens the stop.
- **Take corners slower.** A load at the back or the front shifts the balance.
- **Avoid sudden swerves,** particularly on wet ground. Our guide to [riding in Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) explains how the surface changes.
- **Look at the seat and bag after the first bump.** Things shift after a pothole.
- **Park on level ground,** with the steering locked, and do not leave valuables on show.

## FAQ: carrying shopping on a scooter

### How much can I hang from the hook?

On the Honda Lead, the manual limit for the hook and front compartment together is 1.5 kg. Check the manual for your actual bike.

### Is the under-seat box strong enough for heavy items?

Honda gives 10 kg for the Lead's box. Keep heavy items low and make sure the seat closes and latches.

### Can I carry a bag between my feet?

Only a small, soft, light bag, and only if it cannot slide or block the pedals and levers.

### Can I hang my helmet from the helmet hook while riding?

No. Honda says to use the hook only when parked.

### Does the rental include straps?

Not as listed. Ask the shop whether one is available.

### What is the maximum height of a load on a motorbike?

Law 36/2024/QH15, Article 33(5), sets a maximum of 2 metres from the road. A shopping load should be far lower.
