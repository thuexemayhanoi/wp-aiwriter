Thanh Xuan is a district for riders who can switch between two moods. The big roads, Nguyen Trai, Khuat Duy Tien, Nguyen Xien and Le Van Luong, carry heavy commuter traffic and one notoriously busy five-level junction, while the lanes between them are narrow, residential and slow. A rented automatic scooter handles both, provided you ride the main roads outside rush hours, keep to your lane at the big junction, and let the side lanes set the pace rather than a navigation app set on "fastest". This guide explains where Thanh Xuan sits after Hanoi's 2025 reorganisation, how the main corridor behaves, which destinations are worth a stop, how to park without blocking anyone and when to avoid the area altogether. It is written for visitors, not for commuters, so it favours predictable routes over shortcuts.

## Where Thanh Xuan is, and what changed in 2025

Thanh Xuan lies south-west of the centre, beyond Dong Da. Its northern edge follows Truong Chinh, Nguyen Trai and the To Lich river, according to a property-market profile of the area, and it borders Cau Giay to the north-west. For many years it was one of Hanoi's urban districts, with eleven wards.

From 1 July 2025 Hanoi stopped using the district level. The National Assembly's amended law on local government abolished districts and created a two-tier system, and Hanoi's commune-level units were cut to 126, of which 51 are wards. The area of the former Thanh Xuan District is now covered mainly by three wards named Thanh Xuan, Khuong Dinh and Phuong Liet, with some edges handed to neighbouring wards. Older maps, hotel listings and even some university websites still say "Thanh Xuan District", and that is fine for finding a street. For riding, what matters is the road network, which did not change.

If a map app or a sign shows both names, treat them as the same place. A destination written as "Thanh Xuan Trung" or "Thuong Dinh" is a former ward name that is still used in addresses.

## The main corridor: Nguyen Trai, Khuat Duy Tien and the elevated line

Nguyen Trai is the spine. It runs from the Ha Dong side towards Nga Tu So and the city, lined with university campuses, banks, shopping centres and apartment towers. Khuat Duy Tien and Nguyen Xien feed into it from the Ring Road 3 side, and Le Van Luong runs parallel to the north, famous for a dense wall of towers. A VnExpress report on Hanoi's traffic described Le Van Luong's roughly two-kilometre length as home to some 40 skyscrapers, which explains the volume of office workers on two wheels at the start and end of the working day.

Above the ground, Metro Line 2A, the Cat Linh to Ha Dong line that opened on 6 November 2021, has stations at Thuong Dinh and Vanh Dai 3 in this area. The elevated Ring Road 3 also passes over the Khuat Duy Tien junction. The result is that the pillars, the viaduct and the railway make the junction feel like a tunnel of concrete and the lanes beneath them are sometimes unclear at first sight.

### Why the junction is hard

The Khuat Duy Tien, Nguyen Trai and Nguyen Xien junction is described by Hanoi's Department of Transport as a multi-level intersection with five layers: urban railway, elevated road, elevated pedestrian walkway, a parallel road at ground level and a tunnel. Even so, it has long been a congestion point. From 18 January 2025 the department trialled a new traffic organisation there, with a continuous right-turn lane before the junction, a defined U-turn point under the Ring Road 3 viaduct and a camera system to record violations. Later reports said the junction became more orderly, although queues kept forming on Khuat Duy Tien and Nguyen Xien towards it, including at weekends.

For a visitor, the sensible approach is:

- **Choose your lane early.** Decide before the last 150 metres whether you are going straight, turning right or heading under the viaduct. The new layout rewards riders who stay in a lane.
- **Do not improvise a U-turn.** Use the marked point, or turn at a quieter junction further on.
- **Do not hop onto the pavement** to bypass a queue. Some riders do, and it is a common reason for conflict with pedestrians.
- **Allow extra time.** A short distance on the map can take much longer here at peak hours.

## Timing: when the area is gentle and when it is not

Thanh Xuan is an everyday commuter district, so its rhythm follows the working day. Early morning, from first light until the school and office rush, is calm. Late morning and early afternoon are usually the easiest times to cross the area. The heavy periods are the morning and late-afternoon peaks, and they can stretch on wet days.

Two patterns are worth remembering. First, VnExpress reported on 10 February 2026 that cold rain together with the pre-Tet rush brought the Lang, Truong Chinh, Nguyen Trai and Khuat Duy Tien corridor to a standstill, with a deliveryman describing over 40 minutes for just over 3 km. Holiday weeks and bad weather amplify normal congestion. Second, weekend afternoons are not automatically quiet: a Lao Dong report from February 2025 recorded a jam of about 1.5 km on Nguyen Xien towards the Nguyen Trai junction on a Saturday afternoon.

If you are renting for a few days, plan Thanh Xuan visits for mid-morning or the middle of the day, and leave before the evening peak. ## Local streets: where navigation apps mislead

Between the main roads are residential lanes, called "ngo" and "ngach" in Vietnamese. They are usually wide enough for a single scooter and a pedestrian, often have a sharp bend or a barrier at the entrance, and are shared with children, delivery riders, parked bikes and market stalls. A navigation app set to the fastest route will sometimes push you into them to avoid a queue. As a visitor on a rented bike, you will do better to follow the larger streets and use lanes only for the last stretch to your destination.

Practical rules for the lanes:

1. **Go slowly enough to stop within your visible distance.** Doors, pushcarts and children appear suddenly.
2. **Use the horn briefly and sparingly** at blind corners.
3. **Give way to people on foot,** who often walk in the middle of the lane because there is no pavement.
4. **Do not follow a delivery rider** into a lane you cannot see the end of. If it ends at a wall or a flight of steps, a loaded scooter is hard to turn.
5. **Switch off the "avoid traffic" option** if your app has one, or choose a walking-friendly route and then ride it slowly.

## Parking in Thanh Xuan without blocking anyone

Parking is where visitors most often cause irritation. Road-traffic Law 36/2024/QH15, which took effect on 1 January 2025, sets general rules on stopping and parking. As of October 2026 the points that matter most to a visitor are these, based on Article 18 of the law's published text:

- Do not stop or park at a junction or within 5 metres of its edge.
- Do not stop or park in front of an organisation's gate or within 5 metres either side of a gate with an access route for vehicles.
- Do not stop or park on a pedestrian crossing, at a bus or passenger pick-up point, or where the road is only wide enough for one motor-vehicle lane.
- In the street, park beside the kerb or pavement on the right in your direction of travel, with the nearest wheel no more than 0.25 metres from it.
- Do not park on the roadway or pavement where the rules forbid it.

In practice, use an official guarded lot or a building's basement or yard if one is offered, and keep your ticket. Large complexes and university campuses usually run their own parking, and it is normally easier to use it than to search for a free space on a main road. ## Ring Road 3 and elevated roads

Ring Road 3 passes through Thanh Xuan, in part on an elevated section above Khuat Duy Tien and Nguyen Xien. Whether a motorbike may use an elevated road depends on signs at the ramp, so do not assume. If a sign shows a motorbike with a red bar, stay on the ground-level road. Expressways are different: Article 25 of the same law says motorcycles and mopeds may not travel on an expressway. Our guide to [Hanoi's ring roads and highways](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-ring-roads-highways-motorbikes-allowed/) lists which are open to motorbikes.

## A simple Thanh Xuan route

For a first visit, an out-and-back day plan works better than a loop:

1. Start mid-morning, after the commuter peak, and approach along Nguyen Trai rather than through side lanes.
2. Visit one destination, such as a campus or Thanh Xuan Park, and use its parking.
3. Eat nearby on foot, not by moving the scooter again.
4. If you need a second stop, choose one on the same street.
5. Leave before the late-afternoon peak, retracing the same main road.

## FAQ about riding in Thanh Xuan

### Is Thanh Xuan a good first area for a new visitor?

It is manageable at quiet hours and difficult at peak hours. Visitors with little experience of Hanoi traffic are usually better starting in quieter areas, such as the Long Bien side or the lakeside roads, and coming to Thanh Xuan once they are comfortable.

### Is Thanh Xuan still a district?

Not officially. Hanoi's district level was abolished from 1 July 2025 and the area is now covered by wards, including one called Thanh Xuan. The name remains in everyday use and on older addresses.

### Can I ride the elevated Ring Road 3 on a scooter?

Not automatically. Follow the signs at each ramp, and if you are unsure use the street below. Motorcycles may not use expressways.

### Where do I park near a university campus?

Use the campus or building parking if available, or an official guarded lot nearby. Avoid gates, junctions and narrow lanes.

### What is the best time to cross Thanh Xuan?

Mid-morning and the middle of the day are usually easiest. Avoid the morning and late-afternoon peaks, wet days and the weeks before public holidays.
