The Honda Vision is one of the most sensible scooters for sightseeing inside Hanoi. It is light, slim and easy to turn, it sips fuel, it is simple to park in the crowded streets around Hoan Kiem Lake and the Old Quarter, and on Honda's own figures it weighs only about 97 kg and has a seat height of 785 mm. Its limits show up when a day gets longer or heavier: a small tank of about 4.8 litres, a modest engine that works hard on hills with two adults aboard, a limited luggage allowance and a basic combined brake system. This guide sets out what the Vision does well for a day of looking around the city, where it runs out of margin, and how to tell whether it suits you, using Honda Vietnam's published specifications and owner's manual.

## What Honda says about the Vision

Honda Vietnam's product page for the current Vision lists these figures, which are a good yardstick even if the bike you collect is an earlier model:

- **Kerb weight:** about 97 kg.
- **Length, width and height:** roughly 1,925 mm by 686 mm by 1,126 mm, so it is a narrow machine.
- **Seat height:** 785 mm.
- **Fuel tank:** 4.8 litres.
- **Wheels:** 80/90-16 at the front and 90/90-14 at the rear.
- **Storage:** a covered front pocket with a USB Type C socket, plus a box under the seat.
- **Key:** a smart-key system that lets you locate the bike and unlock it from a distance.

The owner's manual adds that the under-seat box is limited to 10 kg, the front hook and pocket to 1.5 kg and the rear grab handle to 3 kg, and that the bike carries a rider and one passenger. These figures come from Honda, but versions differ by year, so ask the shop which one you will get. The bike actually available is confirmed when you book, and the [motorbikes page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbikes/) shows the models the shop normally offers.

## The strongest points for sightseeing

### It is light and easy to place

Most of a sightseeing day is spent at low speed: turning into side streets, nosing through junctions, finding a gap at the kerb. A kerb weight under 100 kg means the bike is easy to wheel backwards out of a parking row, to hold upright at a stop and to steer around a handcart. The manual gives a turning radius of roughly 1.8 to 1.9 metres depending on version, which is small enough for tight U-turns in a narrow lane. A rider who is not used to automatics will usually feel in control sooner on a Vision than on a heavier scooter.

### It is narrow

At 686 mm wide, the Vision slips through gaps that a full-size scooter would not, which matters in the narrow lanes of the old city. Narrowness also helps when you park, because many pavement spaces are measured in handlebar widths. Our guide to [riding in the Old Quarter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-old-quarter/) explains why that area can be hard going on any scooter and when it is better to walk.

### The seat height suits most riders

A seat height of 785 mm is moderate for a scooter. Many adults can put both feet flat or nearly flat at a stop, which is reassuring in the stop-start rhythm of city traffic. Riders at the short end of the range should still sit on the bike at the shop before accepting it.

### It is gentle on fuel

Idling Stop, described in the manual, switches the engine off briefly at a stop and restarts it when you open the throttle. Honda says the aim is to cut fuel use and noise at traffic lights. It only operates once the engine is warm and you have been above 10 km/h, and the rider must watch the indicator so as not to leave the bike while it is flashing. Many visitors find the first stop disconcerting, so ask the shop to demonstrate the switch.

### It keeps luggage worries small

The Vision does not carry much, but a sightseeing day does not need much: a water bottle, a camera, a rain layer and a phone charger. The front pocket takes small items, and the USB Type C socket there can charge a phone while you ride, as Honda notes it is for charging only. Our guide to [protecting items in an under-seat compartment](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/protecting-items-in-a-scooter-under-seat-compartment/) explains what to put in the box and what to keep on you.

## Where the Vision runs out of margin

### A small tank means planning

At 4.8 litres, the tank is not large, and a day of mixed riding can leave it low before you notice. The shop hands petrol bikes over with roughly 0.5 to 2 litres and expects 0.5 to 1 litre back, and charges a small fee for an empty tank. If you plan a long loop, top up early rather than relying on the last litre. Our guide to [fuel in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/fuel-in-hanoi-petrol-stations-rental-scooter/) explains the stations and the filling-up routine.

### The engine is modest

The Vision is built for city use. On the flat, in town, it is more than adequate. With two adults, luggage and a long incline, it has less reserve than the Air Blade. For a hilly day trip to a mountain road, a stronger scooter or a different plan is better. If you cannot decide, our comparison of the [Honda Vision and Air Blade](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/honda-vision-vs-air-blade-which-scooter-to-rent-in-hanoi/) goes through the trade-offs.

### Braking is basic

Honda's manual describes a combined brake system that shares braking force between the front and rear wheels, and says that for best effect you should use both levers together. It also warns against continuous braking on a long descent, which can overheat the brakes and reduce their effectiveness. The Vision manual describes this combined system and does not describe ABS for the model, so do not assume your bike has it. Honda's pages list front-wheel ABS on certain Lead and Air Blade versions instead. On wet roads, smooth and early braking matters most, and our guide to [riding in Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) helps with that.

### Luggage is limited

The under-seat box takes a helmet in many cases but not always, because Honda notes that some helmets are too big for the box. Two riders with day packs, a camera bag and a souvenir will find the box and the 3 kg rear handle quickly used up. For passenger and luggage tips, see our guide to a [Lead or Vision with two riders and luggage](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/scooter-for-two-riders-and-luggage-hanoi/).

### Rough roads and potholes

The Vision is a light city scooter built around smooth streets. On rough surfaces, such as an unsealed lane near a craft village or a road with patched potholes, slow down well before the bump and keep your weight relaxed.

## A sample day that plays to its strengths

Here is the kind of plan the Vision suits, offered as an idea and not a fixed route.

1. **Morning in the centre.** Park at a guarded lot near Hoan Kiem Lake and walk the Old Quarter on foot. Our guide to [where to park near Hoan Kiem Lake](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/where-to-park-a-scooter-near-hoan-kiem-lake/) covers the options.
2. **Midday move to Ba Dinh.** Ride the quieter streets and read the one-way notes in [Ba Dinh by motorbike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/ba-dinh-by-motorbike/).
3. **Afternoon by West Lake.** The lakeside loop in [Tay Ho by scooter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/tay-ho-west-lake-by-scooter/) suits an easy bike.
4. **Late afternoon return.** Refuel before heading back, and avoid peak-hour streets where you can.

The point is not the route but the pattern: short hops, many stops, low speeds and guarded parking. A Vision handles that pattern well.

## Smart key habits for a day of stops

The smart-key system suits a day of getting on and off, but it needs good habits. The manual says anyone with the fob in range can start the engine and open the seat, so keep it on your body. It also says not to store the fob or a phone in the under-seat box, and to lock the steering and switch the ignition off whenever you leave the bike. Our guide to [parking a motorbike in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-motorbike-in-hanoi/) adds the practical side. The shop does not provide an extra lock, so lock the steering and park in guarded places.

## Who should pick another scooter

A different bike may suit you better if:

- You are tall, heavy or carrying a passenger on long days, because a bigger scooter gives more margin.
- You plan a hilly ride or a long distance, when range, comfort and braking matter more.
- You want a larger box. Honda's pages describe the Lead's under-seat space as above 37 litres on some versions and the Air Blade's as 23.2 litres, which can help if you shop or carry gear.
- You want ABS. Honda's pages describe it on certain Lead and Air Blade versions, so ask the shop.

## Questions to ask the shop

- Which Vision year and version is it, and does it have a smart key or a plain key?
- Is the Idling Stop switch on, and can you show me how it works?
- Does my helmet fit in the box, or where should I carry it?
- How much fuel is in the tank now, and where is the nearest station?
- What are the brake and tyre conditions, as shown in the handover photos?

## FAQ: Honda Vision for sightseeing

### Is the Vision good for beginners on automatics?

Many riders find it approachable because it is light and has no clutch or gears to manage, but anyone new to scooters should practise in a quiet street before heading into traffic.

### Can two people sightsee on a Vision?

The manual allows a rider and one passenger. For a short day it works, but luggage and comfort become limits on longer days.

### How far can a Vision go on a tank?

Honda lists a 4.8-litre tank. Real distance depends on riding style and load, so refuel early and do not plan to the last drop.

### Does the Vision have ABS?

The owner's manual describes a combined brake system and does not describe ABS for the Vision. Ask the shop about the specific bike.

### Is the Vision too small for tall riders?

Seat height is 785 mm and the bike is compact, so leg room is a personal matter. Sit on it before you accept it.

### Is the Vision available at Nguyen Ha?

The Vision is among the automatic models the shop lists. The bike actually available is confirmed at booking, so ask when you enquire.
