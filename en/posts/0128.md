Wet pavement does not make every part of a Hanoi road equally slippery. The danger sits in particular surfaces: the first minutes of rain, painted markings, steel manhole covers and temporary road plates, smooth tiles, mud or sand and anything oily. The rider's answer is the same on all of them: slow down early, keep the scooter upright, and brake and open the throttle gently. Honda's owner's manual for the Lead 125 says road surfaces are very slippery when damp, wet brakes work less well, and the rider should take extra care when braking in those conditions. This guide lists the surfaces to expect, explains how an automatic scooter's brakes behave, and gives a routine for the wet that works on a rented Honda Lead or Air Blade.

## What "slippery pavement" means for a scooter rider

Riders use "pavement" for the road surface they are riding on, and in Hanoi it covers several different things. Asphalt on a main road, concrete on an alley, tiles in front of a shop, paint on a crossing and steel over a trench all grip differently, and all of them grip worst when wet.

Road-safety guidance from several authorities agrees on the main hazards. The New South Wales Government's page on riding conditions lists wet bitumen, particularly just after rain begins, loose gravel, sand or mud on a sealed road, and painted lane markings and steel surfaces such as manhole covers, which can be slippery when wet. Hong Kong's Transport Department says that when wet, metal road studs, manhole covers and road markings can become slippery and may make a front tyre deviate off course. Neither is about Hanoi, but a wet painted line is a wet painted line anywhere.

## The surfaces to treat as ice

### The first minutes of rain

When rain starts after dry days, water mixes with dust, oil and rubber on the surface, and the road can be at its slickest before the rain has washed it clean. If a downpour starts, slow down and give yourself more distance for the first stretch, rather than speeding up to get out of it.

### Painted markings and crossings

Zebra crossings, stop lines, arrows and the yellow paint around some junctions are smooth. They are most dangerous when you are leaning, braking or accelerating on them. Try to cross a painted line upright and at a steady speed, and do not brake on it if you can brake before it.

### Steel plates and covers

Reporting in VnExpress in May 2026 described central Hanoi as a large construction site, with slippery steel plates temporarily covering manholes that caused several motorbike riders to fall during the morning rush hour. Plates, grilles and covers, whether temporary or permanent, deserve the same treatment: look ahead, roll over them upright and do not brake or turn on them.

### Smooth tiles and ramps

Pavement tiles, shop thresholds and polished ramps into car parks and hotel forecourts can be very slick when wet, even for a rider walking the scooter. These are general observations and not figures from a study, so judge by eye: if the surface looks glossy and wet, go very slowly and put a foot down.

### Mud, sand and construction debris

Roadworks in Hanoi leave dust, gravel and wet cement in places. The VnExpress report mentions crushed stone, sand and gravel strewn around a work site in central Hanoi. These surfaces move under the tyre, so keep the bike straight and avoid braking.

### Standing water

A puddle can hide a pothole, a gap or a missing cover. Our guide to [riding in Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) lists the streets that flood. If you cannot see the bottom, go around or stop.

## How an automatic scooter's brakes behave in the wet

Honda's owner's manual for the Lead 125 describes the following, and the wet-weather advice applies to every scooter:

- **Wet brakes are less effective.** If the brakes get wet, apply them lightly while riding at low speed so that they dry.
- **Use both brakes together.** Honda tells riders to close the throttle completely and apply the front and rear brakes at the same time.
- **A combined braking system** shares the brake force between front and rear, depending on which lever you use. It does not replace gentle, early braking.
- **ABS on the front wheel,** fitted to some versions, helps prevent the front brake locking in a sudden stop. Honda notes that the system does not shorten braking distance, may lengthen it in some conditions, does not work below about 10 km/h and does not act on the rear wheel. Honda Vietnam's product pages say the front ABS helps stability on wet and slippery roads, but whether your rental bike has it depends on its version, so ask.
- **Continuous braking can overheat the brakes.** That is mainly a matter for long descents, not city traffic, but it is one more reason to brake early and smoothly.

The practical lesson: the brakes are not the part that fails on wet pavement, the tyre's grip is. Gentle inputs keep the tyre within its grip.

## Quick reference: surface, risk and response

| Surface | Why it is risky when wet | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Fresh asphalt in the first minutes of rain | Dust and oil lift into a slick film | Slow down early and widen the gap |
| Zebra crossings and stop lines | Smooth paint, little texture | Cross upright, brake before the line |
| Steel plates and manhole covers | Metal has very little grip | Roll over in a straight line without braking or turning |
| Polished tiles and ramps | Glossy, often sloped | Walk the scooter or go at walking pace with a foot ready |
| Gravel, sand and cement dust | Loose layer slides under the tyre | Keep the bike upright, avoid braking and steering together |
| Puddles | May hide holes or covers | Go around, or stop and look |
| Leaves and mud on a side street | Soft layer on top of a hard surface | Use a low speed and no sudden inputs |

Keep the table in mind rather than memorising it. The common rule is that anything smooth, metal or loose needs lower speed and gentler inputs than ordinary asphalt does.

## A wet-weather routine for a rented scooter

### Before you ride off

- **Look at the tyres.** Check the tread and any cracks or flat spots at handover. Our [10-minute rental scooter inspection](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-scooter-inspection-checklist/) covers the check.
- **Test the brakes gently in the lot,** at walking pace, on a clean patch.
- **Check your footwear.** Wet sandals on a smooth footrest are a hazard.
- **Plan the route.** Use main roads with good drainage, not shortcuts across tiles and ramps.

### On the move

1. **Reduce speed before you need to.** The slower you go, the more margin you keep.
2. **Increase the gap to the rider in front.** Stopping takes longer.
3. **Look 20 to 30 metres ahead for surfaces:** paint, plates, covers, wet leaves.
4. **Brake in a straight line, before the bend,** using both levers gently.
5. **Turn smoothly and at a lower speed.** Keep lean small, and do not brake mid-turn.
6. **Open and close the throttle slowly,** because an abrupt change can break traction.
7. **Do not weave between vehicles.** Paint and plates hide between lanes.
8. **Be visible.** Switch on the lights and wear bright colours.

### When something slides

If the wheel starts to slip, do not grab the brakes. Close the throttle smoothly, hold the handlebars lightly and straight, and let the bike settle. Stabilise first and then slow down. If you fall, switch off, check yourself, move to a safe place and call the shop on 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969), open 09:00 to 21:00 daily. Our guide to [what to do with a rental bike breakdown](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-bike-breakdown-hanoi-what-to-do/) covers the steps.

## Stopping and parking on slick ground

Slippery ground matters when you stop as well as when you ride. Honda's manual says to park on a firm, level surface, and if you must park on a sloping or slippery one, to park so that the vehicle will not move or fall. Remember that a scooter on its side stand on wet tiles can slide. Put the front wheel against a kerb or wall if you can, lock the steering and choose a guarded area. Our guide to [parking a motorbike in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-motorbike-in-hanoi/) describes the options.

Be careful with your own footing. A wet floor next to the bike is where riders slip while getting on and off. Put your weight on the foot that is on firm ground.

## Rain, night and tired riders

Slippery ground and poor visibility often come together. Rain reduces how far you can see, and at night wet paint and puddles are harder to spot. Our guide to [riding at night in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-at-night-in-hanoi/) explains the lighting and the streets to avoid. A rider who is tired or in a rush is more likely to brake late and hard, which is the very input a wet road punishes. If the rain is heavy, stopping under cover for 20 minutes is a sound choice.

Traffic flow matters too. In a wet junction, vehicles take longer to stop, so give more room, as explained in our guide to [crossing Hanoi intersections](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/crossing-hanoi-intersections/).

## What to ask the shop

- Does this bike have ABS or a combined braking system?
- What is the condition of the tyres?
- Is a raincoat included? For short rentals the shop does not include one, so bring your own.
- Can I test the brakes at the shop before leaving?
- What should I do if the bike slips or I fall?

## FAQ: slippery pavement and scooters

### When is the road most slippery in the rain?

Usually in the first minutes after rain begins, when water mixes with dust and oil. Slow down then.

### Which surfaces should I avoid braking on?

Painted markings, steel plates and covers, smooth tiles and loose gravel or sand. Brake before them.

### Does ABS make me safe on wet roads?

It helps prevent the front wheel locking, but Honda says it does not shorten braking distance and does not work below about 10 km/h. Gentle riding is still essential.

### Should I use the front or rear brake?

Honda says to use both together and to close the throttle fully. Squeeze gently.

### What if the shop bike has no ABS?

Then brake earlier and more gently than you would on a bike that has it, and ask the shop which brake system your bike uses.

### Is it safe to ride at all in heavy rain?

Often not. If water is flowing across the road or you cannot see, stop in a safe place and wait.
