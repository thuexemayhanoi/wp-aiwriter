In Vietnam every electric two-wheeler with a motor, from a pedal-assist electric bicycle to a fast electric motorcycle, requires a certified helmet with the strap fastened for the rider, and for a passenger as well. The class changes the licence, the age limit and the speed limit, but it does not change the helmet duty. What does change by class is the legal wording behind the duty, who is fined when a passenger goes without, and the exceptions for very young passengers. This guide walks through the three classes, explains what counts as a compliant helmet and how to check it, covers passengers and children, and lists what to ask a rental shop. It draws on the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (Law 36/2024/QH15) and the penalty decree in force since 1 January 2025, and is accurate as of October 2026.

## The short table

| Class | Helmet required? | Where the duty comes from |
|---|---|---|
| Motor-assisted bicycle (pedals, assist cut at 25 km/h), including the electric bicycle | Yes, rider and passenger | Article 31 of Law 36/2024/QH15 |
| Electric moped (design speed up to 50 km/h, motor up to 4 kW) | Yes, rider and passenger | Moped and motorcycle rules, penalty decree Articles 7 and 12 |
| Electric motorcycle (faster or stronger than a moped) | Yes, rider and passenger | Same as above |

If you are not sure which class a rental belongs to, the answer for helmets is the same, so you can settle the helmet question before the licence question. ## Motor-assisted bicycles: the helmet myth

Many visitors assume a slow electric bicycle is like a pedal bicycle and needs no helmet. Under Law 36/2024/QH15, Article 31 says the rider and anyone carried on a motor-assisted bicycle must wear a helmet that meets the national technical regulation, with the strap fastened properly. The law defines motor-assisted bicycles, electric bicycles included, as bicycles with motor assistance that is cut when the rider stops pedalling or the bicycle reaches 25 km/h.

The duty in the sources used here is written for motor-assisted bicycles. This article does not address pedal-only bicycles, and a visitor on a hired electric bicycle should treat the helmet as mandatory from the first metre.

The penalty decree, Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, backs this up in its article on bicycles and motor-assisted bicycles. Its points cover a rider with no helmet or with the strap undone, and a rider carrying a passenger who has no helmet or an unfastened strap. The fine is VND 400,000 to 600,000 for each, according to the Thu Vien Phap Luat legal portal.

## Electric mopeds and motorcycles: the same rule as petrol

For electric mopeds and electric motorcycles the duty sits in the same decree and the same article as for petrol scooters. The rider commits an offence by riding without a helmet or with the strap undone, and also by carrying a passenger who has none. The fine is VND 400,000 to 600,000 under Article 7 for the rider. The passenger can be fined separately under Article 12, with the same range. The Thu Vien Phap Luat portal sets out both provisions, and the VOV news site explains that the rider and the passenger are fined separately.

The practical point is that a silent electric scooter attracts the same checks as a noisy petrol one. Officers do not treat quiet motors as a reason to relax. ## What counts as a compliant helmet

Vietnam's national technical regulation for helmets is QCVN 2:2021/BKHCN, issued by the Ministry of Science and Technology in Circular 04/2021/TT-BKHCN. It replaced the older QCVN 2:2008/BKHCN. The regulation is titled "protective helmets for motorcycle and moped users" and relies on the Vietnamese standard TCVN 5756:2017. Under it, a helmet on sale must show:

- A clear label in line with the labelling rules. For helmets made in Vietnam, the official guidance says the product name must include the phrase "Mu bao hiem cho nguoi di mo to, xe may" (helmet for motorcycle and moped riders), together with the maker's name and address, the size and the month and year of manufacture.
- The **CR conformity mark**, printed directly on the helmet or on a waterproof material fixed to it, legible and not peeling.

Under the official implementing letter for the earlier version of the regulation, a shop may not sell helmets that carry no label and no CR mark. For a renter, the sensible check is simple. Look inside and outside the helmet for a CR mark and a label with the Vietnamese product name. If you cannot find them, ask for another helmet.

The regulation describes three basic construction parts: a shell, an impact-absorbing liner and a strap. A helmet with a cracked shell, a liner that crumbles or a strap that will not fasten is not a helmet you should accept, whatever its label says. These are general safety checks and not requirements quoted from the regulation.

## The strap is part of the rule

The offence is worded as no helmet or a helmet with the strap not fastened properly. A helmet perched on the head or hung on the mirror counts as not wearing one. Fasten the strap so that it is snug under the chin before you move off. Our guide to [choosing a helmet that fits](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/choosing-a-helmet-that-fits-for-a-manual-motorbike-journey/) explains sizing and fastening; it is written for manual bikes, but the fit advice applies to any rider.

## Passengers and children

The helmet duty extends to passengers, with narrow exceptions written into the fine provisions. The decree's wording excludes from the passenger offence a person being taken to emergency care, a child under 6 years old, and a person being escorted for breaking the law. These exceptions are about enforcement, not about what is safe. A rental passenger who is a toddler is a case where the law is lenient and safety advice is not, so the helmet question is better handled by common sense and by our [child passengers guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/child-passengers-motorbike-vietnam/).

Two things are worth stating plainly:

- A helmet for an adult head will not protect a small child. If you are travelling with a child, do not borrow an adult helmet for them.
- Carrying more passengers than the rules allow is a separate matter from helmets, and the rules differ by class. Our guide to [two riders and luggage on a scooter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/scooter-for-two-riders-and-luggage-hanoi/) covers the practical side for scooters.

## Does a speed mode or the Eco setting change the rule?

No. Riding in a gentle Eco mode does not change the class, and the helmet duty does not depend on your speed on the day. The duty applies to the class of vehicle. A speed limit for the class is separate; see our [speed limits guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/speed-limits-motorbikes-vietnam/).

## Fines and where they come from

As of October 2026, the penalty decree of 2024 remains the source of the helmet fines. A later government decree, Decree 238/2026/ND-CP, took effect on 15 August 2026 and amended several provisions of Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, but the summaries read for this article describe its changes as concerning other offences, such as the wording on riders aged 16 to under 18 and rules on carrying children in cars. They do not describe a change to the helmet fines. Other offences that commonly catch foreign riders are listed in our [traffic fines guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/traffic-fines-vietnam-foreign-riders/). Check the current text of the decree if the exact amount matters to you.

Beyond the fine, a stop without a helmet is the kind of delay that spoils a rental day, and a helmet that is not worn protects no one.

## FAQ: electric helmet rules

### Do I need a helmet on an electric bicycle in Vietnam?

Yes. Article 31 of Law 36/2024/QH15 requires the rider and passengers on a motor-assisted bicycle to wear a helmet that meets the national regulation, with the strap fastened.

### Is the helmet rule different for an electric moped?

The duty is the same. The rules sit in the moped and motorcycle provisions, and the fine for the rider is VND 400,000 to 600,000.

### Does my passenger need a helmet?

Yes, except in the narrow cases the decree lists, such as a child under 6 years old or a person being taken for emergency care. Safety advice is still to give every passenger a properly fitting helmet.

### How can I tell whether a helmet is compliant?

Look for the CR conformity mark and a label with the Vietnamese product name for motorcycle and moped users. Check that the shell, liner and strap are in good condition.

### Does Eco mode or a slow speed remove the helmet rule?

No. The duty follows the class of vehicle, not the speed you are travelling.
