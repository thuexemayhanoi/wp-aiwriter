Near a construction site or a temporary barrier, slow down before you reach it, follow the temporary sign over any fixed one and expect the road surface to change under your wheels. Law 36/2024/QH15 says operators must slow down or stop for safety at places with warning signs or hurdles on the road and in areas under construction on the road (Article 12), and Article 11 says that where a fixed sign and a temporary sign stand together and mean different things, you obey the temporary one. For a two-wheeler the bigger risk is usually the surface: loose gravel, steel plates, grooves, uneven edges and standing water are all hazards that a car passes without noticing.

This guide explains the legal duties, how to read temporary signs and barriers, the surface hazards that matter on a scooter, how to ride through a lane shift or a narrowed carriageway, what to do at night and in rain and what to tell the shop. It is general information based on the law and published work-zone guidance as of October 2026, not legal advice.

## What the law says

**Slow down and observe (Article 12).** Operators on the road must observe the surroundings, and slow down or stop to ensure safety where there are warning signs or hurdles on the road, in areas under construction on the road, in areas crowded with people, and when visibility is limited. Speed must suit the road, traffic density and other conditions.

**Temporary signs rank above fixed ones (Article 11).** The Law sets an order of priority for commands: a traffic guard's instructions first, then lights, signs, markings and so on. Where a fixed sign and a temporary sign are in the same place and have different meanings, you follow the temporary one. A work crew's cone and sign can therefore override a permanent arrow or marking.

**Do not damage or move them.** Article 9 prohibits destroying or damaging traffic control facilities, and Article 77 requires anyone temporarily using roadways or pavements for other purposes to have approved plans, to follow the Traffic Police's requests and to return the road to its initial state. So moving a barrier to squeeze through is not a harmless act.

**Keep to the lanes laid out for you.** Article 13 requires operators to use their lane and follow lane signs and markings. Our guide to [lane rules in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/lane-rules-motorbikes-vietnam/) explains the basics.

Under Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, not obeying signs and markings is penalised in Article 7, with fines that rise where the failure causes an accident. Read the current text before relying on a figure, and see our guide to [how to verify the current fine for a traffic offence](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-verify-the-current-fine-for-a-traffic-offence/).

## Reading temporary signs and barriers

Temporary arrangements are meant to tell you what to do, but they are often incomplete, so use your eyes.

- **Cones, drums and plastic barriers** show where the lane has moved. Follow the line they make, and do not cut between them.

- **Orange or yellow warning signs** announce a change ahead. Slow down at the first one, not the last.

- **A person with a flag or baton** has the highest priority under Article 11. Stop or proceed as they direct.

- **Tape or rope** across the road means closed. Do not duck under it.

- **Blocked-off areas with no sign** are still closed in practice. If you cannot see a lawful way through, stop and turn back where it is safe and lawful to do so.

Be careful with your map app. It may send you through a closed street, because it does not know about a barrier put up this morning. ## The surface hazards a scooter feels

Road-safety guidance for work zones, including guidelines linked to United States highway agencies, describes the same families of hazard for two-wheelers. They apply to Hanoi's works too, although the materials and layout differ.

| Hazard | Why it matters on two wheels | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Loose gravel, sand, soil | Cuts grip; front wheel can slide | Slow down; keep upright; avoid braking or turning on it |
| Steel plates over trenches | Slippery when wet; the edge can catch a wheel | Cross slowly and straight; do not brake or turn on the plate |
| Milled or grooved pavement | The bike can wander | Hold the bars lightly; do not fight the wobble |
| Uneven lanes and edge drops | A small step can upset a narrow tyre | Approach at as steep an angle as possible; or stay in the lane |
| Raised manhole covers and drains | A hard edge hidden by dust | Look for them; avoid or cross upright |
| Standing water | Hides holes and grooves | Slow down; do not cross if you cannot see the bottom |
| Spilled concrete, mud or oil | Very low grip | Treat as ice; stop and walk if needed |
| Large painted markings | Slippery when wet | Do not brake or lean on them |

The work-zone guidelines describe highway agencies limiting vertical edges on open lanes to about one inch (25 mm) because a small step that a car ignores can upset a motorcycle. A scooter's small wheels are similarly sensitive. Our guides to [dealing with potholes on a rental motorbike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/dealing-with-potholes-on-a-rental-motorbike/) and [braking safely on wet road markings](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/braking-safely-on-wet-road-markings/) cover related techniques.

## How to ride through a work zone

**1. Slow down early.** Slow at the first warning sign. Speed that suits an open road is too much for a lane that may suddenly narrow.

**2. Look far ahead.** Look past the cones to where the line leads. Your bike follows your eyes.

**3. Choose your line before you enter.** If the lane is narrow, decide where you will ride and avoid changing your mind inside it. Do not pass another vehicle in a narrow section.

**4. Keep your distance.** Leave more room than usual. A car ahead may stop suddenly for a worker, or swerve around a hole. Our guide to [how to follow a safe distance in Hanoi traffic](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-follow-a-safe-distance-in-hanoi-traffic/) explains how.

**5. Cross edges and plates upright and straight.** Do not lean, brake or accelerate hard while crossing. Where there is a step, a steep angle of approach gives the front wheel a better chance of climbing it than a shallow one.

**6. Choose the clear path through debris.** Look at the gap, not at the gravel. Tyres of vehicles ahead often push gravel aside and leave two clear tracks, so you can use them if you leave a gap.

**7. Watch for workers and machines.** Trucks may enter or leave the site. Workers may step out. A person with a flag has priority.

**8. Expect other riders to cut in.** At a lane shift, riders squeeze through. Look in your mirrors, signal and hold your line. Our guide to [checking mirrors and blind spots before changing lanes](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/checking-mirrors-and-blind-spots-before-changing-lanes/) shows how.

**9. Use your lights.** Dust, spray and glare reduce how well others see you. Use the low beam in poor conditions.

**10. Be patient at the end.** Traffic often speeds up after the site. Keep your speed down for a short way past the last cone.

## At night and in rain

Work zones are worse in the dark, because unlit barriers, trenches and plates are hard to see, and in the wet, because steel plates and markings become slippery and puddles hide holes.

- **At night:** slow down more than you would by day, use your low beam and do not follow too closely behind a vehicle whose tail lights may be dirty. Our guide to [how to ride after dark with limited street lighting](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-ride-after-dark-with-limited-street-lighting/) gives more detail.

- **In rain:** avoid plates and markings, and if a route is flooded or looks unsafe, choose another one. Our guide to [safe scooter riding when visibility drops in rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/safe-scooter-riding-when-visibility-drops-in-rain/) explains how to adjust.

## When to stop and turn back

You are allowed to say no. Stop where it is safe and lawful, and consider turning back or walking the bike through when:

- **The surface is loose, deep or wet** and you do not have the confidence to cross it.

- **The lane is narrower than your bike** with a vehicle beside you.

- **There is no clear path,** or a worker waves you back.

- **The barrier is across the whole road.**

- **You are tired or the light is poor.**

Do not stop on the carriageway where you block traffic, within 5 metres of an intersection or on a bridge, since Article 18 of the Law bans stopping there. Pull in at a place with room, turn off the engine and think about the route. ## A quick checklist

1. **Slow down at the first warning sign.**

2. **Obey the person with the flag, then the temporary sign, then the fixed one.**

3. **Do not move cones or squeeze past barriers.**

4. **Cross plates, edges and grooves upright, slowly and straight.**

5. **Look for the clear path, not at the debris.**

6. **Leave extra distance, and use your lights.**

7. **Turn back or walk if the surface or the lane looks unsafe.**

8. **Photograph any damage, and call the shop.**

## FAQ: riding past road works

### Do temporary signs override permanent ones in Vietnam?

Yes. Article 11 of Law 36/2024/QH15 says that if a fixed sign and a temporary sign are in the same place and have different meanings, operators must follow the temporary one.

### Must I slow down near construction?

Yes. Article 12 requires operators to slow down or stop for safety at places with warning signs or hurdles, and in areas under construction on the road.

### Can I move a cone or a barrier to get through?

No. Article 9 bans damaging traffic control facilities, and works on roads are controlled under Article 77. Find another route.

### What is the biggest hazard for a scooter at a work site?

Usually the surface: loose gravel, steel plates, uneven edges, grooves and standing water. These are more serious for a two-wheeler than for a car.

### Should I ride through a flooded work zone?

Not if you cannot see the road beneath the water, since holes and plates may be hidden. Choose another route or wait.

### Who do I call if my scooter is damaged?

Call the shop on 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969), after making yourself safe, and call 112 if anyone is hurt.
