Rent and deposit are two different payments with two different jobs. Rent is the price of using the motorbike for the agreed period, and it is earned by the shop as the days pass. A deposit is not a price at all: it is money you hand over as security, which is meant to come back to you once the bike has been returned and every charge has been settled. Vietnam's Civil Code draws the same line, and a good rental receipt shows the two amounts separately. This guide explains the difference in plain terms, shows which costs belong to which payment, flags the confusing uses of the word "deposit" and gives you wording to look for on paper. It is general information, not legal advice.

## Two payments, two jobs

Think of the rent as a fee and the deposit as a pledge. The fee is spent; the pledge is held. The table below sets out the contrasts that matter on a rental counter.

| | Rent | Deposit |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | The price for using the bike for a set time | Security held while you have the bike |
| Who keeps it | The shop, as the rental period runs | Nobody permanently, in the normal case |
| Does it come back? | Not for unused time, unless the shop agrees | Yes, after return and settlement |
| Size depends on | Bike type and package (day, week, month) | Bike value and the contract |
| Typical timing | Paid at handover or as agreed | Paid at handover, returned at the end |
| Can it be reduced? | By agreement before you sign | By deductions the contract allows |

The practical consequence is simple. If you return a bike a day early, the deposit comes back but the extra day of rent does not, because the two were never the same money. If you damage a mirror, the shop looks first to the deposit, not to the rent you already paid.

## How Vietnam's Civil Code separates them

The Civil Code 2015 (Law No. 91/2015/QH13) treats renting a motorbike as a lease of property. Article 472 describes it as an agreement under which the lessor delivers property for use during a fixed term and the lessee is required to pay rent. Article 473 says the rent is whatever the parties agree, and Article 481 requires the lessee to pay it in full and on time as agreed. That is the "rent" side: a price for use.

The "deposit" side lives in a different part of the Code, the section on securing performance of obligations. Two articles are easy to confuse, and the difference is useful.

- **Article 328, deposit (dat coc in Vietnamese).** One party hands money or something valuable to the other as security for entering into or performing a contract. When the contract is made or performed, the deposit is returned or deducted from what is owed. If the depositor backs out, the other side keeps it; if the receiver backs out, it must return the deposit and pay an equal amount, unless agreed otherwise.
- **Article 329, security collateral (ky cuoc in Vietnamese).** Here a lessee of movable property gives money or valuables to the lessor for a set time to secure the return of the leased property. If the property is returned, the lessee can reclaim the security after paying the rent. If the property is no longer available to return, the security belongs to the lessor.

A rental-bike deposit is the second kind in everything but name. It secures the return of the bike, it is held for the rental period, and it is reclaimed once the bike is back and the rent is paid. The Code's wording also confirms the order of events: you reclaim the security after paying the rent, which is why a shop settles rent, late fees and fuel first and releases the deposit after that.

## When "deposit" means something else

The English word is used loosely, and the loose uses cause most misunderstandings. Watch for these four.

1. **A booking deposit.** Some businesses ask for money to hold a bike in advance. That is closer to Article 328: security for making the rental contract, normally counted against the rent when you arrive. Ask what happens to it if you cancel, because the Code lets the parties agree different consequences.

2. **A part-payment of rent.** If the shop calls an advance "a deposit" but means the first instalment of the price, it is rent, and it will not come back after return. The receipt should say "rent paid" or "advance".

3. **A security deposit.** This is the Article 329 kind, held and returned. Nguyen Ha takes this kind: a cash deposit of VND 2–5 million depending on the bike and the contract, separate from the rent, with identity documents photographed for the contract and the originals not kept.

4. **A document held instead of cash.** Some shops hold a passport or ID. That is also security, but it is a very different risk for you. ## Where the shop's own figures sit

Using Nguyen Ha's published terms as a worked example shows how the pieces stay apart. Day, week and month rent depends on the bike type and package, and the final price is confirmed on the actual bike before the contract is signed; the [prices page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/prices/) lists the reference ranges. That is rent.

The deposit is the separate cash amount of VND 2–5 million. It is not a discount, not an advance and not a charge for using the bike.

Then there are the small charges that arise only in particular situations, and they are different again. A late return costs VND 30,000 per hour for one to five hours, and from six hours it counts as one extra rental day. An empty tank at return costs VND 20,000. Early return does not refund unused rent. These are settled in addition to the rent, and the contract decides whether they are taken from the deposit or paid separately. Ask which, and have the answer written down.

## What belongs to which pot

When something goes wrong, the question is which payment pays for it. The usual allocation looks like this.

| Situation | Normally dealt with through |
|---|---|
| Using the bike for the agreed days | Rent |
| Returning a day early | Nothing refunded; rent was for the full period |
| Extending by a day | More rent, at the agreed rate |
| Late return | Late fee or an extra day, as the contract states |
| Empty tank at return | The fixed fuel charge in the contract |
| Damage beyond normal wear | Repair cost, assessed under the contract, often taken from the deposit |
| Lost key or registration paper | The charge named in the contract |
| Bike not returned | The deposit may be kept by the lessor under Article 329 |

A line to notice: normal wear and tear is excused by the Civil Code. Article 479 says the lessee is not liable for normal wear and tear from using the property, and Article 482 requires return in the same condition as received, normal wear and tear excepted. So a deduction from the deposit should be for damage, not for the ordinary ageing that every rented bike shows. ## The timeline of the two payments

Seeing the money in order helps.

**At handover.** You pay the rent for the chosen package and hand over the cash deposit. The receipt or contract should list both lines, with the date and time. Identity documents are photographed for the record. Check the bike with the staff and photograph it; our [10-minute scooter inspection checklist](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-scooter-inspection-checklist/) shows how.

**During the rental.** Rent keeps running at the agreed rate. The deposit stays put. If you want more days, you agree and pay more rent; the deposit normally does not change.

**At return.** The shop checks the bike, the fuel and any extra days or hours. Rent already paid is not recalculated downward. Any agreed charges are totalled.

**After settlement.** The deposit, less any agreed deductions, goes back to you. The Civil Code's phrasing, reclaiming the security after paying the rent, describes this step. Our guide to [returning a rental motorbike in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/returning-your-rental-motorbike-hanoi/) covers the handback in detail.

## Wording to look for on the receipt

Receipts are often short, so every word matters. A clear receipt or contract does five things.

- It shows rent and deposit on **separate lines**, with the currency and the amounts written out.
- It labels the deposit as **refundable security** and says when it will be returned, such as at the end of the rental after checking.
- It states what the deposit **can be applied to**: for example damage, late fees, fuel, lost keys or papers.
- It says whether the deposit is returned **in cash or by transfer**, and to whom.
- It carries the **bike's plate number**, so the deposit is tied to one vehicle.

If a receipt says only "deposit" with a single number and no explanation, ask the staff to add a line. Our explanation of [what the clauses in a Hanoi rental contract mean](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-motorbike-rental-contract-explained/) shows where these statements usually sit.

## Common mix-ups

**"I paid a deposit, so the bike is mine for those days."** Not necessarily. If the amount was a security deposit, rent is still due separately.

**"The deposit covers all damage."** Not by itself. The contract decides what the deposit may be used for and whether the shop can claim more than the deposit if repairs cost more. Ask this before you sign.

**"I will get my deposit back minus the days I did not use."** Unused days are rent, not deposit, and the Nguyen Ha terms do not refund unused rent on early return. A different shop may agree otherwise, so ask.

**"A deposit means I do not need to read the contract."** The opposite is true. The deposit is the money at risk, and the contract says how it can be reduced.

## Questions to ask before you pay

1. How much is the rent, and does the price include the helmet, any extra days and the stated fuel level?
2. How much is the deposit, and is it separate from the rent on the receipt?
3. What can be deducted from the deposit, and in what order?
4. When is the deposit returned, and how?
5. If repairs cost more than the deposit, what happens?
6. Is there any non-refundable element, such as a booking fee?
7. Do you keep any original document, or only a photograph for the contract?

Ask these in plain words, and write the answers in your phone or on the paper. If the answers are vague, treat that as information about the shop.

## FAQ: rent and deposit

### Is a rental deposit the same as rent?

No. Rent is the price for using the bike. A deposit is security that is held and then returned, less any agreed deductions.

### Which Civil Code article covers a rental deposit?

Article 329 on security collateral (ky cuoc) matches the situation most closely: a lessee of movable property gives security to the lessor to secure its return. Article 328 covers a deposit for making or performing a contract.

### Do I get my deposit back if I return the bike early?

The deposit should come back after settlement. Unused rent is a separate matter and, under Nguyen Ha's terms, is not refunded on early return.

### Can the deposit be used for late fees?

Only if the contract says so. Ask whether late fees are taken from the deposit or paid separately.

### How much is the deposit at Nguyen Ha?

VND 2–5 million in cash depending on the bike and the contract, separate from the rent. Confirm the figure on the bike you take.
