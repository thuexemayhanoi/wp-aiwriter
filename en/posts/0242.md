Pedal assist adds motor power only while you pedal, and a throttle drives the motor from a hand control whether you pedal or not. Pedal assist feels like riding a bicycle with strong legs and generally stretches a battery further, while a throttle feels like a small scooter and is simpler in stop-and-go traffic but uses charge faster. In Vietnam the difference is more than comfort: the 2024 traffic law describes an electric bicycle as one whose motor cuts out when you stop pedalling or when the bike reaches 25 km/h, so a bike that keeps pulling without pedalling may be classed differently. This guide explains both systems, the sensors behind pedal assist, how they behave in Hanoi traffic and what to check before you accept a rental.

## How pedal assist works

A pedal-assist bike has a sensor that tells the controller you are pedalling. You pick an assist level on the handlebar display, usually a few steps from eco to a stronger setting, and the motor adds power in proportion to that level. Stop pedalling and the assistance stops within a moment.

There are two kinds of sensor, and they feel different.

- **Cadence sensor.** It detects that the pedals are turning, like a light switch. Once you pedal, the motor gives the amount set by the assist level, whether you push hard or barely at all. It can feel abrupt, and sellers describe a short delay of a fraction of a pedal turn before power arrives.
- **Torque sensor.** It measures how hard you press the pedals and gives help in proportion. It feels natural, like having stronger legs, and tends to spend less battery. It is more complex and usually costs more.

Sellers' blog posts, which are the sources for this section, say a cadence system can use noticeably more battery than a torque one on the same route. They give different percentages, so treat the direction as reliable and the numbers as uncertain.

## How a throttle works

A throttle is a twist grip or a thumb lever. It sends a signal to the controller, which gives the motor power straight away, with no need to turn the pedals. Press harder and the bike goes faster, up to its speed limit.

That is why a throttle bike feels like a moped. Starting from a traffic light is effortless, hills can be climbed without pedalling, and it suits a rider who is tired, carrying shopping or wearing smart clothes.

The cost is battery. With no human power in the mix, the motor supplies everything. Vendor sites describe throttle-only riding using more energy per kilometre, with ranges that differ by one fifth to one half depending on the source. Again, trust the trend.

## What Vietnam's rules say about the difference

As of October 2026, Article 34 of Law 36/2024/QH15 on road traffic order and safety, effective 1 January 2025, defines a powered bicycle, including an electric bicycle, as a bicycle with motor assistance whose motor power cuts out when the rider stops pedalling or when the bike reaches 25 km/h. The Ho Chi Minh City traffic police website quotes that wording in an explanatory article from April 2026.

The same article describes a moped as a two- or three-wheeler with a design speed of no more than 50 km/h and, for an electric motor, a rating of no more than 4 kW. It adds that a moped is a motor vehicle, so it is registered and plated, and that the rider must be at least 16. The traffic police article says there is no minimum age set for powered bicycles and electric bicycles, and that riders must still follow traffic rules and wear a helmet.

An older Ministry of Public Security answer, based on earlier rules, gave a practical test: an electric bicycle must have pedals and be driven by a pedal mechanism so that it can still be pedalled with the motor off.

What does this mean for you as a renter? A bike whose motor runs from a throttle with no pedalling does not match the wording for an electric bicycle. Whether a specific model counts as an electric bicycle or a moped is not something a rider or this guide can settle. Ask the shop how the bike is classed and whether its documents match.

## Which suits Hanoi riding

Hanoi traffic is dense, slow and unpredictable. Both systems have strengths there.

**Pedal assist works well when:**

- You want to ride for an hour or two and have some exercise.
- The route is flat, such as the lakeside loops, and speeds stay low.
- You want a longer range from a modest battery.
- You like the feel of a bicycle and will use the bike for short hops.

**A throttle works well when:**

- You are carrying bags or a child seat and want an easy start.
- The weather is hot and you do not want to sweat on the way to an appointment.
- You are nervous in traffic and want power on demand to clear a junction quickly.
- The route has a few hills, and you would rather not climb them by leg power.

Many bikes offer both, and with both, a sensible approach is to use pedal assist as the default and keep the throttle for starts and short boosts. Whether the bike offered to you has one or both is for the shop to confirm, since the electric group at Nguyen Ha includes electric bicycles, 50cc-equivalent electric scooters and some VinFast electric motorbikes.

## Control and safety differences

Pedal assist ties power to your legs, which makes surges less likely. A throttle puts power under one hand, so a bump or a stray twist can move the bike when you did not mean it to. In a busy Old Quarter lane, that matters.

Habits to build with either system:

1. **Start in a low assist level or with a gentle throttle.** Practise in a quiet street before the traffic.
2. **Keep fingers on the brake levers near people.** Brake levers on many e-bikes cut motor power when pulled, which you can feel in a test.
3. **Do not twist the throttle while parking or pushing.** Walk-assist modes exist on some bikes and are specific to the model.
4. **Wear a helmet.** Our guide to [helmet rules in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/helmet-rules-in-vietnam/) explains that the requirement covers riders on electric bikes. The shop includes one basic helmet per rider at no charge.
5. **Respect speed.** Pedal-assist cut-off is 25 km/h in the definition above. A bike that goes faster is a different vehicle.

## A ten-minute test before you accept the bike

1. Ask whether the bike has pedal assist, a throttle or both, and how many assist levels.
2. Switch on and check the display, battery level and assist setting. The shop confirms the battery level and charging arrangement at handover.
3. Walk the bike, then ride a short loop in the quietest street available.
4. In pedal-assist mode, check the response when you start and stop pedalling. Is it smooth or jerky?
5. Test the throttle gently, if there is one. Does it stop immediately on release?
6. Pull each brake lever and see whether the motor cuts out.
7. Check that the cut-off speed feels sensible and the bike does not push on past your comfort.
8. Ask what the maximum speed is, how the bike is classed and what documents come with it.

If any answer is unclear, ask for a different bike. The [rental guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-guide/) explains handover, and the [electric scooters page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/electric-scooters/) shows the group.

## FAQ: pedal assist and throttle

### Is pedal assist better than a throttle?

Neither is better for everyone. Pedal assist usually gives longer range and a bicycle feel, and a throttle gives instant power with less effort.

### Does a throttle use more battery?

Generally yes, because the motor supplies all the power. Sellers quote different differences, so expect a trend and not a fixed number.

### Does an electric bicycle need to be pedalled in Vietnam?

The 2024 law describes an electric bicycle as one whose motor cuts out when you stop pedalling or at 25 km/h. A bike that drives without pedalling may be treated as another class, so ask the shop how your bike is classed.

### Do I need a helmet on an electric bicycle?

Yes. The traffic police and provincial official pages state that riders should wear a helmet.

### Can I ride a throttle bike without a licence?

Do not assume so. The answer depends on how the specific vehicle is classed, so confirm it with the shop before you ride.
