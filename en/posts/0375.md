Choose your turnaround by the clock, not by the destination. Work out the latest time you want to be back near Hanoi, subtract how long the return will really take, and that is the time you turn around, whether or not you have reached the place you set out for. Then add three more triggers that bring the turn forward: fuel at about half a tank, weather such as thunder, and signs of tiredness. A good turnaround point is also a physical place, somewhere wide, visible and off the traffic lane, so you are not making a risky U-turn on a bend. This guide shows how to set the time, how to read the daylight in the cooler months, how to pick and avoid places to turn, and how to make the decision stick when the view is tempting. It is general planning advice for riders on a rented bike, not a route guide.

## Why a turnaround rule beats good intentions

Long rides go wrong in a predictable way. The outward leg is fresh and fast, the destination is close enough to tempt you, and the return is the part you have not thought about. By the time the tank is lower, the light is yellower and your legs are stiffer, you are further from Hanoi than you would choose to be.

A written rule fixes that because it makes the decision before you are tired and excited. Mountain walkers often use a turnaround time, and the same idea works on a small motorbike. The point is not to be timid. It is to choose, in advance and with a clear head, the moment at which the day's plan stops being a plan and becomes a retreat.

Our guide to [riding at night in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-at-night-in-hanoi/) explains why you want to avoid arriving back in the dark on an unfamiliar road, and that is the main reason to set the rule.

## Step one: count the usable daylight

The shop opens at 09:00, so that is the earliest you can be riding. The sun then sets in Hanoi earlier than many visitors expect. According to timeanddate.com and time.now for 2026:

| Date | Sunset | Civil twilight ends | Time from 09:00 to sunset |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 October | 5:44 pm | 6:07 pm | about 8 hours 45 minutes |
| 1 November | 5:21 pm | 5:43 pm | about 8 hours 20 minutes |
| 1 December | 5:14 pm | 5:37 pm | about 8 hours 15 minutes |

Civil twilight is the period after sunset when there is still enough light to be outdoors without artificial light, and it lasts only about 20 to 25 minutes at this latitude. In the shortest days of December the sun sets around 5:20 pm, and on the December solstice the day is about two and a half hours shorter than at the June solstice. Hills and mountains block the sun, so in a valley or on the west side of a ridge the light fades earlier than the published time.

Your usable day is therefore roughly eight hours from the 09:00 start. That is the budget for riding out, turning, riding back, and every stop in between.

## Step two: set the turnaround time with a simple sum

1. **Pick the time you want to be back at the edge of Hanoi.** A sensible choice is about 45 minutes before sunset, so the last stretch is in daylight even if something goes wrong.
2. **Ask your map app how long the return would take** without stops.
3. **Add half again for stops.** Fuel, a drink, a photograph and a wrong turn are all normal. Half again is our planning assumption, not a measured figure. A rider handbook from Australia's Northern Territory advises stopping every one and a half hours or 150 kilometres on a motorcycle, even if you do not feel tired, so the stops add up.
4. **Subtract the result from your back-by time.** That is the clock time at which you turn around.

Here is an illustration, not a real route. Suppose it is 1 December and you want to be near Hanoi by 4:30 pm. If your map app says the way back takes two hours, add an hour for stops to make three, and your turnaround time is 1:30 pm. If you are still short of your destination at 1:30 pm, you turn there and then.

Write the time on your phone and set an alarm for it, plus a second alarm 30 minutes earlier as a warning. The warning alarm tells you to start looking for a good place to turn.

## Step three: add the other triggers

The clock is the main rule. Three more triggers can bring the turn forward, and any one of them is enough.

### Fuel

Turn back when the tank reaches about half, unless a station is certain on the way. Honda Vietnam's figures put the Wave Alpha 110 tank at 3.7 litres, so a half tank is under two litres and not much of a reserve. Our guide to [when to refuel a manual motorbike during a day trip](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/when-to-refuel-a-manual-motorbike-during-a-day-trip/) explains how to make the fuel plan.

### Weather

Dark towering clouds, a change in the wind or the first rumble of thunder are all reasons to turn. The US National Weather Service advises that if you hear thunder you are within striking distance, that a safe building or hard-topped vehicle is the only safe place, and that you should wait 30 minutes after the last thunder before resuming. For motorcyclists it says to pull over near a safe building when threatening skies are in the distance and wait 30 minutes after the last rumble. Our guide to [riding in Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) covers rain gear and flooded streets.

### The rider

Tiredness is a turnaround trigger too. New Zealand's transport agency lists warning signs including trouble focusing, drifting in your lane, missing signs and yawning, and advises rest breaks about every two hours or 100 kilometres. A South Australian road safety campaign notes that people are naturally sleepy in the mid-afternoon, between about 2 pm and 4 pm. A turnaround set for mid-afternoon therefore falls just when your alertness dips, so make the sum slightly generous.

## Choosing the place to turn

Setting a time tells you when. The place matters just as much, because turning a motorbike around on the wrong stretch of road is a hazard of its own. Look for:

- **A wide forecourt.** Petrol stations, large cafe car parks and roadside parking areas are ideal. You can stop, drink, check the map and set off again without blocking anyone.
- **A clear view in both directions.** You need to see traffic coming and be seen.
- **A flat, firm surface.** Gravel, wet mud and sloping edges are a problem for a small bike with a pillion or luggage.
- **A place you can leave from easily.** If you must cross a busy lane to go back, keep going to the next forecourt.

Choose your place before the turnaround time arrives. When the warning alarm sounds, start looking, and take the first good place after the main alarm, not the first place that looks possible.

## Places not to turn around

Avoid these, even if the alarm has gone off:

- **Bends and the crests of hills,** where others cannot see you in time.
- **Narrow bridges and causeways,** where there is no room and no run-off.
- **Hairpins and steep mountain roads.** Our guide to [manual bikes and steep mountain roads](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/manual-bikes-and-steep-mountain-roads-when-to-choose-another-vehicle/) explains why a small bike struggles there.
- **Expressway slip roads and ring roads,** where turning is not an option at all. Our guide to [Hanoi's ring roads and highways](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-ring-roads-highways-motorbikes-allowed/) explains which ones allow motorbikes.
- **Busy junctions with no space to wait.**
- **Soft shoulders and loose gravel.**

If you pass the last good place, continue to the next one rather than making a rushed turn. A few extra minutes are cheaper than a fall.

## Making the decision stick

The hardest part of a turnaround time is obeying it. These habits help.

- **Tell everyone the rule at the start.** A passenger who knows the plan will back you up when you want to push on.
- **Decide once.** When the alarm goes, you turn. Do not reopen the debate because the next viewpoint is apparently close.
- **Do not trade away safety margins.** If you reach the destination late, you can still have a short stop, but the return starts at the time you set.
- **Pick a fallback.** A closer cafe, viewpoint or village makes the turn feel like a choice, not a defeat.
- **Remember the plan is yours.** The mountains, temples and towns will still be there next time.

## Common situations and what to do

| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| Turnaround time arrives and you are close to the goal | Take a five-minute look if you have a safe place, then turn |
| Thunder starts, no shelter nearby | Pull over at the nearest safe building or forecourt and wait 30 minutes after the last thunder |
| You are well behind schedule and tired | Skip the destination, rest at the next station, and head back |
| Fuel is at half and the next station is uncertain | Turn back now |
| A wrong turn adds time | Recalculate the turnaround time at once and shorten the trip |
| Rain starts and visibility drops | Slow down, use lights, and consider an earlier turn or an early finish |
| The bike feels wrong | Stop safely and call the shop |

## Which day-trip routes need the strictest rule

Short hill and park trips, such as the roads to [Ba Vi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-to-ba-vi-by-motorbike/) and [Tam Dao](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-to-tam-dao-by-motorbike/), end with a climb and a descent, so the turnaround time should include the descent in the return estimate. Longer rides such as [Ninh Binh](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-ninh-binh-one-day-motorbike-itinerary/) leave less slack, and a two-day trip to [Mai Chau](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-to-mai-chau-by-motorbike/) lets you avoid the rule altogether by sleeping there. Whether to take a rental bike beyond the city is covered in our guide to [taking a rental motorbike out of Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/take-rental-motorbike-out-of-hanoi/).

## FAQ: turnaround points

### What is a turnaround time?

It is a clock time that you set before the ride, at which you head back whether or not you have reached your goal.

### How do I calculate it?

Choose your back-by time, estimate the return duration with stops, and subtract. Our sum above shows how.

### What if I am nearly at the destination?

If you are at a safe place and the sum still works, a short look is fine. Otherwise turn.

### Where is it safe to turn a motorbike around?

Use a wide, flat, visible forecourt such as a petrol station or large car park, and avoid bends, bridges and slopes.

### Should I turn back if it starts to thunder?

Find a safe building or forecourt and wait until 30 minutes after the last thunder, or abandon the ride if the sky stays dark.

### Does the turnaround time change with the season?

Yes. Hanoi's days are shortest in December, so the same ride needs an earlier turn in winter than in summer.
