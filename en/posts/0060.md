For a longer ride out of Hanoi, the Yamaha Sirius FI and the Honda Blade are very close, and the better choice is usually the better-kept bike, not the badge. On paper the Sirius has a slightly larger engine, a 4.0-litre tank and a lower fuel-consumption figure, which together give it more theoretical range. The Blade is a little heavier, has a slightly smaller tank and a slightly higher fuel figure, but it offers more ground clearance. This guide compares the two using the manufacturers' own Vietnamese specification pages, turns the numbers into planning figures for a day trip, and lists what to check on the actual bike before you leave the shop. Nguyen Ha's manual fleet includes the Honda Wave, Yamaha Sirius and Honda Blade, and the bike you receive is confirmed at booking.

## The numbers side by side

These figures come from Yamaha Motor Vietnam's Sirius FI disc-brake page and Honda Vietnam's Blade page, checked in October 2026. Models and versions vary, so treat them as the right ballpark.

| | Yamaha Sirius FI (disc version) | Honda Blade 110 |
|---|---|---|
| Engine | 113.7 cc, air-cooled, SOHC, two valves | 109.1 cc, air-cooled, single cylinder |
| Peak output | 6.4 kW at 7,000 rpm | 6.18 kW at 7,500 rpm |
| Peak torque | 9.5 N·m at 5,500 rpm | 8.65 N·m at 5,500 rpm |

| Starting | Electric and kick | Electric and kick on the versions checked |

The engines are within a few per cent of each other. The Sirius has a little more torque, which helps on a climb or with a passenger, but the difference is modest. The bigger practical differences are the tank and the manufacturer's fuel figure.

## What the fuel figures mean for range

Multiplying tank size by the manufacturer's consumption figure gives a theoretical range:

- Sirius FI: 4.0 litres at 1.65 L per 100 km is about 240 km.
- Blade: 3.7 litres at 1.85 L per 100 km is about 200 km.

Those numbers are test results, not promises. Real use with a rider, luggage, hills, headwinds and stop-start traffic burns more fuel, and a bike with a nearly empty tank may run dry before the figure suggests. A sensible habit is to refuel when the tank is about half empty on a trip, which on either bike means every 100 km or so. The difference between the two is useful in remote areas where stations are far apart, but in the countryside around Hanoi you will rarely be far from fuel. ## Power and hills

Neither bike is built for speed. The Sirius's extra torque and 4-5 per cent larger engine give it a small advantage on a climb, and the margin grows with a passenger or luggage. On a hilly route such as the road to Ba Vi or Tam Dao, both bikes will need a low gear and patience. Our articles on [Hanoi to Ba Vi by motorbike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-to-ba-vi-by-motorbike/) and [Hanoi to Tam Dao by motorbike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-to-tam-dao-by-motorbike/) describe what those roads ask of a small bike.

Remember that both engines are air-cooled and sized for city use. Honda's Wave manual cautions that continuous braking on a long descent can overheat the brakes, and the same logic applies to any small bike on a mountain road: use a low gear and engine braking, and rest at the top and the bottom. Our guide to [shifting gears on hills without straining a rental motorbike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/shifting-gears-on-hills-without-straining-a-rental-motorbike/) covers the technique.

## Brakes, tyres and the road

Both bikes use 17-inch wheels with the same tyre sizes, so the difference in grip comes from tyre condition, not from the model. The Sirius FI uses tubed tyres, which means a puncture is repaired by patching or replacing the tube, a repair most roadside workshops handle. Ask the shop what its policy is for a puncture on a longer trip. Our guide to [lost keys, flat tyres and breakdowns](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-bike-breakdown-hanoi-what-to-do/) explains what to do.

Front brakes differ by version on both bikes. A hydraulic disc gives most riders better feel, especially in the wet, but a well-adjusted drum is perfectly adequate on a light bike if ridden within its limits. The Sirius's rear brake is a drum, and the Blade page we read does not list the rear brake, so ask the shop. Test the brakes as described in our guide to [checking the brakes before leaving the rental shop](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/manual-bike-brakes-what-to-test-before-leaving-the-rental-shop/), and add margin on wet roads.

Ground clearance favours the Sirius, at 155 mm against 141 mm. On rough rural roads, high kerbs and the humps that cross many provincial roads, extra clearance is a real, if small, advantage.

## Comfort over a day

The specification pages do not measure comfort, and we do not have reliable figures for seat padding or vibration, so be wary of anyone who claims one bike is far more comfortable than the other. What you can say is this: seat height is similar at 775 mm and 769 mm, both bikes have an upright riding position, and comfort over several hours depends mostly on the seat's condition, the rider's size and how the rear suspension has aged. Sit on both if the shop has both, and sit for a few minutes, not seconds.

Plan breaks every hour or so, whatever you ride. A small bike at a steady speed on a national road is tiring because of wind, noise and the constant vibration of a single-cylinder engine. ## Load and passenger limits

Both bikes are meant to carry the rider and one passenger, and the law allows only one passenger in ordinary cases. We did not retrieve load limits for the Sirius or the Blade, so ask the shop what applies to the bike you are offered. For a Honda Wave, Honda's manuals give 115 kg or 155 kg depending on version, which shows how much difference a version can make. ## Rules for longer trips

As of October 2026, motorbikes may not use expressways under the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (Law No. 36/2024/QH15), in force since 1 January 2025, and entering one is fined VND 4,000,000–6,000,000 under Decree 168/2024/ND-CP. Navigation apps can route a motorbike onto one by mistake, so read our guide to [checking whether a navigation app suggests an expressway](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-check-if-a-navigation-app-suggests-an-expressway/). Speed limits for motorbikes also differ between built-up areas and open roads, which our article on [speed limits for motorbikes in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/speed-limits-motorbikes-vietnam/) explains.

Both bikes are in the class that needs a licence valid for motorbikes up to 125 cc. A foreign visitor needs a home licence with a motorcycle category plus an International Driving Permit. Riding without a valid licence is fined VND 2,000,000–4,000,000 for a motorbike of up to 125 cc, and insurers commonly refuse claims from unlicensed riders. ## A pre-trip check for either bike

Before a longer ride, spend ten minutes on the bike:

1. **Tyres.** Look for cuts, bulges and low tread, and check the pressure when cold.
2. **Brakes.** Test both brakes and look at the pads or shoes if you can.
3. **Chain.** Look for rust, dry links and obvious slack. Honda's guidance is to check chain slack and condition before every ride and to have a dealer adjust it if it is too tight or too loose.
4. **Lights, horn and mirrors.** All must work.
5. **Fuel.** Fill the tank before leaving the city.
6. **Tools and contacts.** Save the shop's number, 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969), open 09:00 to 21:00 daily.

The [10-minute inspection checklist](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-scooter-inspection-checklist/) gives the full routine, and our guide to [checking a semi-automatic motorbike before renting](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-check-a-semi-automatic-motorbike-before-renting-in-hanoi/) applies to both models.

## Which one for which trip

**A day trip of 100 to 150 km with a stop for lunch.** Either bike is fine. If you can choose, the one with the better tyres and brakes wins.

**A multi-day ride with luggage.** The Sirius's tank and consumption figure give it a small range advantage, and its extra torque is welcome with a bag on the rack. Check its load limit first.

**Rougher roads and rural tracks.** The Sirius's extra ground clearance is the only specification advantage that matters here, but tyre condition matters more.

**A rider who prefers the Honda or Yamaha network.** Choose the bike with the better service history at the shop. Nguyen Ha's staff can say which of the bikes in the fleet has been serviced most recently.

## FAQ: Sirius versus Blade for longer trips

### Which has the better range, the Sirius or the Blade?

On the manufacturers' figures the Sirius FI, with a 4.0-litre tank and 1.65 L per 100 km, covers about 240 km in theory. The Blade, with a 3.7-litre tank and 1.85 L per 100 km, covers about 200 km. Real use gives less, so refuel at about half a tank.

### Which is better on hills?

The Sirius has a slightly larger engine and more torque, but both are small bikes. Use a low gear and plan rest stops.

### Do I need a different licence for either bike?

No. Both are motorbikes of up to 125 cc, so the same licence class applies to both.

### Which has better brakes?

It depends on the version. A front disc is better in the wet on either bike. Test the brakes on the actual bike.

### Can I take either bike on an expressway?

No. Motorbikes are banned from expressways, and entering one is fined VND 4,000,000–6,000,000 as of October 2026.

### Which should I choose if I am carrying luggage?

Ask the shop for each bike's load limit and condition. The Sirius's torque and range give it a small edge on paper.
