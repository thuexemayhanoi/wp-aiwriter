If the charger of a rental electric scooter goes missing, stop and phone the shop before you do anything else. The charger is part of the rental, the scooter cannot be recharged without a matching one, and the shop decides how it is replaced and what that costs under your contract. Do not buy a cheap charger from a market stall and plug it in, because manufacturers and fire-safety officials both warn against chargers that do not match the vehicle. This guide explains what to do in the first hour, what may happen afterwards, why the deposit and contract matter and how to make sure the charger never leaves your sight.

## The first hour: what to do

1. **Look properly before you worry.** Check the under-seat compartment, your bag, the hotel room, the socket where you last charged and the place where you parked.
2. **Check the battery level.** If the scooter still has plenty of charge, you have time. If it is low, ride only to a safe place.
3. **Phone the shop.** Nguyen Ha Motorbike Rental is on 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969), open 09:00 to 21:00 daily. Say which bike you have, where it is and how much battery is left.
4. **Do not improvise.** No borrowed adapters, no "it looks the same" chargers and no wiring.
5. **Write down what happened.** Time, place and who saw the charger last. It helps the shop and protects you.

## Why you cannot just buy another one

A charger for an electric scooter is a matched part. VinFast's owner's manual for the Evo 200, one of the models that may be offered, says to use only the charger supplied with the vehicle or an equivalent supplied by VinFast, and says that the wrong charger can damage the electrical system and cause fire. The manual describes the standard charger as 72 V and 5.5 A, drawing about 400 W, which is the kind of detail a stall-bought plug will not match.

The Ministry of Public Security makes a similar point from the facilities side. In an answer on battery fire safety dated 26 May 2026, it says charging areas should have rules that ban incompatible chargers. Hanoi's fire-prevention unit also advises using the charger and power source specified by the manufacturer, as reported in September 2026.

So "I found a charger that fits" is not a fix. A plug that goes in is not the same as a charger that is right for the battery.

## What the shop may do

The answer depends on the bike, the shop and the contract, and it is not something this guide can promise. The usual options are:

- **Supply a matching charger.** Often the cleanest solution, and the cost is agreed before you accept it.
- **Move you to another bike.** Possible if one is available.
- **Charge the bike at the shop.** Useful if you can bring the bike in without riding it far.
- **Charge at the shop's cost or yours.** Depends on the terms.

Ask for the price of a replacement in writing before you agree to anything. A missing accessory is a normal topic for a rental shop, and a calm question gets a better answer than a guess.

### Questions to ask before you ride away

- Which charger comes with the bike, and where is it stored?
- What does a replacement charger cost, and who pays if it is lost?
- What if the charger breaks rather than goes missing?
- Is the battery level and charging arrangement recorded at handover?
- Whom do I call, and when are they open?

The shop confirms the battery level and charging for every electric bike at handover, so use that moment.

## How chargers go missing

Chargers rarely vanish in dramatic ways. The common routes are small and avoidable:

- **Left plugged in.** At a hotel socket, a cafe or a friend's house.
- **Left in the compartment during charging.** VinFast's manual warns against this, because the charger can overheat when it sits in the boot while it is charging.
- **Fallen out.** If the compartment is opened in a hurry, a loose cable can slide out.
- **Taken with luggage.** Packed into a bag and forgotten.
- **Stolen with the bike's contents.** The manual advises against leaving valuables under the seat.

## How to avoid losing it

1. **Photograph the charger at handover,** including the plug, the lead and any label.
2. **Learn where it lives.** On the Evo 200 the charger can be kept in the under-seat compartment, wrapped in shock-absorbing material.
3. **Make a routine.** Charge, unplug, coil, store. Do not leave it in a wall for the night.
4. **Count your items when you leave a place.** Phone, wallet, keys, helmet, charger.
5. **Keep it out of sight.** Do not leave the charger on the seat or in a visible bag.
6. **Check it at return.** The shop will look for it, so do it first.

## Telling the shop what happened

A clear report makes a replacement quicker. When you phone, give the shop four facts: the bike you have, where it is now, how much battery it shows and when you last saw the charger. Mention where you charged last, because that is the likeliest place to find it. If the charger was left at a hotel or cafe, ask whether someone there can keep it safe until it is collected. Offer a photograph of the bike's display if the shop asks for it.

Be straightforward about whether the charger was lost, stolen or damaged. A shop that hears the plain story can offer a plain solution, while a vague account tends to lead to a slower and more expensive one. If theft is involved, say so, and ask the shop what it needs from you.

## Missing, faulty or just not connected?

Before you decide the charger is lost or broken, check what it is telling you. The Evo 200 manual says the charger's light shows red while charging, flashes red when the charger or battery has a fault, and shows green when the battery is full or when the charger is not connected to the vehicle. It also says to switch on the battery switch under the seat before plugging in, because without it the vehicle will not charge. A green light with a flat battery usually means the lead is not properly connected. If the light flashes red, unplug the charger, plug it in again and, if it persists, tell the shop.

Plug the output end into the vehicle first and then the mains plug, the manual advises, and wait at least 30 seconds after plugging in before turning on the ignition. These steps are for that model, so ask the shop for the sequence for your bike.

## Why a public charging point does not solve it

Public charging posts do not remove the need for a charger. VinFast's guide to using its public posts for e-scooters tells riders to turn off the vehicle, open the compartment, connect the charger to the scooter and plug it into a free socket on the post, then start the session in the VinFast E-Scooter app. The charger is stored in the compartment afterwards. Without your own charger, the post cannot help. Swap cabinets are a different system and are aimed at bikes designed for swappable batteries and subscriptions, as the Vietnamese press has reported. ## FAQ: a missing scooter charger

### Who pays for a lost scooter charger?

It depends on the contract and the shop. Ask before you sign, and ask again before you agree to any replacement.

### Can I use a charger from another scooter?

Not unless the shop says it matches. Manufacturers warn that the wrong charger can damage the vehicle and cause a fire.

### Can I charge the scooter at a public station without the charger?

For the VinFast posts described by the manufacturer, you connect your own charger to the post, so no.

### What if the charger is not lost but just stopped working?

Check the lights and the connection, then tell the shop. A faulty charger is a different matter from a lost one.

### Should I keep the charger in the compartment?

Some manuals allow it when wrapped in shock-absorbing material, but never leave it in the compartment while it is charging.

### Will the shop give me another bike?

Possibly, if one is available. Phone and ask.
