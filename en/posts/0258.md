Yes, an electric bicycle can legally carry a passenger in Vietnam, but only within a tight limit. Under Article 31 of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (Law 36/2024/QH15), the rider of a bicycle or motor-assisted bicycle may carry one person, or up to two people if the second is a child under 7 years old. Everyone on board needs a certified helmet with the strap fastened, and nobody may stand on the saddle or rack or sit on the handlebars. A pedal-less electric scooter is not an electric bicycle, so check which class you have, because the passenger rules for mopeds and motorcycles are different. This guide sets out the rule, the penalties, the practical limits of a light pedal-assist bike and the questions to ask a rental shop. It is general information as of October 2026.

## The rule in plain words

Article 31 deals with people who ride bicycles and motor-assisted bicycles, a group that includes electric bicycles. As summarised by the Thu Vien Phap Luat legal portal from the text of the law:

- The rider may carry **one person only**.
- The exception is a **child under 7 years old**: if you carry such a child as an additional passenger, the maximum is **two people** carried.
- Passengers must not carry bulky objects, use an umbrella, hold on to and pull other vehicles, stand on the saddle or the luggage rack, or sit on the handlebars.
- The rider and any passenger on a motor-assisted bicycle must wear a helmet that meets the national technical regulation, with the strap fastened properly.

The law's definition of a motor-assisted bicycle, including the electric bicycle, is a bicycle with motor help that cuts out when the rider stops pedalling or the bicycle reaches 25 km/h. That wording comes from Article 34, which the Ho Chi Minh City traffic police quote on their official website.

## Worked scenarios

| Who is on the bike | Within the rule? | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| Rider alone | Yes | The normal case |
| Rider plus one adult | Yes | One person carried |
| Rider plus one child under 7 | Yes | One person carried |
| Rider plus one adult plus one child under 7 | Yes | Two people carried, the most the rule allows |
| Rider plus two adults | No | More than the permitted number |
| Rider plus three children | No | More than two people carried |

The table is a reading aid, not a guarantee. A child's age is a matter of fact, and an officer may ask. If you are travelling with a young child, carry something that shows their age, such as a passport copy.

## Where the rule does not apply: pedal-less electric scooters

Do not assume the bike in front of you is an electric bicycle because it is electric. If it has a flat floor, a seat box and no pedals, it is a moped or a motorcycle, and a different article governs passengers. According to the Ministry of Public Security's summary of the new law, Article 33 lets the rider of a two-wheel motorcycle or moped carry one person, with a maximum of two in specified cases: a patient going to emergency care, a person being escorted for breaking the law, a child under 12 and an elderly or disabled person. The child limit for a moped or motorcycle is therefore 12, while for an electric bicycle it is 7, and the shape of the vehicle, not its fuel, decides which applies. ## Helmets for the passenger

The helmet duty extends to the person you carry. In the penalty decree, Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, the offence for a motor-assisted bicycle rider includes carrying a passenger without a helmet or with the strap undone, with the usual exceptions for a person going to emergency care, a child under 6 and a person being escorted for breaking the law. The fine for the rider is VND 400,000 to 600,000, as set out on the Thu Vien Phap Luat portal. In other words, the rider answers for the passenger's helmet. Details of what a compliant helmet looks like are in our [helmet rules guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/helmet-rules-in-vietnam/).

## Penalties for carrying too many

Carrying more people than the rule allows is listed among the offences in the penalty decree, in the same group as riding on the wrong part of the road and failing to signal. The group carries a fine of VND 100,000 to 200,000 according to the portal's table of offences for bicycle and motor-assisted bicycle riders, and a person being taken for emergency care is excepted. Fines for helmet offences are higher than the fines for overloading, which is a reminder of where the legal emphasis lies.

None of this is a reason to treat the fine as the only cost. A stop takes time, and an electric bicycle with too many people on it is simply harder to control.

## What the law does not tell you: the bike's own limits

The legal limit is a ceiling. It is not a promise that your bike can safely carry two. An electric bicycle in Vietnam is built to a small-vehicle regulation: the older national technical regulation for electric bicycles, as quoted by the Thu Vien Phap Luat portal, sets a maximum motor power of 250 W, a maximum design speed of 25 km/h and a maximum unladen weight, battery included, of 40 kg. A light frame, small motor and thin tyres are comfortable for one adult and may be strained by two. These are general observations, and the regulation quoted is the 2013 text, so ask about the actual model.

Questions that matter more than the legal number:

1. **Does the bike have a proper rear seat and footrests?** If not, a passenger has nowhere safe to sit, and the law bans standing and handlebar seating.
2. **What is the load rating?** Ask the shop for the maker's maximum load for rider plus passenger plus luggage.
3. **How does it brake with two on board?** Test the brakes at walking pace with the shop's staff beside you before you go into traffic.
4. **How steady is it at low speed?** Extra weight above the rear wheel changes the handling.
5. **Is the battery charged?** Range falls when the load rises; our [electric scooter rental guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/renting-an-electric-scooter-in-hanoi/) covers charging basics.

If the honest answer to any of these is no, ride solo or rent a bigger bike. Our [guide to two riders and luggage on a scooter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/scooter-for-two-riders-and-luggage-hanoi/) shows the scooter side of that choice.

## Carrying a child on an electric bicycle

The legal allowance for a child under 7 is permission, not advice. A small child needs a seat and a helmet that fit, and needs to be able to keep hands and feet away from the wheel. Do not use an adult helmet on a child. A child who falls asleep is a particular risk. If you cannot meet those conditions, do not carry the child on the bike. For children on motorised scooters, use the guidance in the child passengers guide linked above.

## Why a passenger changes how an electric bicycle rides

A second person adds weight high and behind the rider, which moves the balance of a light frame backwards. The effects below are general riding observations, not figures from the sources above:

- **Longer stopping distance.** More mass needs more braking distance, and small-diameter wheels with modest brakes have less margin to start with.
- **Slower pull-away.** A 250 W motor assisting two adults on a slope gives much less help than it gives one, so expect to pedal harder or walk a steep ramp.
- **Twitchier steering at walking pace.** The front wheel feels lighter, so wobble at low speed is more likely when the passenger shifts or points at something.
- **Shorter range.** Battery assistance falls faster with a heavier load.

A good habit is to brief the passenger before moving off: hold the rider or the grab point, keep feet clear of the wheel, lean with the bike and stay still when it is stopped.

## Setting off: a quick checklist

- Confirm the vehicle is a motor-assisted bicycle (pedals, assist cut at 25 km/h) and not a moped.
- Confirm the number of people: rider plus one, or rider plus an adult and a child under 7.
- Check a helmet for each person: certified, sized and strapped.
- Check that the bike has a rear seat and footrests, and ask for its load rating.
- Test the brakes with the load on.
- Keep to the right and expect longer stopping distances.
- Avoid umbrellas, bulky bags and any grip on other vehicles.

## FAQ: passengers on electric bicycles

### How many people can an electric bicycle carry in Vietnam?

One passenger, or two if the second is a child under 7, under Article 31 of Law 36/2024/QH15.

### Does my passenger need a helmet?

Yes, on a motor-assisted bicycle. The exceptions are narrow, such as a child under 6, a person going to emergency care and a person being escorted for breaking the law.

### Can I carry a child under 7 plus an adult?

Yes. That is two people carried, the maximum the rule allows.

### Is it different for an electric scooter without pedals?

Yes. A moped or motorcycle falls under Article 33, which permits one person, or two in listed cases, including a child under 12.

### Can the passenger stand on the rack or sit on the handlebars?

No. The law bans both.

### What is the fine for carrying too many people?

The portal's table puts it at VND 100,000 to 200,000 for a bicycle or motor-assisted bicycle rider, with an exception for emergency care.
