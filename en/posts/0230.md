Ask the hotel three things before you book or at the latest before you collect the bike: where the charging would happen, whether anyone will be around while it charges, and whether the building allows it at all. A good answer names a dry, ventilated spot that is not a corridor, stairwell or entrance, and a person who knows about it. A vague "no problem, just plug it in" is not a good answer. This guide gives you the questions in the order to ask them, short messages you can copy, a way to judge the replies and what to tell the rental shop afterwards. It is about asking well, not about deciding for the hotel. The building's rule always wins over any general tip.

## Why the question is worth asking early

Most guidance on lithium batteries makes the same point: charging is the moment of greatest risk. London Fire Brigade says that most fires involving e-bikes and e-scooters happen while the battery is charging, and that a faulty charger or a damaged battery can overheat and fail. That does not make electric bikes unusually dangerous to rent, but it does explain why a hotel manager may want to know before a guest wheels one into the lobby.

There is also a practical reason. Hanoi's buildings are not uniform. In late 2025, several large apartment blocks in the city announced restrictions on electric motorbikes in their basements, and reports at the time said that national fire rules did not prohibit charging indoors but that detailed guidance for buildings was still missing, so managers set their own policies. A hotel is a different kind of building from an apartment block, but the lesson carries over: there is no single rule you can quote at reception, so you need that particular building's answer.

If you ask on arrival with a half-flat battery, you have no leverage and little time. If you ask at booking, you can change hotel, change bike or change the plan.

## The three questions that matter most

Everything else is detail. Ask these first.

1. **Where would the charging happen?** You want a named place, for example "the courtyard by the back door" or "behind the reception desk". "Anywhere" is not a place.
2. **Who will be there while it charges?** Fire services advise against charging unattended or while people are asleep. London Fire Brigade says never to leave a battery charging unattended or while you are asleep, and the provincial fire-prevention department of Gia Lai in Vietnam advises stopping the charge before you leave or go to sleep. A staffed lobby is better than an empty back corridor.
3. **Does the building allow it?** Some hotels allow a removable battery in a room, some allow the whole bike in a courtyard and some allow neither. The answer needs to come from someone who can speak for the hotel.

If you get clear answers to these three, you are most of the way there. If you do not, there is no point asking about sockets and cables yet.

## The follow-up questions

Ask these once the basics are settled. Each has a reason, which helps you recognise a good answer.

- **Is the spot dry and ventilated?** Provincial fire-prevention guidance in Gia Lai advises charging in a dry, airy place and not in a closed room, away from heat and combustible goods.
- **Is it away from exits and escape routes?** London Fire Brigade's guidance for building managers warns against charging in corridors or stairwells, because a fire there can block the way out, and says to keep bikes off main through-routes and exits.
- **Is there a smoke or heat alarm nearby?** London Fire Brigade recommends a smoke alarm where batteries are charged. A hotel's lobby will usually be covered by the building's alarm system, but it is fair to ask.
- **Is there a fire extinguisher close by, and do staff know where it is?** You are not asking staff to deal with a battery fire. You are checking that the place is not an afterthought.
- **Is it a proper wall socket?** The point is to avoid improvised wiring. Ask whether you would be plugging into a fixed socket or a long extension lead run from somewhere else.
- **Can the battery be removed and carried in, or does the bike have to stay where it is?** This depends on the model, so check it with the rental shop too.
- **Are there times when the hotel would rather you did not charge?** Some places avoid late evening, when reception is quiet.
- **Is there an extra charge for electricity?** Hotels differ, and it is better to know in advance than at check-out.
- **Is the bike itself safe to leave there?** Our guide to [parking a motorbike in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-motorbike-in-hanoi/) covers guarded parking and what to expect.

## Short messages you can copy

A written request is better than a conversation at the desk. It gives the hotel time to check, and gives you something to show the rental shop. These are plain, polite and not tied to any particular hotel.

**At booking:**

> Hello. I plan to rent an electric bicycle (or electric scooter) during my stay. Could you tell me whether guests can charge it at the hotel? If yes, where would the charging take place, and would someone be present while it charges? The charger is the one supplied with the bike. Thank you.

**After the hotel says yes:**

> Thank you. To confirm: I would charge it at [the place you named], between about [time] and [time], while [someone] is present. I will not charge it overnight or leave it unattended. Is that correct?

**If the hotel says no:**

> Understood, thank you. Is there a place nearby, such as a café, a parking operator or a neighbouring business, that you would recommend, or is there somewhere on the street where charging is normal?

Keep it short. A manager who gets a long, anxious message is more likely to say no than one who gets a clear, modest request.

## How to read the answer

| What the hotel says | What it probably means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| "Yes, in the courtyard, the front desk will plug it in for you." | A named place and a person | Ask who will switch it off, and confirm the times |
| "Yes, in your room, the battery comes off." | Allowed in principle | Ask about the socket, and keep the battery off beds, curtains and exits |
| "Yes, leave it overnight, no problem." | Friendly but unsafe | Politely say you will charge only while someone is present |
| "Yes, anywhere." | No real answer | Ask for a specific place |
| "Not inside the building." | A clear rule | Ask where to charge outside, or choose another way to power the bike |
| "I will ask the manager." | The right person has not answered | Wait for the reply before you rely on it |
| "No electric bikes at all." | A firm refusal | Do not argue. Tell the rental shop before you collect the bike |

The key row is the third. Hotels are naturally accommodating, and many staff will say yes to an overnight charge because it is convenient. The convenient answer is not the same as the safe one. Fire services advise against charging overnight, and you can respect that without refusing the hotel's offer: simply say that you will charge in the evening while the lobby is staffed and unplug before you sleep.

## Red flags that mean "ask somewhere else"

Walk away from a charging plan if you see any of the following:

- the socket is in a corridor, a stairwell or beside the only door;
- the cable would run across a walkway or through a doorway;
- there is a tangle of other leads, chargers or extension reels at the same socket;
- the area is full of paper, cardboard, cushions or other things that burn;
- the place is wet, cluttered or very hot;
- nobody is on duty at the time you would be charging;
- the staff member is unsure and cannot say who is.

None of these means the hotel is bad. It means that this spot should not be where the bike charges.

Once you have a reply, do three things.

1. **Tell the rental shop what the hotel said**, before you leave with the bike. If the hotel allows only a removable battery in a room, the shop needs to know, because not every model has one. ## What the hotel may legitimately ask of you

A good hotel will also have questions. Expect some of these, and answer them calmly:

- **Which charger will you use?** Use the one supplied with the bike. London Fire Brigade advises using the charger supplied with the battery or one recommended by the manufacturer. Do not offer a different one.
- **How long will it take?** Charging times vary by model. Say that you will check with the rental shop and tell reception the hours.
- **Will you be here the whole time?** Be honest about it. If the answer is no, charge at another time.
- **Can we see the bike first?** A hotel that wants to look at a cracked case or a loose plug is doing its job. Our [rental scooter inspection checklist](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-scooter-inspection-checklist/) shows what to check before you leave the shop.

## If the hotel says no

A refusal is useful information, and it is easier to deal with at booking than at the door. Your options:

- **Choose a hotel that allows charging.** If your plan depends on electric charging, make it a filter when you book.
- **Ask the rental shop whether another bike suits you better.** The shop's electric range is described on its [electric scooters page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/electric-scooters/), and a petrol bike has no charging question at all. Our guide to [renting an electric scooter in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/renting-an-electric-scooter-in-hanoi/) lists what to weigh.
- **Plan the day around the shop's opening hours** if the shop is willing to arrange charging. Ask first. Do not assume.
- **Use a public charging point or a swap cabinet only if the rental terms allow it**, and only for a bike that can use them.

Do not charge secretly or in a spot the hotel has told you not to use. If something goes wrong, the fact that you were told no will matter a great deal.

## A checklist to take to reception

- Named place, away from exits and not in a corridor or stairwell
- Dry, ventilated, nothing flammable nearby
- A person on duty while it charges
- Fixed wall socket, no tangle of extension leads
- Supplied charger only
- Charge stopped before sleeping or going out
- Hotel's reply saved, shop informed

## FAQ: asking a hotel about charging an electric bike

### Should I ask before I book the hotel?

Yes, if charging matters to your plan. It is the cheapest moment to change your mind. If you have already booked, send the message as soon as you know which bike you will rent.

### Is it normal for a hotel in Hanoi to allow charging?

Practice varies, and the answer depends on the building. Reports from late 2025 showed that several Hanoi apartment blocks had restricted electric motorbikes in their basements while the authorities said that fire rules did not ban charging indoors. Do not assume either way. Ask.

### Can I charge the battery in my room?

Only if the hotel says so and the battery is removable. Keep it on a hard surface away from beds, curtains and the door, and never cover it. Ask the rental shop whether your bike has a removable battery.

### Can I leave it charging overnight if the hotel offers?

Fire-safety guidance advises against it. London Fire Brigade and Vietnamese provincial fire-prevention advice both say not to charge while asleep or away. Accept the hotel's help, but charge during the evening and unplug before you sleep.

### What if the hotel wants me to use its own charger or extension lead?

Politely decline and explain that the bike has its own supplied charger. A different charger is a risk, and so is an extension lead you cannot vouch for. Ask for a fixed socket.

### What if the hotel says yes but the place looks wrong?

Trust what you see. If the socket is in a corridor or beside the exit, tell the staff and ask for another place, or do not charge there.

### What should I say to the rental shop?

Tell them what the hotel allows and where. The shop can say whether your bike's battery comes off and what the safest charging routine is for that model.
