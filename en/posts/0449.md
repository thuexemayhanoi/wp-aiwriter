On a Vietnamese traffic signal, an arrow light is an extra signal for one direction of travel. A green arrow lets you move in the direction the arrow points, and a red arrow means you must stop for that movement. Arrows do not change the basic rule that a red light means stop. Under Article 11 of Law 36/2024/QH15, traffic lights have three colours, green, amber and red, with or without a time display, and the red signal means stop. Turning right on a red light is not allowed by default. The Traffic Police Department has explained that it is allowed where a green arrow light or a supplementary sign permits it, or when traffic police on duty direct you to go.

This guide explains each signal a rider will meet, including the main colours, flashing amber, arrows and time displays, shows when a right turn on red is permitted and when it is not, describes how to approach each junction type and covers the fines. It is general information based on the law as of October 2026, not legal advice.

## The main lights first

Arrows only make sense once the main lights are clear. Article 11(4) of the Law sets them out:

| Signal | Meaning | What a rider does |
|---|---|---|
| Green | Proceed | Go, but slow down or stop for pedestrians or wheelchair users crossing the roadway |
| Amber | Stop behind the stop line | Stop. If you are on or past the stop line when it turns amber, you may keep moving |
| Flashing amber | Proceed with care | Observe the surroundings, slow down or stop to give way to pedestrians, wheelchair users or other vehicles |
| Red | Stop | Stop behind the stop line |

The standard on road signals, QCVN 41:2024/BGTVT, adds a detail for red. As quoted by a provincial broadcaster, it says that red means no entry and a stop before the stop line, and that where there is no stop line you stop before the signal in your direction of travel, the nearest signal being treated as the stop line. Wait behind the line and not on it. The law also says lights may have a time display, which shows the time remaining in a phase. A countdown helps you plan, but it does not change what the colours mean. ## What an arrow light is

An arrow light is a secondary signal. It usually sits on or beside the main signal head, shows an arrow for one direction, and applies only to that direction. It is useful at junctions where turning traffic has its own phase, so that turning vehicles move while straight-ahead traffic waits or the other way round.

The two arrow signals a rider needs to understand are:

- **Green arrow.** Movement in the direction of the arrow is permitted while the arrow is lit. The Traffic Police Department has said that a green arrow light is one of the ways a right turn on red is allowed.

- **Red arrow.** Movement in the direction of the arrow is not permitted. Treat it as a stop signal for that movement.

If you meet an arrow you do not recognise, for example one that is a different colour or flashing, treat it as a stop for that movement until you can see what the traffic around you is doing and the main signal is clear. A moment's wait is cheaper than a fine or a collision.

## Right turn on red: the rule

Many riders assume that a right turn on red is always allowed. It is not. In January 2025 the Traffic Police Department of the Ministry of Public Security explained, as reported by Viet Nam News, that when the light is red drivers must not proceed, but at intersections where right turns are allowed, indicated by a green arrow light or a supplementary sign, drivers may turn right on red without violating the law. It added that drivers may also turn right on red if directed by traffic police on duty, and advised them to yield to pedestrians. The same official said that proposals for a general right turn on red would need study of traffic flow and timing before any change.

In practice, a right turn on red is permitted only when one of these applies:

1. **A traffic police officer or authorised traffic controller directs you to go.** Under Article 11, a traffic guard's command outranks the traffic light.

2. **A green arrow light for the right turn is lit.** It is a separate signal beside the main lights.

3. **A supplementary sign allows it.** The sign is normally mounted at the signal, and you should read the whole sign, because some are worded for particular vehicles or conditions.

Some junctions also have a physically separated right-turn lane or markings that direct right turns around the signal. Where you meet one, follow the signs and markings for that layout and do not assume that it applies to you without a sign.

If none of the three conditions applies, a red light means stop, even if the road to your right is empty and the traffic behind you is honking.

## How to turn right when you are allowed to

Permission to turn is not priority. The rules for a turn still apply.

- **Signal early,** and keep the signal on throughout the turn. Article 15 says direction-change signals must be used continuously during the change.

- **Slow down,** and check the vehicle behind you.

- **Give way to pedestrians.** Article 15 says drivers must give way to pedestrians, rudimentary vehicles and oncoming traffic when changing direction, and the traffic police advice on right turns on red specifically mentions pedestrians.

- **Watch the traffic with a green light.** Vehicles with a green signal from your left or right still have the right to go.

- **Do not force your way,** and be ready to stop in the turn if someone steps out.

## Left-turn and straight-on arrows

Where a junction gives turning traffic its own phase, a left-turn arrow tells you when your movement has a green phase of its own. The practical rules for a rider are:

- **Use the lane that matches the arrow.** If you need to turn left, be in the lane and the phase for the left turn. Our guide to [lane rules for motorbikes in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/lane-rules-motorbikes-vietnam/) explains how to choose.

- **Do not start on someone else's arrow.** If the arrow for another movement is lit and yours is not, wait.

- **Look when your phase starts.** Others may still be clearing the junction, and a vehicle may be late.

- **Do not turn on a straight-ahead green** unless it is safe and your turn is allowed, and then only after giving way to oncoming traffic and pedestrians.

If your direction has no arrow of its own, use the main signal and the signs and markings at the junction.

## Time displays and amber

Because the law allows a time display, you will see countdowns at some junctions. They are useful, but they are a poor guide to the exact moment the light changes. Use them to ease off early and not to decide whether to race. The amber rule is the one to remember: stop behind the stop line, and keep moving only if you are already on or over it. Do not accelerate to beat the amber, because the vehicle beside you may be braking and the one behind you may not expect it. A flashing amber means go with care and give way to pedestrians and other vehicles. ## What outranks what

Article 11 sets an order of priority. A traffic guard's command ranks first, then the traffic light signal, then road signs, then road markings. So:

- If an officer waves you through a red light, you follow the officer.

- If a light is red and a sign tells you something different, the light ranks higher, unless a supplementary sign at the signal specifically permits a movement.

- If a temporary sign and a fixed sign differ, follow the temporary sign.

## Fines and cameras

Not obeying a traffic light is a serious offence. Under Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, as read on the Government portal and as described by the Traffic Police Department, the fine for a motorcycle or moped rider who fails to obey a traffic light is VND 4,000,000 to 6,000,000. Decree 168 also provides for notices for violations found through technical devices, and for a rental motorbike the notice may reach the vehicle's owner first. ## A routine at every signal

1. **Look ahead,** and read the signal head before you reach it.

2. **Check for arrows,** and which direction each applies to.

3. **Check for a supplementary sign** at the signal.

4. **Slow down early,** so that a change does not catch you at speed.

5. **Stop behind the line,** not on it and not over the crossing.

6. **Check mirrors,** then watch pedestrians, then move.

7. **When unsure, stay stopped,** and follow the traffic guard if there is one.

## A quick checklist

1. **Red means stop, whatever the road looks like.**

2. **A green arrow permits movement in its direction.**

3. **A red arrow means stop for that movement.**

4. **Right turn on red: only with police direction, a green arrow or a supplementary sign.**

5. **Signal, slow and give way to pedestrians when you turn.**

6. **Stop behind the line on amber unless you are already on it.**

7. **Do not treat a countdown as a race.**

## FAQ: traffic-light arrows for riders

### Can I turn right on a red light in Vietnam?

Not by default. The Traffic Police Department has said it is allowed where a green arrow light or a supplementary sign permits it, or when traffic police on duty direct you to go.

### What does a green arrow mean?

You may move in the direction of the arrow while it is lit, giving way to pedestrians and following the turning rules.

### What does a red arrow mean?

Do not move in the direction of the arrow. Treat it as a stop for that movement.

### What does a flashing amber light mean?

You may proceed, but you must observe the surroundings and slow down or stop to give way to pedestrians, wheelchair users or other vehicles.

### What if the light turns amber as I reach the line?

Stop behind the line. If you are already on or past the stop line when it turns amber, you may continue.

### What is the fine for running a red light on a motorbike?

VND 4,000,000 to 6,000,000 under Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, as described by the Traffic Police Department. Check the current text.
