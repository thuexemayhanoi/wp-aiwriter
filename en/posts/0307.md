Tran Quoc Pagoda is easy to reach by scooter along Thanh Nien Road, but it is a working Buddhist place of worship, so the polite way to visit is to park outside, walk in with covered shoulders and knees, take off your shoes where asked and keep your voice and camera low. Entry is reported to be free, and most guides give opening hours of about 08:00 to 16:00, with differences on lunar festival days and some visitors reporting a midday break, so check the sign at the gate before you plan around any time. The Vietnam tourism administration calls it the most ancient of the 600 or so pagodas and temples in the capital. This guide covers the approach, the stop, the hours, the etiquette and the time to allow.

## What you are visiting

Tran Quoc stands on a small islet beside West Lake in Tay Ho district, joined to the mainland by the causeway of Thanh Nien Road. The Vietnam tourism administration says it was built in the sixth century and is the most ancient pagoda in the capital, and it names the high stupa, built in 1998, among its outstanding features. VietnamPlus adds that the stupa has eleven floors and stands 15 metres high, that a Bodhi tree in the grounds was grafted from the tree at Bodh Gaya and presented by India's president in 1959, and that the pagoda was listed as a national historical and cultural relic in the first round in 1962.

Why this matters for a rider: it is a heritage site and an active temple at once. Monks, nuns and lay Buddhists use it daily, and visitors come in tour groups, on motorbikes and on foot. Behaviour that is fine at a lakeside cafe may be out of place at the gate.

## Getting there by scooter

One travel site gives the address as 46 Thanh Nien Street, Yen Phu Ward, Tay Ho District, and describes the pagoda as around 3.5 to 4 kilometres from the Old Quarter. Another places it on the road that separates West Lake from Truc Bach Lake. Origin Vietnam gives a travel time of about 15 to 20 minutes from the centre.

For a rider, that means:

- **Use a map app in motorbike mode** and check for one-way sections near the causeway.
- **Expect mixed traffic.** Thanh Nien is a scenic road, and cyclists, joggers, wedding photographers and cafe visitors share it with motorbikes and cars.
- **Do not stop on the causeway for a photograph.** Wait for a place where you can pull in without blocking the lane.
- **Plan an exit as well as an entry.** The road is a through route, and turning across traffic to get back to where you came from can be awkward.

## Where to leave the scooter

Sources do not agree on parking at the pagoda. Origin Vietnam's guide says free parking is available for personal vehicles, but another guide mentions no arrangement at all, and arrangements near busy temples can change on festival days. Treat parking as something to confirm on arrival rather than something promised.

Under Article 18 of Law 36/2024/QH15 on road traffic order and safety, in force since 1 January 2025, a rider parking on a city street must keep close to the right-hand kerb in the direction of travel, no more than 0.25 metres from it, without obstructing pedestrians or other vehicles. The article forbids stopping or parking in places such as pedestrian crossings, pick-up and drop-off points and within 5 metres of a junction. On a causeway road with a steady flow of vehicles, a bike left carelessly can create danger as well as annoyance.

In practice:

1. If there is a marked or attended bike area, use it and take the ticket.
2. If there is not, park a short way along the road in a place that blocks nothing, and walk back.
3. Do not leave the bike in the path of other visitors, on steps, at the gate or against the pagoda wall.
4. Lock the steering and keep the key, because the shop does not provide a spare lock.

## Opening hours: why the sources disagree

Published hours for Tran Quoc differ. Guides from Silk Path Hotel, Your Vietnam Travel and Origin Vietnam give about 08:00 to 16:00 daily. Origin Vietnam adds that on the first and fifteenth day of the lunar month the pagoda opens from 06:00 to 18:00, and one other guide says it may open earlier on those days. A hotel guide lists 07:30 to 17:00 with a closure between 11:00 and 13:30, and a travel-platform listing shows morning and afternoon sessions. One visitor report on that platform says the writer found the pagoda closed for lunch.

You can draw three practical conclusions:

- Aim for mid-morning, which falls inside every reported opening window.
- Avoid midday in case of a break.
- Avoid arriving close to 16:00, because some guides give that as the closing time.

Opening hours may vary slightly during festivals, Tet and special Buddhist events. If the gate is closed, have a plan B, such as a lakeside walk or the nearby Quan Thanh Temple, and our [Ba Dinh by motorbike guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/ba-dinh-by-motorbike/) explains the streets on that side of the lake.

## How long to allow

Your Vietnam Travel suggests 30 to 60 minutes for the visit. Add time for parking, a walk around the grounds and photographs from the shore. If you are combining the visit with the lakeside loop, see our [West Lake by scooter guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/tay-ho-west-lake-by-scooter/).

## Dress and footwear

The standard advice for Vietnamese pagodas applies. Cover your shoulders and knees, whether you are a man or a woman, and avoid tight, sheer or beach clothing. Tank tops and short shorts are the commonest problem for riders in the hot season. A light scarf or overshirt in the bike's storage space solves it. Choose shoes that come off easily, since you remove them before entering a sanctuary or prayer hall. Socks are fine.

If you are riding in a helmet and gloves, take them off before going into a hall, carry the helmet by its strap and do not rest it on an altar, offering table or ledge. Helmet rules for riding are in our guide to [helmets in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/helmet-rules-in-vietnam/).

## Etiquette inside

Take your cue from the people around you:

| Do | Avoid |
|---|---|
| Speak softly and walk slowly | Loud conversation, phone calls or speakers |
| Step aside for worshippers and monks | Walking between someone praying and the altar |
| Observe how locals offer incense before copying them | Touching offerings, statues, bells or the Bodhi tree |
| Follow posted signs on photography | Flash or interior photography where it is banned |
| Ask before photographing people at prayer | Selfies with your back to the main altar |
| Leave any donation in the box provided | Pushing a donation on monks or staff |

A general etiquette guide notes that photography is usually allowed in courtyards but banned inside main sanctuaries, and that Tran Quoc is among the places that restrict interior photos. That is one source, so rely on the signs and staff, and when in doubt copy local visitors. The courtyard stupa and the lake views are the easiest subjects.

## Respect for a working religious site

The pagoda is not only a monument. It hosts worship on ordinary days and larger ceremonies on lunar festival dates. Avoid blocking doors and paths for photographs, and do not climb on steps or ledges. If a ceremony is under way, watch from a distance or come back later.


## Before you ride off

Ride away slowly, because the pagoda exit can put you into fast traffic on the causeway. Check mirrors, signal and merge. If you stopped to buy flowers or offerings from a roadside seller, take them to the pagoda by hand, not hanging from the handlebars. ## FAQ: Tran Quoc Pagoda by scooter

### Is there an entrance fee?

Guides report free entry, with donations optional. Confirm at the gate.

### What are the opening hours?

Most guides say about 08:00 to 16:00, with longer hours on the first and fifteenth day of the lunar month. Other sources differ, so check the sign.

### Can I take photos inside?

Courtyard photography is usually fine, but interior photography may be banned. Follow the signs.

### Where do I park?

One guide says free parking is available, but arrangements can change. Look for a marked area or park clear of the gate and walk.

### What should I wear?

Cover shoulders and knees and wear shoes that are easy to remove.

### How long does a visit take?

Around 30 to 60 minutes, plus time to park.
