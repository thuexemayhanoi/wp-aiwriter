Most visitors who ride a scooter in Vietnam never meet a traffic policeman, and most of the ones who do are stopped for the same handful of reasons. The penalties are no longer the token amounts of a decade ago. Since 1 January 2025 the fines for motorbike riders have been set by Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, which raised many of them several times over and introduced a points system for driving licences, and the decree was amended again by Decree 238/2026/ND-CP, in force since 15 August 2026. This guide picks out the offences that actually catch foreign riders, explains the amounts as of October 2026, and walks through what happens at a roadside stop. It is written for people renting a bike for a few days or weeks in Hanoi, not as legal advice, and the figures are taken from the decree itself with the sources listed at the end.

## Why the rules feel different on the road

The first surprise for many riders is that the traffic around them seems to ignore rules that the police then enforce on them. Locals ride on the pavement to skip a jam, turn left across a red light at a quiet junction and carry three people on a scooter, and you will see all of this within an hour of arriving. None of it is legal, and since 2025 Hanoi police have been noticeably more active, with fixed checkpoints, patrols at busy junctions and a growing network of cameras that issue fines after the event. A foreigner on a rented scooter is easy to spot and has no local excuse.

The second surprise is the size of the bands. A Vietnamese fine is set as a range, for example VND 4–6 million, and the officer who writes the decision normally starts from the middle of the range unless there are aggravating or mitigating circumstances. The amounts below are those ranges, not exact tickets. Paying a fine does not end the matter either: some offences also cost points on a driving licence or lead to the bike being held.

## The offences that catch visitors most often

### Riding without a valid licence

This is the expensive one, and it is the most common problem for tourists because a home licence alone is not valid in Vietnam. You need a licence that covers motorcycles plus an International Driving Permit issued under the 1968 Vienna Convention with the motorcycle category, or a Vietnamese licence. Riding a motorcycle of up to 125 cc without a valid licence is fined VND 2–4 million, and above 125 cc VND 6–8 million, under Article 18 of Decree 168/2024/ND-CP. Holding a valid licence but not carrying it costs VND 200,000–300,000, and you will still be asked to produce it. Our guide to [licence rules for foreigners](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbike-licence-rules-for-foreigners-in-vietnam/) explains which permits are accepted and the under-50cc exception.

### Helmets: missing, unfastened or fake

A rider with no helmet, or with the strap left undone, is fined VND 400,000–600,000, and the same amount applies to the rider for each passenger without one; the passenger can be fined separately as well. Thin "fashion" helmets without the national conformity mark are treated as no helmet. Full details are in our [helmet rules guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/helmet-rules-in-vietnam/).

### Running a red light

Under point c of clause 7, Article 7 of the decree, a motorbike rider who ignores a traffic signal is fined VND 4–6 million and loses 4 points from the licence. This includes turning right on red where no sign or green arrow allows it, which many visitors assume is permitted as it is in some countries. If running the light causes a crash, the fine rises to VND 10–14 million. Red-light offences are a favourite of the camera system, so a ticket can arrive at the rental shop days after you returned the bike.

### Riding on the pavement or the wrong way

Mounting the pavement to get round traffic, and riding against the flow on a one-way street, are both fined VND 4–6 million with 2 points deducted. The only pavement exception is crossing it to get in or out of a house or a building's entrance. In the Old Quarter and around Ba Dinh many streets are one-way, and the signs are easy to miss; our guides to [riding in the Old Quarter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-old-quarter/) and [Ba Dinh by motorbike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/ba-dinh-by-motorbike/) point out where the one-way systems are.

### Using a phone while riding

Holding a phone to make a call, read a map or take a video while riding is fined VND 800,000–1,000,000 under clause 4 of Article 7 and costs 4 licence points. The same point covers riding while holding an umbrella or using headphones and other audio devices other than a hearing aid. A phone mounted on the handlebars for navigation and not touched while moving is the safe arrangement; pull over to change the route.

### Drinking and riding

Vietnam has a zero-tolerance rule: any alcohol in the blood or on the breath is an offence for a rider. The lowest band, up to 50 milligrams per 100 millilitres of blood or 0.25 milligrams per litre of breath, is fined VND 2–3 million and costs 4 points; above that the fines climb to VND 6–8 million and then VND 8–10 million with the licence taken away for 22 to 24 months. Refusing the test is punished as heavily as the top band. Because the rules and the night-time checkpoints deserve a full explanation, we cover them separately in our guide to [drink-driving rules for motorbike riders](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/drink-driving-rules-motorbike-vietnam/).

### Speeding

Riding 5 to 10 km/h over the limit is fined VND 400,000–600,000, 10 to 20 km/h over VND 800,000–1,000,000, and more than 20 km/h over VND 6–8 million. Inside Hanoi's built-up areas the general limit for motorbikes is low, and many roads carry lower posted limits; speed cameras on the ring roads and the approaches to the bridges catch riders who assume the open-looking road is a fast one.

### The bike itself

You are responsible for the condition of the bike you ride. Under point a of clause 1, Article 14, riding a motorbike without a working horn, number-plate light, brake light or left-hand mirror is fined VND 400,000–600,000. These are exactly the things to check before you leave the shop, as we describe in our [10-minute rental scooter inspection](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-scooter-inspection-checklist/).

## How licence points work for foreign riders

Law 36/2024/QH15 gives every driving licence 12 points. Points are deducted for the offences listed above and others, and a licence that has not lost points for 12 months is restored to the full 12. If all 12 are lost, the holder may not drive and must retake the theory test after at least six months. The points are recorded against licences in the Vietnamese database, so they bite hardest on people who hold or convert to a Vietnamese licence for a long stay. A visitor riding on an International Driving Permit still pays the full fine for every offence, and the points rules exist to stop repeat offenders, not to excuse them.

## Camera fines that arrive after you leave

Hanoi now records many violations on camera, especially red lights, wrong-way riding and speeding. The notice goes to the registered owner of the bike, which for a rental is the shop, and it identifies the plate, the place and the time. A rental contract records who had the bike at that moment, so a camera fine can follow you after the rental ends and may be settled against the deposit if it arrives in time. Keep your contract and the handover times, and ask at the shop how fines notified after return are handled before you sign. Our article on [how a rental deposit works](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbike-rental-deposit-hanoi/) explains what a deposit can be used for.

## What to do when you are stopped

Traffic police in Vietnam wear a yellow-green or khaki uniform and signal riders to pull over with a baton or a whistle. Stop where you are told, switch off the engine and take off your sunglasses. Stay calm and polite; a smile and a slow, clear "hello" go further than an argument in a language the officer may not speak.

You will normally be asked for three documents: your licence (with the International Driving Permit), the bike's registration certificate, and the compulsory third-party insurance certificate. Rental shops hand over the registration and insurance documents with the bike or keep copies on the bike; check they are there before you leave, so that you are not caught without them. Carry a copy of your passport as well.

If the officer finds an offence, they write a record (bien ban) describing it, which you are asked to sign. You may write that you do not understand Vietnamese or that you disagree, and you are entitled to a copy. The fine itself is set in a written penalty decision. It is paid at the State Treasury, through a bank transfer to the account named on the decision, or online through the National Public Service Portal, not handed in cash to the officer at the roadside. Some offences allow the police to hold the bike or the licence until the fine is paid. Contact the rental shop straight away if the bike is held, because it belongs to them and they will need to know where it is.

If you are asked for cash on the spot with no paperwork, politely ask for the written record and the decision. Do not offer money. Offering a bribe to an official is itself a crime, and the safest position for a foreigner is always to go through the paperwork, slow as it is.

## How to avoid nearly all of it

Almost every fine above comes from five habits, and changing them costs nothing. Carry a valid licence and IDP, and the bike's papers. Wear a proper helmet with the strap done up, and make sure any passenger does too. Stop at red lights even when the riders around you do not, and wait for green when turning right unless a sign allows it. Stay off the pavement and read the one-way signs. Never ride after drinking, and leave the phone in its mount.

It also helps to ride at the pace of the traffic rather than faster, to keep left-hand mirror and lights working, and to avoid riding with two passengers. ## FAQ

### Can the police fine a tourist in Vietnam?

Yes. Traffic fines apply to everyone riding on Vietnamese roads, whatever their nationality. A foreign rider receives the same written record and decision as a Vietnamese rider and pays the same amount.

### What is the biggest fine a visitor is likely to face?

For common offences, riding a motorcycle above 125 cc without a valid licence (VND 6–8 million) and the higher alcohol bands (up to VND 8–10 million plus losing the licence for 22 to 24 months) are the largest. A red light or pavement offence costs VND 4–6 million.

### Can I pay a traffic fine in cash to the officer?

No. Fines are paid to the State Treasury, by bank transfer to the account on the decision or through the National Public Service Portal. Ask for the written record and decision if someone asks for cash.

### Is turning right on a red light allowed in Hanoi?

Only where a sign or a green arrow explicitly allows it. Otherwise it counts as ignoring the signal and is fined VND 4–6 million with 4 points deducted.

### Did Decree 238/2026/ND-CP change the motorbike fines in this article?

Decree 238/2026/ND-CP, in force since 15 August 2026, amends several articles of Decree 168/2024/ND-CP. As of October 2026 the published summaries do not show changes to the motorbike amounts quoted here, but always check the current text if you are dealing with an actual penalty.
