A left turn on a multi-lane road is the manoeuvre where you cross the path of oncoming traffic, so the safest approach is to treat it as three decisions: whether the turn is allowed here, whether the gap is safe now and whether you can finish in the right lane of the new road. Law 36/2024/QH15 requires you to observe the surroundings, keep a safe distance from the vehicle behind, slow down, signal, move gradually to the lane closest to the direction of the turn, and give way to pedestrians, rudimentary vehicles and oncoming traffic (Article 15). If a sign, a median or a marking does not allow the turn, do not make it. If you are not confident that the gap is safe, wait, or go on to a place where the turn is easier.

This guide breaks the left turn into steps, explains how lanes, signs and traffic lights affect it, describes the typical Hanoi situations, covers the mistakes that cause collisions and offers safer alternatives when a left turn looks too difficult. It is general information based on the law as of October 2026, not legal advice.

## Why left turns are different

When traffic keeps to the right, a left turn is the manoeuvre that crosses the opposing stream. A right turn crosses pedestrians. A left turn crosses oncoming vehicles, which are travelling at speed, may be hidden behind larger vehicles and may include scooters that appear between cars. So a left turn needs more space, a clearer view and a firmer decision. Our guide to [safe right turns at busy Hanoi intersections](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/safe-right-turns-at-busy-hanoi-intersections/) covers the simpler turn, and our guide to [crossing Hanoi intersections](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/crossing-hanoi-intersections/) describes the wider picture.

## What the law requires

Three articles of the Law matter most.

**Article 15, change of direction.** Before turning, the operator must observe the surroundings, ensure a safe distance from the vehicle behind, slow down, send out a signal (or a hand signal where there are no indicators) and gradually move to the lane closest to the direction to be changed. The signal must be kept on throughout the change. The turn may be made only when safety conditions are met and it is not likely to obstruct people or other vehicles. Operators must give way to pedestrians, rudimentary vehicles and oncoming traffic. Article 15 also bans U-turns in a list of places, which we return to below.

**Article 13, lanes.** On a road with several lanes in the same direction, you keep within one lane and change lanes only where it is allowed, one lane at a time, after signalling and checking the vehicles in front, behind and on both sides. For a left turn, this means you move across lanes gradually before the junction, not at the last moment.

**Article 11, signs and signals.** A traffic guard's command outranks the traffic light, which outranks road signs, which outrank road markings. Where a sign bans a left turn, the ban applies. Our guide to [understanding common motorbike road signs in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/understanding-common-motorbike-road-signs-in-vietnam/) explains the signs.

## Step 1: check that the turn is allowed

Before anything else, check three things.

1. **Signs.** Look for a sign banning left turns. Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, as read on the Government portal, sets a fine of VND 600,000 to 800,000 for turning left or right where a sign prohibits it.

2. **The road layout.** On a road with a physical median or barrier, you may turn left only where there is an opening. A gap in the median is a place designed for turning or U-turns, and a closed median means no turn.

3. **Markings.** Our guide to [how to read motorbike lane markings in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-read-motorbike-lane-markings-in-hanoi/) explains that a solid line is one you do not cross or ride on. Where the centre line is solid and there is no junction or opening, treat the road as one where a left turn across the line is not provided for.

If you are in doubt, go on. A left turn you skip costs a few minutes, and a left turn you force can cost a great deal more.

## Step 2: get into the left lane early

Article 15 tells you to move gradually to the lane closest to the direction of the turn. For a left turn, that is the lane next to the centre line or median.

- **Look for the turn early.** Check your route before you set off, so that you are not looking for the turn at the junction.

- **Signal your lane change.** Use the left indicator before each move.

- **Move one lane at a time.** Article 13 allows a change only from a lane to the adjacent one at a time.

- **Check behind and beside you** before each move, with mirrors and a glance.

- **Be in position well before the junction.** A diagonal dash across three lanes at the last moment is the pattern behind many collisions.

If you cannot get into the left lane safely, do not turn at this junction. Go on to the next one.

## Step 3: slow down and keep the signal on

As you approach, slow down smoothly. Keep your left indicator on, and do not turn it off until the turn is finished. Slowing early also tells the rider behind you what you intend to do. Do not brake hard just before the turn, because a sudden stop in the left lane is a hazard to the vehicle behind. Our guide to [how to check a scooter's horn and turn signals](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-check-a-scooters-horn-and-turn-signals/) explains how to make sure your signal works.

## Step 4: wait in the right place

If you must wait for a gap or a light, the position matters.

- **At a traffic light,** wait behind the stop line, in the left lane. Do not creep into the junction.

- **At a junction without lights,** stop behind the line or at the edge of the junction, close to the centre, and keep the signal on.

- **Keep the wheel straight** while waiting, not turned into the oncoming lane. If you are hit from behind, a straight wheel sends you forward, while a turned wheel sends you across the path of oncoming vehicles.

- **Leave room behind you,** so that you are not hemmed in.

- **Do not block the crossing,** and keep the junction clear for traffic in other directions.

## Step 5: choose the gap

The gap decision is the heart of the turn.

1. **Look at oncoming traffic,** and judge the speed and distance of the nearest vehicle, not just whether there is one.

2. **Look past the first vehicle.** A bus or a truck can hide a scooter behind it. Wait until you can see the whole road.

3. **Look for vehicles turning right** from the opposite direction into the same road.

4. **Look at the pedestrians** on the far side crossing the new road. Article 15 requires you to give way to them.

5. **Go only when the turn will not make anyone brake or swerve.** Article 15 says the turn may be made only when it is not likely to cause obstruction or danger.

6. **If in doubt, wait.** A good gap will come, and a rider behind you who is impatient is less of a danger than an oncoming vehicle.

If you are in a queue of vehicles turning left, do not follow the rider in front without looking. A gap that was safe for them may have closed.

## Step 6: make the turn and finish in the right lane

Once you commit, turn smoothly.

- **Look where you want to go,** into the new road, not at the vehicle you want to avoid.

- **Keep a steady, low speed,** and do not accelerate hard in the middle of the turn.

- **Do not cut the corner** into the lane of oncoming vehicles. Pass the centre of the junction before turning, so that you enter the new road on the right side, in your direction of travel.

- **Finish in the right-hand lane of the new road,** as Article 13 requires vehicles to keep to the right. Do not swing out into the opposing lane or the far lane.

- **Cancel the signal** when you are straight.

- **Accelerate gently** once you are straight and clear.

## Traffic lights and arrows

At a signal-controlled junction, the rules interact with the lights.

- **On a green light,** a left turn must still give way to oncoming traffic and pedestrians. Article 11 says operators must slow down or stop to give way to pedestrians crossing on the roadway.

- **With a left-turn arrow,** the arrow indicates a phase for your movement. Our guide to [what different traffic-light arrows mean for riders](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-different-traffic-light-arrows-mean-for-riders/) explains the signals.

- **On a red light,** you stop, for a left turn as for any other movement. A fine for not obeying the signal is VND 4,000,000 to 6,000,000 for motorcycle and moped riders under Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, as described by the Traffic Police Department. Check the current text.

- **On amber,** stop behind the line unless you are already on or over it.

Do not start a left turn at the very end of a green phase if you cannot clear the junction before the light changes.

## Typical Hanoi situations

- **Wide multi-lane roads with a median.** Use the openings provided, and keep to the lane for turning traffic. Do not turn across a closed median.

- **A junction with heavy scooter traffic.** Oncoming scooters tend to fill every gap, so waiting for a gap may take time. Be patient, and take a gap only when it is real.

- **A turn into a side street or alley.** Alley mouths are often narrow and hidden. Slow right down, and watch for vehicles and pedestrians coming out.

- **A turn at a roundabout or a junction with an island.** Follow the signs and the direction of travel. Our guide to [how to ride through a Hanoi roundabout](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-ride-through-a-hanoi-roundabout/) covers roundabouts.

- **A left turn at night or in rain.** Reduce speed further, use your low beam as Article 20 requires when changing direction at intersections and leave a longer gap. Our guide to [riding in Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) covers wet roads.

## U-turns are a separate decision

A U-turn is a change of direction too, and Article 15 bans it at pedestrian crossings, on bridges, at bridge ends, under overpasses, on underground roads, at road-railway intersections, on narrow roads, on slopes, on bends with obscured visibility, on expressways, in road tunnels and on one-way roads, unless a traffic guard or a temporary sign allows it. Do not assume that a U-turn is allowed because other riders make one. Use a marked opening or a junction where it is permitted.

## Safer alternatives to a difficult left turn

If a left turn looks too hard, you do not have to make it.

- **Go straight on,** and turn at the next junction with a light or an arrow.

- **Turn right, then right again,** and approach the destination from the other side. Three right turns around a block can replace one left turn, if the streets allow it.

- **Stop at the roadside** in a legal place, and walk across.

- **Use an opening or junction** provided for the turn.

- **Plan your route** so that it avoids unprotected left turns on the busiest roads. Our guide to [how to check if a navigation app suggests an expressway](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-check-if-a-navigation-app-suggests-an-expressway/) explains how to review a route before you start.

## Common mistakes

1. **Leaving the lane change until the junction.**

2. **Turning from the wrong lane.**

3. **Signalling late, or turning the signal off early.**

4. **Waiting with the wheel turned.**

5. **Looking at the first vehicle only.**

6. **Cutting the corner into the oncoming lane.**

7. **Finishing in the wrong lane.**

8. **Starting the turn on a closing light.**

9. **Making a left turn where a sign, a median or a marking does not allow it.**

## A quick checklist

1. **Check that the turn is allowed.**

2. **Move gradually into the left lane, signalling each move.**

3. **Slow down and keep the signal on.**

4. **Wait behind the line with the wheel straight.**

5. **Choose the gap, and look past the first vehicle.**

6. **Give way to oncoming traffic and pedestrians.**

7. **Turn smoothly, and finish in the right-hand lane.**

8. **If in doubt, go on.**

## FAQ: left turns on a motorbike

### Who must I give way to on a left turn?

Oncoming traffic, pedestrians and rudimentary vehicles such as bicycles (Article 15 of Law 36/2024/QH15).

### Which lane should I be in?

The lane closest to the direction of the turn, which for a left turn is next to the centre line or median. Move there gradually, before the junction.

### How long must I signal?

Throughout the change of direction. Article 15 says the signal must be used continuously.

### Can I turn left where there is a median?

Only through an opening that is provided for turning. Do not cross a closed median.

### What is the fine for turning where a sign prohibits it?

VND 600,000 to 800,000 under Decree 168/2024/ND-CP. Check the current text.

### What if the left turn looks too risky?

Go on and turn somewhere easier, make three right turns, or park and walk. A skipped turn is safer than a forced one.
