Chi Linh is a long day trip, not a quick spin: published sources put the Con Son and Kiep Bac heritage area about 80 km from Hanoi, so on a rented scooter or semi-automatic bike you should plan a full day with a daylight return. The reward is a quiet, hilly landscape of pine forest, Tran-dynasty temples and a pagoda set among maple trees, reached mostly on ordinary provincial roads once you leave the main highway. This guide treats Chi Linh as a region to ride around, not a single sight. It explains how the area is laid out, what the forest roads are like, how to pace the day, and which decisions to make before you leave Long Bien. Temple details and festival timing for the two best-known sites are covered separately, so here the emphasis is on the ride itself.

## Where Chi Linh is, and what it is called now

Chi Linh was a city in Hai Duong province. Since 1 July 2025 Hai Duong has been merged into an enlarged Hai Phong city, a change recorded in a World Bank project document and in Vietnamese government news. The official Con Son - Kiep Bac heritage website now describes Thanh Mai pagoda as lying in Nguyen Trai ward, Hai Phong city. Older maps, road signs, bus boards and travel blogs still say Chi Linh, Hai Duong, so expect both names. If a navigation app or a friendly local says "Hai Duong", you are in the right place.

The practical effect is small for a rider: roads, junctions and numbering did not move. But when you search for opening information, use the official heritage site rather than an old provincial page, and be ready for place names that differ between your map and the signs.

## The three zones that make up the area

Vietnam's Prime Minister approved a master plan in 2010 (Decision 920/QD-TTg) that divides the protected heritage area into three zones, and that division is a helpful way to plan a day:

| Zone | What it is | Riding character |
|---|---|---|
| Con Son (zone A) | Pagoda, stream, pine slopes and hilltop viewpoints | Quiet approach roads, short climbs, shaded |
| Kiep Bac (zone B) | Temple to the Tran general Tran Hung Dao beside the Luc Dau river | Flatter, busier on festival days |
| Phuong Hoang (zone C) | A smaller zone, listed in the plan after the other two | Less visited; ask locally before diverting |

Con Son and Kiep Bac are roughly 5 km apart according to the Vietnam National Authority of Tourism, so you can see both without a long transfer. Thanh Mai pagoda sits further out, on a mountainside north of the main sites, and is worth treating as its own stop rather than a quick add-on. UNESCO's nomination file lists the three at different coordinates, which matches the sense on the ground that this is a spread-out area, not one compound.

## Why Thanh Mai deserves its own slot

According to the heritage management board, Thanh Mai pagoda stands on a mountainside at about 200 m above sea level, surrounded by forest. It is known for its red maple trees, and the board says visitors can continue from the temple yard on a climb of about 3 km towards the summit, through maple and ironwood forest. A season of red leaves draws crowds, but the board's page does not give a date range, so do not plan a trip around a guess; check the heritage site or local news near the time.

Treat the climb as a walk, not part of the ride. Park, change out of anything you would not want to wear on a slope, and bring water. The first part is a gentle forest approach, but it is still a hill walk of several kilometres each way if you go to the summit, which can double your time at this stop. If you are short of daylight, visit the pagoda yard and skip the summit.

## Choosing roads: main route, forest roads and what to avoid

Two broad approaches are described by travel sources: east along National Highway 5 to the Hai Duong area and then north towards Chi Linh, or a route through Bac Ninh and Hai Duong on National Highway 18. I have not verified current road conditions on either, so open a map the evening before, choose a route that stays on ordinary roads, and check it against our guide to [which Hanoi highways allow motorbikes](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-ring-roads-highways-motorbikes-allowed/).

One firm rule applies on any route. Under Law 36/2024/QH15, motorcycles and mopeds are not allowed on expressways. The Hanoi to Hai Phong expressway runs through the same part of the country, and car-oriented navigation will sometimes push you toward it. Our note on [how to check whether a navigation app suggests an expressway](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-check-if-a-navigation-app-suggests-an-expressway/) explains how to catch this before you leave.

### On the hill and forest roads

Once you reach the heritage area, roads narrow, bend and rise. A few habits matter more here than on the flat delta:

- Slow before bends, not in them. Gravel and leaf litter collect at the edges.
- Expect the unexpected around temples: tour minibuses stopping, pilgrims walking, vendors pulling out.
- Use the engine to hold your speed downhill if you are on a semi-automatic bike. Our guide to [engine braking on a semi-automatic motorcycle](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/engine-braking-on-a-semi-automatic-motorcycle/) explains why this saves your brakes.
- Wet leaves and mud are slippery. If rain starts, wait it out; see [riding in Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) for how rain changes grip and visibility.

## Which bike suits the trip

For a ride of this length, a modest bike you know well is more useful than a powerful one. A semi-automatic Honda Wave or Yamaha Sirius is light and fuel-efficient and suits gentle hills; an automatic scooter is easier in stop-and-go traffic on the way out. The rolling terrain in the heritage area is not steep, but if you plan the maple-forest climb or hill detours, read our guide to [small-engine manual bikes on rural roads near Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/small-engine-manual-bikes-for-rural-roads-near-hanoi/) first. A 50cc scooter or an electric model is a poor match for a day of this length, because of speed and range.

Whichever bike you take, do the [ten-minute scooter inspection](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-scooter-inspection-checklist/) before leaving the shop, with particular attention to tyres, brakes and lights.

## A realistic day plan

Start early, since the shop opens at 09:00 and daylight is your limit on the way back. A plan that works, with times as a planning allowance and not a measured schedule:

1. **Depart in the morning.** Allow roughly two hours for the outward ride, plus fuel and a break. Distances of about 80 km on mixed roads rarely go as fast as a map suggests.
2. **Head first to the site that needs the most time.** If Thanh Mai's trail interests you, go there first while you are fresh.
3. **Eat locally and rest in the shade.** Pine forest is a good place to sit out the midday heat.
4. **See Con Son, then Kiep Bac,** a short ride apart.
5. **Leave with enough light.** Aim to be back on the main road by mid-afternoon, not at dusk. Our guide to [riding at night in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-at-night-in-hanoi/) explains why a late return is the weakest part of a day trip.

Remember that the shop is open 09:00 to 21:00 daily, and that a late return has its own cost: the charge is VND 30,000 per hour for one to five hours late, and from six hours late it becomes a further rental day. Our [guide to returning a rental bike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/returning-your-rental-motorbike-hanoi/) lists the checks at the end.

## Fuel, food and breakdown planning

Fuel stations are frequent along the main highways, but they thin out on side roads. Fill up before you leave the main road for the hills, and read [when to refuel on a day trip](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/when-to-refuel-a-manual-motorbike-during-a-day-trip/) for a simple rule of thumb. Food stalls cluster near the main sites. Carry water, a charged phone and the shop's number.

## When not to go

Skip Chi Linh on a day with heavy rain, or if you are tired, or if the only time left is the afternoon. Festival days around the 8th lunar month and the first lunar month bring large crowds to the heritage area, which means slow roads and crowded car parks. If you cannot start by mid-morning, pick a nearer destination from the [Nguyen Ha travel guides](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/guides/) instead, or go by bus and enjoy the sights without the ride.

## FAQ: Hanoi to Chi Linh by motorbike

### How far is Chi Linh from Hanoi?

Vietnamese and English-language tourism sources put the Con Son - Kiep Bac area about 80 km from Hanoi. Treat that as an approximate road distance and check your own route.

### Is Chi Linh still in Hai Duong?

Administratively, no. Hai Duong merged into Hai Phong city from 1 July 2025, and the official heritage site now describes Thanh Mai pagoda as being in Hai Phong city. Signs and older maps still say Chi Linh and Hai Duong.

### Can I ride a motorbike on the expressway to get there?

No. Law 36/2024/QH15 bars motorcycles and mopeds from expressways. Stay on ordinary roads.

### Is it better to see Thanh Mai or Con Son first?

Whichever needs more time. Thanh Mai has a forest climb of about 3 km beyond the pagoda yard, so go there first if you want it.

### Can I do it as a day trip from Hanoi?

Yes, if you start in the morning and leave with daylight. Do not plan it for the afternoon.

### Do I need the shop's permission?

Yes. Tell the shop where you are going. See the [rental guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-guide/) and the [prices page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/prices/) for general terms.
