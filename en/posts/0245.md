If a rented electric scooter will not power on, stop in a safe place, switch the key fully off, wait a few seconds and try again, then check the simple things in a fixed order: the key position, the battery switch under the seat, the side stand, the brake lever, the charging cable and the charge level. Many "dead" scooters are actually awake but refusing to move, because electric models are built to cut power when the side stand is down, when the throttle is switched to parking mode or when a charger is plugged in. If two or three minutes of checking change nothing, do not take anything apart: call the rental shop, say where you are and let a mechanic decide. This guide gives that order, explains what each step does and lists what a renter should never attempt.

## Safe first: where you are matters more than the fault

Before touching any switch, think about where the scooter has stopped. A scooter that goes dead while you are riding is a different situation from one that will not wake up at a parking spot.

- **If it died while moving,** coast to the kerb or into a side street, check mirrors, and stop clear of the traffic lane. Electric scooters are quiet, so other riders may not realise you have slowed down.
- **If it will not start at a hotel or cafe,** you are in a good position. Take your time, and keep the helmet on or beside you.
- **If it is raining or the street is flooded,** move the scooter to dry, higher ground before trying anything. The VinFast Evo 200 owner's manual warns that riding in storms and floods is dangerous and can damage the vehicle's electrical system, and the manufacturer's warranty terms list flood damage among the exclusions. Our guide to [riding in Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) covers which streets tend to flood.

A note on sources: the step-by-step guidance below comes from VinFast's Evo 200 and Motio owner's manuals. Those are different models from whatever scooter you may be handed, and layouts differ, so treat this as a typical pattern and ask the shop about your own bike.

## Step 1: check what "off" and "on" actually mean on this bike

Electric scooters usually have a key (or a smart key) with several positions. The Evo 200 manual describes turning the key clockwise to the ON position, and says you may hear a click as the battery switches on. Some models also have a position that unlocks the seat and one that locks the steering.

Check these in order:

1. **Is the key fully in the ON position?** A key left between positions or a steering lock that has not released can look like a dead scooter.
2. **Is the handlebar lock released?** Gently turn the bars left and right as you turn the key, instead of forcing either.
3. **Does the dashboard show anything?** A lit display means the main system is awake and the problem is probably in the next steps.
4. **Do you hear the click or a start-up tone?** Silence plus a dark display is a different clue from a lit display with no movement.

Do not hold the key at a position for long, and do not try to force a stiff lock. A bent or jammed key is a shop job.

## Step 2: the battery switch under the seat

This is the step most renters never hear about. The Evo 200 manual's "vehicle will not start" section says that if you switch on and nothing responds, you should turn the key off, switch the battery switch in the storage compartment under the seat off and on, then try again, and contact technical support if that does not work. A VinFast page for another model, the Feliz S, also lists the battery switch among the pre-ride checks and says it should be in the ON position.

For a rental, ask at handover:

- Does this scooter have a battery switch, and where is it?
- Should it be left on during normal use?
- Is it all right for me to toggle it if the scooter does not respond?

If the shop has told you it is fine, switching it off and on once is a reasonable test. If the shop has not, call before opening anything. Never open or move the battery itself.

## Step 3: "on" is not "ready" - the safety cut-outs

If the display lights up but the scooter will not move when you turn the throttle, the cause is often a deliberate lock-out, not a fault. The VinFast manuals describe several.

### Parking mode

Some models have a button that switches the throttle off while the scooter is stopped. The Evo 200 manual says that when you hold the brake and press the button once, the parking indicator lights, the electric motor is switched off and will not respond until it is switched on again; the rest of the electrical system stays awake. That is why a scooter in parking mode shows a lit dashboard and does nothing. Its troubleshooting page for "throttle turns but the scooter does not move" begins by telling you to check the parking and brake indicators on the display.

The usual way out, from the manual's start-up sequence, is: unlock the scooter, flip the side stand up, leave parking mode, release the brake and then turn the throttle slowly. Ask the shop how your model leaves parking mode, because the button location differs.

### Side stand

The manuals for the Evo 200 and Motio say the side stand sensor works with the stand to cut propulsion if the stand is down, and that the rider cannot control the motor until the stand is fully up. A stand that has not fully folded, or one that is stiff, can keep a scooter dead. Kick it up firmly with your foot while someone holds the bike upright, or hold the scooter upright yourself, and try again.

### Brake lever

On many electric scooters, squeezing a brake lever cuts the motor. If a lever is sticking, or something such as a bag strap is pressing against it, the throttle may do nothing. Look at both levers and make sure each returns fully.

### Charger still connected

The Evo 200 manual says to check that the charger has been unplugged, and adds that the throttle and motor do not work while the scooter is charging. If you have been charging in a hotel, make sure the cable is out of the scooter, not just the wall.

## Step 4: the charge level and heat

Check the battery level on the display or on the battery gauge, if the model has one. A battery at or near empty can leave the scooter unable to start, and the Evo 200 error list includes battery-management protections for over-temperature and too-low temperature while charging, so a battery that is very hot or very cold may not behave normally.

Other things to remember:

- **Heat:** the VinFast warranty page lists battery swelling, cracking or smoke caused by parking in high-temperature conditions as an exclusion, which is a good reminder to park in shade and not leave a scooter baking in direct sun on a summer afternoon.
- **A charge that has just finished** will show a high level, so a scooter that was fully charged this morning is unlikely to have an empty battery.
- **If it was charging overnight and will not start,** check that the charging cable did not loosen and that the charger indicator was on.

## Step 5: read the display

Modern electric scooters can show a fault icon or number. The Evo 200 manual's first troubleshooting advice is to read the error code on the display, use the phone app to read the fault and get guidance, and take the vehicle to an authorised VinFast dealer or service workshop. Its code list includes battery-management protections (for example over-current on discharge, over-temperature or too-low temperature when charging) and loss of signal between the display and the controller.

For a renter, that means:

- **Take a photo of the display** showing the icon or number.
- **Do not try to clear it** with random combinations of buttons.
- **Send the photo to the shop** when you call.

## Quick reference

| What you see | Likely check | Who fixes it |
|---|---|---|
| Dark display, no click | Key position, battery switch under the seat | You, if the shop said it is fine; otherwise the shop |
| Lit display, throttle does nothing | Parking indicator, side stand, brake lever, charger cable | You |
| Display shows a warning icon or code | Photo of the code | The shop |
| Starts, then cuts out | Side stand, low charge, loose connection | The shop |
| Wet scooter, nothing works | Move it to dry ground, do not keep trying | The shop |
| Smell of burning, smoke, swollen battery | Move away and call for help | Emergency services and the shop |

## What a renter should not do

- **Do not open the battery compartment beyond the seat storage,** or touch battery terminals.
- **Do not jump-start the scooter** or connect any other charger or device to it.
- **Do not wash it with a hose** to "reset" it. The Evo 200 manual says not to use high-pressure water for cleaning and not to spray water directly under the seat.
- **Do not keep retrying for ten minutes.** Repeated key cycling is unlikely to fix a real fault, and a mechanic needs to know what you tried.
- **Do not force a stiff steering lock, seat latch or stand.** Forcing parts can turn a small problem into a repair.

A smell of burning, smoke or visible swelling is a different category. Move away from the scooter and call emergency services if there is a fire risk, then the shop.

## Prevent it at handover

Most of this advice can be put to use before you leave the shop:

- Have the staff turn the scooter on and off in front of you, including leaving parking mode.
- Ask where the battery switch is and whether it should be on.
- Confirm the battery level and charging arrangements. The shop confirms battery level and charging at handover.
- Test the side stand and both brake levers.
- Photograph the display at start-up.
- Save the shop number in your phone.

## FAQ: electric scooter will not power on

### Is a scooter that lights up but does not move broken?

Not necessarily. Parking mode, a lowered side stand, a brake lever signal or a plugged-in charger can all stop the motor while the display stays on.

### Should I switch the battery switch off and on?

The Evo 200 manual says to do this after switching the key off, then try again. Ask the shop whether your scooter has such a switch before you touch it.

### Can I push the scooter to the shop?

Ask first. Pushing a heavy scooter along a road is tiring and may not be safe, and the shop may prefer to send help to your location.

### Will rain stop it from starting?

Possibly. Manufacturers warn against riding in floods because water can damage the electrical system. Move to dry ground and call the shop.

### Do I pay for the call-out?

It depends on the cause and on your contract. Read the breakdown clause before you sign, and ask the shop.
