On a lightweight 50cc scooter, check four things on each tyre before you ride: the surface for cracks, cuts, bulges and embedded objects, the tread for the wear indicator, the pressure against the label on the bike and the age of the tyre from the date code on the sidewall. Then look at the wheel itself for dents, a loose valve and wobble. Honda's guidance is that tyres do four jobs at once, carrying the bike's weight, absorbing bumps, steering and transferring braking and driving force, so a poor tyre affects everything. A 50cc scooter has small wheels and little weight, which leaves it sensitive to potholes and to low pressure on Hanoi's uneven surfaces. This guide explains each check in plain terms, notes what is general knowledge rather than a manufacturer figure and tells you what to say to the shop. It is not a mechanic's inspection.

## Why tyres matter more on a small scooter

Honda describes the tyre as the only part of the bike in contact with the ground when running, stopping or turning. On a 50cc scooter that contact patch is small. The wheels are usually smaller in diameter than those on a 110cc scooter, which is general knowledge and varies by model. A small wheel drops into holes and meets the edge of a bump more sharply than a large one, so the tyre, the rim and the suspension take a bigger share of every impact.

Honda's pre-ride advice also says that worn tyres slip easily in rain and lengthen the stopping distance, and that wrong pressure shortens a tyre's life. In Hanoi's rainy months a worn tyre on a lightweight bike is a real problem, so the check is worth doing carefully.

## Check 1: the surface

Walk around each tyre and look at the entire circumference. Honda's pre-ride inspection advice lists what to look for: cracks, damage, nails, stones or other objects stuck in the rubber. Also look at the sidewalls, which are the sides of the tyre, for cuts, bulges or deep cracks. Honda's owner's manual wording for the Super Cub 125 warns about cuts, slits or cracks that expose fabric or cords, and about bumps or bulges in the sidewalls.

Honda's article adds that you should check the tyres after any contact with a kerb or a large bump, because unattended damage raises the risk of a tyre bursting while riding. As a renter, you are checking the tyres in the condition they were handed over, so be specific. Take a clear photograph of any cut, crack or patch you find, and ask the shop what it is.

## Check 2: tread and the wear indicator

Honda says to check tread depth by looking for the wear indicator, often called a wear bar. Tyres commonly carry a small triangle on the sidewall that marks where the indicator sits in the tread. Honda's maintenance page explains that the grooves are very shallow at the point marked with the triangle, and that this is the place to check wear. If the bar is visible, replace the tyre immediately, Honda says.

Look also for abnormal wear. A tyre worn flat in the middle, worn on one edge or patchy suggests wrong pressure, misalignment or a hard life, and Honda notes that extremely worn areas shorten tyre life and bring vibration and noise. A tyre with almost no tread pattern left is worn out, whatever its age.

Note that Honda's daily-inspection advice says a minimum tread depth is specified for models with engines of 126cc or larger, and that other models should follow the owner's manual. For a 50cc bike, therefore, rely on the wear indicator and on common sense, and ask the shop if you are unsure.

## Check 3: pressure

Honda says pressure falls gradually over time even when the bike is not used, and recommends checking it with a gauge at least once a month. Honda also advises checking when the tyres are cool, because warm tyres can show a higher reading than the true one. The right pressure is different for each bike, and is often on a label on the frame or on the chain guard, and in the owner's manual.

For a rental, this gives you a simple routine:

1. Look for the pressure label on the bike and note the figure for the front and rear.
2. Ask the shop to check the tyres with a gauge, or to show you the bike's pressure.
3. Squeeze the tyres by hand as a rough guide only. It is easy to misjudge pressure by eye, as Honda notes, and a tyre that looks firm may be low.
4. If the bike will carry two people, ask whether the label gives a different rear figure for a passenger.

We do not quote a pressure here, since it depends on the model and the tyre, and the label on your bike is the authority. ## Check 4: how old the tyre is

Rubber ages even when a tyre is hardly used. Honda's pre-ride inspection article says older tyres are at greater risk of bursting at extreme road temperatures, and that tyres in use for more than five years should be given periodic inspections by a dealer. Most tyres carry a DOT code on the sidewall, and the last four digits give the week and year of manufacture. The US safety regulator explains that the last four digits show when the tyre was made: for example, 0109 means the first week of 2009. The full code may appear on one sidewall only, so check both sides.

Not every small-scooter tyre carries a DOT code, because the markings depend on where the tyre was made and sold. If you cannot find a date, that is not a reason to panic, but it does mean you should rely more on the condition of the rubber. Fine cracking on the sidewall or in the tread grooves, a hard and shiny surface and a tyre that looks dry all suggest age. Describe what you see and ask the shop how long the tyres have been on the bike.

## Check 5: the wheel, valve and bearings

The tyre sits on a wheel, and the wheel deserves a quick look. With the bike on its stand if possible:

- **Rim.** Look for dents and bends, especially on the side where kerbs and potholes strike. A dent can make the tyre lose air slowly.
- **Valve.** Check that the valve stem is straight and has its cap. A missing cap lets dirt in and is a common source of slow leaks.
- **Spokes or cast wheel.** On a spoked wheel, press a few spokes to see whether any are loose. A cast wheel should have no cracks.
- **Wobble.** Spin each wheel by hand, if the stand lets you, and watch the tyre against a fixed point. A wobble from side to side or a hop up and down points to a bent rim or a bad tyre.
- **Play.** Hold the wheel at the top and bottom and rock it. Noticeable knocking can mean worn bearings, which a mechanic should see.

These are general checks and not manufacturer procedures.

## FAQ: 50cc tyres

### How often should tyre pressure be checked?

Honda recommends a gauge check at least once a month, with cold tyres. On a rental, ask for a check at handover.

### What is the triangle on the tyre?

It marks the position of the tread wear indicator. If the bar in the groove is visible, the tyre is worn out.

### How do I find a tyre's age?

Look for the DOT code. The last four digits show the week and year of manufacture. It may be on one side only.

### Is a small crack in the sidewall acceptable?

Honda says to stop using a tyre with cracks or scratches and to consult a dealer. Photograph the crack and ask the shop.

### Do lightweight bikes need less pressure?

Each bike has its own specified pressure, so use the label on your bike.

### Can I top up the air myself?

Ask the shop. Wrong pressure affects handling, and the shop knows the correct figure.
