In Hanoi's hot season, a motorbike day trip works if you ride in two short blocks and rest in the shade in between. Aim to be moving from the shop's 09:00 opening until about 11:00, stop somewhere cool through the middle of the day, and ride again in the late afternoon only if the sky is clear of storms. Drink water regularly, wear light long clothing and a ventilated helmet, and choose destinations with shade, lakes or higher ground rather than long open highways. Health agencies and Hanoi's authorities advise avoiding strenuous outdoor activity at the hottest hours, and a rider sits in the sun on a hot machine with no roof. This guide covers how hot it actually gets, how to shape the day, what to carry, which signs of heat illness to act on, how afternoon storms fit in, and how to look after the bike and where you park it. It is general advice, not medical guidance.

## How hot the hot season gets

Hanoi's hot months run roughly from May to August, with the worst heat spells often in late spring and early summer. Recent figures show why the season deserves planning.

- On 25 May 2026 Hanoi reached 40.7 degrees Celsius at the Lang station, according to the National Center for Hydro-Meteorological Forecasting, as reported by VnExpress International. That figure is the air temperature in a meteorological shelter about two metres above ground.
- The same report says actual outdoor temperatures, especially over concrete and asphalt and in dense built-up areas, run two to four degrees higher.
- VnExpress also reported that in many areas the sun shone hard from about 9 am to 7 pm, with overnight lows of 30 to 31 degrees, so there was little relief at night.
- Forecasters expected one or two more widespread heat waves in June 2026, with northern highs of 35 to 36 degrees and above 37 in places, and described 35 degrees as the threshold for a heat wave.
- A local tourism guide lists July as Hanoi's hottest and wettest month, with temperatures of about 26 to 34 degrees and about 285 mm of rain.

These are 2026 observations, and they will differ from year to year. The practical lesson is that a hot-season midday can be harsher than a forecast number suggests.

## Why riders feel more heat than the forecast

A forecast is measured in the shade. A rider is not in the shade. Three effects stack up.

- **Sun and road surface.** Asphalt and concrete radiate heat, which is why VnExpress notes street temperatures two to four degrees above the shelter figure.
- **Perceived temperature.** The World Health Organization notes that perceived temperatures in the sun can be 10 to 15 degrees higher than the air temperature.
- **Equipment and idling.** A helmet traps heat, and sitting in slow traffic with the engine running keeps you in the hot air coming off the road and other vehicles.

Moving air feels cooling, which can mislead you into thinking you are fine. Sweat dries quickly in the wind, so you may not notice how much water you are losing.

## Shape the day: ride, rest, ride

The standard advice from health bodies is to avoid strenuous activity and the sun at the hottest time of day. Vietnamese news reports on the May 2026 heat wave quoted authorities advising people to avoid outdoor activity from 11 am to 4 pm where possible. A rented motorbike day usually starts at 09:00, so the simplest shape is:

| Time | What to do |
|---|---|
| 09:00 to about 11:00 | Collect the bike, fuel up, ride the main outward leg while the sun is lower |
| About 11:00 to 15:00 or 16:00 | Rest in shade or an air-conditioned cafe, eat, drink, and let the worst heat pass |
| After about 16:00 | Ride the return or a short second loop if the sky is clear |
| Before dark | Be back on familiar roads |

These times are a template, not a fixed rule. If the forecast shows a heat warning, shorten the ride or choose a day with milder weather. Our guide to [planning breaks on a long semi-automatic bike ride](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-plan-breaks-on-a-long-semi-automatic-bike-ride/) gives a sample rhythm, and the long summer days, more than 13 hours of daylight according to timeanddate.com, mean you can still finish in the light even after a long midday stop.

## Pick destinations that cope with heat

Choose places that give shade, water or elevation, and treat long, shadeless roads with caution.

- **Lakeside and river rides.** A loop around West Lake has trees and cafes, and the Red River bank near the shop has open breezes. See our guides to [Tay Ho by scooter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/tay-ho-west-lake-by-scooter/) and [Long Bien and the Red River](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/long-bien-red-river-first-ride/).
- **Hill and park trips.** Higher ground is generally cooler than the delta, which is why people head to Tam Dao and Ba Vi in hot weather, but check the forecast and note that hill roads can bring afternoon thunderstorms. See [Hanoi to Tam Dao](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-to-tam-dao-by-motorbike/) and [Hanoi to Ba Vi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-to-ba-vi-by-motorbike/).
- **Shorter village loops.** Craft villages on the east bank give a short run and plenty of shade. See [Gia Lam and Bat Trang by scooter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/gia-lam-east-bank-bat-trang-by-scooter/).
- **Be careful with long highway runs.** A full-day trip to a distant place such as [Ninh Binh](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-ninh-binh-one-day-motorbike-itinerary/) means many hours in the sun, so be strict about breaks or split it over two days.

If a place has no shade or water, you will need to bring your own rest stop, which makes the trip harder.

## Water, food and clothing

The World Health Organization advises drinking water regularly, about one cup per hour and at least two to three litres a day, and keeping out of the sun at the hottest time. The US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention advises drinking plenty of non-alcoholic fluids, wearing a hat and sunglasses, loose lightweight light-coloured clothing, and sunscreen of SPF 15 or higher reapplied about every two hours. It also recommends sports drinks with electrolytes if you show signs of heat exhaustion.

For a rider, that translates into:

- **Carry water,** at least a litre per person, in a bag or the under-seat storage, and top up at every stop. Do not wait until you are thirsty.
- **Wear light, loose, long sleeves and trousers.** Covering skin protects it from sunburn and from wind heat, and light colours reflect sun.
- **Choose a ventilated helmet that fits.** One basic helmet per rider is included, and you may bring your own. Never remove it to cool down while riding. Our guide to [choosing a helmet that fits](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/choosing-a-helmet-that-fits-for-a-manual-motorbike-journey/) explains fit, and [helmet rules in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/helmet-rules-in-vietnam/) explains what riders and passengers must wear.
- **Wear closed shoes** if you can. Sandals are comfortable in heat but offer little protection. See [riding in sandals](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-a-semi-automatic-bike-in-sandals-practical-safety-risks/).
- **Use sunscreen and sunglasses,** with a neck cover or buff if you burn easily.
- **Eat light meals** at rest stops, and avoid a heavy meal just before riding.

## Heat illness: what to watch for and what to do

The CDC describes heat exhaustion as a mild heat illness with excessive thirst, heavy sweating, headache, dizziness or confusion and nausea. It advises getting out of the sun at once, cooling down with a fan, air conditioning or cool water, and drinking cool non-alcoholic drinks. Heat stroke is more serious, with lack of sweating, very hot skin and loss of consciousness, and the CDC calls it a medical emergency.

| Signs | What to do |
|---|---|
| Heavy sweating, thirst, headache | Stop riding, move into shade, drink, cool the neck and wrists |
| Dizziness, confusion, nausea | Stop, lie down in a cool place, drink, do not resume riding until recovered |
| Hot dry skin, no sweating, fainting | Treat as an emergency: call local emergency services and cool the person while waiting |

The World Health Organization notes that people over 65 and those with heart, lung or kidney conditions are more vulnerable, and the CDC lists the elderly and people with high blood pressure among those at highest risk of heat exhaustion. If you or a pillion have a health condition, a hot-season day trip may not be worth the risk. For children, our guide to [child passengers on a motorbike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/child-passengers-motorbike-vietnam/) explains the safer choices.

If you feel unwell, stop. A late return costs less than a collapse on the road.

## Afternoon storms after the heat

Hot days often end in storms. A national forecast for 9 to 19 August 2026, reported by Vietnam.vn, described hot days across the north with showers and thunderstorms, lightning and hail in the late afternoon and evening, and advised people to watch the weather and shelter at the first sign of a storm. VnExpress also noted that when a heat wave breaks, the clash of hot air and weak cold fronts can bring thunderstorms, lightning and damaging gusts.

For a rider, that means:

- **Check the sky from mid-afternoon,** and watch for towering dark clouds.
- **Start the return earlier** if cloud builds in the west.
- **Carry your own raincoat.** The shop does not include a raincoat for short rentals.
- **If thunder starts,** the US National Weather Service advises getting into a safe building and waiting 30 minutes after the last thunder.

## The bike and where you park it

Heat affects the bike too.

- **Engine.** Honda's Wave Alpha 110 has an air-cooled engine, according to Honda Vietnam's specification page, so it relies on airflow and a healthy oil level. Ask the shop to confirm the oil level, since Honda's manual includes an engine oil check in its maintenance section.
- **Tyres.** Ask the shop to confirm the tyre pressure for your load, and look for cracks or bulges before you leave.
- **Parking.** Park in shade whenever you can, because a seat in full sun can be too hot to sit on. Lock the steering and use a guarded place, since the shop does not provide an extra lock.
- **Slow traffic.** An air-cooled engine idling in a jam is working hard, so avoid midday congestion.
- **Fuel.** Fill before you leave and top up at about half a tank. See [fuel in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/fuel-in-hanoi-petrol-stations-rental-scooter/).

## A hot-day checklist

- Forecast checked for heat warnings and storms
- Midday rest booked or identified
- At least a litre of water each, and a plan to top up
- Light long clothing, ventilated helmet, sunscreen and sunglasses
- Raincoat in the bag
- Fuel and oil confirmed
- A shaded, guarded parking plan
- Back-by time set before dark

## FAQ: hot-season day trips

### What time should I ride in the hot season?

Ride in the morning and the late afternoon, and rest in the shade in the middle of the day.

### How much water should I drink?

The WHO suggests about one cup an hour and at least two to three litres a day. Carry plenty and drink regularly.

### Is it safe to ride when it is 40 degrees?

It is risky, and authorities advise avoiding outdoor activity at the peak. Consider staying in the city or choosing another day.

### What should I wear?

Light, loose, long clothing, a ventilated helmet that fits, closed shoes and sunscreen.

### What if I feel dizzy?

Stop in the shade, drink and cool down. If you stop sweating or feel confused, treat it as an emergency.

### Do I need a raincoat in the hot season?

Carry one, because storms are common after hot days. The shop does not include a raincoat for short rentals.
