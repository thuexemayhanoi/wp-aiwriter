The easiest way to do Co Loa from Hanoi is as half of a history day: ride out to the ancient citadel in Dong Anh in the morning, come back into the city at lunchtime and finish at the Imperial Citadel of Thang Long, whose official site lists daily opening from 8:00 to 17:00. The two places are two different capitals of Vietnam, roughly twelve centuries apart, and seeing them on the same day makes the story click. Co Loa lies about 17 to 20 kilometres north of the centre by published figures, so the ride out is a short one, and most of the visit is on foot among temples, a lake and earth ramparts. This guide gives the timeline, the order of the day, the road and the pacing, plus what to check on the day.

## The idea: two capitals in one day

Co Loa was the capital of the kingdom of Au Lac in the third century BC, according to tradition, and again in the tenth century, when Ngo Quyen set up his court there. Thang Long, today's Hanoi, became the capital in 1010 under the Ly dynasty. A rider who visits both gets the beginning and the middle of the story of the city, and the order matters: Co Loa first, then Thang Long.

Here is the timeline in short.

| Period | What happened | Where to see it |
|---|---|---|
| Third century BC, by tradition | Thuc Phan, King An Duong Vuong, makes Co Loa the capital of Au Lac | Co Loa ramparts and temple |
| Tenth century | Ngo Quyen sets up his capital at Co Loa, restoring a national centre after more than a thousand years of domination | Co Loa |
| 1010 | The Ly dynasty sets up the capital at Dai La, later called Thang Long | Imperial Citadel of Thang Long |

The dates for the earliest period vary in the sources, from 257 BCE to 208 BCE, because the story mixes legend with archaeology, so treat the exact year as approximate.

## Why do it as a loop

Most day trips out of Hanoi end with a long ride home and nothing after. This one ends in the city, in the afternoon, when the Co Loa countryside has become hot and the ride back is short. It also avoids the problem of arriving at Thang Long just before it closes, since you can start the second half after a long lunch.

If you only have a short window, do Co Loa alone and skip the afternoon. If this is your first day on the bike, the [Long Bien and Red River first ride](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/long-bien-red-river-first-ride/) is a gentler outing to start with. This guide is for a full day.

## Getting to Co Loa

VOV describes one route: follow Highway 1A, cross the Duong River bridge, turn left at Yen Vien town onto National Highway 3 and ride about 5 kilometres to the citadel. Use it as a guide, not a script, and check it against a map app on the day.

Two rules for the road:

- **Stay off expressways.** Article 25 of Law 36/2024/QH15, in force since 1 January 2025, says motorcycles, mopeds and similar vehicles may not use expressways. Our guide to [checking whether a navigation app suggests an expressway](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-check-if-a-navigation-app-suggests-an-expressway/) explains how to catch a bad route before it starts.
- **Follow officers' signals.** Under Article 11 of the same law, the signals of a person directing traffic come before lights and signs.

If you start from the east bank, the ride is shorter. The shop is near Gia Lam bus station, in Long Bien District, and our [Gia Lam page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/gia-lam/) and [Long Bien page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/long-bien/) describe the area.

## Morning: Co Loa, in the order that makes sense

### Understand the layout first

VietNamNet describes three surviving rings: an outer one with a perimeter of 8 kilometres, a middle one of 6.5 kilometres and an inner one of 1.6 kilometres. Vietnam's national tourism authority says today's walls average 4 to 5 metres in height, reach 12 metres in places, and have bases 20 to 30 metres wide. Sources disagree about the area covered, with figures of about 500 and 830 hectares, so do not rely on a single number.

You will not see the whole citadel in one go. It is spread across three communes, and what a visitor sees is mostly a village with temples set among earth banks and lakes.

### The village entrance and the communal house

VietNamNet's 2022 description says the village entrance is the entrance of the citadel, and that the communal house keeps a horizontal lacquer board linked to the legend of a meeting palace. Start here for context.

### Princess My Chau's temple

Close to the communal house, a small temple honours Princess My Chau, the king's daughter, according to the same article. It sits under an old banyan root, and a headless stone figure there is said by local people to represent her. The story is bound up with the magic crossbow legend, in which the princess's husband Trong Thuy takes the crossbow's trigger and the citadel falls.

### An Duong Vuong's temple and the well

The main temple stands on the hill that is said to have held the king's palace. VOV says it was built in 1687 under King Le Hi Tong and restored in 1893. In front of it, a semicircular lake holds the Trong Thuy and My Chau well. Vietnam's tourism authority says its water looks turbid red against the blue lake, and legend has it that pearls washed in it become brighter. A bronze statue of the king stands inside, and archaeological finds from the citadel are displayed there, including bronze arrows and spears.

### Cao Lo temple

The temple to general Cao Lo, the inventor of the multiple-shot crossbow in the legend, stands with a statue of him in the middle of a lake in front of it, according to VOV. It is a quick stop and a good photo.

Give the morning two to three hours at a walking pace. We cannot confirm opening hours or any entrance charges for the temples, so check on the day. The temples are working places of worship, so dress modestly and keep your voice down.

## Lunch and the ride back

Stop for lunch before you leave Dong Anh or on the way back. Small restaurants near the site and along the main road serve simple meals, but opening hours and prices change, so ask before you order.

Plan to leave Co Loa by about 11:30 to 12:00. That leaves time for lunch and the ride into the city, and it keeps you out of the worst of the afternoon heat.

## Afternoon: the Imperial Citadel of Thang Long

The Imperial Citadel of Thang Long, at 19C Hoang Dieu Street in Ba Dinh, lists on its official site that it is open from 8:00 to 17:00 on all days of the week. The same official site also hosts pages about Co Loa and its people, which is a useful reminder that the two sites are presented as parts of one story.

This is the part of the day to be careful with time. If you arrive at two in the afternoon, you have about three hours, which is enough for the main areas at an easy pace. We would not count on arriving after 4 p.m. We cannot confirm the last entry time or the ticket price, so check the official site or ring the visitor line it lists.

## When not to do this loop

- **During the Co Loa festival.** VietnamPlus says villagers hold it on the sixth day of the first lunar month. Crowds and parking make the visit harder and the loop longer.
- **After heavy rain.** Lake paths and village lanes turn muddy.
- **On a very hot afternoon.** You may already be tired from the morning.
- **If you have only half a day.** Do one of the two sites properly.

## FAQ: Co Loa and Thang Long in one day

### How far is Co Loa from central Hanoi?

Published figures range from about 17 to 20 kilometres north of the centre, depending on the starting point.

### Is a day enough for both sites?

Yes, if you split them as above: Co Loa in the morning and Thang Long after lunch.

### How long should I spend at Co Loa?

Two to three hours on foot covers the main temples, the lake and the well at an easy pace.

### Can I ride a scooter on the expressway to save time?

No. Law 36/2024/QH15 keeps motorcycles off expressways.

### What time does the Imperial Citadel of Thang Long close?

Its official site lists opening from 8:00 to 17:00 every day. Confirm the last entry on the day.

### Who was Ngo Quyen?

The founder of the Ngo dynasty, who set up his capital at Co Loa in the tenth century, according to the official Thang Long site.
