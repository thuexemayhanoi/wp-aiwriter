Hanoi's Ceramic Mosaic Mural is a long wall of ceramic tile pictures along the Red River dike, made for the city's 1,000th anniversary in 2010 and recognised by Guinness World Records as the world's largest ceramic mosaic. It is free, always open and spread over several busy roads, so the best way to see it from a rented scooter is to ride to one stretch, park in a guarded place and walk a kilometre or two, rather than trying to take in the whole wall from the saddle. This guide explains what the mural is and why quoted lengths differ, which streets it follows, how to read the sections, what condition to expect, and how to build a relaxed half-day around it from the east bank.

## What the mural is

The idea came from the journalist and artist Nguyen Thu Thuy, who won a Hanoi architecture contest with a proposal to turn the dike wall into a ceramic mosaic. According to Wikipedia, decoration began in 2007 and the work was meant to be ready for the Millennial Anniversary of Hanoi in October 2010. The Ford Foundation funded the first 450 metres and supported the planning and training that followed.

The tiles are ceramic tesserae from Bat Trang, the pottery village on the east bank, and the designs draw on successive periods of Vietnamese history: Phung Nguyen, Dong Son, Ly, Tran, Le and Nguyen. Between them you will find modern artworks, paintings of Hanoi and children's drawings. A Dan Tri report from the opening says artists from around the world sent tiles, with nearly 100 ceramic artists and masters from countries including Hungary, Mexico, Brazil, Argentina, the United States and Taiwan contributing. It describes designs that include the architecture of Vietnam's 54 ethnic groups.

On 5 October 2010 a Guinness World Records adjudicator reviewed the mural and confirmed 3,850 linear metres and 6,950 square metres as the world's largest ceramic mosaic. A Chilean artist completed a new segment on 18 September 2017, so it has never been entirely frozen in time.

## Why the length is quoted so differently

You will see three figures and none of them is wrong. The Guinness-certified length is 3,850 metres of mosaic. Lonely Planet describes it as spanning almost 4 km. Wikipedia opens with about 6.5 km, which describes the longer dike system the project was developed along, and then gives the 3,850 metre figure for the mosaic itself. Other travel pages quote 4 km or 6.5 km.

The practical point: you will not walk the whole thing in one go, and it is not a single continuous wall at street level. The mosaic is interrupted by junctions, gates, steps and stretches where the dike wall is bare or damaged. Think of it as an art trail you can join and leave anywhere, not a monument with a start and finish line.

## Which streets it follows

Wikipedia lists the decorated dike wall as running along Au Co, Nghi Tam, Yen Phu, Tran Nhat Duat, Tran Quang Khai and Tran Khanh Du, ending at the pier of Long Bien Bridge. Another summary places the mural's start at Tran Khanh Du and its end at Nghi Tam. That puts one end close to Long Bien Bridge and the other towards the Tay Ho side of the city.

| Stretch | What a visitor can expect (from published descriptions) |
|---|---|
| Near Tran Nhat Duat and Tran Quang Khai | Historical panels from the Dong Son, Ly, Tran, Le and Nguyen periods. |
| Around Yen Phu | Designs inspired by Dong Ho and Hang Trong folk painting. |
| Towards Au Co and Nghi Tam | More modern city scenes, children's drawings and sections sponsored by foreign contributors. |

Those descriptions come from a tour-operator page, so use them as a guide to what to look for, not a precise map.

## Condition: set your expectations

Be ready for a mixed picture. In July 2014 Tuoi Tre News reported that the mural had sustained serious damage, and in September 2014 it reported that Hanoi's People's Committee had instructed the culture and tourism department to repair it. In November 2015 DTiNews wrote that the artwork was suffering wear and tear within five years of installation, blamed on a lack of foresight and minimal funds for restoration and upkeep.

No verified 2026 survey of the whole length turned up in the sources used here, so the honest advice is to expect variety. Some sections will be bright; others will have missing tiles, stains or faded glaze. A 2026 travel page also warns that the pavement is uneven in places and that some sections are narrow, with motorbikes passing close. That makes comfortable shoes more important than a camera lens.

Tiles are sharp when broken and they are public art, so look and photograph but do not pry, chip or climb the wall.

## Riding it versus walking it

The mural lines busy commuter roads, so riding along it is for getting to a stretch, not for sightseeing. Stopping a scooter on a dike road to look at the wall can block a lane or a pavement. A routine that works:

1. **Pick the stretch first.** Choose either the area near Long Bien Bridge or the Yen Phu section, and decide where you will park.
2. **Park in a guarded place.** Use an attended motorbike parking area and keep the ticket. Our guide to [guarded parking, tickets and towing](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-motorbike-in-hanoi/) explains how to tell a proper facility from a pavement gap.
3. **Walk 1 to 2 km.** One source suggests a 2 to 3 km stroll from Tran Quang Khai towards Yen Phu takes one to two hours; plan less if it is hot or you stop often.
4. **Return to the bike by the same side of the road.** Crossing a busy dike road repeatedly is where most of the risk is.
5. **Ride on to the next stop only after a rest.**

Do not ride on the pavement beside the wall, even when a scooter in front of you does. Our guide to [riding in Hanoi's Old Quarter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-old-quarter/) explains why the Old Quarter edge is a place to walk.

## Building a half-day from the east bank

Nguyen Ha's shop is at Alley 5 Nguyen Van Cu Street, Bo De Ward, Long Bien District, so the east bank is where you start. A few combinations that make sense:

- **Short and simple.** Cross to the Long Bien Bridge end of the mural, park, walk for an hour, and cross back. Our guide to [a first ride between Long Bien and the Red River](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/long-bien-red-river-first-ride/) covers the bridge approach, and bridge lanes can change, so check signs.
- **Art and craft.** The tiles come from Bat Trang, so see the mural and then ride out to the pottery village on another day or in the afternoon. Our guide to [Gia Lam, the east bank and Bat Trang by scooter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/gia-lam-east-bank-bat-trang-by-scooter/) describes that side of the river.
- **Market and mural.** Long Bien Market is near the bridge, but it is a working wholesale market. If you plan to include it, read [how to visit Long Bien Market without blocking deliveries](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-visit-long-bien-market-without-blocking-deliveries/).
- **Lake finish.** The Yen Phu and Nghi Tam end is close to the Tay Ho and West Lake area, covered in our [Tay Ho by scooter guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/tay-ho-west-lake-by-scooter/), a pleasant place to finish.

## Light, heat and timing

There is little shade along the dike, so go early or late. A travel page advises soft morning light or about 5 to 6 pm for photography, and suggests sunscreen, a hat and water. Use that as common sense rather than as a rule. In October, Hanoi daytime highs average around 28 to 31 degrees Celsius depending on the source, with roughly 12 rainy days in the month, so check the forecast. If rain arrives, dike roads and painted markings become slippery, and our guide to [riding in Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) explains what to do.

## Manners and safety

- **Do not touch or pick at the tiles.** One guide notes that oils from hands can damage ceramics, and loose tiles are sharp.
- **Stay off the carriageway.** Walk on the pavement or the dike-side path, and hold children's hands.
- **Keep the pavement clear.** The pavement is also a commuter and street-seller space.
- **Do not leave valuables on the bike.** Take bags with you or leave them at your lodging.
- **Do not stop on the road for a photograph.** Find a parking spot first.
- **Respect neighbours.** Keep noise down in residential stretches and stay off private plots beyond the dike.

## FAQ: the Ceramic Mosaic Mural

### Is there a ticket or an opening time?

No. It is a public wall along roads and can be seen at any time, though daylight is better.

### How long is it, really?

Guinness certified 3,850 metres of mosaic. Some sources say 6.5 km, which refers to the longer dike system. Plan to see a section, not all of it.

### Can I see it all from a scooter?

You can ride past much of it, but you will see very little and it is hard to ride safely while looking. Park and walk a stretch.

### Is the mural in good condition?

Reports from 2014 and 2015 describe damage and wear, and no verified 2026 survey was found for this guide. Expect some sections to be worn.

### Where do the tiles come from?

Wikipedia states that the ceramic tesserae are products of Bat Trang, a village near Hanoi that is famous for its pottery.

### What is the best way to end the day?

A cafe stop in the Tay Ho area or a walk through the Old Quarter both work, as long as you choose a place with proper, attended parking.
