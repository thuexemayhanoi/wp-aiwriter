Yen So Park is Hanoi's largest park, a landscape of lakes, lawns and islands of about 323 hectares on the southern edge of the built-up city, and it is reachable on a rented motorbike if you take the ordinary roads and avoid the expressway beside it. Entry is free, the park is commonly listed as open from 5:00 to 19:00 daily, and a visit works best as a half-day outing on a dry weekday. The one thing that catches visitors out is the Phap Van to Cau Gie expressway, which runs right next to the park and is closed to motorcycles and mopeds, so a navigation app that sends you onto it is wrong for a scooter. This guide explains the route, the gates and parking, what the park contains, the rules to follow, when to go and how to plan the day.

## What Yen So Park is

Yen So Park surrounds a series of lakes that were once a run-down regulating area. A report from Vietnam Investment Review in April 2014 describes the 323-hectare park as having entered full operation after the Malaysian company Gamuda Land Vietnam rebuilt it, following an assignment by Hanoi's People's Committee in December 2007. The facilities listed at that time included an art gallery, a boathouse, an amphitheatre, a maze, a traditional village and numerous gardens. An April 2014 handover to the city is also mentioned by other sources.

A travel guide published by Origin Vietnam adds that the park has five large and small lakes covering about 132.8 hectares of water, and that the lakes act as a regulating basin that temporarily stores rainwater to reduce flooding in the city. That explains the landscape: a park designed around water, with broad shores and islands rather than a compact garden.

For the visitor, the size is the important point. You cannot see everything in an hour, and distances between the main areas are long enough that most people walk, cycle or use the park's rental carts rather than carry a bag around on foot all day.

## Where it is and how to ride there

The park is in Yen So Ward, formerly part of Hoang Mai District, next to the intersection with the Phap Van to Cau Gie expressway. Origin Vietnam puts it about 8.5 km south of the city centre, while Vietnam Airlines' travel guide suggests roughly 20 minutes by car from the centre, which on a motorbike will depend on traffic.

The directions given by those guides are consistent: from the centre, head for Giai Phong Street, follow the signs towards Ring Road 3 and the Phap Van area, and look for the park's gate. In practice, check the route on a map app before you set off, and when the app offers a faster road, look at its name. If the name or the shield shows "Phap Van - Cau Gie" or "expressway" and not a street name, choose another route.

### Why the expressway is off limits

Road-traffic Law 36/2024/QH15, in force since 1 January 2025, says in Article 25 that motorcycles, mopeds and similar vehicles, animal-drawn and hand-drawn vehicles and pedestrians may not travel on an expressway, except for vehicles and people maintaining it. As of October 2026 that is the rule that applies to the Phap Van to Cau Gie expressway, which is a posted expressway with its own lane rules. In September 2025 the Vietnam Road Administration also reported a change of lane organisation on that expressway, with a minimum speed of 80 km/h in the inner lane, which is a reminder that it is built for fast traffic, not for scooters.

The park's gates are on ordinary roads beside it. Our guide to [Hanoi's ring roads and highways](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-ring-roads-highways-motorbikes-allowed/) explains which fast roads are closed to motorbikes, and our page on [speed limits for motorbikes in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/speed-limits-motorbikes-vietnam/) explains the posted limits.

## Gates, parking and entry

Entry is free every day, according to the park's own pages as relayed in listings, and visitors pay only for services they use. The park's published rules also state that parking is charged at a small per-vehicle rate, which is why you should have small change ready; our guide to [paying for fuel, parking and tolls](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/paying-fuel-parking-tolls-hanoi-visitor/) explains how this works. Prices at the gate may change, so do not rely on an old figure.

Hanoi's approved planning decision for the Yen So Park area, issued in 2015, describes the intended layout of gates. It places the main gate on the Ring Road 3 side, leading towards a large central round island with a square, fountain and gardens, and places other main gates to the east and south, reached from the surrounding roads. It also calls for parking in basements and podiums of the surrounding buildings. A planning decision is not a visitor guide, and the way things look on the ground may differ, so use it as background and not as directions.

At the gate, follow these steps:

1. Choose the gate that your map shows on an ordinary road, not on the expressway side.
2. Park where the staff direct you and take the ticket.
3. Lock the steering and carry your helmet. The shop does not supply an extra lock.
4. Photograph the bike and note the gate you used; the park is large and the gates look alike.
5. Do not assume you may ride a motorbike along the internal paths. The planning decision describes paths of 3 to 6 metres wide, but no published rule on visitor motorbikes was found, so ask at the gate.

## What there is to do

The park is best for open-air time. The Vietnam Airlines guide lists a spacious parking area, public toilets in several places, stone benches, and rental services for bicycles, tandem bicycles, electric carts, pedal ducks and rowing boats, with a children's play area. Those are reference listings, so confirm availability and cost on arrival.

Typical uses, according to the guides:

- **Walking and cycling** around the lakeside paths.
- **Picnics and camping** on the lawns in designated service zones; tents, mats and grills can be rented on site.
- **Photography,** including wedding photographs, around the lakes and gardens.
- **Events.** The park's own site says its space can hold events with more than 10,000 people, so large gatherings do take place.
- **Boating** on the lakes.

If you want to cycle, a rental bike inside the park is usually easier than trying to carry your scooter's pace into a pedestrian setting.

## Rules to follow

The park's official page asks visitors to keep pets under control and to put dogs in a muzzle, and to camp and barbecue only in the designated areas. A dog play area is provided. Follow the same basic good manners that you would in any park:

- Take your litter with you or use the bins.
- Keep to the paths and the lawns that are open to visitors.
- Do not light fires or grills outside the service zones.
- Keep music at a level that does not disturb others.
- Respect fenced areas and lake edges.

## When to go

Opening hours are commonly given as 5:00 to 19:00 every day, including holidays, but check before a special trip because hours can change. For timing, consider the following:

| Choice | Why |
|---|---|
| Weekday morning | Quieter paths, easier parking, cooler air |
| Weekend | More families, more picnics, slower exits, as Origin Vietnam notes that weekends are busier |
| Late afternoon | Soft light but a short window before closing; allow time to ride back before dark |
| Public-holiday weekends | Heavy traffic on the roads that lead south, including the expressway beside the park |

Traffic around this part of the city can be severe at the end of long holidays. VnExpress reported that vehicles backed up for two kilometres on the Phap Van to Cau Gie expressway on the last day of the four-day Independence Day holiday in September 2022, and Lao Dong noted slow, dense traffic there on 30 August 2026 as people travelled before the National Day holiday. Those are expressway reports, but they show how crowded the surrounding approaches can be, and they are good reasons not to plan this outing on a travel-peak day.

Leave early enough to be out of the park by closing time and back on the main roads before dusk. Our guides to [riding at night in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-at-night-in-hanoi/) and [riding in Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) explain why a late return is less pleasant.

## A half-day plan

1. **Check the weather and the day's traffic news** before you leave.
2. **Fill the tank** before heading south. Our guide to [fuel in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/fuel-in-hanoi-petrol-stations-rental-scooter/) explains stations and what to expect.
3. **Ride to the park in the morning** by Giai Phong and the ordinary roads, avoiding the expressway.
4. **Park at the gate you chose** and take your helmet.
5. **Walk or cycle one section** of the lake, rather than attempting everything.
6. **Eat and rest** near the park, drink water and use the shade where you can.
7. **Ride back before the late-afternoon peak,** retracing the same roads.

A small automatic scooter is a comfortable choice for this kind of half-day. If you are comparing models, see our guide to [Honda Vision versus Air Blade](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/honda-vision-vs-air-blade-which-scooter-to-rent-in-hanoi/).

## FAQ about Yen So Park by motorbike

### Is Yen So Park free?

Entry is free according to the park's own pages and travel guides. You pay for parking and for services such as bike rental or camping equipment.

### Can I ride my rental scooter on the Phap Van to Cau Gie expressway?

No. Under Article 25 of Law 36/2024/QH15, motorcycles and mopeds may not travel on an expressway. Use the ordinary roads that lead to the park gates.

### What are the opening hours?

Listings and the park's own page say 5:00 to 19:00 daily. Confirm before you go.

### Can I ride inside the park?

No published rule for visitor motorbikes was found, and the paths are designed for walking and cycling. Park at the gate and ask staff.

### How far is it from central Hanoi?

About 8.5 km south of the centre according to Origin Vietnam, with travel time varying greatly with traffic.
