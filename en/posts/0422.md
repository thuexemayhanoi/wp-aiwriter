To report a mechanical problem to the rental shop, stop in a safe place if the fault could affect control, then contact the shop straight away with the bike's plate number, your location, what you noticed and when, a photograph or short video and what you have done so far. Keep the message short and factual. A prompt, clear report does three things: it lets the shop decide whether the bike is safe to ride, it creates a written record of when you first told them and it separates a fault that developed during use from damage you caused. Vietnam's Civil Code treats a motorbike rental as a lease of property, and it says the lessor must repair defects and a lessee should tell the lessor about them. This guide explains what to say, how to describe common faults, a message template, what to photograph, when not to ride, what the shop may do and how to keep a record. It is general information, not a mechanic's diagnosis.

## Why prompt reporting matters

A small fault that is reported early is usually cheap to fix. The same fault ridden through for a week can become a large one. A rattling chain, a spongy brake or a weak starter may be signs of wear, and a shop would much rather hear about them than find a seized part at return.

There is a second reason. When a problem is reported at the time, nobody can later argue about when it started. Honda's maintenance pages make a similar point: a symptom noticed early, such as a slow starter or a longer brake pedal, is the cue for a check, and Honda tells riders to take faults to a dealer. Our guide to [what counts as normal wear on a rented Honda Wave](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-counts-as-normal-wear-on-a-rented-honda-wave/) explains why a symptom reported early is treated as wear, while the same symptom ridden through for weeks can become damage.

## What the Civil Code says

Vietnam's Civil Code (Law No. 91/2015/QH13), in force since 1 January 2017, sets the general rules for a lease of property. As of October 2026, the articles that matter here, which we read in the text published by the Tuyen Quang Department of Justice, say:

- **Article 477:** the lessor must keep the leased property in the agreed condition and fit for its purpose throughout the lease and must repair damage and defects, except minor damage that custom requires the lessee to repair. If the lessor has been notified and does not repair, or repairs late, the lessee may repair at reasonable cost, must tell the lessor and may ask for the cost to be paid.
- **Article 479:** the lessee must preserve and maintain the property and carry out minor repairs, must compensate if it is lost or damaged and is not responsible for natural wear.

The reference to "being notified" is the key point for you. A report to the shop is what starts those rights and duties working. The contract fills in the details. This is general information, not legal advice.

## When to stop riding first

Report a problem as soon as you notice it, but if it could affect control or safety, stop riding before you report.

Stop at once and do not ride on if:

- the **brakes** feel very soft, go to the end of their travel or do not hold;
- a **tyre** is flat, cut or bulging;
- the **steering** feels loose, bent or stiff;
- the **chain** has come off, is hanging or is making a violent noise;
- the engine **stalls repeatedly** in traffic or the bike will not hold a gear;
- there is a **fuel or oil leak**;
- the **lights** have failed after dark.

For these, our guide to [requesting a replacement bike after finding a safety defect](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/requesting-a-replacement-bike-after-finding-a-safety-defect/) explains what to ask for. For less urgent faults, such as a noise, a small leak of nothing in particular or a slightly slow start, you can usually ride carefully to a safe place and then report, but if you are unsure, stop.

## What to include in your report

A good report answers six questions in a few lines.

1. **Who and which bike:** your name and the plate number.
2. **Where:** your location, with a map pin if you can.
3. **What:** a plain description of the symptom.
4. **When:** when you first noticed it and whether it is getting worse.
5. **What you did:** whether you stopped, switched off or kept riding.
6. **What you would like:** a check, a repair, a swap or advice.

Add a photograph or a short video if it helps. Avoid guessing at causes. "The rear brake pedal goes nearly to the footrest" is better than "the brake shoes are worn".

## How to describe common faults

Plain words work best. The table gives examples of how to describe symptoms, and what extra detail helps.

| Symptom | A clear description | Useful extra detail |
|---|---|---|
| Brakes | "The rear pedal travels much further than yesterday before it bites" | Front or rear, dry or wet, any noise |
| Chain | "A loud rattle from the rear when I open the throttle" | When it began, whether it squeals |
| Gears | "It will not go into second, or jumps out of third" | Cold or warm, under load or not |
| Starting | "The starter turns slowly and the engine takes several tries" | Cold or warm, lights dim |
| Idle | "The engine idles unevenly and almost stalls at lights" | When warm, after rain |
| Pulling away | "It jerks hard when I pull away" | Every time or sometimes |
| Tyre | "The rear tyre loses air overnight" | Which wheel, any object seen |
| Lights | "The brake light does not come on with the pedal" | Front lever too, or only one |
| Leak | "There are drops under the engine" | Colour, smell, where |
| Noise | "A knocking from the engine at low speed" | Constant or intermittent |

## What to photograph or film

- **The whole bike,** with the plate number.
- **The problem itself:** a leak, a damaged part, a warning light on the dashboard.
- **A short video of the symptom,** such as the brake pedal being pressed, the indicators flashing or the chain moving.
- **The dashboard,** showing the warning lamps and the odometer. Our guide to [semi-automatic motorbike dashboard lights explained](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/semi-automatic-motorbike-dashboard-lights-explained/) explains what the lamps mean.
- **The location,** so the shop can find you.

Record sound as well as picture when the fault is a noise. A ten-second clip is far more useful than a paragraph of description.

## A message template

You can copy and adapt this.

"Hello, this is [name]. I have the Honda Wave, plate [number]. I am at [place, map pin]. [Time]: I noticed [symptom, in one sentence]. It [is / is not] getting worse. I have [stopped and switched off / ridden slowly to a safe place]. I have attached [a photo / a video]. Could you advise, or arrange a check or another bike? I can be reached on [number]."

Keep it polite and factual. You are not accusing anyone, and you are not trying to prove who is at fault. You are giving the shop the information it needs.

## What the shop may do

Depending on the fault, a shop may:

- **Give advice** over the phone, such as checking a switch or waiting a few minutes.
- **Ask you to bring the bike in,** if it is safe to ride.
- **Send a mechanic** to you.
- **Collect the bike** and arrange another.
- **Tell you to use a repairer nearby,** and agree the cost first.

Which of these applies depends on the fault, where you are and the shop's own arrangements. Nguyen Ha's published terms do not describe a standard procedure for mechanical faults, so ask what to expect, and ask for the answer in writing. ## Do not repair it yourself

It can be tempting to fix a fault yourself or to have the nearest mechanic do it. Resist, unless the shop agrees.

- **A poor repair can make the fault worse,** and a modification can affect safety.
- **The contract usually says you must not modify the bike.** The Civil Code says a lessee must use the property as agreed.
- **Article 477 allows a lessee to repair at reasonable cost** if the lessor has been told and does not act, but you must tell the lessor and the work should be reasonable. Tell the shop first, ask for its agreement and keep every receipt, as our guide to [how to keep receipts for repairs on a rental motorbike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-keep-receipts-for-repairs-on-a-rental-motorbike/) explains.
- **Do not open the engine or touch brakes, steering or fuel parts.**

## Keep a record

- **Save the messages** and call logs.
- **Note the time of each contact** and what was said.
- **Keep photographs and videos.**
- **Ask for the shop's decision in writing,** even a short message.
- **Keep any receipt.**
- **Tell the shop when the problem is fixed,** and how.

Our guide to [how to document existing scratches on a rental bike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-document-existing-scratches-on-a-rental-bike/) describes a photograph method that works for faults too. Prices and terms are on the [prices page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/prices/).

## A quick checklist

1. **Stop if the fault affects control or safety.**
2. **Contact the shop at once, by call and message.**
3. **Give the plate number, location, symptom and time.**
4. **Attach a photo or video.**
5. **Say what you did, and what you would like.**
6. **Do not repair or modify it yourself.**
7. **Keep every message, photograph and receipt.**

## FAQ: reporting a mechanical problem

### Should I report even a small fault?

Yes. A small fault reported early is cheap to fix, and a clear record protects you.

### Should I call or message?

Call for anything urgent and send a message too, so that there is a written record.

### What if the shop is closed?

Send a message and call when it opens. If the fault is dangerous, keep yourself and the bike safe and do not ride.

### What should the message say?

Your name, the plate number, your location, the symptom, when it began, what you did and what you would like.

### Can I get the bike repaired myself?

Only with the shop's agreement. The Civil Code allows a lessee to repair at reasonable cost after notifying a lessor who does not act, but safety parts need skilled work.

### Will I be charged for a fault I report?

It depends on the cause and on the contract. Ordinary wear is not the renter's cost under the Civil Code, but damage from misuse may be.
