The display on a rental electric scooter is the bike's only way of telling you something is wrong, so check it twice: once at the shop, with the bike switched on and nothing happening, and again whenever a symbol changes during the ride. A clean start-up screen, a battery reading that matches what the shop told you and no fault symbol are what you want before you leave. If a fault icon, a flashing battery symbol or a code appears, the safe rule is to stop in a sensible place, switch off and call the shop. This guide walks through what a typical display shows, using the VinFast Evo 200 manual as an example, how to triage what you see and what to photograph and say. Every bike differs, so ask the shop to point out the symbols on yours.

## Why the display deserves a proper look

A petrol scooter gives clues through sound, smell and vibration. An electric scooter is quiet, and most of what it knows about itself goes to the screen. The battery management system watches temperature, voltage and current. The motor controller watches the motor. When either sees something outside its limits, it can reduce power, cut output or show a code.

For a renter that makes the display a handover tool as well as a riding tool. A screen that already shows a fault before the first kilometre is evidence that the problem was not yours. ## What a typical display shows

The VinFast Evo 200 manual lists the items on its multi-function display. It is a different model from whatever you may be given, but the list shows how much information a modern electric scooter packs onto one screen:

- turn signal indicators and a high-beam indicator
- a parking-mode indicator
- speed, speed unit, odometer and a trip distance
- a clock
- an ECO or SPORT riding-mode symbol
- a battery temperature symbol
- an indicator for the electric brake and the side stand
- a symbol for lost connection to the server
- a charging-in-progress symbol
- a general system fault indicator
- the lithium-ion battery bar, with remaining capacity and charging state

Cheaper electric bicycles and some scooters have simpler screens, perhaps only a few bars and a speed. The point is the same: know the normal state, so that the abnormal state stands out.

## Step 1: read the screen before you set off

With the bike on its stand and a staff member beside you, switch on and watch the screen settle:

1. **Everything lights, then most things go out.** Many displays run a brief self-test. After it, only a few symbols should remain, such as the battery bar and perhaps the parking indicator.
2. **No fault symbol.** A system fault indicator that stays on is the one to ask about before you leave.
3. **Battery reading.** The shop's rule is that battery level and charging are confirmed at handover, so agree the reading aloud and photograph it.
4. **Parking mode.** On the Evo 200 the bike starts in parking mode, which disables the throttle, and it re-enters parking mode on its own if it has not moved for two minutes. If the throttle does nothing, check this symbol first.
5. **Side stand.** The manual says that if the side stand is lowered while riding, the bike cuts drive and the brake indicator flashes. Make sure the stand is fully up before you pull away.
6. **Charger.** The manual says the bike cannot be ridden while charging is active, and that a charging symbol shows on the screen.

Photograph the display with the bike on, and keep the picture. Our [rental scooter inspection checklist](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-scooter-inspection-checklist/) shows what else to photograph.

## Step 2: know the battery warnings

The manual gives specific thresholds for its own display. The battery bar starts to flash when the remaining charge falls below 20 per cent, and the battery icon itself starts to flash when it falls below 10 per cent. Other bikes use different thresholds, so ask what the first and second warnings mean on yours.

The practical reading is a staged one:

- **First warning:** start heading for a charging point or back to the shop. This is not an emergency, but it is the time to stop adding detours.
- **Second warning:** the margin is gone. Stop wandering, avoid hills and acceleration, and phone the shop.
- **Bike slows or cuts out:** pull over safely. A low battery is one reason, but heat and faults are others.

Remember that a displayed range figure is an estimate. Our guide to [charging an electric scooter in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/charging-an-electric-scooter-in-hanoi/) explains how to build a day around the charge.

## Step 3: fault symbols and codes

When the system fault indicator lights, the Evo 200 manual says to look at the codes on the display, read the fault and instructions through the phone app and, as a recommendation, take the bike to an authorised VinFast dealer or service centre. For most simple faults, the manual says, the owner only needs the phone app to read the error.

As a renter you will normally not have the app, so do not try to diagnose. The code is still useful because you can read it out to the shop. The manual's table lists codes of this kind, which gives a sense of what is being reported:

| Kind of fault | Example from the Evo 200 list |
|---|---|
| Motor | Motor jammed |
| Controller | Temperature sensor fault, memory error |
| Battery protection | Over-voltage, over-current when charging, over-current when discharging |
| Temperature | Over-temperature when charging, too cold to charge |
| Short circuit | Protection triggered on battery output |
| Battery hardware | Hardware fault in the battery pack |
| Communication | Lost signal between display, controller and battery system |

Do not treat any of these as something to try again and see. The manual notes that when the battery protections trigger, the battery can stop responding and cut its output. That is the system protecting itself, not a glitch to override.

## Step 4: triage what you see

| What you see | What it often means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Fault indicator on at start-up | Something was already wrong | Do not ride; ask for another bike |
| Fault indicator appears while riding | A protection or sensor has tripped | Stop safely, switch off, call the shop |
| Battery bar flashing | Low charge | Head for charging; call if unsure |
| Battery icon flashing | Very low charge | Stop wandering; call the shop |
| Battery temperature symbol | Battery running hot or cold | Stop in shade, do not charge hot; ask the shop |
| Brake indicator flashing | Side stand down or brake input | Check the stand and levers |
| Parking symbol and no throttle | Throttle disabled | Hold the brake and press the throttle-enable button as the manual describes |
| Server or connection symbol | Lost link to the vehicle's online service | Note it; usually not an emergency |
| Display blank or flickering | Power or connection issue | Do not experiment; call the shop |
| Display stays dark 45 seconds after switching on | Cause not stated in the manual | The manual's remedy is to charge for at least a minute, unplug and restart; ask the shop first |

Treat the middle column as general guidance, not a diagnosis. Where the manual states a specific behaviour it is the Evo 200's. Other models vary.

## Heat, cold and the battery symbol

The Evo 200 manual says that in very hot conditions performance can drop to reduce heat build-up, and that the battery stops working when its internal temperature goes above 60 degrees Celsius. It also lists protections for over-temperature and low temperature during charging. For a Hanoi summer ride the practical points are simple: park in shade, do not plug in a hot battery and report a temperature symbol instead of ignoring it.

## What not to do

- **Do not keep riding with a fault icon on.** Go to a safe place, switch off and call.
- **Do not switch the battery off and on repeatedly to clear a fault.** The manual does mention a single off-on of the key and the battery switch under the seat for a bike that will not start, but only for that case, and it adds that if it fails you should contact technical support.
- **Do not open the bike or the battery compartment beyond the storage box.** The manual says not to repair the battery yourself.
- **Do not hose the dashboard area.** The manual warns against spraying water at the lamps, handlebar switches and ignition lock.
- **Do not ignore an odd smell or heat.** Stop and switch off.

## What to say to the shop

A short message makes the call faster:

- the bike you have, or its plate, and where you are
- what the display shows, with a photo if you can take one safely
- the battery reading
- what you were doing when it appeared

## At return

Switch the bike on once at the end of the rental and photograph the screen again, with the battery reading and no new symbols. If a warning appeared during the rental, say so when you return the bike, rather than leaving the next person to find it. ## FAQ: electric scooter display warnings

### Is a flashing battery symbol an emergency?

Not at first. On the Evo 200 the bar flashes below 20 per cent and the icon below 10 per cent. Treat it as a prompt to head for charging, and call the shop if you are far from it.

### What should I do if a fault code appears?

Stop safely, switch off, note the code and a photo, and call the shop. Do not try to clear it yourself.

### Why will the throttle not respond?

Check the parking indicator, the brake indicator and whether the charger is still connected. The manual lists these first.

### Does a connection symbol mean the bike is broken?

Not necessarily. On the Evo 200 it means the vehicle has lost its link to the online server, which is not a riding fault, but it is worth mentioning.

### Should I photograph the display?

Yes, at pickup and at return, with the battery reading visible.
