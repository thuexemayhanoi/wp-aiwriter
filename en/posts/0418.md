A theft clause says what happens if the rented motorbike is stolen, and in most rentals it puts the loss on the renter, usually up to the value of the bike, subject to conditions about how the bike was secured. Before you sign, find out four things: whether you are liable for the full value or a capped amount, what you must do to secure the bike, what you must do if it is stolen and whether the deposit or any insurance covers part of the loss. Vietnam's Civil Code treats a motorbike rental as a lease of property, and it says a lessee who loses the leased property must compensate the lessor. The rest is left to the contract. This guide explains how to read a theft clause, what the published terms of Nguyen Ha say about the deposit, what the compulsory motorbike insurance and travel insurance do and do not cover, the questions to ask and how to protect yourself in practice. It is general information, not legal advice.

## What a theft clause is for

A rental shop hands over an expensive asset and gets it back days later. The theft clause answers the obvious worry: what if it never comes back? Most clauses do three things.

1. **They say who bears the loss** if the bike is stolen during the rental.
2. **They set conditions,** such as keeping the key safe, locking the steering and parking in a secure place.
3. **They set a process,** such as reporting the theft to the police and telling the shop at once.

A good clause is clear about all three. A poor one is silent on one of them, or so broad that it makes the renter liable whatever happens.

## What the Civil Code says

Vietnam's Civil Code (Law No. 91/2015/QH13), in force since 1 January 2017, sets the general rules for a lease of property. We read the lease articles in the text published by the Tuyen Quang Department of Justice, and as of October 2026 the points that matter here are:

- **Article 479** says the lessee must preserve the leased property, maintain it and carry out minor repairs, and that if the lessee loses or damages it, the lessee must compensate. The lessee is not responsible for natural wear from use.
- **Article 482** says the lessee must return the property in the condition in which it was received, apart from natural wear or as agreed, and that the lessor may claim compensation if its value has dropped. During a delay in returning it, the lessee bears the risk.
- **Article 480** says the lessee must use the property for its proper purpose and as agreed.

The Code does not set a formula for theft. It gives the general duty to preserve the property and to compensate for loss, and it leaves the practical detail, such as the amount, the proof and the conditions, to the parties. That is why the clause matters. ## The deposit and what it covers

Nguyen Ha takes a cash deposit of VND 2,000,000–5,000,000, depending on the bike and the contract, and the deposit is separate from the rent. As we describe in our guide to [how a motorbike rental deposit works in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbike-rental-deposit-hanoi/), it is held against damage, loss and unpaid charges. Identity documents and the licence are checked and photographed for the contract, and the originals are not kept.

The key point is that the deposit may be smaller than the value of the bike. A cash deposit of a few million dong does not necessarily equal the price of a motorbike, so a theft clause that makes you liable for the full value could exceed it. Ask directly: "If the bike is stolen, is my liability limited to the deposit, or can it be more?" The answer should be in the contract. ## What to look for in the clause

Read these parts one at a time.

**1. Who bears the loss, and how much?**
Look for wording such as "the renter is responsible for loss of the vehicle". Then look for the amount: the full value, the market value on the day, a fixed price list, the deposit only or "the cost of replacement". If no amount is stated, ask what it would be and have it written in.

**2. What counts as "theft"?**
Some clauses distinguish theft from loss, from a bike taken by force and from a bike taken because the key was left in it. Others use one word for all. The distinction can matter for the amount and for whether the clause applies at all.

**3. What security the renter must provide.**
Typical duties include locking the steering, removing the key, parking in a guarded or enclosed place and not leaving the bike unattended overnight in the street. Nguyen Ha does not provide an extra lock, so the published advice is to lock the steering and park in guarded places. If the clause lists conditions, check that you can meet them in practice.

**4. The key.**
Many clauses make the renter responsible for the key. Losing the key is a separate event from theft, but a stolen key can lead to a stolen bike. Ask what happens if the key is lost. Our guide to [lost rental bike keys: reporting and replacement steps](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/lost-rental-bike-keys-reporting-and-replacement-steps/) covers it in more detail, and our guide to [smart key versus traditional key on rental scooters](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/smart-key-versus-traditional-key-on-rental-scooters/) explains the differences between key types.

**5. The reporting duty.**
Look for a requirement to tell the shop at once and to report to the police, and for any time limit. A clause that demands a police report within a very short time, without saying where or how, can be hard to meet. Ask what the shop expects.

**6. What the renter must hand over.**
After a theft, the shop may ask for a copy of the police report, the key or an account of what happened. Check that this is reasonable.

**7. Whether the rental continues.**
Some clauses say that rent stops at the time of the theft, others that it runs until the matter is settled. Ask.

**8. Other costs.**
Check whether you are also liable for the shop's own losses, such as the days the bike is out of service, a replacement registration, a new plate or a new key.

## What insurance does and does not do

Insurance is the most common misunderstanding.

**Compulsory motorbike insurance.** Under Decree 67/2023/ND-CP, as we explain in our guide to [travel insurance and motorbike rentals](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/travel-insurance-motorbike-rental-vietnam/), the compulsory civil-liability insurance covers injury or damage to third parties. It does not pay for the theft of the rental bike itself.

**Your travel insurance.** A travel policy may exclude hired two-wheelers, motorbike use or theft of a vehicle altogether. Some policies cover theft of your own property, but not property you have hired, and some require a police report within a short time. Read the policy wording, and ask the insurer before you travel.

**Shop-side cover.** Some shops carry their own insurance or write their own waiver. Ask whether the shop has any cover that reduces your liability, and what you must do to use it. Do not assume it exists.

If you rely on any insurance for a theft, ask for the details in writing before you ride.

## A worked example, with invented figures

To see why the clause matters, imagine a contract that says: "The renter is responsible for the full replacement value of the vehicle if it is stolen." Suppose the renter paid a cash deposit and the bike is stolen from an unguarded street at night. Under that clause the shop could claim the replacement value, which could be much more than the deposit, and the renter would owe the difference. Now imagine a contract that says: "If the vehicle is stolen while locked and parked in a guarded place, the renter's liability is limited to the deposit." The renter's exposure is smaller, but so is the protection if the conditions are not met.

These figures and clauses are illustrations, not Nguyen Ha's terms, but they show why the amount and the conditions both matter.

## Questions to ask before you sign

- If the bike is stolen, is my liability limited, and to what amount?
- Is that amount written in the contract?
- What security do you expect me to use, and do you provide anything?
- What do I do if it is stolen: whom do I call and what must I report?
- Do I need a police report, and what do you do with it?
- What happens to the deposit?
- Does any insurance, yours or mine, apply?
- Does the rent stop when the bike is stolen?
- What if the key is lost or stolen with the bike?

Ask for the answers to be written on the contract or in a message. ## FAQ: the theft clause

### Who pays if a rental motorbike is stolen?

It depends on the contract. The Civil Code says a lessee who loses the leased property must compensate, and the contract sets the amount and conditions.

### Is my liability limited to the deposit?

Not necessarily. Ask the shop, and have the answer written in the contract.

### Does compulsory motorbike insurance cover theft of the rental bike?

No. It covers injury or damage to third parties, not the theft of the bike itself.

### Does travel insurance cover a stolen rental motorbike?

Often not. Read the policy and ask the insurer before you travel.

### What security must I use?

Check the clause. Locking the steering and parking in a guarded place are the usual conditions, and Nguyen Ha does not provide an extra lock.

### Should I report a theft to the police?

Yes, and tell the shop at once. Our guide on what to do if a rental motorbike is stolen explains how.
