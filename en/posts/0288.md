Dong Da is a dense inner part of Hanoi, south-west of the Old Quarter, known for main roads, schools and commerce, and it suits a rented scooter for two kinds of trip: a short hop to the Temple of Literature and a longer loop that also takes in the Dong Da Mound and the main avenues in between. Roads here are wide but busy, the sights are spread out along a few long streets, and the best way to cope is to pick two stops, park once at each and walk the last stretch. This guide explains what Dong Da means after the 2025 reorganisation, how to combine its main sights, where the official sites say to leave a motorbike, what to expect on the big roads and how the metro can take the strain off a day. It is general information, not a live traffic report.

## What "Dong Da" means now

For decades Dong Da was a district with 17 wards. Hanoi's reorganisation under Resolution 1656/NQ-UBTVQH15 ended the district level from 1 July 2025 and replaced the old wards with 126 new wards and communes citywide. The Hanoi culture department's account of the resolution lists the new wards in this part of town as Dong Da, Kim Lien, Van Mieu - Quoc Tu Giam, Lang and O Cho Dua. The Temple of Literature's own website now gives its address as 58 Quoc Tu Giam Street in "Van Mieu - Quoc Tu Giam Ward".

For a rider this is mostly a labelling issue. Older articles, maps and booking sites still say "Dong Da District", and both labels point to the same streets. Search by street name and number instead of ward and you will not go wrong.

## Choose your anchor: two sights, not six

Dong Da's famous places are not clustered like those around Hoan Kiem Lake. Two are worth planning around.

**The Temple of Literature (Van Mieu - Quoc Tu Giam).** The official site describes a complex of 54,331 square metres, founded in 1070 for the worship of Confucius and later the seat of the country's highest school, with 82 doctoral stelae recognised by UNESCO as documentary heritage. The official visitor page gives opening hours of 08:00 to 17:00 every day, with an evening programme from 18:00 to 22:00 on Wednesdays, Saturdays and Sundays that has its own ticket. Entry is at 58 Quoc Tu Giam Street and the exits are on Van Mieu Street and towards Giam Garden. Ticket prices are on the official page and change, so check there.

**Dong Da Mound.** The park's own site calls it a national special relic site, commemorating the 1789 victory of Emperor Quang Trung over the Qing army. The address is 4 Dang Tien Dong, and the park lists opening hours of 08:00 to 18:00 every day. The same site names nearby Boc Pagoda on Chua Boc Street as an associated relic. The mound is a calmer stop than the temple, with a statue, an exhibition house and a shrine.

You can do both in one half-day, but do not add a third. Our guide to [Ba Dinh by motorbike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/ba-dinh-by-motorbike/) explains the neighbouring Mausoleum area if you want a second day.

## Where the official site says to park

The Temple of Literature's visitor page is unusually clear about parking: private cars park along Van Mieu Street, tourist buses also along Van Mieu Street, and motorbikes and bicycles park in Giam Garden. That is the first stop on any Dong Da day because it means you do not have to hunt for kerb space on a busy street.

Some general rules apply anywhere in Dong Da. Law 36/2024/QH15, in force from 1 January 2025, does not allow stopping or parking within 5 metres of a junction, on the left of a one-way street, on a pedestrian crossing, in front of a gate with a vehicle entrance or where you hide a road sign. On city streets you must stop close to the kerb, with the nearest wheel not more than 0.25 metres away. The same law lets traffic police move an illegally parked vehicle that causes obstruction and makes the owner pay for the move. Our [parking guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-motorbike-in-hanoi/) explains how ticketed lots and towing work in practice.

For other spots, such as Dong Da Mound or a cafe, look for an attendant and a marked row. An empty patch of pavement is not parking just because others use it.

## The roads that connect everything

Rather than learn every lane, recognise the arteries. Dong Da is crossed by long, multi-lane roads, and the Temple of Literature sits on a quieter one. Hanoi's traffic police have identified 51 points and routes in the city that frequently jam, according to a Lao Dong report from January 2026, and the metro now runs over this district, so rush hours can be heavy. A few ways to keep the ride calm:

- **Avoid the morning and late-afternoon peaks if you can.** Roughly 07:00 to 09:00 and 16:30 to 19:00 is when commuter traffic is heaviest, so plan the temple for mid-morning.
- **Stay in the lane that goes with your turn.** Long avenues have bus lanes and fast lanes, so check the markings before a turn. Our [lane rules guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/lane-rules-motorbikes-vietnam/) explains the basics.
- **Do not trust a navigation app blindly at a ramp.** Some elevated decks nearby are closed to motorbikes. See the [expressway check guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-check-if-a-navigation-app-suggests-an-expressway/).
- **Slow down near schools.** This is an education district, and school drop-off adds stopped vehicles and children on foot.

## Using the metro to cut a day short

Two metro lines serve this part of town. Line 2A runs from Cat Linh, in Dong Da, out to Ha Dong. Line 3's elevated section from Nhon to Cau Giay is operating, and its underground section toward Van Mieu and Ha Noi station was reported about 80 per cent complete in June 2026, so it is not open yet. Because of that, Cat Linh and Cau Giay are the stations to know.

A simple pattern works well for visitors: ride to one anchor such as the Temple of Literature, park in Giam Garden, and use the metro or a ride-hailing car for the rest if the weather turns or you are tired. The scooter is more useful for the stretches between places than for each short hop.

## A sample half-day route

1. Collect the scooter in the morning and cross to the west side of the Red River using a bridge open to bikes. The [Long Bien route guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/long-bien-red-river-first-ride/) is a good first crossing.
2. Ride to the Temple of Literature, park in Giam Garden and visit.
3. Have lunch on foot nearby.
4. Ride to Dong Da Mound, park in a marked space at 4 Dang Tien Dong and spend half an hour in the park.
5. Return before the evening peak.

The distance and time for each leg depend on traffic, so this guide gives no fixed times. Allow slack.

## Rain and heat

Hanoi's low junctions can hold water in heavy rain, and Dong Da is no exception. If the sky darkens, pull in somewhere safe off the road rather than pressing on. Our [rain riding guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) lists the streets that flood and what to wear. In hot weather, the temple's old banyan and frangipani trees give shade, and it is sensible to carry water between sights.

## The Ring Road 1 plan

Parts of Dong Da, including the Van Mieu - Quoc Tu Giam and O Cho Dua wards, are among the nine wards that a VnEconomy report lists inside Ring Road 1. Under Hanoi's low-emission zone plan, the first pilot phase, from 1 July 2026, covers only parts of Hoan Kiem ward; VOV World and Lao Dong both reported this around the launch. The same reports say the zone will widen to all of Ring Road 1 from 2028. As of October 2026, a petrol rental scooter can still be ridden in Dong Da, but check recent notices before a long stay.

## FAQ: Dong Da by motorbike

### Where do I park at the Temple of Literature?

The official visitor page says motorbikes and bicycles park in Giam Garden. Cars and tourist buses use Van Mieu Street.

### Is Dong Da still a district?

No. The district level was dropped on 1 July 2025 and the area is now covered by several wards such as Dong Da, Kim Lien, Van Mieu - Quoc Tu Giam, Lang and O Cho Dua.

### What are the temple's opening hours?

The official site lists 08:00 to 17:00 daily, plus an evening programme on three days each week. Check before going, especially on holidays.

### Can I visit both the temple and Dong Da Mound in one day?

Yes, they make a comfortable half-day. Dong Da Mound's park lists 08:00 to 18:00 daily.

### Is it easy to ride in Dong Da for a first-timer?

The roads are wide but busy. Choose off-peak hours, keep to your lane and park once per stop.
