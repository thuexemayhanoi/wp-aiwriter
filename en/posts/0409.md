If your plans are not fixed, choose the shortest rental period you are confident about and plan to extend it, rather than paying for the longest period you might use. At Nguyen Ha in Long Bien, early return does not refund unused rent, a return that is late costs VND 30,000 per hour for the first 1 to 5 hours and a full extra day from 6 hours, and any extension has to be agreed before the current period ends. A period that is too long wastes money you cannot get back, while a period that is a little short is cheap to repair if you act early. This guide explains how a rental period is counted, how the day, week and month packages differ, how to weigh the cost of being wrong in each direction and how to settle the choice with the shop before you sign.

## How a rental period is counted

Everything starts with the definition of a day. According to the shop's published terms, a rental day is 24 hours from the handover time written in the contract. If you take a scooter at 10:00, it is due back at 10:00 the next day unless the contract says otherwise. A rental of one hour, six hours or twelve hours is still charged as one day, because ordinary rentals are not priced by the hour.

Two consequences follow for anyone with uncertain plans. First, the clock runs from the moment of handover and not from midnight, so the time you collect the bike matters as much as the date. Second, a "day" is a unit of 24 hours, so a trip that ends the next morning may need two days even though it spans only a single night. Write down the handover time when you sign, because it is the anchor for every later calculation. ## Day, week or month: what each package is for

The shop publishes reference prices for three periods. For manual and automatic bikes the daily range is VND 150,000 to 200,000. Weekly packages run from VND 700,000 to 800,000 for manual bikes and VND 600,000 to 1,000,000 for automatic scooters, and monthly packages from VND 1,000,000 to 1,200,000 for manual bikes and VND 1,200,000 to 2,000,000 for automatic scooters. The [prices page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/prices/) has the current table, and the exact figure for your bike is confirmed before you sign.

The important point is that weekly and monthly packages are priced on their own. They are not the daily rate multiplied by seven or thirty, so the way to compare is to look at the package price directly instead of counting days. The table below shows what each period is generally good for.

| Period | Best when | Main risk if plans change |
|---|---|---|
| Day (24 hours) | One fixed excursion or a single city day | Needing a second day at short notice |
| Week | A stay of several days to a week, or a longer stay with an uncertain end | Leaving a few days early, with unused rent not refunded |
| Month | A firm stay of several weeks, or commuting | Leaving weeks early, with a large unused amount not refunded |

The shop's own advice is worth repeating: a renter who is unsure whether a stay will last three weeks or four is usually better off with a weekly package that can be extended than with a month paid up front. Our guides on [weekly and monthly rental questions](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/weekly-monthly-motorbike-rental-hanoi/) and on [whether to rent a manual bike by the day or by the month](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/manual-motorbike-rental-daily-versus-monthly-use-cases/) go further into how the packages behave over time.

## The cost of being wrong in each direction

A good way to choose is to compare the two mistakes you can make.

**Booking too long.** If you return the bike before the end of the period you paid for, the unused rent is not refunded. This is written into the contract so that both sides know the bike's schedule from the start. The loss is the unused part of the period and it cannot be recovered, however good the reason for leaving early.

**Booking too short.** If you need the bike for longer, you have two options: extend before the period ends, or keep it late. Extending is an agreement about price and return arrangements, while a late return is charged at VND 30,000 per hour for hours one to five and a full extra rental day from six hours. Telling the shop that you are running late is appreciated but does not remove the charge. The shop's terms also say that any extension must be agreed before the existing period ends.

Put side by side, an extension is generally the cheaper and calmer mistake, provided the shop can say yes. The catch is availability. Models are confirmed when you book rather than promised in advance, so an extension on the same bike is something to ask for and not something to assume. That is a reason to avoid the tightest possible booking if you absolutely need the same bike for the last day.

## A simple method for choosing

Work through these steps before you ask for a price.

1. **List the fixed points.** Flights, train tickets, tours or work obligations that cannot move. These set the earliest and the latest sensible return time.
2. **List the soft points.** Days you might add, shorten or swap because of weather, health or a good place found along the way.
3. **Estimate the firm minimum.** The number of days you will ride in almost any scenario. Book at least that.
4. **Decide the realistic maximum.** The longest the rental could plausibly run. This is what you should be ready to extend towards, not what you should pay for now.
5. **Compare packages at the firm minimum.** If it is close to a week, ask for the weekly price, because the package is priced independently of the day rate.
6. **Ask how an extension would be handled.** Do this at the counter, before signing, so that the answer is on record.

If your fixed points are tight and the soft points are many, this method nearly always ends with a shorter booking and a planned extension. If your plans are firm and the soft points are few, the longer package is simply better value.

## Scenarios worth thinking through

### Two or three days with a possible detour

Book the days you are sure about and decide the detour later. A rental day is 24 hours, so a half-day detour that pushes you past the due time moves you into a late-return charge, and from six hours late into another full day. If the detour is likely, choose one more day in advance, or ask for the extension while you are still inside the period.

### A week that might turn into ten days

A week is a natural unit, and it can be extended. Ask when you sign how extra days would be priced, since the shop describes extensions as an agreement about price and return arrangements. Do not assume the extension will cost the same per day as the original package.

### A stay of three or four weeks

This is the case the shop itself describes. A monthly package is priced as a package, so it is good value only if you really use most of it. If your stay could end after three weeks and the unused rent is lost, weeks may suit better. If you are certain of four weeks or more, the monthly package is the straightforward choice.

### Weather-dependent travel

Heavy rain can change a trip at short notice. Our guides on [riding in Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) and on [heavy rain on a semi-automatic bike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-a-semi-automatic-bike-in-heavy-hanoi-rain/) describe the conditions. If weather could shorten your riding, avoid the longest period, because early return does not refund unused rent.

### A trip outside Hanoi

If you plan a day trip or an overnight ride away from the city, tell the shop when you book, so that it can suggest a suitable bike and confirm the return time. Our guide on [taking a rental motorbike out of Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/take-rental-motorbike-out-of-hanoi/) explains what to ask. A longer route is also the most common reason a period turns out too short.

## What the contract and the law say about the period

The shop's FAQ describes its terms as published in October 2026 and stresses that the contract you sign for a specific bike is what finally applies. That matters here because the contract records the rental period, the rent, the deposit and the return time. Vietnam's Civil Code points in the same direction. Under Article 481, a lessee must pay rent in full and on time as agreed, and under Article 482 must return the property in the condition in which it was received, with normal wear and tear excepted. Both articles work from what the parties have agreed, so the agreed period is the one you will be held to.

## Settling the choice at the counter

Before you pay, ask these questions and note the answers.

- What is the exact due time of the rental period, given the handover time?
- Is the weekly or monthly price lower than counting days for my dates?
- If I want to extend, how and by when do I ask, and how would the extra days be priced?
- Is the same bike likely to be free if I extend, or might I be offered another one?
- How is a late return counted, and at what point does the extra day apply?
- What is the arrangement if I return after closing time?

## FAQ: choosing a rental period

### Should I book a week if I am not sure I need seven days?

Only if the weekly package suits the days you are sure of. The unused part of a period is not refunded, so book what you are confident about and ask how an extension works.

### Is the weekly price seven times the daily price?

No. Weekly and monthly packages are priced on their own, so compare the package price directly.

### Can I get a refund if I return the bike early?

No. Rent paid for the unused part of the period is not refunded.

### What if I need the bike for longer?

Ask the shop for an extension before the current period ends. Any extension has to be agreed, including price and return arrangements, and the same bike is not promised.

### What does a late return cost?

The published reference is VND 30,000 per hour for a return 1 to 5 hours late and one extra rental day from 6 hours.

### Does telling the shop in advance cancel the late charge?

No. Advance notice helps the shop plan but does not waive the charge.
