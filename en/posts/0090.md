The numbers moulded into a Honda Wave's tyre sidewalls tell you the tyre's width, height, rim size, construction, how much weight it can carry and how fast it may go, and the pressure figures in Honda's manual tell you what to inflate it to. Once you can read them, you can confirm in a minute that a rental bike is wearing the right tyres at the right pressure for your load. On the Wave RSX FI and the Yamaha Sirius FI, the front tyre is marked 70/90-17 M/C 38P and the rear 80/90-17 M/C 50P. This guide decodes each part, converts the pressure figures Honda gives, shows where to look on the bike and in the manual and lists a handover check. It is general information. The correct figures for the bike you rent are on the bike or in its manual, and the shop should confirm them.

## Decoding the front tyre: 70/90-17 M/C 38P

Take the front tyre marking one piece at a time.

- **70:** the section width of the tyre in millimetres, so 70 mm across.
- **90:** the aspect ratio, which is the height of the sidewall as a percentage of the width. Ninety per cent of 70 mm is 63 mm, so the sidewall is a tall, soft one. That is typical for small bikes, and it gives the tyre cushioning on rough roads.
- **A dash between the numbers and the rim size:** this marks a bias-ply (cross-ply) construction. Michelin's guide to tyre sizes says a bias tyre is identified by a dash, while a radial carries the letter R. Honda's Wave RSX FI manual describes the tyres as cross-ply with inner tubes, which matches.
- **17:** the diameter of the wheel rim in inches, the part of the wheel the tyre sits on.
- **M/C:** the tyre is for a motorcycle. Dunlop and Michelin both explain it that way.
- **38:** the load index. On the standard load-index chart, 38 corresponds to a maximum load of 132 kilograms per tyre.
- **P:** the speed symbol. Bridgestone's motorcycle tyre designations list P as 150 km/h.

## Decoding the rear tyre: 80/90-17 M/C 50P

The rear tyre follows the same pattern:

- **80:** 80 mm wide, a little wider than the front.
- **90:** the aspect ratio is again 90 per cent, so the sidewall is about 72 mm.
- **17:** the same rim diameter as the front.
- **M/C:** a motorcycle tyre.
- **50:** the load index. On the standard chart, 50 corresponds to 190 kilograms per tyre. The rear carries more of the load, so it has a higher rating.
- **P:** the same speed symbol, 150 km/h.

Honda lists the same tyre sizes for the Wave RSX FI and the Blade, and Yamaha lists them for the Sirius FI, so the same decoding applies to all three. The Wave Alpha uses the same 70/90-17 front tyre.

## What the numbers mean in practice

The load and speed ratings are far above what a rental will demand. A front tyre rated for 132 kg and a rear rated for 190 kg, together 322 kg, comfortably cover the bike's weight of about 98 kg plus Honda's maximum load of 115 kg or 155 kg, depending on version. The speed symbol of 150 km/h is well above any legal speed for these bikes. So the ratings are a way to check that the tyres fitted are the correct type, not a limit you are likely to approach.

What matters more is whether the tyre is the right size. A wrong-sized or wrong-rated tyre, for example one with a lower load index fitted by a workshop to save money, affects the bike's handling. If the markings on a rental bike's tyres differ from these, ask the shop why. ## Reading Honda's pressure figures

Honda's Wave RSX FI manual gives cold tyre pressures in three units at once: kilopascals, kilograms-force per square centimetre and pounds per square inch. For that model:

| | Rider only | Rider and passenger |
|---|---|---|
| Front | 200 kPa (2.00 kgf/cm2, 29 psi) | 200 kPa (2.00 kgf/cm2, 29 psi) |
| Rear | 225 kPa (2.25 kgf/cm2, 33 psi) | 280 kPa (2.80 kgf/cm2, 41 psi) |

A tyre pressure gauge in Vietnam may show any of these units, so check which one yours uses before you compare. The three columns are the same pressure in different units. The key point is the rear tyre: it needs 55 kPa more, about 8 psi, when a passenger is aboard.

Honda's aftersales advice is to check pressure with a gauge at least once a month, because under-inflation is hard to see, and to check when the tyres are cool, because riding warms them and raises the pressure. A reading taken just after a ride will be higher than the cold figure, so it should not be used to deflate a tyre.

## Common mix-ups when reading the label

A few misreadings come up often:

- **The load index is per tyre, not per bike.** A 38 on the front is the limit for that one tyre, and the bike's own maximum load in the manual is a separate figure that also covers the frame, suspension and brakes.
- **The speed symbol is a test rating, not a speed to ride at.** P tells you what the tyre was rated for, not what is sensible or legal on the road you are on.
- **Pressure units are easy to swap.** A gauge marked in bar reads 2.0 for Honda's 200 kPa front figure and 2.8 for the 280 kPa rear figure with a passenger. A gauge marked in kg/cm2 reads almost the same numbers.
- **A tyre that looks fine can still be low.** Honda notes that under-inflation is hard to see by eye, so the gauge matters more than a glance.

## Where to find the right figure for your bike

The table above is for the Wave RSX FI. Other models and versions can differ, so find the figure for your bike:

- **The owner's manual,** which lists cold pressures for rider only and for rider and passenger.
- **A label on the bike,** where the maker fits one. Honda's manuals for some of its models describe a label that gives tyre information, so ask staff whether your bike has one and where it is.
- **The shop,** which should know the right pressure and have a gauge.

## The wear indicator mark

Besides the size, look for the letters TWI, or a small triangle, on the tyre's shoulder. This marks the position of the tread wear indicator, a small raised bar in the main grooves. Honda's manual says that if you can see the indicator, you should replace the tyre immediately, and Honda's aftersales article says the same about the wear bar. Find the mark, look into the groove beside it and see whether the tread has worn level with the bar.

## Other things moulded on the sidewall

You may also see:

- **A brand and model name,** which tells you nothing about condition.
- **A manufacturing date code,** often a four-digit number showing the week and year, on many tyres. Honda notes that tyres in use for more than five years should be inspected periodically, and older tyres are more likely to fail in extreme temperatures. A faded or missing code is not a fault on its own, but hard, cracked rubber is.
- **Arrows showing the direction of rotation,** which should point the way the wheel turns.
- **TUBELESS or TUBE TYPE markings,** where present. Honda describes the Wave RSX FI tyres as having inner tubes, so a tube-type tyre is expected there.

## A handover check using the label

Take two minutes with the shop's staff:

1. **Read the front and rear sidewalls.** Do they match 70/90-17 and 80/90-17 for the models above?
2. **Find the TWI marks** and check the tread is clearly above the wear bars.
3. **Ask the pressure figure for your bike** with and without a passenger.
4. **Ask staff to check the pressure with a gauge,** cold, and to inflate if needed.
5. **Look for cracks, bulges and objects** around the whole tyre.
6. **Check the valve caps** are present and the valves are straight.
7. **Photograph the sidewalls** with the size marking, to record what was on the bike.

If anything is odd, ask for another bike. ## FAQ: reading a tyre label

### What does 70/90-17 mean?

A tyre 70 mm wide, with a sidewall height of 90 per cent of that width, for a 17-inch rim.

### What does M/C mean?

That the tyre is made for motorcycles.

### What do 38P and 50P mean?

The numbers are load indexes, which correspond to 132 kg for 38 and 190 kg for 50 on the standard chart. The letter P is the speed symbol, 150 km/h.

### What pressure should my tyres be?

It depends on the model. For the Honda Wave RSX FI, Honda gives 200 kPa at the front and 225 kPa at the rear for a lone rider, with 280 kPa at the rear for rider and passenger.

### Are kPa, psi and kgf/cm2 different pressures?

No. They are different units for the same pressure. 200 kPa is about 29 psi.

### When should I check tyre pressure?

When the tyres are cool, at least once a month, and before a long ride.
