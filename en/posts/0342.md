Bat Trang is one of the easiest craft villages to visit from Hanoi by scooter, because it lies on the east side of the Red River in Gia Lam district, about 12 to 13 kilometres from the city centre by published figures, and the roads are flat. The real planning question is not the ride but the shopping: pottery is heavy, breakable and awkward on a small bike.

This guide covers what the village is, how the pottery market and the workshops differ, a half-day plan, a route outline from the east bank, and a practical way to carry ceramics on a rental scooter without risking the bike, the pottery or your balance. It is general information, with no promises about prices, opening hours or what any shop will sell or pack.

## What Bat Trang is

Google Arts and Culture, citing the Bat Trang Museum, describes a village of about 700 years on the left bank of the Red River, 12 kilometres from the centre. Unlike most craft villages in northern Vietnam, it says, Bat Trang does not specialise in farming. Its residents devote themselves to ceramics, and as of 2023 research the vast majority of about 850 families work in pottery or in trade and services around it.

Vietnam's national tourism authority adds that the name of the commune is first recorded in 1352 in the history book Dai Viet Su Ky Toan Thu, and that villagers settled here because the land held porcelain clay and the river gave easy access by water. Ceramic making, it says, has survived many setbacks, and many kilns are now fired by gas, which helps reduce pollution. Google Arts and Culture says modern products are mostly fired in electric or gas kilns.

The result is a place that is both a working industrial village and a visitor destination. You will see kiln walls, drying racks and workshop doors, and you will also see shops aimed at tourists.

## The market, the workshops and the museum

These are three different experiences, and it helps to decide which you want before you go.

**The pottery market.** Google Arts and Culture says the Bat Trang Pottery Market was founded in 2004 as a place where residents gather to trade and display ceramics, including household items, worship objects, fine art and construction ceramics. This is where most visitors buy. Prices vary by shop and piece, so look at several stalls before you decide, and ask for the final price before you pick anything up.

**Workshops where you try it yourself.** Many places invite visitors to shape a piece on a wheel or paint a plate. We cannot confirm any workshop's current opening hours or charges, so ask on arrival. A hands-on class takes time, so decide in advance whether you would rather spend an hour shaping a bowl or spend the same hour browsing.

**A museum.** The Bat Trang Museum of Viet Soul Art, founded by the late National Artist Vu Thang, is described by Google Arts and Culture as the first government-licensed private museum in the village, licensed since 2016, with traditional ceramics spanning from the 18th and 19th centuries to the early 20th. Check its current opening arrangements before you plan around it.

The old village is the fourth thing and the one most often missed. Google Arts and Culture notes that Bat Trang's temples, pagodas and communal house have been recognised as national cultural heritage, so a short walk through the lanes away from the market pays off.

## Getting there by scooter

Bat Trang is in Gia Lam district, a little south-east of the city centre. Simple English Wikipedia describes it as lying across the Chuong Duong Bridge, about 13 kilometres south-east of central Hanoi. If you start from the Old Quarter, that is the direction of travel. If you start on the east bank, as our shop in Long Bien does, a map app may route you without crossing the Red River at all, so let it choose and compare the result with a second map.

Our guide to [Gia Lam and the east bank](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/gia-lam-east-bank-bat-trang-by-scooter/) describes the quiet roads on this side of the river, and our [Gia Lam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/gia-lam/) and [Long Bien](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/long-bien/) area pages cover the neighbourhood. This guide focuses on the pottery itself.

Practical points for the ride:

- The final approach is through village lanes, so slow down. Pedestrians, delivery carts, small lorries and parked bikes all share the space.
- Keep to the right and avoid squeezing past a vehicle that is loading.
- Do not ride through the village at speed with a fragile load.
- If the map suggests an expressway or a ring road you are not sure about, check it. Motorcycles may not use expressways under Article 25 of Law 36/2024/QH15, in force since 1 January 2025. See our guide to [Hanoi's ring roads and highways](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-ring-roads-highways-motorbikes-allowed/).

## Carrying pottery on a rental scooter

This section is general safety advice, not a manufacturer rule. A small scooter's balance and steering are affected by every kilo you add, and ceramics are both heavy and brittle.

1. **Decide how much before you buy.** A few small bowls, cups or a tea set are realistic. A large vase, a stack of plates or a lamp base is not.

2. **Ask the seller to pack it.** Ask for firm wrapping, and for the pieces to be boxed or bagged in one compact load. If the seller offers to ship a larger purchase, consider it instead of riding with it.

3. **Keep the load low and central.** On a step-through scooter the floor space between your feet is the most stable place for a box, if the bike has one. Ask the shop whether the model you have is suitable.

4. **Do not hang bags from the handlebars.** A swinging load changes how the front wheel steers. Our guide to [carrying a passenger and luggage on a Honda Lead or Vision](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/scooter-for-two-riders-and-luggage-hanoi/) goes through the limits.

5. **Do not carry a box on your lap or against your chest.** If you fall, it is a hazard.

6. **Ride more gently on the way back.** Brake earlier, take bumps slowly and keep both hands on the grips. Speed bumps hurt pottery.

7. **Stop and look.** If a strap slips or a box shifts, pull over and fix it safely.

If you are travelling with a pillion, remember the pillion also needs space and balance. And if you buy a lot, be honest: a taxi or a parcel service may be the better way to bring it home, with the scooter ridden back empty.

## FAQ: Bat Trang by scooter

### How far is Bat Trang from central Hanoi?

Published figures are about 12 to 13 kilometres, on the left bank of the Red River in Gia Lam district.

### Do I need to book a workshop?

We cannot confirm any workshop's booking rules. Ask on arrival, or check with the venue first if you have a fixed time.

### Can I take a big vase home on a scooter?

Probably not safely. Choose smaller pieces, or ask the seller to ship a large one.

### Are the shops and market open every day?

We cannot confirm opening days or hours, so check on the day.

### Is it better to go on a weekday?

Weekdays tend to be quieter, according to one commercial guide. Weekends are busier.

### Do I need to cross the Red River from Long Bien?

Not necessarily, since Bat Trang is on the same bank as our shop. Check the route on a map app.
