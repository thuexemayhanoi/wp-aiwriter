Before you ride a rented manual bike after dark, test every light while it is still in the shop's hands: switch the ignition on, check the dashboard indicators, flick the headlight between low and high beam, squeeze each brake separately to see the brake lamp, run all four turn signals and press the horn. Do it once in daylight at pickup and again at dusk if you will ride later, because a bulb can fail between the two. If anything does not work, ask for another bike or a repair before you leave, since Honda's manual says a replacement bulb must be tested before riding and that an indicator which does not light properly should be looked at by a mechanic. This guide gives the order of the checks, what a good result looks like, how to spot problems that a quick glance misses and what to do when something fails. It is general information, not an inspection.

## Why the lights deserve their own check

Most pickup checklists mention lights in one line, yet on a small manual bike they are the main way other riders find you at night. A Honda Wave is narrow and dark-coloured bikes are easy to lose among brighter traffic and shop-front glare. The single headlight and the single tail and brake lamp are the whole of your visibility, so one dead bulb removes half of it.

Honda's Vietnam owner's manuals put this in plain terms. The pre-ride inspection for the Wave RSX FI lists lights, indicators and horn as items to check for correct function before each ride, and the general safety section tells riders to wear reflective clothing at night, signal before turning or changing lane and use the horn when necessary. The shop can fix a bulb in minutes at the counter. Fixing one on a dark road is a very different job.

For the wider picture of night riding, including streets to avoid and tiredness, see our guide to [riding at night in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-at-night-in-hanoi/). This page is only about the bike's lights.

## What lights a Honda Wave has

Honda's Wave RSX FI manual describes bulb replacement for five groups of lights, which is a useful map of what to test:

- **Headlight**, with a switch for high and low beam.
- **Position light**, the small light at the front.
- **Brake lamp and tail lamp**, combined in one unit at the back.
- **Two front turn signals** and **two rear turn signals**, all amber.
- **Dashboard indicators** for turn signals, high beam and neutral, among others.

Models differ. A Yamaha Sirius or Honda Blade has its own layout, and on some bikes the headlight comes on automatically with the engine while on others it is switched by hand. Ask staff how it works on the bike you are given, and do not assume a light is working because it looks like the one on a different model.

## The two-minute test, step by step

Park on the stand on level ground, put the key in and turn the ignition on. Ask a staff member to stand behind the bike while you work through the controls. It is easier with a second person, because you cannot see the tail lamp from the saddle.

| Light | How to test | What good looks like | If it fails |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dashboard indicators | Ignition on, gear lever in neutral | Neutral light glows, no warning light stays on | Ask the shop to check it |
| Headlight, low beam | Switch to low beam | Steady, white-yellow beam that does not flicker | Ask for another bike |
| Headlight, high beam | Switch to high beam and back | Beam changes and the high-beam indicator lights | Ask the shop to look at the switch |
| Position light | Look at the front from the side | A small light shows at the front | Report it |
| Brake lamp, rear pedal | Press only the rear pedal | Rear lamp brightens at once | Ask for the switch to be checked |
| Brake lamp, front lever | Squeeze only the front lever | Rear lamp brightens at once | Ask for the switch to be checked |
| Turn signals | Left, then right | All four flash, dashboard indicator ticks | Report which one is dead |
| Horn | Press the button briefly | A clear, firm sound | Ask for repair |

Honda's manual states that the brake lamp lights when you use the brakes, and that the brake lamp switch can be adjusted if it works too late or too early. That adjustment is a mechanic's job, but you can notice the symptom: a lamp that only comes on after the pedal has travelled far, or after a hard squeeze on the lever, gives following riders too little warning.

## Headlight: beam, switch and aim

Look at the beam rather than only checking that it is on. Stand the bike a few metres from a wall or garage door in dim light and compare low and high beam. The low beam should light the ground ahead of the bike without blasting at the height of a rider's face. The high beam should be clearly brighter and higher. If the beam points at the sky or at the ground a metre in front of the wheel, the headlight aim is off. Honda's manual lists headlight aim as a periodic inspection item, so a mechanic can correct it.

A heavy load on the back can tip the beam upward, which dazzles oncoming riders. If you will carry a bag or a passenger at night, ask staff to look at the aim with that load on the bike. Our guide to [how much luggage you can safely carry on a Honda Wave](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-much-luggage-can-you-safely-carry-on-a-honda-wave/) explains why weight moves the beam.

Also check that the dipping switch moves cleanly. A switch that sticks halfway can leave you on high beam in traffic.

## Brake lamp: test each brake separately

The brake lamp is the light riders most often discover is dead from the car behind them. Test the front lever and the rear pedal one at a time, because each has its own switch. A bike can show a lamp with the rear pedal and show nothing with the front lever.

With a helper behind the bike, squeeze lightly. A good lamp reacts early, before the brake has started to grip. Ask the helper to say "now" the moment the lamp changes, and compare the two brakes. If the lamp is slow on one, report it as a fault. Our guide to [checking the rear brake on a Honda Wave](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/checking-the-rear-brake-on-a-honda-wave/) explains how pedal travel and the lamp relate.

## Turn signals and the dashboard

Switch left, then right, and watch all four lamps. A signal that is dead, dim or very slow is easy to miss from the saddle. Many riders also notice a different tick from the indicator on the dashboard when one bulb has blown. That is a general observation and not a Honda statement, so confirm it by looking at the lamps.

Make sure the switch cancels. Honda's manual says to press the switch in to cancel the signal, and a signal that keeps flashing after a turn confuses everyone around you.

Then look at the whole dashboard with the ignition on. Honda says that if an indicator does not light as it should, the bike should be taken to an authorised dealer. In practice, tell the shop and ask what the light means. Our explanation of [semi-automatic motorbike dashboard lights](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/semi-automatic-motorbike-dashboard-lights-explained/) shows what the common ones look like.

## Horn: the forgotten light-check partner

The horn is not a light, but Honda groups lights and horn in a single pre-ride check, and at night the two work together. Press it briefly while parked. Vietnam's traffic law allows the horn to warn others of a hazard or to signal an intention to overtake, and it bars continuous use and use in populous areas and near medical facilities between 22:00 and 05:00 (Law 36/2024/QH15, Article 21). In practice that means: confirm that it works, then use short, deliberate taps at night.

## Glass, mounting and moisture

A bulb that works can still give a poor light. Look at each lens and the headlight housing:

- **Cracks or holes** let in water and scatter the beam.
- **Dirt or a sticker over the lens** cuts brightness. Wipe it before you set off.
- **A loose headlight case** can vibrate and shift the aim. Press gently. It should not rattle.
- **Tape on a lens** is a sign of an earlier repair. Ask what happened.
- **Misting inside the headlight** can occur after washing or rain. Honda's Wave manual says that misting can appear after washing or in rain and clears within a few minutes once the engine is running and the light is on, but that heavy water or ice inside the lens means the bike should be taken to a dealer.

## Test the battery with the lights on

A weak battery shows itself at night, when the headlight, brake lamp and signals all draw current together. Honda Global suggests a simple battery check: turn the key on with the headlight, turn signals and brake lamp on, then try to start the engine. If the starter turns strongly, the battery is probably fine. If it sounds weak or will not turn, treat it as a warning.

Do this on the stand, at the shop. Our guide to [what to check on a manual bike's electric starter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-to-check-on-a-manual-bikes-electric-starter/) covers the starter, and the kick starter on older bikes is a backup.

## What the traffic law expects of your lights

Vietnam's Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (Law 36/2024/QH15), in force since 1 January 2025, covers lights in Article 20. As of October 2026 it says that drivers must turn on the front lights between 18:00 and 06:00, or in fog, smoke, dust, rain or other bad weather that limits visibility. It also says that drivers must switch from high beam to low beam when they meet pedestrians crossing, on road sections through populous areas with working street lighting, when they meet oncoming traffic (unless the median is anti-glare) and when turning at an intersection.

For a rider this means two habits. Switch the headlight on at 18:00, even if the sky is still bright, and use low beam in the lit city. High beam belongs on dark open roads when nobody is coming. ## If something fails

Report faults at the counter, not after you have left. Say which light, describe what happens and ask for another bike or a repair. Honda's manual describes how a mechanic replaces each bulb: switch the ignition off, let the bulb cool, use only the specified bulb, avoid touching the glass of the headlight bulb and test the new bulb before riding. Those are steps for the shop, not for a renter in the street.

Do not ride away with a known dead headlight or brake lamp and plan to "be careful". A bike that cannot be seen is the main risk after dark. If the shop cannot supply an alternative before dusk, change your plan, perhaps with a daytime ride and an early return.

## A dusk checklist you can screenshot

1. Ignition on, neutral light on, no warning light staying lit.
2. Headlight low and high beam both work, and the beam looks level.
3. Position light on.
4. Brake lamp lights with the rear pedal and with the front lever.
5. All four turn signals flash and the switch cancels.
6. Horn works.
7. Lenses clean, uncracked and firmly mounted.
8. Starter turns strongly with lights on.
9. Headlight switched on from 18:00, low beam in the lit city.
10. Phone charged and the shop's number saved.

## FAQ: lights on a manual bike at night

### Do I have to switch on the headlight in the evening?

Yes. Article 20 of Law 36/2024/QH15 requires front lights to be on between 18:00 and 06:00 and in poor visibility.

### Which light is most often dead without the rider noticing?

The brake lamp, because you cannot see it from the saddle. Test each brake separately with a helper.

### Can I change a bulb myself?

Not on a rental. Honda's manual describes a procedure for mechanics, and a wrong bulb or a touched headlight bulb can cause problems.

### Is the headlight always on at night?

It depends on the bike. Ask staff how the switch works on the bike you are given.

### What if one turn signal flashes much faster than normal?

Treat it as a possible dead bulb and ask the shop to look at it. That is a common sign, but only a check can confirm it.

### Should I use high beam in the city?

Generally no. The law asks for low beam on lit sections through populous areas and when meeting other traffic.
