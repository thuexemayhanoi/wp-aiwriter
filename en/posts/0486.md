For a short scooter trip in Hanoi, wear a properly fastened helmet, long trousers, closed shoes that cover the ankle, light gloves and a long-sleeved top or thin jacket, and keep everything snug enough that nothing can catch on the bike. Of these, only the helmet is a legal requirement: Article 33 of Law 36/2024/QH15 requires the driver and every person carried to wear a helmet that meets the national technical regulation with the chin strap fastened. The rest is a safety choice, and a sensible one. Shorts, flip-flops and a T-shirt give almost no protection against road rash or against hot parts of the bike, and most falls happen at low speed on short trips.

This guide explains why short trips still need clothing, lists the basics in order of importance, shows how to stay cool in Hanoi's heat without giving up protection, covers visibility and rain, tells you what to avoid and what to ask the shop. It is general information based on the law and published rider guidance as of October 2026, not legal or medical advice.

## Why a short trip still needs protection

A fall at city speed can still skid you along the road. Riders and rider-education writers make the same point: your skin has no protection against asphalt, and your hands, knees, elbows and ankles are the first things to meet the ground. The World Health Organization notes that more than half of all road traffic deaths are among pedestrians, cyclists and motorcyclists, and that correct helmet use cuts the risk of death by more than six times. Clothing does not stop every injury, but it can reduce the severity of the ones that happen most often.

A short trip also tends to be a casual one. People ride to a cafe in sandals, to the market in shorts or to dinner in a dress, because it is only ten minutes. That is exactly when protection is least likely to be worn, and ten minutes through Hanoi's traffic is not a safe place to be unprotected.

## The basics, in order of importance

| Item | Why | What to choose |
|---|---|---|
| **Helmet** | Protects your head; the only item the law requires | A snug, certified helmet, strap fastened. See our guide to [how to fit and fasten a motorbike helmet properly](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-fit-and-fasten-a-motorbike-helmet-properly/) |
| **Closed shoes** | Protect toes and ankles; give grip on the footboard | Shoes or light boots that cover the ankle, with non-slip soles |
| **Long trousers** | Reduce road rash; protect your legs from hot parts | Jeans or thicker cotton as a minimum; fitted at the ankle |
| **Gloves** | Your hands go out first in a fall; they also reduce sunburn and grip sweat | Full-finger gloves with a firm palm |
| **Jacket or long sleeves** | Protect your arms and shoulders | A thin jacket with long sleeves; sturdier is better |
| **Eye protection** | Wind, dust and insects | A helmet visor or sunglasses |
| **Visibility** | Others must see you | Light or bright colours, and reflective strips |

Rider-guidance writers recommend abrasion-resistant materials, such as leather or reinforced textile, with armour at the elbows, shoulders and knees, and closed shoes or boots that cover the ankles and give a secure grip on the footrests. They also note that regular jeans offer only modest abrasion resistance, so riding jeans or proper riding trousers are better, and that even a jacket without armour is better than none. For a short rental trip in Hanoi you may not own such gear, so the practical goal is to wear the best ordinary clothing you have, not to skip protection.

## Staying cool in Hanoi's heat

Hanoi is hot and humid for much of the year, and riders are tempted to dress down. Dressing down is not the only way to stay cool.

- **Choose breathable fabrics.** Light cotton and technical fabrics are cooler than heavy denim, and mesh jackets give airflow.

- **Wear light colours.** They reflect heat and are easier to see. Guidance on heat illness advises loose, lightweight, light-coloured clothing, but on a bike loose means something that can flap, so use layers that are light but fitted.

- **Cover your skin from the sun.** A thin long-sleeved top is cooler than you might expect, and it reduces sunburn.

- **Drink water before and after the trip.** Our guide to [heat and dehydration on a motorbike day trip](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/heat-and-dehydration-on-a-motorbike-day-trip/) explains what to watch for.

- **Take a cool break.** Stop in shade, and let your helmet air out.

- **Use the shade and plan around midday,** if you can.

If the weather is hot enough that you do not want to wear proper clothes, consider whether a scooter is the right choice. Our guide to [choosing between electric and petrol vehicles in hot weather](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/choosing-between-electric-and-petrol-vehicles-in-hot-weather/) and our guide to [when to choose walking or public transport over a 50cc rental](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/when-to-choose-walking-or-public-transport-over-a-50cc-rental/) help you weigh it.

## Being seen

- **Wear bright or light clothing** by day, and reflective strips, patches or a light vest at night.

- **Use your lights,** and keep the plate and tail light visible. Our guide to [using a rain poncho without obscuring lights or wheels](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/using-a-rain-poncho-without-obscuring-lights-or-wheels/) explains how to avoid hiding them.

- **Choose a light helmet colour,** if you have a choice.

Rider-safety guidance stresses that dark clothing makes you harder to see, especially at night, and that reflective material on your clothing and helmet helps. ## What to avoid

- **Flip-flops, sandals and slip-ons.** They can slip off or catch on the footboard, and they protect nothing.

- **Shorts and short skirts.** Legs are close to the exhaust and the stand, and skin has no protection in a slide.

- **Long scarves, shawls and loose ends.** They can catch in moving parts. Gear commentary advises avoiding scarves unless they are short and tucked in. Our guide to [why loose scarves and bags are dangerous on motorbikes](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/why-loose-scarves-and-bags-are-dangerous-on-motorbikes/) explains how.

- **Wide trouser hems and long dresses** that can catch on the pedal, the stand or the wheel. Tuck or fasten them.

- **Heavy backpacks** that shift your balance.

- **Umbrellas.** Article 33 bans their use while riding.

- **Earphones.** Article 33 bans sound devices for riders other than hearing aids.

- **Novelty or loose headgear** under the helmet, which changes the fit.

## Helmet and eyes

The helmet is the most important item, and the shop's published terms say one basic helmet per rider is included at no charge. Check that it fits, and that the strap is fastened. If it does not fit, ask for another, as our guide to [what to do if a helmet does not fit at pickup](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-to-do-if-a-helmet-does-not-fit-at-pickup/) explains. Wear sunglasses or a visor, because wind and dust in the eyes lead to squinting and slow reactions. Do not wear dark glasses at night.

## Children and passengers

A passenger needs the same protection as the rider, including a helmet, closed shoes and long trousers. A child's helmet must fit properly. 2. **Closed shoes with grip.**

## FAQ: clothing for short scooter trips

### What does Vietnamese law require me to wear on a scooter?

Article 33 of Law 36/2024/QH15 requires a helmet that meets the national technical regulation, with the chin strap fastened, for the driver and every person carried. It does not require other clothing, but protective clothing is sensible.

### Can I ride in shorts and sandals?

The law does not prohibit it, but it is unsafe. Shorts and sandals offer no protection in a fall and sandals can slip off.

### Do I need gloves?

They are not legally required, but your hands are often the first to hit the ground. Light full-finger gloves reduce injury and sunburn.

### What should I wear when it is very hot?

Light, breathable, fitted layers with long sleeves and long trousers, plus water and shade breaks. Our guide to heat and dehydration explains the warning signs.

### Is a raincoat provided?

The published terms say not for short rentals. Ask the shop.

### Can I wear a dress or skirt?

A long skirt can catch. If you must, wear leggings or shorts underneath, tuck it in and avoid wide hems.
