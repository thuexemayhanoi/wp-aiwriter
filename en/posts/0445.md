To avoid missing your agreed return time, treat the time on your contract as a deadline, not a target: read it exactly, plan backwards from it, aim to reach the shop well before it, refuel earlier in the day and message the shop before the deadline if anything threatens to delay you. Nguyen Ha's published terms explain why it matters. A late return is charged at VND 30,000 per hour for 1 to 5 hours late, and from 6 hours late one extra rental day is added, and telling the shop you are late does not automatically waive the charge. Any extension has to be agreed before the existing period ends.

This guide is about prevention. Our guides to [what to do if your return time changes](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-to-do-if-your-return-time-changes/) and [late rental returns: how to calculate the possible charge](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/late-rental-returns-how-to-calculate-the-possible-charge/) cover what happens after plans slip. Here we cover how to stop them slipping: how to read the time, how to build a buffer, how to plan the last leg, what to do the evening before and what to say to the shop if you are running late. It is general information, not legal advice.

## What the published terms say about time

As of October 2026, these are the published points on time.

- **A rental day is 24 hours.** Shorter use is charged as a full day.
- **Late return:** VND 30,000 per hour for 1 to 5 hours late. From 6 hours late, one extra rental day is added at that bike's daily rate.
- **Advance notice does not automatically waive** a late charge.
- **Early return** does not automatically refund unused rent.
- **Extensions** must be agreed before the existing period ends, including the price and the return arrangements.
- **Opening hours** are 09:00 to 21:00, every day.

The published pages do not say what happens if you arrive after 21:00, or whether a bike can be left outside opening hours. Ask before you need to know. Our guide to [what to do if the rental shop is closed when you arrive](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-to-do-if-the-rental-shop-is-closed-when-you-arrive/) covers the problem from the pickup side.

## Step 1: read the return time properly

Many missed returns start with a misreading rather than a delay.

1. **Find the return time on the contract,** not just the date. A date alone leaves the hour open.
2. **Check how the day is counted.** Because a rental day is 24 hours, a one-day rental that starts at 15:00 would normally run to 15:00 the next day, but only the contract can tell you for certain.
3. **Check the shop's closing time.** A return time of 22:00 would fall outside the published hours, so a time that looks odd is worth querying.
4. **Check the place.** Confirm that the return is at the shop and not somewhere else.
5. **Write it down twice:** in your phone calendar and on paper with your other documents.

If you are unsure, ask the shop to confirm the return time by message, so that it is on record. Our guide to [how to check the rental shop's opening hours and pickup location](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-check-the-rental-shops-opening-hours-and-pickup-location/) explains how.

## Step 2: plan backwards from the deadline

Work backwards from the return time, not forwards from your sightseeing. The table shows an example for a return due at 18:00. The times are illustrations, not recommendations, so adjust them to your own trip.

| Step | Example time | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Return due | 18:00 | The time on the contract |
| Arrive at the shop | 17:15 | Gives room for the inspection and the paperwork |
| Refuel | 16:45 | A stop that can overrun without costing you the deadline |
| Leave your last stop | 16:15 | Includes a margin for traffic |
| Final sightseeing ends | 15:45 | Your real finish time |

The point is the pattern: the shop arrival is earlier than the deadline, the fuel stop is earlier than the arrival and the last stop is earlier than the fuel stop. ## Step 3: build a buffer for what you cannot predict

Return times are most often missed by small things that add up.

- **Traffic** can be slower than you expect, especially in a city you do not know.
- **Navigation** can take you the wrong way, or to a place that looks close and is not.
- **Weather** can force you to stop, and stopping is sometimes the safer choice. Our guide to [riding in Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) is a good starting point.
- **A fuel queue or a closed station** can cost time.
- **A puncture or a fault** can stop you altogether. Our guide to [punctured tyre during a rental: who to contact](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/punctured-tyre-during-a-rental-who-to-contact/) explains what to do.
- **Getting lost on the way back** after dark. Our guide to [avoiding night riding on a Hanoi return trip](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/avoiding-night-riding-on-a-hanoi-return-trip/) covers the problem.

A buffer of an hour or more on a day with a firm deadline is cheap compared with a late-return charge. Our guide to [how to estimate travel time conservatively on a 50cc moped](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-estimate-travel-time-conservatively-on-a-50cc-moped/) explains how to avoid optimistic estimates, and our guide to [returning to Hanoi from the countryside in afternoon traffic](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/returning-to-hanoi-from-the-countryside-in-afternoon-traffic/) covers a typical trap.

## Step 4: refuel before the last stretch

For a petrol bike, the published terms say it comes back with about 0.5 to 1 litre and that an empty tank at return costs VND 20,000. A fuel stop is easy to forget, and a queue at the last minute is a classic cause of lateness. So:

- **Check the fuel gauge at lunchtime,** not at the end of the day.
- **Refuel in the early afternoon** if the day is long.
- **Do not leave it to the last stretch** into the city.

For an electric bike, plan where you will charge, and see our guide to [what to check before returning an electric scooter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-to-check-before-returning-an-electric-scooter/).

## Step 5: set reminders you cannot ignore

You do not need an app, only two alarms.

1. **An early warning,** set for the time you must start heading back.
2. **A hard stop,** set for the latest time you can leave your last stop.

Give each a label such as "start heading back" and "leave now, return due 18:00". Save the shop's number, 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969), in your phone before you start, so that a call or message takes seconds. A reminder that says only "return the bike" is easy to dismiss.

## Step 6: plan the last day around your onward travel

Many tight returns happen on the last day, when the bike has to be back before a bus, a train or a flight.

- **Do not put the return and the onward journey back to back.** Leave a gap for a delay, the inspection and getting from the shop to your next stop.
- **Allow for the walk or ride from the shop.** You will not have the bike for the last leg.
- **Consider returning the day before,** or the morning of departure if the shop is open, and spend the last day without a bike.
- **Do not count on delivery or collection** unless you have agreed it. The published terms offer it only for eligible weekly and monthly agreements within a stated distance, and say there is no airport delivery or pickup. For short rentals, plan to bring the bike to the shop yourself.

## The evening before the return

A short check the evening before can prevent a morning scramble.

1. **Confirm the return time and place** with the shop, in writing.
2. **Check the route** and the likely time it takes.
3. **Check the fuel** and plan where to stop.
4. **Gather everything to hand back:** keys, helmets and any other items. Our guide to [what to photograph when returning a rental scooter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-to-photograph-when-returning-a-rental-scooter/) lists the return photographs.
5. **Set the two alarms.**
6. **Decide the latest time you will call the shop** if things are running late.

## If you are going to be late anyway

If, despite your planning, you will not make it:

1. **Tell the shop as soon as you know,** and not after the deadline. Give your name, the plate number, where you are and a realistic new time.
2. **Ask whether an extension can be agreed,** and what it would cost. The published terms say any extension must be agreed before the existing period ends, including the price and the return arrangements.
3. **Do not assume that a message cancels the charge.** The published terms say advance notice does not automatically waive it.
4. **Get any agreement in writing,** as a message.
5. **Keep riding safely.** Do not rush, run a red light or ride tired to save minutes. A late charge costs far less than a fine or an accident.
6. **If the problem is a breakdown or an accident,** deal with safety first and report it. Our guide to [what to do after a minor collision on a rental scooter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-to-do-after-a-minor-collision-on-a-rental-scooter/) explains the steps.

For longer rentals, our guide to [extending a monthly motorbike rental contract: questions to ask](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/extending-a-monthly-motorbike-rental-contract-questions/) explains how to ask for more time.

## What the Civil Code says about late return

Vietnam's Civil Code (Law No. 91/2015/QH13), in force since 1 January 2017, says in Article 482 that if the lessee returns the property late, the lessor may demand its return and the rent for the period of delay, together with compensation for damage, and may apply any contractual penalty the parties agreed. It also says the lessee bears the risk to the property during the delay. We read the lease articles in the text published by the Tuyen Quang Department of Justice. As of October 2026, that is the general rule, and your contract sets the specific charges. This is general information, not legal advice.

The practical point is that being late can do more than cost a fee. If the bike is damaged or lost while you are late, the risk is on you. That is a further reason to tell the shop early and to agree any extra time in writing.

## Quick scenarios

| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| You will be 20 minutes late | Message the shop now, give a new time, and ride safely |
| You will be several hours late | Message now, ask about an extension and its cost, and get it in writing |
| You want an extra day | Ask before the existing period ends, and agree the price and return arrangements |
| The bike breaks down | Stop safely, contact the shop, and keep photographs and messages |

## A quick checklist

1. **Read the return time and place on the contract.**
2. **Confirm it in writing.**
3. **Plan backwards from the deadline, with a buffer.**
4. **Refuel early.**
5. **Set two alarms, and save the shop's number.**
6. **Keep a gap before any onward travel.**
7. **Message the shop before the deadline if you will be late.**
8. **Get any extension in writing.**

## FAQ: avoiding a missed return time

### What is the late-return charge?

The published terms say VND 30,000 per hour for 1 to 5 hours late, and one extra rental day from 6 hours late.

### Does telling the shop in advance cancel the charge?

Not automatically. The published terms say advance notice does not automatically waive it. It does give the shop and you a chance to agree something.

### Can I extend my rental?

Any extension must be agreed before the existing period ends, including the price and the return arrangements. Ask early, and get the answer in writing.

### How early should I plan to arrive?

Early enough for the inspection and the paperwork, with a buffer for delays. An hour before the deadline is a sensible starting point for a firm deadline.

### What if I return after the shop closes?

The shop's published hours are 09:00 to 21:00 daily. The published pages do not describe out-of-hours returns, so ask in advance.

### Does returning early get me a refund?

Not automatically. The published terms say early return does not refund unused rent.
