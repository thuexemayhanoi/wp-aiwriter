The under-seat compartment of a rental scooter is a useful luggage box, not a safe. Honda's owner manuals for the Vision, Lead and Air Blade give it a maximum load of 10 kg and say plainly not to keep valuables or fragile items in it, nor anything flammable or easily damaged by heat. So the practical rule is to use it for a helmet, a rain layer, a charger and light shopping, to keep passports, cash and cameras on your body, and to check the box, the latch and the key arrangement before you ride away. This guide explains what the manuals say, what actually goes wrong with stored items, how smart-key scooters change the picture, and what to do at parking, at handover and at return.

## What the compartment is designed to do

On the automatic scooters in the Nguyen Ha fleet range, such as the Honda Vision, Lead and Air Blade, the box sits under the seat and opens when you lift the seat. The Honda manuals describe it consistently: a load limit of 10 kg, a place for a helmet turned so its front faces the front of the bike, and, on several models, a bag for the vehicle documents and a small tool kit stored inside. The Lead and Air Blade manuals also describe a divider that can be moved or removed, and a light that comes on inside the box.

Three points matter for protecting your things.

- **It is a shared space.** The tool kit and a document pouch may already live in there. Do not remove them and do not pile your own items on top of them.
- **It is not sealed against water.** Honda's washing instructions warn against spraying water under the seat because water in the box can damage documents and other contents. A box that cannot take a hose is not a dry bag in a downpour.
- **It is not a locked vault.** The seat is opened with the key or, on smart-key models, from the ignition area while the key system is active. Anyone who has the key, or the bike running, can open it.

## Honda's limits in plain English

Here is what the manuals set out, checked against the Vision (December 2025 edition), the Lead 125 (November 2025 edition) and the Air Blade (September 2025 edition). Models and years differ, so the sticker or manual for your actual bike has the last word.

| Place | Honda's maximum | Honda's wording in brief |
|---|---|---|
| Under-seat box | 10 kg | Do not exceed the limit; no valuables or fragile items; nothing flammable or heat-sensitive |
| Front hook and front pocket (Vision, Lead) | 1.5 kg | Do not hang bulky goods on the hook, because they can slip or catch your leg |
| Rear grab handle (Vision, Lead) | 3 kg | Do not exceed the stated load |

The Air Blade manual, by contrast, gives only the 10 kg figure for the under-seat box. Whatever the bike, the total weight of rider, passenger and luggage has its own limit, shown on the tyre and loading label. ## What belongs in the box and what does not

A sensible split follows the manual's wording and common sense.

**Good candidates for the box**

- A spare layer or a thin rain jacket, folded flat.
- A phone charger cable and a plain cloth for wiping the seat or mirror.
- A small bag of shopping that is not fragile, such as bottled water or fruit in a sturdy bag.
- A helmet, if it fits. Honda notes that some helmet sizes and designs do not fit inside.

**Keep on your body or in a bag you carry**

- Passport, phone, cash and bank cards.
- The rental contract copy, if you want it close at hand. The shop photographs identity documents for the contract and keeps its own record, so you do not need to leave originals in the box.
- Cameras, laptops, glasses and anything you would be upset to lose.
- Prescription medicine, which you will want if the bike is away from you.

**Avoid altogether**

- Lighters, gas canisters, aerosol cans, petrol in a bottle and similar flammables.
- Phones or power banks left to bake on a hot afternoon. Honda's warning is about heat-sensitive items in general, and a dark seat in direct sun is a good example of a hot environment. A sealed lithium battery pack left in a closed compartment in summer heat is a poor idea as a matter of general caution.
- Open drinks, sauces or anything that can leak, because a spill soaks the contents and the document pouch.
- Anything sharp that could puncture a bag or scratch the plastic.

## Smart key scooters: where the key goes

Many current Honda scooters use a smart-key fob. The Vision, Lead and Air Blade manuals each carry specific warnings about it, and they matter to anyone who stores things.

1. **Do not put the fob inside any storage box.** The Vision and Air Blade manuals say that if the ignition is on and the fob is in the box, anyone can operate the bike, because the system will not be checking for the fob in a place the rider has left.
2. **Do not store a phone or other radio-emitting device in the box.** Honda says that radio waves from such devices can interfere with the smart-key system. This is a reason to keep your phone in a pocket and not under the seat.
3. **Do not keep the emergency key in a storage box.** The manuals describe the emergency key as the backup that opens the seat, so storing it where it can be locked away from you defeats its purpose.
4. **Do not close the seat with the key inside.** Honda's manuals warn against forgetting the key in the box when closing the seat. On a traditional key model, check the key is in your hand before you press the seat down.

If the shop has handed you a spare key, ask whether the box is a safe place to leave it. The usual answer on a smart-key bike is no.

## Heat, rain and washing

Hanoi summers are hot and humid, and the wet season brings heavy showers. Neither is rare, so treat the box as a place that can be hot and can get damp.

- **Heat.** A seat in direct sun heats the box. Electronics, sunscreen, medicines and chocolate suffer first. Park in shade when you can and do not leave heat-sensitive items inside for hours.
- **Rain.** Honda's manuals say water in the box can damage documents and other contents. In a long downpour, or if the bike is washed, put anything that must stay dry in a plastic bag or a zipped pouch, and place it in the middle, away from the seat edges.
- **Washing.** If you take the scooter for a wash, empty the box first and tell the washer not to spray under the seat, as Honda advises.

## Ten-minute handover checks for the box

Before you leave the shop, spend a few minutes on the compartment. They fit naturally into the wider routine in our [10-minute rental scooter inspection](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-scooter-inspection-checklist/).

1. **Open and close the seat twice.** It should lift smoothly and latch with a clear click. Honda tells riders to test it by lifting the seat gently after closing, to be sure it is locked.
2. **Look inside.** Note what is there already: tool kit, document pouch, divider. Take a photograph so you can show the box as it was.
3. **Check for damage.** A cracked hinge, a loose rubber seal or a broken latch is a good reason to ask for another bike.
4. **Check cleanliness.** A damp or sticky box is a risk to your luggage.
5. **Try your helmet.** If the shop's basic helmet does not fit inside, plan where else it will go. The helmet hook under the seat is for parked use only; Honda warns not to ride with a helmet hung on it.
6. **Ask who holds a spare key,** and whether a smart-key fob is the only one.
7. **Confirm the rules for the bike's tools.** The tool kit is part of the bike, so it should be there when you return.

## Parking habits that protect what is inside

Whatever the box holds, good parking is the best protection. The shop does not provide an extra lock, so lock the steering every time and park in guarded places, as the shop recommends. Our guide to [parking a motorbike in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-motorbike-in-hanoi/) explains guarded lots, tickets and towing.

A few habits help:

- **Do not leave bags on the seat or hooked on the handlebar** when you walk away. Someone passing sees the bag and not the lock.
- **Move valuables before you park,** not after. If you open the seat at the kerb and start sorting things, you show passers-by what is inside.
- **Take the key or fob with you.** Do not hide a spare on the bike.
- **Choose a guarded lot** for long stops such as a museum, a restaurant or an evening out. For a five-minute coffee stop in sight of the bike, the risk is lower, but nothing valuable should be left behind.
- **Use the front hook for light items only,** within the 1.5 kg limit, and take heavier shopping into the box.

## Packing the box well for a day out

A tidy box protects your things and keeps the bike stable.

- **Heavy items low and central.** The box is under the seat, so a heavy bag in the box is lower than the same bag on the floor board. Do not put a single dense item at the back edge.
- **Soft items around hard ones.** A rolled jacket stops bottles rattling and a thin towel stops chargers scratching.
- **Bags in a bag.** A mesh or fabric bag lets you lift everything out in one go.
- **Check the seat shuts without force.** If you have to push hard, the load is too large and the hinge or seat latch will suffer.
- **Check the weight.** A rough guide: if you struggle to lift the bag with one hand, it may be near the 10 kg limit.

## At return: empty it, then check it

Many lost items are simply left behind in the box. Before returning the bike, open the seat, remove your own belongings, and check the tool kit and the document pouch are in place. ## If something goes wrong

- **The seat will not open.** Do not force it or prise it. Turn the handlebar straight, because the manuals describe opening the seat with the handlebar pointing forward, and try again. If it still refuses, call the shop.
- **An item is missing.** Note when you last saw it, tell the shop promptly and, if it is a serious loss, report it to the local authorities in the usual way. Do not accuse anyone; give the facts.
- **The key is trapped in the box.** Stay with the bike and call the shop. Our guide to [lost keys, flat tyres and breakdowns with a rental bike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-bike-breakdown-hanoi-what-to-do/) explains the steps.
- **The box is wet.** Dry it with a cloth, leave the seat open in shade for a few minutes and report damage to the bike, not just to your things.

## FAQ: under-seat storage on a rented scooter

### How much can I put in the under-seat box?

Honda's manuals for the Vision, Lead and Air Blade give 10 kg as the maximum. Check the manual or label for your actual bike, because other models differ.

### Is it safe to leave my passport in the box?

No. Honda's manuals say not to keep valuables in the box, and the box opens with the key or the smart-key system. Keep your passport and cash with you.

### Can I leave my helmet in the box?

Only if it fits. Honda says some helmets are too large or oddly shaped. The helmet hook is for use while parked, not while riding.

### Can I charge my phone from the box?

Some models have a USB socket in the box or in the front pocket, and Honda says it is for charging only. Keep the cover closed when not in use and avoid wet conditions. Phones should not be stored in the box on a smart-key bike.

### Will the box keep things dry in rain?

Not reliably. Honda warns that water in the box can damage documents and other items. Use a plastic bag or zipped pouch for anything that must stay dry.

### What should I do if the seat will not close?

Do not slam it. Check that nothing is trapped at the edge, that the load is within the limit and that the key is not in the way. If it still will not latch, ask the shop.
