Before you collect a rental scooter in Hanoi, send the shop a short message with six things: your exact pickup date and the time you expect to arrive, how long you want the bike, how many people will ride, the type of bike you prefer, the licence you hold and where you plan to ride. Keep documents, card details and payment for the shop to request through its own channel. A clear message of this kind lets the shop confirm a suitable bike, tell you the deposit and have the contract ready, so the counter visit takes minutes instead of an hour.

Some shops want more, some less. The aim is to give enough information that a bike can be set aside for you, and not so much that you hand sensitive data to a stranger over an unsecured chat.

## What the shop needs to know, in order of importance

Think of the shop's problem. It has a limited number of bikes, each in a different state, and every booking must end with a contract and a deposit. Anything that helps it match you to a bike and prepare the paperwork is worth sending. Anything it cannot use is noise.

### 1. Your pickup day and a realistic arrival time

Give the date and a time window. Nguyen Ha Motorbike Rental is open 09:00 to 21:00 daily, so a pickup outside those hours is not possible without a prior agreement. Remember that a rental day at Nguyen Ha is 24 hours and shorter use is charged as a full day, so your pickup time is also your return time the next day. If your flight lands late, say so and ask whether to collect the next morning instead of rushing from the airport with luggage. There is no airport delivery service.

### 2. How long you want the bike

State the number of days, or say "one week" or "one month". It changes which packages apply and whether delivery is possible. Delivery is offered only for weekly or monthly agreements, within 10 km of Hoan Kiem Lake, so a two-day renter should not expect the bike to arrive at a hotel. Our article on [what to ask before a weekly or monthly rental](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/weekly-monthly-motorbike-rental-hanoi/) covers the long-term questions.

### 3. Who will ride, and whether there is a passenger

Tell the shop how many people will ride, and whether one carries a bag or a child. This affects the model, because a bench that suits one rider may be tight for two. It also tells the shop how many helmets to expect; one basic helmet per rider is free at Nguyen Ha, and a better helmet for a child or a long trip is something to bring or ask about.

### 4. The type of bike you would like

Say whether you want an automatic scooter, a manual or semi-automatic bike, an electric scooter or a 50cc model, and name one or two models you would accept. Nguyen Ha's models include Honda Vision, Air Blade, Click and Lead among automatics and Honda Wave, Blade and Yamaha Sirius among manuals, but the bike actually available is confirmed when you book. Treat your preference as a request, not a guarantee. If you are unsure, our comparison of the [Honda Vision and Air Blade](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/honda-vision-vs-air-blade-which-scooter-to-rent-in-hanoi/) and of the [Honda Wave and Yamaha Sirius](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/honda-wave-vs-yamaha-sirius-rental/) will help you choose.

### 5. Your licence class and riding experience

Say which licence you hold and which country issued it, and whether you also carry an international driving permit. This is the single detail that most often decides whether a rental can go ahead. Vietnam's Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (Law 36/2024/QH15), in force from 1 January 2025, requires the rider to carry a licence suitable for the vehicle, and says holders of an international permit from a Vienna Convention country must carry both the permit and the national licence. Class A1 in the law covers two-wheeled motorcycles up to 125 cm3 or an electric motor up to 11 kW.

That is the law as read in October 2026, so confirm the current rules and the shop's own policy rather than relying on this summary. Also be honest about experience. A first-time rider is better served by a lighter automatic than by a bike that looks impressive in the listing.

### 6. Where you plan to ride

Hanoi city riding, a half-day trip to Bat Trang and a three-day loop to the hills are three different rentals. Tell the shop which one you plan, because the right bike, the fuel expectations and whether leaving the city is allowed all depend on it. Our guide to [taking a rental motorbike out of Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/take-rental-motorbike-out-of-hanoi/) explains why this matters before you ride off.

## A message you can copy and adapt

> Hello Nguyen Ha. I would like to collect a scooter on [date] at about [time], for [number] days. One rider, no passenger. I would like an automatic, such as a Honda Vision or similar. I hold a [country] licence with a motorcycle category and an international driving permit. I plan to ride in Hanoi only. Please confirm the bike, the total price for my dates, the deposit and what I need to bring.

Notice what the message does not contain: no passport number, no card details and no photo of documents. It gives the shop what it needs to decide, and it asks for confirmation of price and deposit, which is the answer you need most. The [rental guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-guide/) lists the shop's basic terms if you want to check your message against them.

## What to hold back until the shop asks

It is tempting to send everything at once. Resist that for three categories.

**Document images.** At Nguyen Ha the staff check identity documents and the licence and photograph them for the contract at handover, and the originals are not kept. Because the shop does that in person, you do not need to send a scan of your passport in advance. If a shop does ask for images, ask why, how long they are kept and through which channel to send them. Send only the pages it needs.

**Card numbers and online payments.** Do not send card numbers by message. If a deposit or advance payment is requested before you arrive, confirm the payee name and the account by phone using the number on the shop's own [contact page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/contact/), because paying into an account you have not verified is a common risk with online bookings. Nguyen Ha takes the deposit as cash at handover; do not assume that other shops do.

**Cash or deposits to individuals.** Send nothing to a personal account unless the shop explains why and you have verified it independently.

## What to prepare for the counter visit

The preparation list is short and worth putting together the night before:

| Item | Why it is needed |
|---|---|
| Passport | Identity is checked and photographed for the contract |
| Driving licence, plus international permit if applicable | Class and validity are checked against the bike |
| Cash for the deposit | The deposit at Nguyen Ha is VND 2,000,000 to 5,000,000 depending on bike and contract |
| A phone with a local or roaming number | The shop and you need a reachable number during the rental |
| Your own helmet, if you prefer it | One basic helmet per rider is provided free |
| A rain jacket | A raincoat is not included for short rentals |

Our guide to [what documents to carry when riding in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/documents-to-carry-riding-in-vietnam/) goes into the papers in more detail, and the [deposit guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbike-rental-deposit-hanoi/) explains how the money is handled.

## What happens at pickup, so you can plan the message

Knowing the counter routine helps you decide what to send. The shop confirms the bike and the final price on the actual bike before the contract is signed. It checks and photographs your documents. You take a few minutes to inspect the bike, including the tyres, brakes, lights and fuel gauge, and you agree a record of any scratches. Our [10-minute inspection checklist](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-scooter-inspection-checklist/) shows what to look at, and our note on [fuel at handover and return](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/fuel-in-hanoi-petrol-stations-rental-scooter/) explains the litre figures. At Nguyen Ha a petrol bike is handed over with about 0.5 to 2 litres.

If you want to keep a record, photograph the bike from all sides before leaving and send those pictures to yourself; that is a better use of your phone than sending the shop pictures of your passport.

## When to follow up

Send one reminder message the day before pickup: "Confirming tomorrow at [time] for [bike], deposit [amount]." If the shop does not reply and you have a tight schedule, call the number on the shop's own page, 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969), during opening hours. Do not pay for or plan around a booking that no one has confirmed.

## FAQ about preparing for pickup

### Do I need to send my passport photo before I arrive?

Not at Nguyen Ha, where documents are checked and photographed at handover. If any shop asks for images in advance, ask why and how they are stored, and send only what is needed.

### Should I pay a deposit online before I travel?

Only if the shop explains it clearly and you have confirmed the payee through a channel you trust. Nguyen Ha's deposit is cash, between VND 2,000,000 and 5,000,000, depending on the bike and contract.

### Can I change my pickup time after I have confirmed?

Ask the shop as early as you can. The 09:00 to 21:00 opening hours limit what is possible, and the rental day is counted in 24-hour blocks, so a later pickup moves the return time too. Message as soon as your plans change.

### What if I cannot tell the shop my licence class?

Look at the licence itself. Check the motorcycle category on it and note the issuing country. If you are unsure whether it covers the bike, ask the shop before travelling rather than at the counter; that avoids the worst outcome, a booking with no bike you can legally ride.
