For the Vietnamese Women's Museum at 36 Ly Thuong Kiet Street, your first choice is the museum's own parking, your second is a guarded lot nearby and your third is to park near Hoan Kiem Lake and walk. The museum's official website says that parking places for motorbikes and cars are available at the site, though it makes that statement in the context of its event spaces, and a Vietnam Airlines travel guide says there is a parking area near the gate for visitors travelling by themselves. Neither source gives capacity, rules or opening hours for the lot, so treat the museum's parking as likely but unconfirmed and keep a fallback ready. This guide sets out the options in order, explains how to judge a guarded lot, covers helmets and lockers, and suggests how to fit the museum into a day on a scooter.

## The basics before you ride

The museum's official site gives the address as 36 Ly Thuong Kiet Street, Hoan Kiem, Hanoi, and the opening hours as 08:00 to 17:00, Monday to Sunday. Some older travel pages list different opening days, so the official page is the one to trust, and it is worth a quick look before you go in case the schedule has changed.

It sits near the lake and the Old Quarter. Published walking estimates vary, from about five or ten minutes from Hoan Kiem Lake to fifteen or twenty from the Old Quarter, depending on where each guide measures from. The official site lists the cathedral, the Hoa Lo Prison relic, Hoan Kiem Lake and the Old Quarter as other sights close by, so it is a natural stop in a morning around the centre.

## Option 1: the museum's own parking

The official page about visiting the museum has a section headed "Personal Vehicle", but the text I could read does not describe the parking in detail. The reference to parking places for motorbikes and cars appears under the heading about the museum's event spaces, which is why I describe it as stated but not fully specified. The Vietnam Airlines guide puts it more directly, saying the museum has a parking area for motorbikes and cars near the gate, suitable for visitors who travel on their own.

What to do in practice:

- **Approach slowly and look for the entrance and any signs or attendants.** Ly Thuong Kiet is a busy street, and a sudden stop at the gate is hazardous for you and the traffic behind.
- **Ask the attendant, not the map.** An attendant at the gate can tell you whether visitor parking is open that day and where to go.
- **Take any ticket, and keep it,** because you will need it when you leave.
- **Lock the steering, and take your helmet and valuables with you or put them in a locker.**

If the lot is full, closed to visitors or reserved for an event, move straight to option 2. Do not leave the scooter on the pavement outside, since blocking the way for walkers and wheelchair users is both discourteous and risky for the bike.

## Option 2: a guarded lot nearby

Hanoi's central streets have many pay-to-park spaces, some run by the city and some by local businesses or hotels. When the city launched its pilot low-emission zone in July 2026, the Department of Construction said it had reviewed and arranged 220 parking facilities in the area, and wards were asked to ensure security at the parking facilities. That is a useful sign that official or checked parking exists in the centre, but it does not mean there is a space beside the museum, and I could not find a list of lots by street.

A guarded lot normally has these features:

| Feature | What to look for |
|---|---|
| An attendant | Someone who is clearly in charge and issues tickets |
| A ticket or number | A matching ticket or a numbered tag for your bike |
| Order | Bikes in rows, not piled in a tangle |
| Visible price | A displayed rate, so there are no surprises |
| Sensible location | Off the carriageway and not blocking a doorway |

Compare what you see with the advice in our guide to [parking a motorbike in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-motorbike-in-hanoi/), and read [how to pay for fuel, parking and tolls as a visitor](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/paying-fuel-parking-tolls-hanoi-visitor/) for what to carry in cash. Walk the last few minutes rather than hunting for a space on the museum's own street.

## Option 3: park by the lake and walk

If the streets nearest the museum are full, use the lake area as your base. Our guide to [where to park a scooter near Hoan Kiem Lake](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/where-to-park-a-scooter-near-hoan-kiem-lake/) lists the options. The advantages are that you reach the museum on foot in a few minutes, you can combine it with the lake or a meal and you will not have to manoeuvre in a tight street. The disadvantage is that you walk back to the bike in the heat.

Do remember the weekend evening restriction. Since 1 July 2026 the pilot low-emission zone around the lake and the Old Quarter has kept motorbikes and cars out of its core between 19:00 and midnight every Friday, Saturday and Sunday, according to VnExpress and Vietnam News. The museum closes at 17:00, so this will not affect your visit, but it can affect how you return if you stay on for dinner. The reports do not name Ly Thuong Kiet among the streets bounding the core zone, but follow signs and check the latest news, since the scheme is a pilot.

## Option 4: leave the scooter out of it

You can also skip the parking problem by walking in from a hotel, a taxi or a ride-hailing car. If you are staying in the Old Quarter and the museum is the only central stop on your list, this is often simpler. Our guide to [riding in the Old Quarter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-old-quarter/) explains when walking is the better choice, and the same logic applies here.

## Helmets, lockers and what to carry in

The official site says the museum offers free lockers, free wifi, a lift, a path for wheelchairs and accessible toilets. I could not confirm whether the lockers are big enough for a helmet, so ask before you assume. The shop supplies one basic helmet per rider, and you should expect to carry it. A practical approach:

1. **Clip or carry the helmet** rather than leaving it hanging on the mirror.
2. **Ask at the ticket desk** whether helmets can be stored in a locker or at the desk.
3. **Do not leave a bag in the seat storage** if the lot is unattended.
4. **Keep your phone and wallet on you.**
5. **Wear flat, comfortable shoes,** since the museum has several floors and a lift.

## What the visit involves

The official site describes three permanent exhibitions, "Women in Family", "Women in History" and "Women's Fashion", with an audio guide covering more than 60 objects in Vietnamese, English, French, Japanese and Korean. There is also a museum shop selling goods made by women from ethnic minority groups and craft villages, with part of the proceeds returned to the makers, and an outdoor café. A travel review suggests allowing two to three hours, and early morning for a quieter visit, though that is one writer's view.

A realistic plan is therefore to arrive around opening time, spend about two hours inside and have time for a coffee. I have not quoted the ticket price, because different guides give different amounts and I could not confirm the current one from the official pages, so check at the desk.

## A morning plan around the museum

| Time | Plan |
|---|---|
| About 08:00 | Ride in from Long Bien, leaving margin for traffic |
| 08:30 | Park, take the ticket and walk to the entrance |
| 08:45 to 10:45 | Exhibitions, audio guide, shop |
| 11:00 | Coffee at the museum or a short walk to the lake |
| Around noon | Lunch, then collect the scooter and ride out of the centre |

If you are running late, call the shop on 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969), open 09:00 to 21:00 daily. The [prices page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/prices/) explains late-return charges.

## When the area is busy

The centre fills up at certain times: weekday mornings when offices and schools start, lunchtime and the late afternoon. Weekends bring local families and school groups to museums, and a travel review of this museum notes that weekends can be busier, with early morning quieter. If you arrive at a busy moment, avoid circling. Pick one guarded lot, park, and walk, rather than trying three streets in a row. A few minutes on foot costs less than the time and risk of looping through traffic with a helmet on and a map in your head.

Also think about the return. If your day continues elsewhere, choose a parking spot that makes it easy to ride out in the direction you need, away from one-way streets that would force a detour.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Parking in front of a doorway or on a wheelchair path.**
- **Leaving without a ticket** or throwing it away at the museum.
- **Riding the wrong way down a one-way street** to reach the gate. Walk the bike or go around.
- **Forgetting the heat.** A scooter seat in direct sun can burn, so look for shade.

## FAQ: parking at the Vietnamese Women's Museum

### Does the museum have parking for motorbikes?

Its website says parking places for motorbikes and cars are available, and a travel guide says there is a parking area near the gate. Confirm on arrival.

### What are the opening hours?

08:00 to 17:00, Monday to Sunday, according to the official site.

### Can I leave my helmet inside?

The museum lists free lockers. Ask whether a helmet fits.

### How far is it from Hoan Kiem Lake?

Guides give walking times from about five to twenty minutes, depending on the starting point.

### Is the weekend evening ban relevant?

Only if you return to the lake area after 19:00 on a Friday, Saturday or Sunday, since the museum closes at 17:00.

### What if the museum's lot is full?

Use a guarded lot nearby or park near the lake and walk.
