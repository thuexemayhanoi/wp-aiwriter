Vietnam has one of the strictest drink-driving rules in the world, and it applies to a rented scooter exactly as it applies to a car. There is no small allowance for a beer with dinner: a rider with any measurable alcohol in the blood or on the breath is committing an offence. The rule has been in place since the Law on Prevention and Control of Harms of Alcohol took effect on 1 January 2020, it was carried into the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (Law 36/2024/QH15) from 1 January 2025, and the fines and licence penalties are set by Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, as amended by Decree 238/2026/ND-CP in force since 15 August 2026. This guide explains what the rule means in practice as of October 2026, what a checkpoint looks like, what it costs to fail one, and how visitors who enjoy Hanoi's beer culture can plan around it.

## Zero means zero

Article 9 of Law 36/2024/QH15 prohibits driving any vehicle on the road with alcohol in the blood or the breath. Unlike many European countries, Vietnam does not set a legal limit below which a rider is allowed to ride; the thresholds in the penalty decree only decide how large the punishment is. A single can of beer, a glass of rice wine offered at a family meal or a cocktail at a rooftop bar can all leave a reading that counts.

The rule covers every kind of motorbike, scooter and moped, including electric ones and 50cc bikes that do not need a licence. It also covers bicycles and electric bicycles, with lower fines. For a visitor the practical message is short: if you have had anything to drink, do not ride.

## How police test riders

Alcohol checks in Hanoi are carried out by traffic police at checkpoints set up on main roads, most often in the evening and late at night, and around weekends and public holidays. Officers wave riders into a coned-off area, sometimes stopping every bike in the stream. The first test is usually a quick screening device held in front of your mouth while you speak or blow; if it detects alcohol, you are asked to blow into a breathalyser that gives a measured reading in milligrams per litre of breath, and the result is printed or recorded.

Stopping when signalled and taking the test is compulsory. Refusing, or riding away from a checkpoint, is punished at least as severely as the highest alcohol band. If you dispute a reading, say so calmly and ask for it to be recorded in the written report; the procedure allows for a blood test in some circumstances, but you cannot simply decline.

## The penalty bands for motorbike riders

Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, Article 7, sets three bands for riders of motorcycles and mopeds, measured either in blood or on the breath:

| Alcohol level | Fine | Licence consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Up to 50 mg/100 ml blood or 0.25 mg/l breath | Fine of VND 2–3 million | 4 points deducted |
| Over 50 up to 80 mg/100 ml blood, or over 0.25 up to 0.4 mg/l breath | Fine of VND 6–8 million | 10 points deducted |
| Over 80 mg/100 ml blood or 0.4 mg/l breath | Fine of VND 8–10 million | Licence revoked for 22 to 24 months |
| Refusing the alcohol test | Fine of VND 8–10 million | Licence revoked for 22 to 24 months |

The first band's fine is in point a of clause 6 and the 4-point deduction in point b of clause 13 of the article; the higher bands follow the same structure. The fine amounts are ranges, and the officer's decision fixes the final figure. If riding under the influence causes a crash, the case can move beyond an administrative fine to criminal liability under the Penal Code, depending on the injuries and damage.

In addition to the fine, the police can hold the motorbike for up to seven days while the case is processed. On a rental bike that is a real problem: the scooter belongs to the shop, which loses the use of it, and you will be expected to tell them immediately and sort out the release once the fine is paid.

## Licence points and what they mean for a visitor

Law 36/2024/QH15 gives every driving licence 12 points. Deductions are recorded against the licence; a licence that loses no points for 12 months returns to 12, and a licence that reaches zero cannot be used until the holder has waited at least six months and passed the theory test again. Losing 10 points in one night, as the middle alcohol band does, leaves almost nothing in reserve.

Most short-stay visitors ride on a home licence plus an International Driving Permit. The fine applies to them in full, and the police can take the right to use the permit in Vietnam for the period of a revocation. Long-stay residents who hold or convert to a Vietnamese licence feel the points system directly. Either way, a drink-riding record is not something to test.

## Insurance and your rental contract

Two practical consequences matter as much as the fine. First, travel insurance policies routinely exclude claims for accidents that happen while the insured person is over the legal alcohol limit, and in Vietnam the legal limit is zero. A crash after two beers can therefore leave you paying your own hospital bills, which in an international hospital can be far larger than any traffic fine. Read the exclusions in your own policy rather than assuming you are covered.

Second, the rental contract makes you responsible for the bike while you have it. If it is impounded after an alcohol check, or damaged in a crash after drinking, the costs fall on the rider. ## The morning after

Alcohol leaves the body slowly, at a rate that varies from person to person and that coffee, a shower or a big breakfast do not speed up. A late night of beer or rice wine can still show on a breathalyser the next morning, and checkpoints do not only run at night. If you drank heavily, leave the bike parked the following morning and plan a later start, or take a taxi to your first stop.

Food and mouthwash can occasionally cause a brief false reading on a screening device, which is why the police use a measured breath test before recording an offence. If you have only eaten ripe fruit or used mouthwash, wait a few minutes and ask for the test to be repeated; if you have been drinking, a repeat test will not help.

## Planning a night out without the bike

Hanoi makes it easy to leave the scooter behind. Ride-hailing apps cover the whole city with both cars and motorbike taxis, and metered taxis wait outside the main beer streets and nightlife areas. Many hotels and guesthouses in the Old Quarter are within walking distance of Ta Hien and the bars around Hoan Kiem Lake. A few simple habits keep you on the right side of the rule:

- Decide before you go out whether you are riding or drinking, and do not change the plan halfway through the evening.
- Ride to dinner only if you will not drink, and park in a guarded lot near the restaurant; our guide to [parking a motorbike in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-motorbike-in-hanoi/) explains how the lots and tickets work.
- If you drank, leave the bike in the lot overnight and collect it the next afternoon by taxi.
- Do not ride as a passenger behind someone who has been drinking; the passenger is not fined, but is the person most likely to be injured.
- On a road trip, plan the evening around the hotel so that you do not need to ride after dinner.

Locals will sometimes insist on a toast at a family meal or a village festival. Raising a glass of tea or water and explaining that you are riding is understood and accepted; "I'm driving" works everywhere.

## Riding at night is a separate risk

Even sober, riding after dark in Hanoi demands extra attention: other riders may have been drinking, lighting varies street by street and delivery traffic peaks in the evening. Our guide to [riding at night in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-at-night-in-hanoi/) covers lights, visibility and the streets to avoid. Combining night riding with even a small amount of alcohol is the situation the law and the police checkpoints are designed to stop, and for good reason.

## FAQ

### What is the legal alcohol limit for riding a scooter in Vietnam?

Zero. Law 36/2024/QH15 prohibits riding with any alcohol in the blood or on the breath. The thresholds of 50 and 80 mg per 100 ml of blood, or 0.25 and 0.4 mg per litre of breath, only set the size of the penalty.

### How much is the fine for drink-riding a motorbike?

Under Decree 168/2024/ND-CP the fine is VND 2–3 million for the lowest band, VND 6–8 million for the middle band and VND 8–10 million for the highest band, with 4 or 10 licence points deducted or the licence revoked for 22 to 24 months. Refusing the test is fined like the highest band.

### Does the rule apply to electric scooters and 50cc bikes?

Yes. Every motor vehicle on the road is covered, including electric scooters and 50cc mopeds that need no licence. Bicycles and electric bicycles are also covered with lower fines.

### Can the police keep my rental bike?

Yes. For alcohol offences the motorbike can be held for up to seven days while the case is processed. Tell the rental shop at once, because the bike is theirs.

### Will my travel insurance cover an accident after one beer?

Probably not. Most policies exclude accidents that happen while over the legal limit, and in Vietnam that limit is zero. Check the exclusions in your own policy before you travel.
