Photograph a 50cc bike in this order: first one slow, narrated video that circles the whole machine with the shop staff in view, then a fixed set of still photos (all four sides, every existing mark in close-up, tyres, lights switched on, mirrors, helmet), then the odometer, the fuel gauge, the plate and the signed handover paper. Do it in daylight, before you pay and before you ride, and send the files to yourself straight away so each one carries a time. The whole routine takes about five minutes, and its value shows up at return, when "this scratch was already there" is either an argument or a comparison of two sets of pictures. This guide gives the sequence, a shot list for a small moped, the mechanical checks worth photographing, and how to repeat the process at return.

## Why the photos matter more on a small bike

A 50cc moped is light and low, mostly made of plastic panels, and usually ridden in city traffic where scuffs at low speed are common. That makes cosmetic marks the most likely subject of any disagreement at the end of a rental. Rental guides for Vietnam describe the same pattern again and again: damage that was there before the rider arrived becomes disputed on return, and a timestamped walk-around is the usual remedy. Documentation does not accuse anyone. A careful shop expects it, and a clear record protects the shop as much as the renter.

Photographs also make you look properly. A tyre that is nearly bald, a headlight that works only on low beam, or a cracked mirror is much easier to see on a screen than when you glance at it across a counter.

## Before you start

1. **Choose good light.** Daylight outside the shop beats a dim indoor corner. If the bike is parked in shade, ask to wheel it to a bright spot.
2. **Clean the lens.** A smudged phone camera hides fine scratches.
3. **Check your storage and battery.** A 5-minute video and thirty photos need space. Your phone's date and time should be correct.
4. **Ask first and keep it friendly.** Say you like to record the bike's condition, and invite a staff member to stand by. This is routine and should not cause offence.
5. **Do it before the paperwork is finished.** Once you have signed and taken the key, the record is weaker. Nguyen Ha's contract process involves photographing identity documents, and originals are not kept, so the same handover moment is the natural time to record the bike too.

## Step 1: the slow walk-around video

Start the video before you touch the bike. Say the date, the shop name and the plate number out loud, then walk slowly all the way around the machine, keeping the camera about a metre away. Narrate what you see: "scratch on the left side panel, dent in the rear mudguard, mirror a little loose." Narration matters because it ties each mark to a place, and the audio is a record of what you noticed.

Keep the video continuous. A single unbroken clip is harder to challenge than a series of cuts. Include the seat, the handlebars, the underside of the front panel and the rear light. If any staff member is nearby, let them appear in the frame, so there is no doubt about where and when it was shot.

## Step 2: the still-photo shot list

Video shows everything at once, but stills are easier to compare later. Use a consistent set so you can repeat it at return.

| Shot | What to capture | Why it helps |
|---|---|---|
| Front | Whole bike from the front, including the front panel and headlight | Shows impact marks and cracked lenses |
| Left side | Full side view, plus the footboard or leg shield | Shows panel scratches and the chain or engine cover |
| Right side | Full side view, including the exhaust shield | Exhaust shields and side covers often get scuffed |
| Rear | Rear mudguard, rear light and number plate | Shows tail-light cracks and plate condition |
| Top | Seat, handlebar, mirrors, switch gear | Shows seat tears and loose controls |
| Each mark | One close-up with a finger, coin or pen for scale, plus a wider shot to show where it is | Pairs the detail with its location |
| Tyres | Tread and sidewall, front and rear | Shows wear and cuts |
| Brake levers | Both levers, from the rider's seat | Shows bent or loose levers |
| Mirrors | Both mirrors from behind | Missing or cracked mirrors are easy to overlook |
| Plate and papers | Number plate close-up, and the registration page if the shop shows it | Confirms you rode the bike on the contract |
| Helmet | Outside, inside and strap of each helmet supplied | Helmet damage is a common return dispute |
| Keys | The key and any key tag | Proves you received them |

If the bike is a Cub-style semi-automatic with a foot shift, add the shift pedal, the chain cover and the leg shields. If it is a 50cc scooter, add the footboard and the under-seat storage lid. These are the parts that take scrapes when a bike is parked or dropped.

## Step 3: record the mechanical condition, not just the paint

A cosmetic scratch costs little, but a faulty light or tyre is a safety issue and also a basis for saying the bike was not fit when you took it. Honda's safe-riding guide for customers lists the lights and horn among the checks to make before moving off, specifically the turn signals, the brake light and the high and low beams. Honda's owner's manual for the Vision scooter, a different and larger model than a 50cc, gives the same advice to check that the lights, indicators and horn work. Use that as a template.

- **Lights.** Switch on the headlight on low beam, then high beam, and photograph the lit lamp. Switch on each indicator and press the brake lever to check the brake light, with a staff member to watch the rear. A short video is better than a photo here.
- **Horn.** Press it while filming so the clip has sound.
- **Tyres.** Press with a thumb and photograph any sidewall cracks, cuts or visibly flat tread.
- **Brakes.** Squeeze both levers and film the travel. If a lever feels spongy or touches the handlebar, say so on camera and ask for adjustment.
- **Chain or drive.** On a Cub-style bike, look at the chain and its cover, and photograph anything rusty or dry. Our guide to [what to check before you ride off](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-scooter-inspection-checklist/) goes through the whole inspection.

If any of these fail, do not ride. Ask the shop to fix it or swap the bike, then repeat the photographs on the replacement.

## Step 4: the instruments and the fuel

Photograph the odometer and the fuel gauge, close enough that the numbers and the needle are readable. At Nguyen Ha, petrol bikes are handed over with about 0.5 to 2 litres and come back with about 0.5 to 1 litre, and an empty tank at return carries a fee of VND 20,000, so a clear picture of the gauge at pickup removes any doubt about the starting level. The shop's [guide to returning a rental motorbike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/returning-your-rental-motorbike-hanoi/) explains the fuel and return terms in more detail.

If the gauge looks unreliable, say so on the video and ask what the shop recommends. A small tank on a small bike means a refill is never far away.

## Step 5: the handover paper

Photograph the contract or handover sheet after you have signed, including any list of existing marks, the price and the deposit terms. The deposit is a cash sum, between VND 2,000,000 and VND 5,000,000 depending on the bike and the contract, separate from the rent, and the deposit page explains how it is returned. Our guide to [how a motorbike rental deposit works in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbike-rental-deposit-hanoi/) shows what it covers, and the guide to [what the clauses in a rental contract mean](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-motorbike-rental-contract-explained/) explains the wording on damage.

Ask the staff to note any existing marks that you found, rather than assuming they will remember. If they write them on the sheet, photograph it. If they decline, your video and photos carry more weight, so make sure they are complete.

## Step 6: save and back up straight away

Before you ride away:

1. Send the video and the key photos to yourself by email or a messaging app that records the time.
2. If the shop agrees, also send the main shots to the shop's number, such as 0334 699 969 on a messaging app, so both sides hold the same set.
3. Do not edit, crop or filter the originals.
4. Keep everything until the deposit is back in your hands.

## At return: repeat the same set

Return the bike in similar light, with staff present, and take the same shots in the same order. Compare side by side. If a new mark has appeared during your rental, say so and agree what to do. If not, the paired photos show that the bike came back as it left. Do this before you hand over the key and before you leave the shop.

## Common mistakes

- **Starting after you have signed.** The record is weaker than one taken at the counter.
- **Close-ups with no context.** A photo of a scratch with no view of where it is on the bike proves little.
- **Photographing in the dark.** A dim shop corner hides marks.
- **Skipping the lights.** A working headlight matters most for night riding, so it is worth the extra shot.
- **Deleting files too early.** Keep them until the deposit is returned.
- **Filming other customers' faces.** Keep the camera on the bike.

## FAQ: photographing a 50cc rental

### How many photos should I take?

About twenty to thirty stills plus one walk-around video is enough for a small moped. Quality and a consistent set matter more than a high count.

### Should I film or take photos?

Both. Video proves the sequence and the sound, and stills are easier to compare at return.

### Will the shop mind?

A shop that handles rentals regularly should not. If a shop refuses to let you record the bike, treat that as information about the shop.

### What if I find damage after I have left?

Take photos immediately, with the date visible, and message or call the shop at once on 0334 699 969. Do not wait until return.

### Do I need to photograph the helmet?

Yes. One basic helmet per rider is included, and a short photo of its inside, outside and strap settles any later question about its condition.

### Does this replace the contract?

No. Photos support the contract and do not replace it. Read the terms and ask about anything unclear.
