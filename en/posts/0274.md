The battery level at pickup is the starting line for an electric scooter rental, and it works best when it is written down and photographed by both sides at the same moment. A good record has five parts: the battery reading as the display shows it (percentage, bars or both), the odometer or trip distance, the time, what was done about charging and the charger that goes with the bike. At Nguyen Ha Motorbike Rental the battery level and the charging arrangements are confirmed at handover. This article explains how to make that confirmation useful: what to read off the screen and when, how to photograph it so it cannot be misread, what to do if the bike is not fully charged, how to avoid the common mix-ups between percentage and range and how the pickup reading is used later. The screen details come from VinFast's Evo 200 manual as an example of one model, and your rental may show things differently.

## Why the pickup reading matters

A petrol scooter has a fuel level, and a rental shop expects it back with about the same amount or a little less. An electric scooter has the same idea in a different unit. The battery level you collect with is the baseline for three things:

- **Your plan for the day.** The percentage you start with is the energy you can spend. A bike handed over at 70 per cent is not the same as one at 100 per cent, and you should know before you plan a route.
- **The return.** The level at pickup is what the shop looks at when you come back. If you are asked to return with a similar or specified level, the starting figure is the reference.
- **Any disagreement.** If someone later says the bike left the shop at a different level, a clear record settles it quickly and politely.

## What the display shows, and when to read it

On the Evo 200, the instrument cluster comes on when the key is turned to ON, and the battery information includes remaining capacity and charging status. The capacity bar flashes below 20 per cent, and the battery symbol flashes below 10 per cent. Other models may show a number, bars or a distance in kilometres, so ask staff to point out which element is the battery level on this bike.

When you read the screen matters as much as what you read:

1. **Key on, bike stationary.** Read the level before you pull away. A reading taken just after a hard acceleration can dip as the battery works, and a reading with the bike at rest is the fairest one.
2. **Wait a few seconds.** Let the display finish starting up. The Evo 200 manual says the cluster lights after the key is turned on, and it is better to read a screen that is fully awake than one that has just flickered on.
3. **Check that nothing is plugged in.** On the Evo 200, a charging symbol shows on the display while the charger is connected, and the motor does not run while charging. A bike handed over mid-charge should be clearly noted as such.
4. **Look at the figure straight on.** In bright sun, shade the screen with your hand to read it accurately.

If the display shows a fault symbol or a code instead of a normal reading, do not accept the bike until the shop has explained it. Our guide to a [10-minute rental scooter inspection](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-scooter-inspection-checklist/) explains the wider handover routine.

## What a useful record contains

A record is only helpful if two people reading it would reach the same conclusion. Aim for these items:

| Item | Why it helps | Example of a clear entry |
|---|---|---|
| Date and time | Fixes the moment of the reading | Example: 21 October, 10:15 |
| Battery reading | The baseline | 85 per cent on the display; four of five bars |
| Reading type | Avoids confusion between units | Percentage, not range |
| Range figure, if shown | Gives context, though it is only an estimate | 62 km shown, for information only |
| Odometer or trip distance | Links the level to the bike's use | 4,812 km |
| Charge status | Shows whether it was charging | Not charging; off charge since morning |
| Charger | Records what is to be returned | Charger handed over; one cable |
| Existing marks | Separates battery from damage | Noted on the contract |
| Who confirmed | Shows both sides saw it | Staff and renter both looked |

The Evo 200 owner's manual does not tell shops what to write. This table is a practical suggestion, not a legal form. It is fine to ask the shop to note the reading on the contract or the handover sheet and to take your own photograph. The shop confirms battery level and charging at handover; how it writes this down is the shop's own procedure, so ask.

## Photographing the pickup level

A good photograph is the simplest record and the easiest to share later. Take it in this order:

1. **The dashboard with the key on,** close enough to read the battery level and the odometer, with no glare.
2. **A wider shot** showing the dashboard and some of the bike, so it is clear which bike it is.
3. **The charger and cable,** if one is handed over.
4. **A short video** of the display starting up, if the shop agrees, since a video shows the sequence and any warnings.
5. **Your phone's clock** will stamp the time. Do not edit or crop the photographs.

Keep them until the rental ends and any deposit has been returned. Our explanation of [how a rental deposit works in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbike-rental-deposit-hanoi/) describes what the deposit covers.

## If the battery is not fully charged

Not every scooter will be handed over at 100 per cent. The bike may have just come back from another rental, or the shop may have charged it only part way. VinFast's manual advises keeping the battery between 20 and 80 per cent for a longer battery life, so a level below full is not necessarily a fault. What matters is that you know the figure and that it is enough for your plans.

Sensible responses:

- **Ask whether it can be charged before you go.** If it takes hours, you may prefer to collect later or to take another bike.
- **Decide what is acceptable.** If you plan a short ride around the lake, 60 per cent may be fine. If you plan a long day, ask for a fuller battery or a different bike.
- **Write the actual figure down,** and do not agree to a vague "about full".
- **Ask for the reasoning** if the level is very low. Below 20 per cent the Evo 200's capacity bar flashes, and the manual says performance is reduced below 30 per cent, so a very low pickup level is a poor start.

## Percentage, bars and kilometres: avoid mixing them up

The commonest source of confusion at pickup is that the figures are not the same kind of number. A battery percentage describes how full the battery is. Bars are coarse steps of the same idea. A range figure in kilometres is a forecast that changes with how the bike is ridden.

- If the display shows both, record both and label them.
- If the display shows only bars, record the number of bars and the total, for example "four of five".
- If the reading at pickup is a range, note that it is only an estimate and ask for the percentage or bar count as well.
- If the shop says "full" and the screen says something else, record what the screen says.

The short rule is to trust the percentage as the measure of what is left and to treat a range figure as a guide that changes with how you ride.

## What if the shop does not write it down?

Every shop has its own routine. If you are given a contract with no space for the battery, ask politely whether the level can be added by hand or entered in a message. If that is not possible, your photograph and the time on it are your record. You can also send yourself or the shop a message, with the photograph attached, so there is a timestamp outside your own phone. The tone should be friendly, not suspicious: most shops are happy to agree on the starting level because it protects them too.

## How the pickup reading is used later

During the rental you will probably check the percentage yourself, so the starting figure gives you a point of comparison. Note how much the percentage has fallen over the distance you have ridden, and you have your own estimate of consumption. At the end, staff compare the return level with the pickup record, which is why the figure should be clear. If you are returning with less charge than you started with, ask what the shop expects; if there are charging or fee arrangements in the contract, they will be written there. ## FAQ: how the pickup battery level is recorded

### Does the shop record the battery level when I collect the bike?

The shop confirms battery level and charging at handover. Ask how it is written down and take your own photograph as well.

### Should I photograph the dashboard?

Yes. Photograph the battery reading and the odometer with the key on, so the starting figure is clear.

### What if the bike is not fully charged?

Ask whether it can be charged before you go, decide whether the level suits your plans, and have the actual figure noted.

### Is the range figure enough?

No. It is an estimate. Record the percentage or the bars as well.

### Does the battery level at pickup affect my plans?

Yes, because it is the energy you have to spend. A lower start means a shorter day.

### What level do I return with?

Ask the shop at pickup, because it depends on the bike and the contract.
