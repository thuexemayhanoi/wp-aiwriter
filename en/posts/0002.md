Walk into almost any rental shop in Hanoi and the first two automatic scooters you will be offered are the Honda Vision and the Honda Air Blade. Both are made by Honda Vietnam, both are everywhere in the city, and both sit in the same reference price band at Nguyen Ha: VND 150,000–200,000 per day for an automatic scooter, with weekly and monthly packages priced separately. So the question is not which one is cheaper. It is which one fits the way you plan to ride.

This guide compares the two from a renter's point of view: how they feel in Hanoi traffic, how much they carry, what engine class they fall into, what that means for your licence, and the trade-offs that only show up after a few days on the road. The facts about the bikes come from Honda Vietnam's published model information; the rental terms come from the Nguyen Ha [price guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/prices/), as of October 2026. Availability of a specific model is confirmed when you book, not promised in advance.

## The short answer

Choose the **Vision** if you are a first-time rider in Vietnam, you are travelling light, you will mostly ride inside the city, and you want the lightest, easiest scooter to park and push around. Choose the **Air Blade** if you are a confident rider, you often carry a passenger or a bag, you want more under-seat storage and a more planted feel at speed, or you plan day trips on the open roads outside Hanoi.

If you are unsure, start with the Vision. It is forgiving, it is the model most locals learn on, and the difference in daily cost at Nguyen Ha is small or nil because both fall into the same automatic-scooter range.

## What the two scooters are

The **Honda Vision** is Honda Vietnam's entry-level automatic scooter, built around an engine of roughly 110 cc. It is light, narrow and low, with a flat floor between your feet, a small fuel tank and an under-seat compartment big enough for a half-face helmet or a daypack. Recent versions add a keyless smart key on the higher trims and an idling-stop system that cuts the engine at red lights; whether your rental has those features depends on the year and trim of the actual bike.

The **Honda Air Blade** is the sportier sibling. It is sold in Vietnam with two engine sizes, a 125 cc version and a 160 cc version, and both are physically larger than the Vision: longer, heavier, with a taller seat and a bigger under-seat box. The 160 in particular is a quick scooter by Hanoi standards and feels more stable on fast, straight roads. In the city, that extra size is mostly felt when you thread through a jam or lift the bike onto its centre stand.

Neither is a "big bike". Both are twist-and-go automatics: no clutch, no gears, one brake lever on each side. If you have ridden any scooter before, you can ride either within minutes.

## Size, seat height and how they feel in traffic

Hanoi traffic rewards small, light, narrow bikes. You spend a lot of time at walking pace, filtering between cars, pulling out of alleys and turning across streams of other scooters. The Vision's low seat and light weight make that easy: shorter riders can plant both feet flat at a red light, and the bike changes direction with very little input.

The Air Blade asks a little more of you. The seat is higher, the bike is heavier, and the front end feels longer, so U-turns in a narrow lane take more care. In return, it tracks straighter over the ridges and patches of the main roads, and it is less upset by a passenger moving around on the back. On the Long Bien side of the river, where Nguyen Ha is based, the dyke roads and the bridge approaches are where you notice the difference most.

A practical note for taller riders: on the Vision, knees can touch the handlebar shield in a tight turn. If you are over about 1.80 m, try both before you commit to a week.

## Storage and carrying things

Under-seat storage is the most common reason renters regret picking the smaller scooter. The Vision's compartment fits one half-face helmet, a rain poncho and a small bag, but not a full-face helmet and not a laptop bag. The Air Blade's compartment is noticeably larger and swallows more, including many full-face helmets, although you should test your own helmet rather than trust a spec sheet.

Both have a hook under the handlebars for a shopping bag and a flat floor for a small box, but Hanoi police do fine riders for bulky loads that stick out, and an overloaded floor makes any scooter wobbly. If you plan to carry camera gear, groceries for a week or a second person's bag, the Air Blade's extra storage is worth more than the Vision's lighter handling.

## Engine class and your licence

This is the part most visitors skip, and it matters more than storage. Since the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (Law 36/2024/QH15) took effect on 1 January 2025, Vietnam's licence classes for two-wheelers are split at 125 cc: class A1 covers motorcycles up to 125 cc, and class A covers anything above. The Vision and the Air Blade 125 fall under A1. The Air Blade 160 does not: it needs a class A licence, or the equivalent foreign licence with an International Driving Permit that covers that category.

For a visitor, the simplest rule is this: if your home licence and permit cover motorcycles of any size, both scooters are fine; if they cover only the small class, stay with the Vision or the Air Blade 125. Read our full guide to [licence rules for foreigners in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbike-licence-rules-for-foreigners-in-vietnam/) before you choose, because riding a bike your licence does not cover also voids most travel insurance.

Nguyen Ha checks and photographs the identity document and licence used for the contract, and keeps no originals once the cash deposit and the rental fee are paid. The deposit is normally VND 2–5 million depending on the bike and the agreement.

## Fuel, range and running costs

Both scooters are frugal. Honda's fuel-injected engines with the idling-stop system sip petrol, and a tank of four to five litres is enough for several days of city riding on either model. The Air Blade 160 uses more than the Vision, but in Hanoi traffic the difference over a week is the price of a coffee, not a meal.

Petrol is sold at staffed stations all over the city; you stay on the scooter and the attendant fills the tank. Nguyen Ha hands bikes over with the fuel level noted on the contract, and you return the bike with a similar level. There is no fuel surcharge hidden in the daily rate, and the shop does not charge by the hour for ordinary rentals: a rental day is 24 hours from the agreed handover time, and anything shorter is still billed as a day.

## Comfort on longer rides

If a day trip to Ninh Binh or a loop around West Lake is on your list, the Air Blade earns its keep. The seat is longer and better padded for two, the suspension copes better with the broken shoulders of the national road, and the 160 holds a steady cruising speed without feeling busy. The Vision will make the same trip, but after two hours you feel the smaller seat and the firmer ride, and with a passenger on board the little engine works hard on any incline.

For the reverse case, commuting across the city every day for a month, the Vision's lighter weight and easier parking are the features you will thank yourself for. Monthly rates for automatic scooters at Nguyen Ha run from VND 1,200,000 to 2,000,000 depending on the model, so ask which model the quote is for.

## Which one to choose: three typical renters

**The first-timer.** You have ridden a scooter on holiday before, maybe in Thailand or Bali, and you are nervous about Hanoi. Take the Vision. The low seat, light steering and modest power let you concentrate on reading the traffic rather than managing the bike.

**The couple.** Two adults, one bag between you, a week of city sightseeing with one or two trips out of town. Take the Air Blade 125: the storage and the stronger pillion seat matter every day, and the engine class keeps your paperwork simple.

**The experienced rider.** You hold a full motorcycle licence and a 1968-convention permit, you ride at home, and you want something that feels planted on the Hanoi–Ninh Binh road. Take the Air Blade 160, after confirming your licence covers it.

Whichever you choose, do the same handover routine: check both brakes, the horn, the lights and the tyres, photograph every scratch, and note the fuel level. ## FAQ

### Is the Honda Vision powerful enough for two adults?

In the city, yes. Two adults and a light bag are fine at Hanoi speeds. On hills, on the open road or with heavy luggage, the Vision feels strained and the Air Blade is the better choice.

### Do I need a bigger licence for the Air Blade?

For the 125 cc version, no: it falls in the same class as the Vision (up to 125 cc, class A1 under Law 36/2024/QH15, effective 1 January 2025). The 160 cc version needs the higher class A, or a foreign licence and permit that cover motorcycles above 125 cc.

### Which one is cheaper to rent?

At Nguyen Ha both are automatic scooters, priced at VND 150,000–200,000 per day, with the exact figure depending on the model and condition of the actual bike. Weekly and monthly packages are set separately and are usually better value than multiplying the daily rate.

### Can I fit a full-face helmet under the seat?

Usually not on the Vision. Often on the Air Blade, but helmet shapes vary; test it at the shop before you ride off.

### Can I swap models during a long rental?

Ask the shop when you book. Swaps during weekly and monthly rentals depend on what is available at the time, and the rate may change if you move between classes of scooter.
