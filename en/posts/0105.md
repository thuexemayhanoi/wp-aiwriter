To compare two semi-automatic rental bikes that are actually available, ignore the badge at first and judge the two machines in front of you on four things in this order: safety condition (tyres, brakes, chain, lights), how each one starts, shifts and stops on a short test ride, how well each fits your body and luggage, and only then the paper specifications. At the same model level the numbers are very close, so the difference between two bikes is usually the individual machine, not the logo. This guide gives a repeatable side-by-side method, a scorecard you can fill in with your phone, a table of the real manufacturer figures that do differ, and the questions to put to the shop before you choose. It is general information, not an inspection.

## What "available" really means

A rental shop does not stock every model in every condition. Nguyen Ha Motorbike Rental lists Honda Wave, Yamaha Sirius and Honda Blade as the manual-gearbox models it can offer, and its own notes say the bike you actually get is confirmed when you book. So the realistic situation is rarely "Wave versus Sirius" in the abstract. It is "this Wave and that Sirius, parked next to each other, with different mileage, tyres and little histories".

That changes how to compare. Reviews and spec sheets describe a model; you are renting a specific frame number. Two Honda Waves can feel quite different if one has fresh tyres and a well-adjusted chain and the other is nearing a service. Treat model reputation as a tie-breaker, not the decision.

If you are still deciding between manual, semi-automatic and automatic types, start with our guide to [which motorbike type suits you in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/manual-semi-automatic-or-automatic-motorbike-hanoi/). This article assumes you have already chosen a foot-shift bike and the shop has put two in front of you.

## Step 1: Park them side by side

Ask staff to put both bikes on their stands in the same place, in daylight if possible. Side by side, small differences become visible that you would never notice separately: one tyre looks flatter, one handlebar sits crooked, one seat is torn, one body panel is loose.

Do the checks in the same order on both bikes so that the second does not get a faster look than the first. A thirty-second delay is not a problem; staff who set up a bike for hire expect customers to look.

## Step 2: Compare condition, part by part

Use the same short list for each machine. Our guide to [checking a semi-automatic motorbike before renting](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-check-a-semi-automatic-motorbike-before-renting-in-hanoi/) goes deep on each item; this table is the comparison version.

| Check | What to look at | Why it separates two bikes |
|---|---|---|
| Tyres | Tread depth, cracks, the date or wear marks, pressure by thumb | The cheapest difference to spot and one of the most important in rain |
| Front brake | Lever travel and firmness, any grinding | A soft or very long lever is a reason to switch bikes |
| Rear brake | Pedal travel and the feel when pressed | Drum brakes vary a lot with adjustment |
| Chain | Rust, dryness, visible slack | Our [chain tension guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/checking-chain-tension-before-a-longer-ride/) explains the check |
| Lights and horn | Headlight, brake light, indicators, horn | A dead bulb is a small thing that tells you about upkeep |
| Mirrors | Both present, tight, adjustable | Loose mirrors are a daily annoyance |
| Seat and grips | Torn foam, loose grips | Comfort and control |
| Fuel gauge or fuel level | Gauge moves, tank is not empty | Handover fuel is part of the deal |
| Dents and scratches | Any existing damage | Photograph it so it is on record |

Count the problems on each bike rather than only noting them. A bike with one trivial scuff and good tyres beats a cleaner-looking bike with a spongy front brake.

## Step 3: Know the paper differences, and how small they are

Manufacturer figures for the common underbone bikes are close enough that they should rarely decide the matter. The figures below come from official pages and manuals for the models named, and they may not match the exact variant or age of the bike you are offered.

| Figure | Honda Wave RSX FI | Honda Blade | Yamaha Sirius FI |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weight | 97 to 98 kg (Honda manual) | 98 to 99 kg (Honda page) | 98 kg with fuel and oil (Yamaha page) |
| Seat height | Not stated in the manual | 769 mm | 775 mm |
| Wheelbase | 1,227 mm | 1,217 mm | 1,235 mm |
| Ground clearance | 135 mm | 141 mm | 155 mm |
| Fuel tank | 4.0 L | 3.7 L | 4.0 L |
| Gearbox | 4-speed | 4-speed rotary | 4-speed rotary |

What these figures say in practice: the three bikes weigh about the same, sit at a similar height and carry a similar amount of fuel. The Sirius has the most clearance on paper and the Blade the smallest tank, but you would barely notice either in Hanoi traffic. The one number that can matter is seat height, if you are short, and even there a few millimetres are less important than how the seat is shaped. Sit on both.

Older or different variants can change these numbers, for example a drum-brake or disc-brake front end, so do not assume the sheet describes the exact bike. If the shop offers a model that is not in the table, ask which version it is.

## Step 4: Ride each one for five minutes

A short test ride is the most informative comparison. With the shop's agreement, ride each bike around the same short loop, and keep to a quiet route.

On each bike, note these five things:

1. **Starting.** Does it start promptly on the electric starter, and does it hold a steady idle? Does the neutral light work?
2. **Shifting.** Is first gear found cleanly? Are upshifts smooth, or does the bike clunk or lurch? Our guide to [avoiding lurching when changing gears](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-avoid-lurching-when-changing-gears/) will help you tell your technique from the bike.
3. **Braking.** Brake gently in a straight line from a low speed. Does it stop evenly, without a pull to one side, a squeal or a graunch? See [what to test on manual bike brakes](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/manual-bike-brakes-what-to-test-before-leaving-the-rental-shop/).
4. **Steering and stability.** Does it track straight with light hands? Does the front end shake when you let go gently? A bike that pulls to one side or wobbles is not the one to take.
5. **Noise.** Any rattle, grinding, whine or knock that the other bike does not make.

Ride them in a row, one after the other, so your memory of the first is fresh. Do not ride far on either; this is a comparison, not the rental.

## Step 5: Score them, and let safety outrank comfort

Write a quick score from 1 to 5 for each line on both bikes. A simple scorecard:

| Line | Bike A | Bike B |
|---|---|---|
| Tyres | | |
| Brakes (front and rear) | | |
| Chain and drive | | |
| Start and idle | | |
| Shifting | | |
| Steering and stability | | |
| Fit (seat, reach) | | |
| Storage and luggage | | |
| Lights, mirrors, small parts | | |

Then apply one rule: any score of 1 or 2 on tyres, brakes, chain, steering or starting outweighs a better score elsewhere. Comfort, colour and styling are tie-breakers once both bikes are safe. If both bikes have a safety-line problem, ask for a third bike or a quick fix before you ride, rather than choosing the lesser of two problems.

## Fit: your body, your luggage

Sit on each bike and put both feet down. Can you reach the ground comfortably with the sidestand up? Can you reach the brake lever and shift lever without straining? Is the seat long enough for you, and if you ride two-up, for the passenger?

Then consider what you are carrying. Honda's Wave RSX FI manual limits the under-seat box to 5 kg, and Yamaha describes the Sirius FI's under-seat compartment as big enough for a half-head helmet and a raincoat. Two bikes may therefore swallow different amounts. Our guide to [how much luggage a Honda Wave can safely carry](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-much-luggage-can-you-safely-carry-on-a-honda-wave/) explains the limits.

Finally, think about the use. A bike for a week of city riding is judged differently from one for a day trip; our piece on [choosing a manual motorbike for a week-long rental](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/choosing-a-manual-motorbike-for-a-week-long-hanoi-rental/) covers that.

## Questions to put to the shop

For both bikes, ask the same questions so that the answers are comparable:

- How many kilometres has it done, and when was it last serviced?
- When were the tyres and chain last changed or adjusted?
- Is there any known fault or recent repair?
- Which exact model and version is it?
- What are the handover fuel and the condition notes on the contract?

Our guide to [questions to ask about a high-mileage rental](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/questions-to-ask-about-a-high-mileage-manual-rental-bike/) gives more detail. If the answers differ a lot, that is information. The shop confirms the final price on the actual bike before the contract is signed, and the [prices page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/prices/) shows the usual ranges. ## Decide, then record

Pick the bike with the better safety score. If the two are level, pick the one that fits you better. ## FAQ: comparing two semi-automatic bikes

### Which is better, a Wave, a Sirius or a Blade?

On paper they are very close. The individual bike's condition matters more than the model.

### Can I ask for a test ride of both bikes?

It is reasonable to ask for a short ride on each. The shop decides, so ask politely and keep it brief.

### Is the newer-looking bike always better?

No. A clean body can hide worn tyres or a soft brake. Check the parts, not the paint.

### What if both bikes have a problem?

Ask for another bike or a repair before riding. Do not choose the lesser of two faults.

### Do I need to know the exact model year?

It helps, because specifications and brakes vary by version. Ask the shop which version it is.

### Does the price differ between the two bikes?

Ranges are on the prices page, and the final price is confirmed on the actual bike before you sign.
