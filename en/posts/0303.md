The best time to reach Dong Xuan Market by motorbike is on a weekday morning or early afternoon, or at the weekend before 7 p.m., and the best parking plan is to leave the bike in a guarded lot on the edge of the Old Quarter and walk the last few minutes. The indoor market is described by Vietnam Airlines as open daily from 6:00 to 18:00, while the weekend night market outside it runs after dark. Since 1 July 2026, news reports say that motorbikes and cars are not allowed into the core pedestrian zone, which includes the night market, from 19:00 to midnight on Fridays, Saturdays and Sundays. This guide sets out the hours, the traffic window to avoid, where bikes can and cannot stand and what to do with shopping bags.

## What Dong Xuan Market is and where it sits

Dong Xuan is a large covered market in the northern part of Hanoi's Old Quarter. Vietnam Airlines gives its address as 15 Cau Dong Street and describes the surrounding streets as Dong Xuan to the west, Hang Khoai to the north, Cau Dong to the south and Nguyen Thien Thuat to the east. Inside, the daytime market has three floors linked by stairs and lifts. Another travel site calls it one of the largest indoor markets in the city, with fabrics, clothing, household goods and wholesale lines.

The practical point for a rider is that the market sits inside a tight grid of one-way and narrow streets rather than on a wide road with an obvious forecourt. Our guide to [riding in Hanoi's Old Quarter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-old-quarter/) explains how those streets behave, and the [Old Quarter area page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/old-quarter/) gives the wider picture.

## Opening hours: day market and weekend night market

Sources agree on the broad pattern but not on every minute, so treat the times below as a planning guide and look at what is posted when you arrive.

| Part of the market | Typical hours reported | Source type |
|---|---|---|
| Indoor day market | Daily, about 06:00 to 18:00 | Vietnam Airlines travel guide |
| Weekend night market outside | Friday to Sunday, from about 18:00 | Several travel guides |
| Night market closing | Between about 22:30 and midnight, depending on the guide | Guides differ |

Vietnam Airlines says the kiosks inside the building are closed at night and that the weekend activity takes place on the streets outside. Other guides place the night market on Hang Dao, Hang Ngang and Hang Duong, ending at the main gate of Dong Xuan. One recent guide adds that some stalls pack up earlier on Sundays. The practical lesson is that the day market and the night market are different experiences on different schedules, and they call for different transport plans.

## The 2026 evening window when motorbikes cannot enter

This is the single most important timing fact for anyone arriving by bike. On 1 July 2026 Hanoi began the first phase of a low-emission zone pilot in Hoan Kiem ward. VOV and VnExpress report that Zone 1, which covers the Hoan Kiem Lake pedestrian area, the Old Quarter and the weekend night market, bars motorbikes, motorcycles and all cars, apart from defence and public-security vehicles, from 19:00 to midnight every Friday, Saturday and Sunday.

Earlier draft plans spoke of a wider petrol-motorbike ban, and the wording of those drafts changed several times before launch. The reports on the launch itself describe the Friday to Sunday evening closure as the main rule for private motorbikes. The pilot is scheduled to run from 1 July to 31 December 2026, with later phases planned, so a December visit and a 2027 visit may not follow the same rules.

What this means in practice:

- Do not plan to ride through Hang Dao, Hang Ngang or Hang Duong toward the market on a Friday, Saturday or Sunday evening.
- If you want the night market, park outside the pedestrian area before 19:00 and walk in.
- If you arrive from the north side, check the signs rather than assuming where the zone boundary falls. The reports name the Old Quarter as a whole, and a map app will not show a temporary barrier.
- Treat any map-app route that enters the Old Quarter on a weekend evening as unreliable.

## Which time slot suits which kind of visit

A weekday morning is the calmest way to see the building itself, because shoppers and traders are active but the evening crowds are not there. Early afternoon works too. Late afternoon on any day is when many streets in this part of town fill with people finishing work, so allow extra time to leave.

Friday to Sunday before 19:00 is workable by bike, but you are racing the closure. Allow at least a quarter of an hour to find a space and walk back to the market, and do not leave it until the last minutes before the barriers go up, because a lot that is already full may force you into a longer detour.

From 19:00 on those days, the right choice is on foot. Our guide to [riding at night in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-at-night-in-hanoi/) covers lights and visibility if you must ride home after dark, and the [street food loop by scooter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-street-food-loop-by-scooter/) suggests places to eat that do not depend on the Old Quarter core.

## Where to leave the bike

Under Article 18 of Law 36/2024/QH15 on road traffic order and safety, in force since 1 January 2025, a rider who parks on a city street must park close to the right-hand kerb in the direction of travel, with the nearest wheel no more than 0.25 metres from the kerb, and must not obstruct pedestrians or other vehicles. The same article lists places where stopping and parking are not allowed, including within 5 metres of a junction, on a pedestrian crossing, at pick-up and drop-off points and on the pavement or carriageway where the rules do not permit it. Reading it in plain terms: a bike left across a market lane or on a crossing is a problem even if it seems convenient.

So the sensible options are these:

1. **A guarded lot or ticketed parking point.** Our guide to [parking a motorbike in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-motorbike-in-hanoi/) explains how tickets and attendants work.
2. **A lot on the edge of the Old Quarter, then a walk.** The earlier guide on [where to park near Hoan Kiem Lake](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/where-to-park-a-scooter-near-hoan-kiem-lake/) lists the general approach for the lake side, and the same idea applies on the Dong Xuan side.
3. **Park in a quieter street and walk.** A few extra minutes on foot is usually quicker than circling.

VnExpress reported at the pilot's launch that the city had reviewed 220 parking facilities and planned park-and-ride sites linked to public transport. These details are about the wider zone, not specifically about Dong Xuan, so do not expect a particular lot to be free at a particular time.

## Checks before you walk away from the bike

Whatever lot you use, take a few seconds for the same routine:

- Lock the steering and keep the key on you. The shop does not provide an extra lock, so a guarded place matters more.
- Take the ticket or tag and keep it somewhere safe. Photograph the bike and the lot sign.
- Ask until what time the lot is open, especially on weekends, before you go into a crowded market that you may not leave quickly.
- Take your helmet with you if the attendant will not store it, and do not leave valuables in the bike.

A short [pre-ride inspection](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-scooter-inspection-checklist/) before you leave the shop also means you will recognise any new mark if something happens in a busy lot.

## Carrying purchases back

A market of this kind tempts buyers to carry more than they planned. Keep bags off the handlebars, since a swinging bag affects steering and can catch on a mirror or lever. Hang weight low and in the centre, never over a wheel, and buy the bulky things last. If you have bought something large, such as fabric rolls or boxed goods, consider a courier or taxi for the item and ride back with the lighter ones. Our guide to [how much luggage a Honda Wave can carry](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-much-luggage-can-you-safely-carry-on-a-honda-wave/) goes into weight and balance.

Keep cash safe and zipped away while walking through crowds, and check the lot's closing time before you shop, so a long afternoon does not end at a locked gate.

## Getting there and leaving

A good habit is to decide your exit as well as your entrance. When you leave the market, a long queue of bikes and pedestrians can move slowly, and one-way streets mean that the road you came by may not be the road you can leave by. Set the map app to motorbike mode before you start the engine, and walk the bike for the first few metres if the lane is packed.

If rain starts, remember that short rentals at Nguyen Ha do not include a raincoat, so see our guide to [riding in Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) before heading out on a wet afternoon.

## A simple plan by day

**Weekday:** arrive mid-morning, park at the edge of the Old Quarter, walk in, shop for an hour or two, eat nearby and be on the road before the evening rush.

**Saturday or Sunday daytime:** go early, park outside the walking area and expect pavements to be busy.

**Friday to Sunday evening:** leave the bike parked well before 19:00, or leave it at the shop and use other transport. ## FAQ: Dong Xuan Market by motorbike

### Is Dong Xuan Market open every day?

Vietnam Airlines lists the indoor market as open daily from 06:00 to 18:00. Check the posted hours on arrival, particularly around public holidays.

### Can I ride a motorbike to the night market on a Friday evening?

Reports on the July 2026 pilot say motorbikes and cars are barred from the core pedestrian area from 19:00 to midnight on Fridays, Saturdays and Sundays. Park outside before then and walk.

### Is there free parking at Dong Xuan?

None of the sources I read promise a free or guaranteed space. Expect a ticketed lot or a ticketed street spot and ask the attendant about the charge before you leave the bike.

### Can I park on the pavement outside the market?

The road traffic law requires parking next to the kerb without obstructing pedestrians, and local rules can prohibit pavement parking. Use a marked or guarded space instead.

### Is a rental scooter practical for a market trip?

For a short visit, yes, as long as you plan parking and avoid the Friday to Sunday evening window. For a full evening, walking is easier.
