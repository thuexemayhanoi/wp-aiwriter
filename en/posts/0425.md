After a minor collision on a rental scooter, stop at once, check that nobody is hurt, photograph the scene and the damage, exchange names, phone numbers and plate numbers, call the shop and do not agree to pay anyone on the spot. Vietnam's traffic law sets duties for people involved in an accident: stop immediately, warn of danger, keep the scene, help anyone who is hurt and report to the police or the local authorities, and stay until the police arrive unless you must leave for emergency care.

A small bump can feel trivial, but leaving the scene or arguing can turn it into something serious, and the rental bike belongs to the shop, so the shop needs to hear about it from you. This guide explains what a minor collision is, the steps in order, what to say and not say, how roadside settlements work and why they are risky, how insurance fits in and what happens at return. It is general information, not legal advice.

## What we mean by a minor collision

A minor collision is a low-speed contact in which nobody appears seriously hurt and the damage looks small. Examples include:

- two bikes touching at a junction or in a queue;

- a scooter clipping a parked car or a wing mirror;

- a scooter brushing a pedestrian or a bicycle who walks away unhurt;

- a rider falling after a bump with another vehicle and getting up without serious injury.

Even when everything looks small, treat it seriously. People can be hurt without knowing it, a scratch on a car can lead to a large demand, and a bent frame or fork on a scooter can be a safety problem you cannot see. If anyone is hurt, or you are unsure, use the guidance in our guide to [what to do after a serious accident on a rental motorbike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-to-do-after-a-serious-accident-on-a-rental-motorbike/).

## What the law says you must do

Vietnam's Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (Law No. 36/2024/QH15), in force since 1 January 2025, deals with accidents in Article 80. We read the text on the Government portal. As of October 2026, it says that the driver who causes an accident, the people involved and the people present must:

- **stop immediately, warn of danger and keep the scene intact;**

- **help the injured** and report to the nearest police, medical facility or People's Committee;

- **stay at the scene until the police arrive,** unless leaving to take a victim for emergency care or because of a threat to safety, in which case they must report immediately to the nearest police or People's Committee;

- and bystanders must help protect victims' property and help with rescue.

Whether a particular small bump counts as an accident for these purposes is a matter for the police, so the safe approach is to follow the duties in any collision: stop, check people, and call the police if anyone is hurt, if there is a dispute or if the damage is more than trivial.

The penalty decree reinforces this. Under Article 7 of Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, which we read on the Government portal, a rider who is directly involved in an accident and does not stop at once, keep the scene and help the injured is fined VND 1,000,000–2,000,000, and a rider who causes an accident and fails to stop, stay at the scene or report to the police or local authorities is fined VND 8,000,000–10,000,000. Decree 238/2026/ND-CP, which amends the decree from 15 August 2026, does not change Article 7. Do not leave.

## Step 1: stop and make the scene safe

Stop as soon as it is safe, switch off the engine, put the bike on its stand and take the key. If you are in the traffic lane, get yourself and the bike to the side if you can do so without risk, but see the note on the scene below. Put on hazard lights if the bike has them, and ask someone to warn traffic. Do not stand in the lane. Smell for fuel, and keep away from sparks and cigarettes if there is a leak.

## Step 2: check people first

Before you think about the bikes, check the people. Ask yourself and the other party if anyone is hurt. Adrenaline hides pain, so do not rely on "I am fine" for a few minutes. If anyone is hurt, call 115 for an ambulance or 112, the national emergency number set up by Decree 200/2025/ND-CP, which is free, answers 24 hours a day and is linked with the 113 police, 114 fire and rescue and 115 medical centres. Do not move a person with a neck, back or head injury unless there is immediate danger. Stay with them and help as far as you can.

## Step 3: photograph before things move

Photographs are your best evidence, and they are quickest at the start.

- **The whole scene,** from a few steps back, showing both vehicles and their positions.

- **The damage on both vehicles,** close up and wider.

- **The plate numbers** of every vehicle involved.

- **The road:** the junction, the markings, any signs, the surface and any hazards.

- **Skid marks, oil or debris,** if there are any.

- **Any injuries,** if the person agrees.

The law says to keep the scene intact. If vehicles are blocking the road and nobody is hurt, photograph the positions first, and then follow the instructions of the police or, if they are not yet there, move the vehicles only as far as needed to prevent a second collision. If you are unsure, call the police and ask. Take a short video as well, narrating the date and place.

## Step 4: exchange information

Collect and give the basics.

- **Names and phone numbers** of the other party and any passengers.

- **Plate numbers and vehicle types.**

- **Driving licence details,** by photograph if the person agrees.

- **Insurance certificate details,** if one is offered. The compulsory certificate should name the plate and the cover period.

- **Your own details,** including the shop's name and the rental bike's plate number. You may show the rental contract or a photograph of it.

Be polite and calm. You do not need to share your passport number or home address beyond what is reasonable, but the police may ask for your documents. Our guide to [what identity documents a rental shop may ask to see](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-identity-documents-a-rental-shop-may-ask-to-see/) explains what a shop keeps on file.

## Step 5: find witnesses and cameras

Ask anyone who saw what happened for a name and number, and note shops, cafes and car parks with cameras that cover the spot. Tell the police, who can request footage. Do not try to take footage yourself without permission.

## Step 6: call the shop

Call Nguyen Ha on 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969), open 09:00 to 21:00 daily. Tell the shop what happened, whether anyone is hurt, the state of the bike and where you are. The shop needs to know quickly, may have advice for the situation and will need to decide whether the bike can be ridden. Send photographs afterwards. Our guide to [when a rental motorbike needs to be collected](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/when-a-rental-motorbike-needs-to-be-collected/) explains when the right answer is collection.

## Step 7: call the police when you should

Call the police if:

- **anyone is hurt,** even slightly;

- **there is a dispute** about what happened or who is at fault;

- **the other party refuses to give details** or tries to leave;

- **the damage is more than trivial,** or involves a public or third-party property;

- **a pedestrian or a child is involved;**

- **you are not sure what to do.**

The national emergency number 112 connects with the 113 police centre. You can also go to the nearest ward police station. Ask for a written record or a reference number for the report. If you are asked to sign a document you cannot read, ask for a translation or an explanation first, and do not sign anything you do not understand. The shop can sometimes help explain.

## Roadside settlements: be careful

After a small collision, the other party may offer to settle on the spot: "Give me some money and we will forget it." This can seem quick and easy, but it carries risks.

- **You do not know the true cost** of the damage, which may be much larger than it looks.

- **The other party may change their mind later** or claim for an injury that appears afterwards.

- **You may pay twice,** once on the roadside and again through the shop or an insurer.

- **The bike is not yours.** Damage to the rental bike is a matter between you and the shop, and the contract usually requires you to report it.

- **A private agreement may leave you without a record** that you can show the shop, the police or an insurer.

If you are tempted to settle, call the shop first, ask what it advises and do not pay without understanding what the payment is for. If you do agree something, have it written down, with both names, phone numbers, the date and the amount, and photograph the note. Our guide to [how to keep receipts for repairs on a rental motorbike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-keep-receipts-for-repairs-on-a-rental-motorbike/) explains how to keep a clear record.

## What not to say or do

- **Do not admit fault or blame others in a heated way.** State the facts: what happened, where and when.

- **Do not leave the scene** unless you must, as explained above.

- **Do not sign anything** you cannot read or do not understand.

- **Do not hand over your passport,** or your licence, to anyone but the police.

- **Do not post accusations online** before the facts are clear.

- **Do not ride away on a damaged bike** if the brakes, steering or wheels are affected.

- **Do not hide the collision from the shop.** Honesty at once costs less than a surprise at return.

## Is the bike safe to ride?

After the exchange, look at the bike before you ride it. Check the levers, the handlebars, the front fork and wheel, the tyres, the lights and the brakes, and watch for leaks. A bent lever, a wobbling wheel or a misaligned handlebar is a reason to stop. If you are unsure, ask the shop. Our guides to [checking the rear brake on a Honda Wave](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/checking-the-rear-brake-on-a-honda-wave/) and [what to do after dropping a rented semi-automatic bike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-to-do-after-dropping-a-rented-semi-automatic-bike/) explain what to inspect, and the same checks apply to scooters.

## Insurance and the contract

Two kinds of insurance come up after a collision.

**The compulsory insurance on the bike** is civil-liability insurance. Under Decree 67/2023/ND-CP, as we explain in our guide to [travel insurance and motorbike rentals](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/travel-insurance-motorbike-rental-vietnam/), it compensates third parties for injury and property damage caused by the vehicle, within limits. It does not pay for your own injuries or for repairs to the rental bike. Tell the shop, which holds the certificate.

**Your own travel insurance** may cover your medical costs, but only if the policy covers the bike and your licence, and many policies expect you to report quickly. Call the insurer's assistance line and ask what it needs.

Damage to the rental bike is governed by your contract. Vietnam's Civil Code (Law No. 91/2015/QH13), in force since 1 January 2017, says a lessee must preserve the leased property and compensate for damage, apart from natural wear, and the contract sets the amount and conditions. Our guide to [what to ask about damage liability before renting](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-to-ask-about-damage-liability-before-renting/) explains the wording. Prices and terms are on the [prices page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/prices/).

## At return

Tell the shop about the collision at return if you have not already, and go through the damage together. Show your photographs, the details of the other party and any written record. Ask for any charge to be itemised and written down. Our guide to [returning your rental motorbike in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/returning-your-rental-motorbike-hanoi/) explains the walk-round.

## A quick checklist

1. **Stop, switch off, make the scene safe.**

2. **Check people first; call 115 or 112 if anyone is hurt.**

3. **Photograph the scene, damage and plates before things move.**

4. **Exchange names, phones, plates and insurance details.**

5. **Note witnesses and cameras.**

6. **Call the shop on 0334 699 969.**

7. **Call the police if anyone is hurt, there is a dispute or the damage is not trivial.**

8. **Do not settle, sign or admit fault in a rush.**

9. **Check the bike before riding.**

10. **Tell the shop everything, and keep every record.**

## FAQ: a minor collision on a rental scooter

### Do I have to stop after a small bump?

Yes. Vietnam's traffic law sets duties to stop, keep the scene and help anyone hurt, and the penalty decree fines riders who leave.

### What is the fine for leaving the scene?

As of October 2026, VND 1,000,000–2,000,000 for a rider directly involved who does not stop and help, and VND 8,000,000–10,000,000 for a rider who causes an accident and does not stop, stay or report, under Article 7 of Decree 168/2024/ND-CP.

### Should I pay on the spot?

Not without calling the shop and understanding what you are paying for. Roadside settlements carry risks.

### Who should I call first?

If anyone is hurt, call 115 or 112. Otherwise call the shop and the police if there is a dispute.

### Does the compulsory insurance cover my own injuries or the bike?

No. It covers third parties, not the rider or the rental bike.

### Should I tell the shop even if the damage is small?

Yes, straight away.
