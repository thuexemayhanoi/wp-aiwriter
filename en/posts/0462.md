Riding after dark where street lighting is poor comes down to one rule: never ride faster than you can stop within the distance your headlight lets you see. Law 36/2024/QH15 requires operators to turn on front lights between 18:00 and 06:00 (Article 20), to switch from high beam to low beam when they meet oncoming traffic, pedestrians crossing, lit populous areas or when changing direction at intersections, and to observe the surroundings and slow down when visibility is limited (Article 12). On an unlit road, that means a lower speed, a clean visor, a working light aimed correctly, a bigger gap to the vehicle ahead and a readiness to stop for things you cannot yet see, such as an unlit bicycle, a pedestrian, an animal or a hole.

This guide explains the legal rules, why dark roads are different, how to match speed to sight distance, how to use high and low beam properly, what hazards to expect, how to deal with glare and fatigue, what to do if the bike fails in the dark and when it is better to wait for daylight. It is general information based on the law and published riding guidance as of October 2026, not legal advice.

## What the law requires at night

Law 36/2024/QH15 contains three rules that apply directly.

- **Article 20(1): front lights on.** Drivers and operators must turn on front lights between 18:00 and 06:00 the next day, and whenever fog, smoke, dust, rain or bad weather limits visibility.

- **Article 20(2): low beam in four cases.** They must turn off high beams and use low beams when meeting pedestrians crossing the road, when moving through populous areas with working street lighting, when meeting oncoming traffic (except where the median is anti-glare) and when changing direction at intersections.

- **Article 12(3): slow down.** Operators must observe the surroundings and slow down or stop for safety when they have limited visibility, at intersections, on narrow or winding roads, on bridges, in areas with schools, hospitals and crowded places and when they encounter livestock on the road.

Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, as read on the Government portal, sets a fine of VND 200,000 to 400,000 for riding without front lights between 18:00 and 06:00 or in poor visibility, and the same range for using high beam where low beam is required. ## Why poorly lit roads are different

In a lit street, your eyes pick up shapes from the lamps and shopfronts. On a poorly lit road, three things change.

1. **You see only what your headlight shows.** A scooter's light covers a short stretch ahead, and things outside the beam are invisible until the last moment.

2. **People and vehicles without lights disappear.** A pedestrian in dark clothes, a bicycle with no light or a parked vehicle with no reflectors may be seen only at short range.

3. **Road defects are hidden.** Potholes, speed bumps, loose gravel and open drains look like shadows.

Your eyes also need time to adjust after bright light, and the glare from one oncoming vehicle can cost you your night vision for a moment. All this argues for lower speed and more space.

## Rule one: ride within your sight distance

Stopping distance has two parts: the distance you travel while you react, and the distance you travel while you brake. Together they must be less than the distance you can see.

- **If you can see only 20 metres,** your speed must be low enough to stop in less than 20 metres, including reaction time. For a scooter on dry asphalt, that is a modest speed, and on a wet road it is lower still.

- **Rider-training guidance** makes the same point: you should be able to stop within your sight distance.

- **If you must ride in the dark,** slow down until your stopping distance is comfortably inside your headlight beam.

- **Do not follow another vehicle's tail light** as if it were a guide. It does not tell you what is ahead of it.

Circular 38/2024/TT-BGTVT also requires you to keep a safe distance from the vehicle ahead and to increase it in rain, fog, on slippery roads and where visibility is limited. Honda Vietnam's safe-riding training document suggests a gap of four seconds, rather than two, at night or in poor light. ## Using high beam and low beam

Where there is no street lighting and no traffic, high beam shows much more of the road. The law lets you use it, and then requires you to switch in the four cases above.

1. **Use low beam in lit areas and in traffic.** If streetlights are working, or if other vehicles are near, use low beam.

2. **Dip early for oncoming vehicles,** well before they reach you, so that the other rider is not dazzled. Do the same when you come up behind another vehicle.

3. **Dip for pedestrians crossing and for junctions,** as Article 20(2) requires.

4. **Switch back to high beam** once the other vehicle has passed, if the road is unlit.

5. **Do not flash high beam as a warning habit.** Use your horn briefly where it is needed, in line with Article 21, which allows horns to warn of circumstances affecting traffic safety and bans continuous use, and bans horn use between 22:00 and 05:00 in populous areas.

If your light is poorly aimed, high beam may dazzle others even when you think it is on low. Our guide to [why motorbike headlight alignment matters at night](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/why-motorbike-headlight-alignment-matters-at-night/) explains how to check the aim.

## Hazards to expect on unlit roads

- **Pedestrians and cyclists without lights,** especially at the roadside, near villages and at junctions.

- **Animals.** Article 12 lists livestock moving on the road or grazing at the roadside as a case for slowing down.

- **Parked or broken-down vehicles** with no lights, including trucks.

- **Potholes and debris.** Our guide to [dealing with potholes on a rental motorbike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/dealing-with-potholes-on-a-rental-motorbike/) explains how to treat them.

- **Speed bumps, ramps and uneven joins** that are hard to see.

- **Construction areas** and temporary barriers.

- **Unlit junctions and side roads,** where vehicles emerge with no warning.

- **Level crossings.** Article 24 requires you to stop to the right, behind the stop line, when there is a signal, and to stop and look both ways at an unguarded crossing.

- **Bends,** where your light points straight ahead and not round the corner.

- **Delineators and reflectors,** which are useful, because the law lists reflective nails, reflective posts and kilometre markers as road signals (Article 11).

## Dealing with glare

Glare from oncoming vehicles is one of the most common problems at night.

- **Look to the right edge of your lane,** not into the lights, as an oncoming vehicle passes.

- **Slow down** if you are dazzled, and keep your line.

- **Keep your visor clean and unscratched,** inside and out, because scratches scatter light. Published guidance warns that dry-wiping grit can scratch a visor, which is worse at night.

- **Wear a clear visor,** and not a tinted one, at night.

- **Do not wear tinted glasses.**

- **Keep your windscreen or visor free of stickers.**

## Being seen on a dark road

Others have the same problem seeing you.

- **Keep your headlight and tail light working and clean.**

- **Check your brake light,** front and rear, before you set off.

- **Signal early,** and keep the signal on.

- **Wear light or reflective clothing,** if you have it. A reflective strip on a bag or jacket helps.

- **Do not stop in an unlit part of the road.**

- **Keep to the right,** as Article 10 requires.

## Fatigue and planning

Night riding is more tiring, because your eyes work harder and the body clock expects rest.

- **Plan to finish before dark** where you can. Our guides to [how to plan a scooter route with limited daylight](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-plan-a-scooter-route-with-limited-daylight/) and [avoiding night riding on a Hanoi return trip](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/avoiding-night-riding-on-a-hanoi-return-trip/) explain how.

- **Stick to roads you know,** and avoid shortcuts on unlit lanes.

- **Take breaks.** If you feel your eyes tiring or your attention fading, stop in a safe, lit place.

## If the bike fails in the dark

1. **Slow down, signal and move off the carriageway** where you can.

2. **Switch on the emergency lights.** Article 18 says that if a technical incident forces a stop on part of the carriageway or in a place not designated for parking, the operator must signal with emergency lights or place a warning sign behind the vehicle.

3. **Stand somewhere safe,** well off the road, behind a barrier if there is one.

4. **Call the shop** on 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969), open 09:00 to 21:00 daily, and the emergency number 112 if anyone is hurt or you are in danger. Our guide to [rental bike breakdown in Hanoi: what to do](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-bike-breakdown-hanoi-what-to-do/) explains the steps.

5. **Do not leave the bike on the road** without lights.

If the light itself fails, slow down at once, move to the side and stop. Do not ride on without a headlight. Prices and terms are on the [prices page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/prices/).

## When not to ride after dark

Consider waiting for daylight, or using a taxi or bus, if:

- **The road is unlit and unfamiliar.**

- **It is raining, foggy or dusty,** which together with darkness cuts visibility sharply.

- **You are tired,** or have been riding for hours.

- **The bike's light is weak or poorly aimed.**

- **You are carrying a passenger or luggage** that changes the beam angle.

- **You are on a mountain or country road.** Our guide to [when an intercity bus is safer than a rental motorbike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/when-an-intercity-bus-is-safer-than-a-rental-motorbike/) explains how to decide.

## A quick checklist

1. **Light on from 18:00, and check that it works.**

2. **Ride slowly enough to stop within your headlight beam.**

3. **Dip for oncoming traffic, pedestrians crossing, lit areas and junctions.**

4. **Keep a bigger gap, and do not follow tail lights blindly.**

5. **Expect unlit people, animals and potholes.**

6. **Look to the right edge when dazzled.**

7. **Keep your visor clean and clear.**

8. **If the bike fails, use emergency lights and get off the road.**

## FAQ: riding after dark with limited street lighting

### When must I turn on my headlight?

Between 18:00 and 06:00, and whenever fog, smoke, dust, rain or bad weather limits visibility (Article 20 of Law 36/2024/QH15).

### When must I use low beam?

When meeting pedestrians crossing, in lit populous areas, when meeting oncoming traffic and when changing direction at intersections.

### How fast can I ride on a dark road?

Slowly enough to stop within the distance your headlight lets you see. The law and Circular 38/2024/TT-BGTVT also require safe distances and lower speeds when visibility is limited.

### What is the fine for riding without lights?

VND 200,000 to 400,000 under Decree 168/2024/ND-CP for no front lights at night or in poor visibility, and the same range for high beam where low beam is required. Check the current text.

### How do I deal with oncoming glare?

Look to the right edge of your lane, slow down and keep your line. Keep your visor clean.

### What if my light fails?

Slow down, move off the carriageway and stop. Use emergency lights and call the shop.
