Near a school in Hanoi, slow down well before the gate, expect a child to step out from between parked bikes at any moment and be ready to stop in less than the distance you can see. Law 36/2024/QH15 says operators must observe their surroundings and slow down or stop for safety in areas with schools, hospitals, bus terminals or public works crowded with people, and where pedestrians are crossing (Article 12). The law gives no single school-zone speed figure, so the number that matters is the one you can stop within. The World Health Organization recommends speeds of 30 km/h or less where children live, learn and play, and its road safety fact sheet notes that a pedestrian hit at 65 km/h is 4.5 times more likely to die than one hit at 50 km/h.

This guide explains why children move the way they do, what a school street looks like at the busy times, how to adjust speed, position, horn use and eyes, what the law bans around crossings and what to do if the worst happens. It is general information based on the law and published safety guidance as of October 2026, not legal advice.

## Why school streets are different

A school street combines three things that rarely meet elsewhere: a crowd of people who are small, unpredictable and hard to see; a surge of vehicles all stopping at once; and adults who are looking at their children, not at you.

- **Children are hard to see.** A seven-year-old stands lower than a parked scooter's seat. A child between two parked bikes or behind a vendor's cart is invisible until the moment of stepping out.

- **Children decide quickly.** They may see a parent across the street, a friend or a seller and move at once, without looking at traffic. Article 30 of the Law requires children under 7 to be escorted by adults when crossing, and says all road users must help children cross, which tells you how the law sees the risk.

- **Adults are distracted too.** A parent holding a bag, a phone and a child's hand may step off the kerb without looking.

- **Vehicles stop without warning.** Scooters and cars stop in the lane to drop children, often with no signal.

UNICEF's 2022 technical guidance on child and adolescent road safety, drawing on WHO data, reports that pedestrians account for nearly 40 per cent of child road traffic deaths worldwide, and that 30 km/h zones are associated with a 70 per cent reduction in fatal child pedestrian injuries. Those figures describe whole populations, not your street, but they show why speed is the first thing to change.

## What the law requires

Three provisions matter most for a rider near a school.

**Slow down or stop (Article 12).** Operators must observe the surroundings, and slow down or stop for safety in areas with schools, hospitals, bus terminals or public works crowded with people, populous areas and marketplaces. The same article lists places with pedestrian crossings or where pedestrians are crossing, places with warning signs, intersections and limited visibility. Speed must also suit traffic density and other conditions.

**No overtaking at crossings (Article 14).** Overtaking is prohibited on pedestrian crossings and when pedestrians or wheelchair users are crossing the road. A bike that has stopped in the next lane may be stopped for a person you cannot see, so do not pass it near a crossing.

**Horn use (Article 21).** Horns may be used only to warn of circumstances that may affect traffic safety, or to signal an intention to overtake. Other than priority vehicles, you may not use the horn continuously, and you may not use it between 22:00 and 05:00 in populous areas and near medical facilities. A short, light tap to warn a child who is about to step out is allowed. A long blast, which can startle a child into running, is not a safe habit.

**Stopping (Article 18).** You may not stop or park on pedestrian crossings, within 5 metres of the edge of intersections, or where you obscure signs and signals. Dropping a child off, or waiting for one, is no exception.

Under Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, as reported by an official provincial portal, a rider who fails to observe, slow down or stop as required and causes an accident faces a fine of VND 10,000,000 to 14,000,000. Check the current text if a figure matters to you. ## The busy times

Schools in Hanoi run to their own schedules, which vary by school, age group and season, and this article does not give times because they cannot be verified for every school. Instead, treat these as busy:

- **Around the start of the school day,** when parents drop children off.

- **Around the end of each school session,** when parents collect them, often in a crowd at the gate.

- **On rainy days,** when more parents drive, more people shelter under awnings and visibility is worse. Our guide to [safe scooter riding when visibility drops in rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/safe-scooter-riding-when-visibility-drops-in-rain/) explains how to adjust.

- **Near events,** such as a ceremony or a parents' meeting, when the street may be partly closed.

If your route passes a school, the easiest way to reduce risk is to choose a different street at these times, or to leave 20 minutes later or earlier. A map app may not know that a street is blocked by parked bikes.

## How to ride past a school

**1. Slow down early.** Begin to slow before you can see the gate. Speed that feels slow in traffic is still fast if a child steps out.

**2. Give your brain time.** At 30 km/h you still travel more than 8 metres each second. Reaction time and braking add up, so a gap of a few metres is not enough.

**3. Hold a lane position that gives you room.** Keep away from parked vehicles on the right and from the kerb, since a child can come out from either side. Our guide to [passing parked cars without entering oncoming traffic](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/passing-parked-cars-without-entering-oncoming-traffic/) explains how to leave a margin.

**4. Cover the brakes.** Rest two fingers on the front brake lever and a foot near the rear brake, so that you can stop without searching.

**5. Scan the gaps.** Look under and between parked vehicles for legs and feet, which often show before the child does. Look at the heads of adults, because a parent looking down the street may be about to cross.

**6. Watch for a pattern of stopped vehicles.** If a line of vehicles has stopped on the street ahead, there may be a crowd you cannot see. Do not weave around them.

**7. Use the horn lightly, if at all.** A short tap warns, a long blast can alarm. Do not use it as a substitute for slowing down.

**8. Do not follow another bike closely.** A rider ahead may block your view of a crossing child, and may brake late.

**9. Look at the road surface.** Dropped bags, water bottles and balls on the road signal that people are moving.

**10. Leave extra distance from buses.** A stopped school bus or minibus may hide children getting off. Our guide to [sharing the road with buses while riding a scooter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/sharing-the-road-with-buses-while-riding-a-scooter/) covers the rules.

## Sudden movements to expect

| Situation | What may happen | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Child between parked bikes | Steps out without looking | Slow down; give lateral space; cover brakes |
| Child runs across to a parent | Crosses diagonally and fast | Look across the street, not only ahead |
| Ball or bag rolls into the road | Child follows it | Slow down at once; stop if a child is near |
| Vehicle stops in the lane ahead | Door opens, child jumps out | Do not overtake; wait |
| Group of children at a crossing | Some cross, some change their mind | Stop short and let them clear the whole road |
| Vendor cart or crowd at the gate | Narrow gap, people step into it | Move slowly or choose another route |
| Rain | Umbrellas block vision; people hurry | Reduce speed further; brake earlier |

## At a pedestrian crossing

Slow down as you approach, and be prepared to stop. Article 11 of the Law says that at a green light, vehicles must slow down or stop for pedestrians and wheelchair users on the roadway. Do not creep across the crossing. Stop behind the line so that people can see you, and wait until the crossing is clear before moving off. If a vehicle in the next lane has stopped at the crossing, stop too. Do not overtake it, since Article 14 bans overtaking on pedestrian crossings.

## If you must stop near a school

You may need to pull over to check a map or answer a call. Our guide to [when to stop and check a map instead of looking down](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/when-to-stop-and-check-a-map-instead-of-looking-down/) explains how. Near a school:

- **Do not stop on a crossing, at an intersection or in a bus stop.**

- **Do not stop in the middle of a drop-off line.**

- **Move on to a quieter street,** and stop there.

- **Keep your helmet on,** and keep the engine in a state where you can move quickly.

## If someone is hurt

Stop at once, switch on your hazard lights if you have them, keep the scene as it is and call for help. Article 80 of the Law requires people involved in an accident to stop, warn of danger, help victims and report to the nearest police. ## A quick checklist

1. **Slow down before the gate, not at it.**

2. **Assume a child may step out from every gap.**

3. **Keep a margin from parked vehicles and the kerb.**

4. **Cover the brakes.**

5. **Tap the horn lightly, if you must.**

6. **Never overtake at a crossing or a stopped vehicle.**

7. **Stop behind the line, and wait for the crossing to clear.**

8. **Choose another street at drop-off and pick-up times if you can.**

## FAQ: riding near schools

### Is there a speed limit near schools in Vietnam?

Law 36/2024/QH15 does not set a single figure. Article 12 requires you to slow down or stop in areas with schools, and the limit on a given road is shown by signs and by Circular 38/2024/TT-BGTVT. The WHO recommends 30 km/h or less where children live, learn and play.

### Can I overtake a vehicle that has stopped near a school?

Not at a pedestrian crossing or when pedestrians are crossing (Article 14). Elsewhere, only if you can see that the road ahead is clear, and in practice it is safer to wait.

### Can I use my horn near a school?

Only to warn of a danger or to signal overtaking, and not continuously (Article 21). A short tap is allowed. Do not use it between 22:00 and 05:00 in populous areas.

### Who is responsible if a child steps out in front of me?

That depends on the facts and on the investigation. Article 12 gives you a duty to slow down and observe, so ride as though you will be asked to show that you did.

### What should I do first after a collision with a child?

Stop, keep the scene, call 112 and help the child. Then call the shop.

### What if I do not understand what people are saying at the scene?

Stay calm, show that you are ready to help and ask a local person or the shop to translate. Do not sign anything you cannot read.
