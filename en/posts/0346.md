Ecopark is close enough to Hanoi that many people assume it is just another suburb, but it sits across the provincial boundary in Van Giang, Hung Yen province, roughly 13 to 14 km south-east of Hoan Kiem Lake by most reports. It is an easy scooter outing if you cross the Red River on a bridge that suits motorbikes, join the Hanoi to Hung Yen provincial road and ignore the expressway that runs close by. Parking is organised area by area rather than at one visitor car park, so deciding which part of Ecopark you want matters as much as the road. This guide explains the approach routes, the one road you must not follow, what the road surface and traffic are like, and how to park without guesswork.

## Where Ecopark is, and why that matters for a rental

Ecopark's own website gives its address as the Ecopark urban area in Xuan Quan, Van Giang, Hung Yen, with a representative office on Thanh Nien Street in Hanoi, and describes the township as about a 25-minute drive from the centre of the capital. Different sources estimate the distance at 13 to 15 km and the time at 30 to 40 minutes, so the sensible reading is that it is a short trip in good conditions and a longer one at peak hours. Administrative names in the area were rearranged in 2025, so older maps and guides may use different commune names, while the township's address on its own site still reads Van Giang, Hung Yen.

The important point for a renter is that this is outside Hanoi's boundary. Many rental shops care where their bikes go, so tell the shop your plan before you leave. Our article on [taking a rental motorbike out of Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/take-rental-motorbike-out-of-hanoi/) explains the conversation, and the [rental guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-guide/) covers the basics.

## What Ecopark offers visitors

Ecopark says it has four large parks, millions of trees and facilities for picnics, kayaking and cycling, plus shopping at Pho Truc and events, festivals and markets. Park names that come up in visitor guides include Swan Lake Park, Summer Park and Spring Park.

That matters for planning because Ecopark is not one attraction with a single entrance. It is a large residential township with public-facing parks, and the parts you want to visit may be some distance apart. Decide on one sub-area before you go, then plan your road, your parking and your route home around it.

## Three approaches from Hanoi

VM Style, a Vietnamese lifestyle site, sets out several routes from Hoan Kiem Lake, and a second guide repeats them. They fall into three groups.

| Approach | Outline | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Chuong Duong or Vinh Tuy bridge | Cross the Red River, then use the Co Linh junction or the Red River dyke road to reach the Hanoi to Hung Yen provincial road (DT379) | Natural for riders starting on the east bank |
| Thanh Tri Bridge | Cross Thanh Tri Bridge, then join DT379 | Suits riders coming from the south or west of the city |
| National Highway 5 | Follow the highway, then the Phu Thuy overpass and road 179, or cross Vinh Tuy or Thanh Tri and join DT379 | Heavier freight traffic; the guide says to prefer the bridges when it is crowded |

Hanoi's own bridge-diversion notices in 2026 also name a Long Bien to Xuan Quan road, which links the Long Bien area with the commune where Ecopark sits, so it is another arterial road you may meet. Which approach is best depends on your start point and on the day, so compare them in a map app set to motorbike, and check the final junctions by sign.

If you collect a bike from our shop in Long Bien, you are already on the east bank, which shortens the cross-river part of the trip. Our guide to [Gia Lam and the east bank](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/gia-lam-east-bank-bat-trang-by-scooter/) covers the area on this side of the river.

## The road you must not take

The Hanoi to Hai Phong expressway runs close to the roads around Ecopark. A Hung Yen transport planning document on a Vietnamese legal database lists a connecting section between DT379, National Highway 39 and the expressway, and a 2024 news report on the upgrade of the Hanoi to Hung Yen road describes a roundabout beside the expressway toll station where one exit leads to the expressway and another to Hung Yen city. A developer's route page for Ecopark, meanwhile, lists a route from Ring Road 3 via Thanh Tri Bridge that uses the expressway. That is a sensible option for a car, but it is not for a motorbike.

Motorbikes and mopeds may not use expressways. This is set out in Article 25 of the Road Traffic Order and Safety Law, Law 36/2024/QH15, in force since 1 January 2025. The practical risk is a map app that defaults to the quickest route for a car and sends you up a ramp near the interchange. Before you ride, read our guides on [checking whether a navigation app suggests an expressway](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-check-if-a-navigation-app-suggests-an-expressway/) and on [which ring roads and highways allow motorbikes](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-ring-roads-highways-motorbikes-allowed/), which also explains that the elevated decks of the ring roads are car-only.

## Road conditions and traffic

The Hanoi to Hung Yen interprovincial road was built in 2009 and opened in 2014, according to a 2024 Znews report, and the stretch through Hung Yen was being upgraded from August 2021, with completion expected in 2025. The same report notes that the road passes several large industrial zones. That has two implications for a rider. There can be works, temporary barriers and changes in lane layout, and there may be trucks and shift-change traffic around the industrial zones.

VM Style advises avoiding the morning peak from 07:00 to 09:00 and the afternoon peak from 16:30 to 19:00 at the junctions, and preferring Thanh Tri or Vinh Tuy bridges when National Highway 5 is congested. A weekday late morning is therefore easier than a Saturday afternoon. Our guide to [crossing Hanoi intersections](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/crossing-hanoi-intersections/) explains how to merge, which is useful at the bridge approaches.

## Parking: what I could and could not confirm

The official Ecopark pages I found do not publish a single visitor parking policy or a price list for motorbikes, so I have not quoted fees. Tourism guides say only that parking is by area, and an older residents' guide on a property site (2020) describes arrangements such as parking near the Pho Truc and Rung Co area, restrictions on parking along some streets and clamping for vehicles that overstay in short-stay areas. Those details may have changed, so treat them as a reason for caution rather than a rule.

A sensible routine:

1. **Choose your sub-area first.** Parks and shopping streets are in different parts of the township.
2. **Use signed parking.** Follow the signs and the guards' directions, and do not leave the bike on a verge, in front of a gate or on a street marked for no parking.
3. **Keep the ticket and note where you parked.** A township this size looks the same from several angles.
4. **Do not leave the bike overnight.** The older guide says overnight parking is restricted in several areas, so plan to ride home the same day.
5. **Lock the steering and take your helmet and valuables.** The shop does not provide an extra lock, so choose a guarded place.

For general advice on tickets, guards and towing, read [parking a motorbike in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-motorbike-in-hanoi/) and the guide to [paying for fuel, parking and tolls as a visitor](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/paying-fuel-parking-tolls-hanoi-visitor/), which explains how to be ready with small amounts of cash.

## Pairing it with Bat Trang

VM Style suggests combining Ecopark with Bat Trang pottery village, which lies close by, for an easy east-bank day: Ecopark parks in the morning and the village in the afternoon, or the reverse. That fits well with a rental from Long Bien because both are on the south-east side of the city. Our guide to [Gia Lam, the east bank and Bat Trang by scooter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/gia-lam-east-bank-bat-trang-by-scooter/) covers the pottery village side of the plan. Keep the day modest: two stops on a scooter is a comfortable day, three is a rush.

## FAQ: Hanoi to Ecopark by scooter

### Is Ecopark in Hanoi?

No. Its address is in Van Giang, Hung Yen province, close to the Hanoi boundary.

### How far is it?

Sources give 13 to 15 km from the centre and 30 to 40 minutes, with Ecopark itself describing about a 25-minute drive. Allow longer in peak hours.

### Which road should I use?

Cross the Red River by Chuong Duong, Vinh Tuy or Thanh Tri Bridge, then join the Hanoi to Hung Yen provincial road. Do not use the Hanoi to Hai Phong expressway.

### Where do I park?

In signed parking near the area you visit. I could not confirm a single visitor car park or its fees, so ask on site.

### Can I take a rental scooter there?

Tell the shop first. Ecopark is outside the Hanoi boundary, and the shop confirms what is allowed for the bike you are given.
