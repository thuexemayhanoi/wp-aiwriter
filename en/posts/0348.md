Thai Nguyen city sits roughly 80 kilometres north of Hanoi on National Highway 3, and that single number hides the real question for a rider: which road do you actually use? A fast, modern expressway runs almost parallel to the old highway, but it is closed to motorbikes. The route you can legally ride is the older National Highway 3 (QL3) through Soc Son and Pho Yen, a busy working road with trucks, industrial traffic and frequent junctions. This guide compares the road choices, explains what to do when floods or roadworks close sections, and suggests how to build a day around tea hills and a lake rather than around speed.

## The short version: which road is yours

For a scooter or small motorbike, plan on the old QL3 corridor north from Hanoi through Soc Son, Pho Yen and Song Cong into Thai Nguyen city. Do not follow a car-style route that uses the Hanoi - Thai Nguyen expressway, which Vietnamese media also call the "new National Highway 3" or CT07.

As of October 2026, Vietnam's Road Traffic Order and Safety Law (Law 36/2024/QH15, effective 1 January 2025) states in Article 25 that motorcycles, mopeds and similar vehicles may not travel on expressways. The only exceptions are vehicles and people working on managing or maintaining the road. A navigation app that proposes the quickest car route may therefore send you somewhere you are not allowed to be. Our guide to [checking whether a navigation app suggests an expressway](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-check-if-a-navigation-app-suggests-an-expressway/) shows how to catch this before you set off.

## Two roads that look like one

When the expressway opened in 2014, it was presented as a faster replacement for the old highway. Reporting from the time describes a new route of about 64 kilometres, designed as a four-lane expressway with a 100 km/h limit, linking Hanoi, Bac Ninh and Thai Nguyen provinces. VnExpress noted that motorbikes were barred from it even during its early technical opening, and under today's rules expressways remain closed to them.

The old QL3 still carries local traffic, buses, lorries and motorbikes. It passes through town centres, past factories and across many side roads. That makes it slower and more demanding, but it is the legitimate option. A useful rule of thumb is this: if the signs turn green, show an expressway symbol, or the road suddenly has no side junctions and a central barrier, you are on the wrong road for a motorbike. Our guide to [Hanoi's ring roads and highways](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-ring-roads-highways-motorbikes-allowed/) explains the sign types in detail.

## Getting out of Hanoi first

The first stretch is not Thai Nguyen yet. It is the crossing of the Red River and the run north toward Noi Bai airport and Soc Son. Which bridge and which service road you use depends on your starting point, and arrangements differ between bridges. Because Nguyen Ha Motorbike Rental is in Long Bien on the east bank, some riders cross toward Dong Anh or Soc Son by a different bridge from riders starting in the Old Quarter.

Do not rely on memory for this part. Choose your bridge the night before, check that your map is set to avoid highways, and read the lane signs on approach. Leave early enough to be beyond the airport area before the commuter rush builds. Expect Soc Son town to be a bottleneck: the crossroads at roughly kilometre 24 of QL3 is a landmark, and Hanoi's Department of Construction used it as a named reference point in its 2025 flood diversion notice.

## Using kilometre markers to stay on the right road

QL3 is marked with kilometre posts counted from Hanoi, and local planning documents use them as addresses. A Thai Nguyen provincial council resolution naming new streets in Pho Yen, for instance, places junctions with QL3 at about Km42+250 and Km45+115. Those numbers are useful to a rider. If your phone says you are at Pho Yen but the posts do not match, you may have drifted onto a service road or the expressway's side roads.

You do not need to memorise anything. Watch for the roadside kilometre posts and match them against a rough mental map: Soc Son in the mid-20s, Pho Yen in the low-to-mid 40s, and Thai Nguyen city beyond that. If the count jumps unexpectedly, stop at a safe place and recheck the route rather than continuing on instinct.

## What happens when floods close the highway

In October 2025, after storm No. 11, sections of QL3 inside Hanoi were deeply flooded. Hanoi's Department of Construction published diversion plans. One option for vehicles heading to Thai Nguyen was to use the Hanoi - Thai Nguyen expressway to a Bac Phu interchange, which is not a route a motorbike may take. A second option used the Soc Son crossroads and a road toward Nui Doi and Bac Phu.

The lesson is practical rather than historical. Official diversion notices are often written with cars and trucks in mind. If a storm has recently passed, check the current notice, then ask a local, a hotel host or the rental shop which parts are passable on two wheels. If water is across the road, turn back. Our [rain-riding guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) covers flooded streets and gear in more detail.

## How long to allow

Travel sites commonly describe Hanoi to Thai Nguyen as one to one-and-a-half hours by car on the expressway. That figure is not a motorbike time. On the old road you share lanes with lorries, pass through several towns and slow for industrial entrances. A cautious plan is to treat the ride as well over two hours each way and to add time for fuel, a drink and a few minutes of rest.

A single day trip is therefore long: roughly 160 kilometres of return riding before you add any detours. If you are not already comfortable with several hours in the saddle, consider a shorter ride first or make the trip an overnight stay. Our guide on [planning breaks on a long ride](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-plan-breaks-on-a-long-semi-automatic-bike-ride/) gives a rhythm that works for most riders.

## Province name and map confusion

Thai Nguyen province merged with Bac Kan on 1 July 2025, and the combined province kept the name Thai Nguyen. That has two consequences. Older articles may describe Thai Nguyen as a small province with a particular set of districts, while newer ones include Ba Be Lake in the north. For this ride, the city and its nearby tea hills are still the destination, so the change mostly affects how you read old maps and signs.

Commune and ward names also changed in the 2025 reorganisation, so a very recent address may not match an older guidebook. Use a map pin for the destination, not just a name, and confirm any homestay or cafe location by message before you leave.

## What to see at the end of the road

Thai Nguyen is best known for tea. Tan Cuong, about 10 kilometres west of the city according to VOV World, is the famous tea-growing commune, and visitors can ride or cycle through the hills. The Tea Cultural Space near the city, described by Vietnam's culture and tourism ministry portal, is a museum-style building that explains tea history and culture. Check its opening hours on the day, because attractions can change their schedules.

Nui Coc Lake is a reservoir of about 2,500 hectares, according to Vietnam's tourism information sites, and has been developed for boat trips and leisure. A recent VTV report said local authorities plan to develop it further. If you want something more cultural, the city also has a museum dedicated to Vietnam's ethnic groups; travel guides list it as a common stop. Choose one or two places rather than all three. With the return leg in mind, one tea stop and one short lakeside break is plenty for a single day.

## A sample day plan

An example that works for a confident rider on a reliable scooter:

- **Early start:** leave Hanoi soon after sunrise and reach the Soc Son area before the heavy lorries bunch up.
- **First break:** a fuel stop and a drink somewhere north of Soc Son.
- **Midday:** arrive in Thai Nguyen, ride the short extra distance to the tea hills, and eat lunch.
- **Afternoon:** one short stop at the lake or a museum, then begin the return by mid-afternoon.
- **Evening:** be back on lit, familiar roads well before dark.

Treat these as planning blocks, not a promise. A wet road, a roadworks section or a late start changes everything, and the correct response is to shorten the plan, not to ride faster.

## When to choose another way

There is no shame in swapping the ride for a coach or train if the weather is poor, you are tired or the road conditions look uncertain. Intercity coaches also run between Hanoi and Thai Nguyen, and some riders prefer to use one and arrange a shorter local ride at the destination. Riders who want a less demanding first outing might start with [the east bank and Bat Trang](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/gia-lam-east-bank-bat-trang-by-scooter/), then build up.

## FAQ: Hanoi to Thai Nguyen road choices

### Can I ride a motorbike on the Hanoi - Thai Nguyen expressway?

No. As of October 2026, Law 36/2024/QH15 excludes motorcycles and mopeds from expressways, and the Hanoi - Thai Nguyen road (CT07) is an expressway. Use the old National Highway 3 instead.

### How far is Thai Nguyen from Hanoi by motorbike?

Tourism sources give about 80 kilometres on National Highway 3. The exact figure depends on where you start in Hanoi and which road you take, so use a map set to avoid highways.

### Is the old National Highway 3 dangerous?

It is a busy working road with lorries and many junctions, so it demands steady concentration. Keep your distance, avoid overtaking on blind sections and take breaks.

### Can I do Thai Nguyen as a day trip?

A confident rider can, but it is a long day with roughly 160 kilometres of return riding before detours. An overnight stay is more relaxed.

### What if storms have flooded the road?

Check current local notices, remember that car diversions may use the expressway, and turn back if the road is under water.
