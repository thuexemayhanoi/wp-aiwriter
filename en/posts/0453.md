The safest way to share the road with a bus on a scooter is to give it space, stay out of its blind spots and never rely on the driver having seen you. Keep well behind rather than close beside it, slow down at bus stops because passengers step into the road, stay out of lanes reserved for buses, overtake only where Article 14 of Law 36/2024/QH15 allows it, and give a turning bus the whole corner. A bus is long, heavy and slow to stop, and a scooter is small and easy to miss, so most of the margin has to come from you.

This guide explains why buses need special care, where their blind spots are, how to ride around bus stops, what the law says about reserved lanes, overtaking, following distance and doors, how to deal with buses at junctions and roundabouts and what changes in rain and at night. It is general information based on the law as of October 2026, not legal advice.

## Why buses need special care

A bus differs from a scooter in four ways that matter on a road.

- **Size.** It is long and wide, and it can hide other vehicles and the view ahead.

- **Mass.** It takes longer to stop than a scooter, and longer still in rain.

- **Turning.** A long vehicle needs more room to turn and may swing out before it turns.

- **Stopping pattern.** Buses stop often, at stops and sometimes at the roadside outside them, and passengers get on and off.

None of this means a bus driver is careless. It means that the geometry of the vehicle limits what the driver can see and what the vehicle can do. Our guide to [crossing Hanoi intersections](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/crossing-hanoi-intersections/) describes the mixed traffic at junctions, and this guide concentrates on the bus.

## Blind spots: where the driver cannot see you

Every large vehicle has areas that the driver cannot see in the mirrors.

- **Directly in front** of the bus, close to the bumper.

- **Directly behind,** close to the rear.

- **Along both sides,** especially the side nearest the kerb, close to the body.

- **Ahead on the left or right,** just beyond the cab, where a mirror cannot cover.

A practical rule is that if you cannot see the driver's mirror, the driver cannot see you. Stay out of the close zones beside and just ahead of a bus, and do not ride in the narrow space between a bus and the kerb. If you must pass, do it quickly and fully, in a place where overtaking is allowed, and do not linger alongside.

## Following a bus

Article 12 of the Law requires drivers and operators to comply with the regulations on speed and on the minimum safe distance from the vehicle in front in the same lane, and to keep their speed suited to the road, traffic density, weather and other factors. For a scooter behind a bus:

1. **Leave a larger gap than you would for a car.** A bus can brake hard for a pedestrian or a stop.

2. **Stay slightly to one side of the lane** where it is safe, so that you can see past the bus and the driver can see you in the mirror.

3. **Watch for brake lights and indicators,** and for passengers moving toward the doors.

4. **Do not tailgate to avoid being cut off.** Tailgating removes your reaction time.

5. **Be ready for a sudden stop** when the bus approaches a stop, a crossing or a junction.

Our guide to [speed limits for motorbikes in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/speed-limits-motorbikes-vietnam/) explains the limits, and the safe distance depends on the speed you are travelling.

## Bus stops: the highest-risk spot

Bus stops are where scooters and buses interact most closely.

- **Slow down on approach.** Article 12 lists roadside stops and parking places with passengers embarking onto and disembarking from buses as places where operators must observe the surroundings and slow down or stop.

- **Expect passengers to step out** from behind or in front of the bus. People board and alight at the edge of the carriageway, and some cross the road after leaving the bus.

- **Do not pass a bus that is stopped** at a stop if you cannot see the road beside it. A child or an adult may be crossing in front of the bus, hidden from you.

- **Expect the bus to pull out.** A bus signalling to leave a stop may move before you expect it. Give it room.

- **Do not stop or park at a stop.** Article 18 bans stopping and parking at pick-up and drop-off points, and on roads exclusively reserved for buses.

If a bus is stopped on a road with one lane in your direction, the safest thing is often to wait behind it, and not to overtake into oncoming traffic.

## Bus lanes and reserved lanes

Some roads have a lane that is reserved for buses. Article 13 of the Law says that for a lane exclusively reserved for a certain type of vehicle or group of types, operators of other types may not enter. Article 18 also bans stopping and parking on roads exclusively reserved for buses.

- **Look for the sign and the marking.** A reserved lane is shown by a sign over or beside the lane and by lane markings. Our guide to [how to read motorbike lane markings in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-read-motorbike-lane-markings-in-hanoi/) explains the lines, including that a solid line around a reserved lane means other vehicles may not enter except in emergencies.

- **Treat a signed bus lane as closed to your scooter,** even if it looks empty. Hanoi has run a bus rapid transit (BRT) line with a dedicated lane since 2017, and a lane signed for BRT or buses is not a shortcut for motorbikes.

- **Cross a reserved lane only where a junction or an opening allows it,** and follow the signals.

- **Do not assume a dashed line makes it a shared lane.** Read the sign.

Entering a reserved lane is an offence under Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, and the amounts and the exact item depend on the type of lane and the sign. Check the current text of the decree if you need a figure. Camera notices for this kind of offence can reach the vehicle's owner, so ask the shop how a notice would be handled before you rent. Our guide to [traffic fines in Vietnam that catch foreign riders out](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/traffic-fines-vietnam-foreign-riders/) covers the main offences.

## Overtaking a bus

Overtaking a bus is not forbidden, but it needs the right conditions. Article 14 sets out the rules.

- **Overtake on the left,** except where the vehicle in front is signalling a left turn or turning left, or a special-purpose vehicle is operating, in which case the right may be used.

- **Only when there is no obstacle ahead and no oncoming traffic** in the section where you intend to overtake, and the vehicle in front is not overtaking and has signalled right and moved to the right.

- **Signal your intention** with a flash of the front lights or the horn, and signal the direction of the change. In urban and populous areas from 22:00 to 05:00, use lights only, because Article 21 also restricts horns in populous areas between 22:00 and 05:00.

- **Keep a safe distance** from the vehicle in front and behind throughout.

- **Do not overtake** on narrow single-lane bridges, on bends with limited visibility, at crests of slopes with limited visibility, at intersections, on pedestrian crossings, in tunnels or when a priority vehicle is approaching.

The bus driver also has duties. Under Article 14, when the driver sees a vehicle behind that intends to overtake, they should check the road ahead, and if it is safe, slow down, signal right and move close to the right to let it pass. If it is not safe, they signal left to warn you not to pass. So read the bus's signals: a right signal and a move to the right may invite you to pass, and a left signal warns you not to. Do not rely on this alone. Check for yourself.

Overtaking a long bus takes more time and road than overtaking a car. If you are not sure there is room, stay behind it.

## Buses at junctions and roundabouts

Buses move differently at junctions.

- **Turning right,** a bus may swing out to the left first, leaving a gap on its right that looks like space for a scooter. Do not enter it. Our guide to [safe right turns at busy Hanoi intersections](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/safe-right-turns-at-busy-hanoi-intersections/) explains why.

- **Turning left,** a bus can take several lanes. Wait for it to finish. Our guide to [left turns across multi-lane roads on a motorbike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/left-turns-across-multi-lane-roads-on-a-motorbike/) covers the turn.

- **At roundabouts,** a bus may use the full width of the circle. Stay behind it, and do not ride beside it. Our guide to [how to ride through a Hanoi roundabout](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-ride-through-a-hanoi-roundabout/) explains the rule.

- **At traffic lights,** stop behind a bus rather than beside it in its blind spot, and move off after it.

- **At a stop line,** do not squeeze into the gap between a bus and the kerb.

## Doors, horns and signals

- **Bus doors.** Article 19 says vehicle doors may be opened only when the vehicle has completely stopped, and that the person opening must first observe the front, rear and outer side. Do not pass close to a stopped bus, because a door may open or a passenger may step out.

- **Horns.** Article 21 allows horns only to warn of circumstances likely to affect traffic safety and to signal an intention to overtake, and bans continuous horn use. A short tap is enough. Do not lean on the horn at a bus, because a startled driver or passenger is not safer.

- **Lights.** Article 20 requires front lights from 18:00 to 06:00 or in fog, smoke, dust, rain or bad weather that limits visibility. Make yourself visible to bus drivers.

## Rain, night and heavy traffic

A bus is harder to deal with when conditions are poor.

- **In rain,** a bus needs more distance to stop, and so do you. Increase your gap. Our guide to [riding in Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) explains how.

- **At night,** a bus driver may not see a scooter with weak lights. Use your low beam, and check that your brake light works. Our guide to [riding at night in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-at-night-in-hanoi/) covers lights.

- **In heavy traffic,** do not weave through gaps beside buses. A bus that moves a metre sideways can close a gap in an instant.

- **Spray and fumes** can reduce your view behind a bus. Drop back.

## If a bus does something unexpected

If a bus pulls out, brakes suddenly or drifts toward you:

1. **Brake smoothly** with both brakes, in a straight line.

2. **Move away from the bus** if there is room, and not into oncoming traffic.

3. **Use a short horn tap** to alert the driver, if there is time.

4. **Stay calm,** and do not follow with a confrontation. If there is a collision, deal with safety first and follow the steps in our guide to [what to do after a minor collision on a rental scooter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-to-do-after-a-minor-collision-on-a-rental-scooter/).

## A quick checklist

1. **If you cannot see the driver's mirror, the driver cannot see you.**

2. **Keep a bigger gap behind a bus.**

3. **Slow down at bus stops, and watch for people stepping out.**

4. **Stay out of lanes reserved for buses.**

5. **Overtake only where Article 14 allows, and only with room.**

6. **Give turning buses the whole corner.**

7. **Do not ride between a bus and the kerb.**

8. **Take extra care in rain and at night.**

## FAQ: scooters and buses in Hanoi

### Can a scooter use a bus lane?

Not where the lane is reserved for buses. Article 13 says that operators of other types of vehicle may not enter a lane exclusively reserved for a certain type of vehicle. Check the sign and the marking.

### Where is a bus driver's blind spot?

Directly in front, directly behind and along both sides, especially close to the body. If you cannot see the driver's mirror, the driver may not see you.

### Can I overtake a bus?

Yes, on the left, when there is no obstacle or oncoming traffic ahead and the overtaking rules are met, and not at intersections, crossings, bends with limited visibility or in tunnels (Article 14).

### Why slow down at bus stops?

Because passengers get on and off at the edge of the carriageway. Article 12 lists bus stops with passengers embarking and disembarking as places where you must observe and slow down or stop.

### Can I stop or park in a bus lane or at a bus stop?

No. Article 18 bans stopping and parking on roads exclusively reserved for buses and at pick-up and drop-off points.

### Should I sound my horn at a bus?

Only briefly, to warn of a hazard or to signal an overtake. Continuous horn use is banned (Article 21).
