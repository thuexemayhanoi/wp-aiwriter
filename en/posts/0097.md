Normal wear is the slow, expected using-up of parts that are designed to wear out, such as tyre tread, brake shoes, the chain and sprockets, bulbs, the battery and the air filter. Damage is something else: a fall, a knock, misuse or neglect that harms a part faster or differently than ordinary riding would. On a rented Honda Wave the difference matters because it decides what a shop may reasonably charge you for. This guide goes through the bike part by part, using Honda's own maintenance guidance for what each part does as it ages. It then covers the grey areas such as punctures, shows how to record the bike's condition at handover so that wear is not later mistaken for damage and says what to ask at return. Whether a charge applies is set by the rental contract, so this is general information and not a ruling on any case.

## The idea in one paragraph

Every part that moves, rubs, burns, carries current or touches the road is consumed a little by use. Honda's maintenance pages describe this repeatedly: tyre tread wears, brake shoes and clutch discs wear, spark plug electrodes wear away, oil ages, an air filter element clogs and a battery weakens. Those are the maintenance items a shop expects to service or replace as a bike accumulates kilometres. Damage is different in cause and often in look. A scuffed footrest from a fall, a bent lever, a cracked mirror or a dented panel did not arrive through use. Our guides to [the clauses in a Hanoi rental contract](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-motorbike-rental-contract-explained/) and [returning your rental motorbike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/returning-your-rental-motorbike-hanoi/) explain how the two are usually treated: ordinary wear is the owner's cost, and new damage is charged at the cost of parts and labour, subject to the contract you sign.

## Part by part: what wear looks like and what does not count as wear

| Part | Normal wear looks like | Not normal wear |
|---|---|---|
| Tyres | Tread worn gradually, shallower grooves | Cuts, cracks, bulges, flat spots from skidding |
| Brake shoes | Pedal and lever free play slowly increasing | Scored drum, shoes ruined by water, oil or a dragging brake |
| Chain and sprockets | Slight stretch, teeth wearing | Rusted solid, broken link, teeth stripped by running dry |
| Clutch | Friction material thinning over many kilometres | Sudden slipping after rough misuse, for a mechanic to judge |
| Engine oil | Ageing and losing protection with use | Oil level allowed to run very low |
| Spark plug | Electrode corners rounding | Fouled or broken by a fault left unreported |
| Air filter | Element clogging with dust | Soaked after riding through water |
| Battery | Starter turning more slowly over months | Drained by lights left on, or cracked by a fall |
| Paint and plastics | Fading, light surface marks | Fresh deep scratches, cracks, dents |
| Seat and rubbers | Dulling, slight softening | Cuts, tears, missing parts |
| Levers and mirrors | Slightly looser pivots | Bent, snapped, cracked glass |

The table is a general guide. A single symptom can have either cause, and the rental contract decides who bears the cost.

## Tyres

Honda's tyre page explains that a tyre carries the bike, absorbs bumps, steers it and passes driving and braking force to the road. It points out the tread wear indicator, a triangle mark where the grooves are very shallow, and says to look there to judge tread wear. It also says to stop using the bike if the tyres show cracks or scratches. Worn tyres slip more easily in rain and lengthen the stopping distance, and pressure that is too high or too low shortens tyre life.

For a renter the line is simple. Even tread wear all round, shallower than new, is wear. A cut, a crack, a bulge or a flat patch is not, and a bike with those should not be on the road. Our guide to [reading a Honda Wave tyre size and pressure label](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-read-a-honda-wave-tyre-size-and-pressure-label/) shows how to find the wear indicator and photograph the marking.

## Brake shoes and pedal feel

Honda says that brake shoes wear down as they are used and that free play at the lever or pedal increases as they do, until the brake no longer works well. So a rear pedal that has slowly grown longer is a normal sign of a brake approaching service, not a fault of the rider. A brake that drags, or a drum scored or contaminated after water or oil got in, points to something else. Our guide to [checking the rear brake on a Honda Wave](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/checking-the-rear-brake-on-a-honda-wave/) gives Honda's pedal free play figure and what to do if it feels wrong.

## Chain and sprockets

Honda's chain page says that even with the right slack, both the chain and the sprockets wear, and that the engine-side sprocket wears too. A chain that is too tight stretches sooner, and one that is too loose does not engage the sprocket properly. Honda's Wave RSX FI manual asks for the chain to be checked and lubricated regularly, with an interval of every 500 km and sooner on poor roads or with hard riding. A gradually stretching chain is wear. A chain that is rusted, dry and noisy after weeks of no lubrication is closer to neglect, and a chain thrown off by a rider who ignored a loud rattle is damage. Our guides to [recognising chain noise](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/recognising-chain-noise-on-a-honda-wave-or-yamaha-sirius/) and [noticing a worn chain and sprocket set](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-notice-a-worn-chain-and-sprocket-set/) explain the signs.

## Clutch, oil, spark plug, air filter and battery

These are the internal service items. Honda's pages describe each:

- **Clutch.** The friction discs wear gradually with use, and a worn clutch slips, making it hard to gain speed or even to move off. The Wave's clutch is automatic, so there is no lever, but the same wear principle applies. Our guide to [what a slipping gear feels like on a rented motorbike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-a-slipping-gear-feels-like-on-a-rented-motorbike/) describes the symptoms.
- **Engine oil.** Oil lubricates, cools, seals, cleans and protects against rust, and its protection weakens as it ages. Honda says that using old oil can shorten engine life, so it needs regular replacement.
- **Spark plug.** The electrode wears away and rounds off, so the spark weakens, acceleration suffers and in the end the engine may not start. Honda says plugs should be replaced periodically.
- **Air filter.** The element clogs with dust, which can cause unstable idling, weaker acceleration, worse fuel economy and trouble on hills.
- **Battery.** Honda suggests a simple check: switch the ignition on with the headlight, indicators and brake lamp lit, then try the starter. If it sounds weak or does not turn, the battery may be weakening or at the end of its life.

All of these are shop service items. A renter does not service them, but a renter notices their symptoms: slow starting, flat acceleration, a hesitant idle. Report those promptly, because a symptom reported early is wear, and the same symptom ridden through for weeks can become damage. Our guide to [what to check on a manual bike's electric starter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-to-check-on-a-manual-bikes-electric-starter/) covers the starting side.

## Paint, plastics, seat and fittings

Sun and rain fade paint, rubber dulls and a seat cover softens. Light surface marks around footrests and the heel guard are part of any bike's life. Fresh, deep scratches, scraped plastic on one side, a bent lever, a cracked mirror or indicator lens, a dented exhaust guard or a missing part usually come from a fall or a knock. Our guide to [what to do after dropping a rented semi-automatic bike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-to-do-after-dropping-a-rented-semi-automatic-bike/) explains what to do and say, and why honesty at once is better than a surprise at return.

## The grey areas

Some cases do not fit neatly.

- **A puncture.** A nail can strike any rider on any road. Ask how the contract treats punctures and repairs before you sign, and tell the shop at once if one happens. Our guide to [what to do about lost keys, flat tyres and breakdowns with a rental bike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-bike-breakdown-hanoi-what-to-do/) explains the usual steps.
- **A flat battery.** If the lights were left on, it follows from use. If the bike has simply refused to start after weeks, it may be wear. Ask the shop.
- **A broken chain after a long rental.** If nobody lubricated or checked it, who is responsible is a question for the contract.
- **A fault that was reported.** If you told the shop about a symptom and were told to carry on, keep a message or note of that.
- **A fault that was not reported.** If you rode with a known problem and said nothing, the shop may treat the result as your responsibility.

In each case the answer turns on the written contract and on what was recorded at handover. Our explanation of [what the clauses in a rental contract mean](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-motorbike-rental-contract-explained/) covers how to ask.

## How to record the bike's condition at handover

A good record turns later arguments into a comparison of photographs.

1. **Photograph all four sides and both ends,** including the seat, mirrors and exhaust.
2. **Photograph both tyres' sidewalls and tread,** with the size marking and the wear indicator in view.
3. **Photograph the odometer,** so the kilometres you add are visible.
4. **Photograph the chain and rear sprocket area** if you can reach it.
5. **Note the feel of both brakes,** and ask the shop to confirm the rear pedal's free play. Our [10-minute inspection checklist](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-scooter-inspection-checklist/) gives the full routine.
6. **Ask for existing marks to be written on the paper,** and keep your copy.
7. **Keep the photographs** until the deposit is back in your hand.

On a rental of a month or more, repeat a few photographs after a few weeks, so that a gradual change is visible.

## What to ask at return

- Compare the bike with the handover photographs together with a staff member.
- Ask which items are wear and which are damage, and why.
- Ask for any charge to be itemised and written on the contract, with the reason.
- Ask to see a replaced part or a receipt if a repair is charged.
- Keep your copy of the contract and your photographs.

## FAQ: normal wear on a rented Honda Wave

### Is a worn tyre my fault?

Not if the tread has simply worn down through ordinary use. A cut, crack or bulge is a different matter, and Honda says to stop using a bike with cracked or scratched tyres.

### Is a longer brake pedal normal?

Honda says free play grows as the shoes wear, so a slowly lengthening pedal is a sign of wear. Tell the shop, because a brake that works poorly needs attention.

### Is a stretched chain wear or damage?

Gradual stretch and sprocket wear are wear. A broken or thrown chain after a loud rattle was ignored is a different case.

### Who pays for a flat tyre?

That is set by the contract. Ask before you sign and report it to the shop at once if it happens.

### Do paint scratches count as wear?

Light surface marks and fading are ordinary. Fresh, deep scratches or cracked plastic usually come from a knock or fall.

### How do I avoid an argument at return?

Photograph the bike carefully at handover, report problems promptly and go through the walk-round with staff.
