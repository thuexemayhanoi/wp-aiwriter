To stop a parked scooter from rolling, switch off the engine, put the stand down on firm ground so that the bike's weight leans onto it, apply any parking-brake lock the bike has, lock the steering, remove the key, face the bike uphill if there is any slope and give it a gentle push before you walk away. An automatic scooter has no gear to hold it, so it relies on the brake, the stand and the ground more than a manual bike does. Article 18 of Law 36/2024/QH15 allows an operator to leave a parked vehicle only after using the parking brake or taking other safety measures, and it says vehicles parked on steep roads must steer toward the roadside with the wheels chocked.

This guide explains why scooters roll, which measures work and what their limits are, how to check them, how to handle mounting and dismounting on a slope, what electric scooters need, what to do if the bike starts to move and what to ask the shop. It is general information based on the law and published parking advice as of October 2026, not legal advice. Follow the owner's manual for the bike you rent.

## Why a parked scooter can roll or fall

A two-wheeler cannot stand on its own. Honda's published parking advice for motorcycles says that, unlike a car, a two-wheeled motorcycle is parked using a side stand or a centre stand, and that on sloping ground it may not be possible to park in the correct position, which can make the bike fall over. For a scooter, the risk has several parts.

- **No gear.** A manual bike can be left in gear to resist rolling. A scooter with an automatic transmission has no gear to select, so with the engine off the rear wheel can turn freely.

- **Slope and camber.** Gravity pulls the bike forwards, backwards or sideways, and changes the lean on the stand.

- **Soft or slippery ground.** The stand can sink or slide.

- **Knocks.** A passing bike, a person or the wind can push it.

- **A stand that is not fully down,** or a bike whose weight is not properly on it.

- **A bike that was moved by someone,** for example a child climbing on it.

## What the law says

Article 18 of Law 36/2024/QH15 contains several relevant rules.

- **Parking brake or other safety measures.** When parking on the road, the operator may leave the vehicle only after using the parking brake or taking other safety measures.

- **Steep roads.** Vehicles parked on steep roads must steer toward the roadside with wheels chocked. The provision is written for vehicles in general, so for a scooter treat it as a reason to avoid steep roads and, if you cannot, to use a purpose-made chock, a kerb and the steering lock together.

- **Stopping.** While stopping on the road, the operator may not turn off the engine or leave the driver's seat except in limited cases, and then must use the parking brake or take other safety measures.

- **Not obstructing.** The parked bike must not cause obstruction or danger to people or vehicles.

The practical meaning is simple: a rider who walks away from a bike that then rolls into a person or a car has not taken the safety measures the Law expects. ## The measures, and their limits

| Measure | What it does | Limits |
|---|---|---|
| **Stand fully down, weight on it** | Holds the bike upright | Fails if the ground is soft, wet, uneven or sloped too much |
| **Steering lock, key removed** | Makes the bike harder to push over and deters theft | Does not stop the rear wheel rolling |
| **Parking-brake lock on the brake lever,** if fitted | Holds the wheel with the brake | Not all scooters have one; the cable may be worn or loose |
| **Facing uphill** | Reduces the tendency to roll forward and collapse the stand, according to Honda | Cannot always be done lawfully; see below |
| **Centre stand, where the manual allows** | Lifts the rear wheel off the ground, so it cannot roll | Honda's training advice for a manual bike warns against the main stand on slopes or uneven ground |
| **Kerb or wall behind or in front of the wheel** | Gives a stop | The bike may press against the kerb and be hard to leave |
| **Purpose-made wheel chock** | Blocks the wheel | Needs to be carried; a stone or a bag may slip |
| **Parking in a guarded, level lot** | Avoids the problem | Not always available |

No single measure is enough. Use two or three together.

## Step by step: parking safely

1. **Choose a level, firm place.** Avoid slopes if you can. If you cannot, face the bike uphill, as Honda's advice recommends, and remember that Article 18 asks you to park on the right in your direction of traffic and within 0.25 metre of the roadside. If those two cannot both be met, find another place.

2. **Stop and switch off the engine.** Use the kill switch or the key, and wait until the engine has stopped.

3. **Put the stand down.** Put the side stand down with your foot, and lean the bike gently onto it so that its weight is on the stand and the foot is firm. On a bike with a centre stand, use it only on firm, level ground and as the manual describes.

4. **Apply the parking brake or brake lock,** if the bike has one. Hold the brake lever, operate the lock and release the lever slowly to see that the lock holds. Ask the shop to show you how the lock on your bike works, because designs differ.

5. **Turn the handlebars** to the side your manual recommends and lock the steering. Honda's manual for the Wave RSX FI advises turning fully left on a side stand, and our guide to [safely parking a semi-automatic motorbike on a slope](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/safely-parking-a-semi-automatic-motorbike-on-a-slope/) explains why. Remove the key.

6. **Check the surroundings.** Make sure the bike is clear of crossings, gates, drains and other bikes, and not blocking a path.

7. **Do a gentle push test.** Push the bike lightly from either side and from the front and back. It should stay put. If it rocks, slides or rolls, move it.

8. **Take your helmet and valuables.** Our guide to [how to transport a helmet on a scooter while parked](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-transport-a-helmet-on-a-scooter-while-parked/) explains how.

9. **Step back and look.** If the bike looks too upright or leans too far, adjust it.

10. **Photograph where you left it** if the place is unfamiliar.

## Mounting and dismounting, especially on a slope

Many rolling incidents happen as someone gets on or off.

- **Hold the front brake** while a person mounts or dismounts. A passenger guide advises the rider to pull in the front brake and keep it applied as the passenger climbs on, so the bike does not move.

- **Keep the stand's position clear.** Some guides advise raising the side stand before a passenger gets on, since a passenger's weight can compress the suspension and push the stand into the ground or shift the bike. On a slope or soft ground the opposite risk applies, so ask the shop for the right approach for your bike, and in all cases have the rider holding the bike upright with both feet down before anyone mounts.

- **Do not let children climb on a parked scooter.** It can tip.

- **Do not leave the engine running** while you step away. Article 18 is explicit that the operator may leave the seat while stopping only in limited cases, with the parking brake or other safety measures.

- **When you start, hold the brake,** start the engine, check your mirrors, release the brake and move off smoothly. On a slope, hold the brake until you are ready to go, to avoid rolling back.

## Electric scooters

An electric scooter is silent, so you cannot tell by sound whether it is switched on.

- **Ask the shop how to switch it off and lock it.** Know what the "ready" indicator looks like.

- **Do not touch the throttle** when you get on or off unless you mean to move. A bike that is on can drive away if the throttle is turned.

- **Apply the same stand, steering lock and brake-lock measures,** and check whether your model has a parking function.

- **Keep the key or fob with you.**

## If the bike starts to roll or fall

- **Do not try to stop a heavy bike with your body.** Step clear, especially if people are in the way.

- **If you are on it and it starts to roll,** squeeze the brakes, both of them, and put your feet down.

- **If it falls,** check for people, fuel and oil, switch off, and lift it carefully as the manual describes.

- **If it hits something,** do not leave the scene. Check for injuries, take photographs and call for help. Our guide to [what to do after a minor collision on a rental scooter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-to-do-after-a-minor-collision-on-a-rental-scooter/) explains the steps.

## A quick checklist

1. **Engine off, stand fully down, weight on the stand.**

2. **Brake lock on, if the bike has one.**

3. **Steering locked, key removed.**

4. **Firm, level ground; if a slope, face uphill.**

5. **Gentle push test before you walk away.**

6. **Hold the front brake when anyone mounts or dismounts.**

7. **Never leave the engine running or children on the bike.**

8. **Photograph the spot, and tell the shop if anything happens.**

## FAQ: preventing a parked scooter from rolling

### Does a scooter need to be left in gear?

An automatic scooter has no gear to select, so it relies on the brake, the stand and the ground. Ask the shop about the parking-brake lock.

### Can I leave the engine running while I step off?

No. Article 18 of Law 36/2024/QH15 limits when an operator may leave the seat while stopping, and requires the parking brake or other safety measures. Switch off before you leave.

### Is the centre stand safer than the side stand?

On firm, level ground it holds the rear wheel off the ground, but Honda's training advice for a manual bike warns against using it on slopes or uneven ground. Follow the manual.

### Which way should I face on a slope?

Honda advises uphill, because facing downhill can let the bike roll forward and collapse the side stand.

### What if the stand sinks into soft ground?

Move to firm ground. Do not leave the bike on it.

### Who is responsible if the bike rolls into a car?

Responsibility depends on the facts and the agreement. Photograph the scene, do not leave, and call the shop.
