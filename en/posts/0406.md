Delivery terms differ by rental length because every delivery is a separate handover, and the longer the rental, the better the cost and effort of that handover are spread across the days you pay for. At Nguyen Ha Motorbike Rental the published terms are that delivery is offered only for weekly and monthly agreements, within 10 km of Hoan Kiem Lake, at VND 50,000 per trip or VND 100,000 both ways, with anything beyond 10 km agreed in advance and no airport service. The shop has not published its reasons, so the explanations below are general rental logic, not statements from the shop. They help you see why a short rental is normally collected in person, and what to ask if you want to try for an exception.

## What the published terms actually say

Start with the facts, because they are narrower than people expect.

- Delivery applies to **weekly and monthly** agreements only.
- The area is **within 10 km of Hoan Kiem Lake**. Beyond that, delivery is agreed in advance.
- The fee is **VND 50,000 per trip**, or **VND 100,000 for both ways**.
- There is **no airport delivery**.
- Day rentals are collected from the shop in Long Bien, open 09:00 to 21:00 daily.

A day at Nguyen Ha is 24 hours, and weekly and monthly packages are priced on their own, not as a day rate multiplied by the number of days. That already tells you the shop treats short and long rentals as different products with different terms. The [prices page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/prices/) shows the ranges, and the final price is confirmed on the actual bike before the contract is signed.

## A handover is a small job with a fixed length

Whether a bike is handed over at a counter or at a hotel door, the same steps have to happen. Staff check identity documents and the licence and photograph them for the contract. The renter and a staff member walk round the bike and note existing marks. The contract is read and signed. A cash deposit is taken and recorded. Fuel and helmet are checked. Our guide to the [10-minute rental scooter inspection](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-scooter-inspection-checklist/) shows how much that covers.

At the counter, those steps happen where the staff already are, with the paperwork on the desk. At a hotel, someone has to ride or drive across the city, find the spot, and carry out the same steps on a pavement. Travelling time is added on top of the handover time, and the person doing it is not available for other customers meanwhile. That is the first reason a short rental is a poor fit for delivery: the fixed effort is the same, but there are few days to pay for it.

## The arithmetic of a delivery fee

Compare the published fee with what a renter pays for different lengths. The fee for one trip is VND 50,000, and for a round trip VND 100,000. Against a day rate in the range of VND 150,000 to 200,000, a single trip is a quarter to a third of the day's rent, and the round trip is half to two thirds of it. On a one-day booking, delivery would add a very large share to a small sum.

Against a weekly or monthly package, the same fee is a small slice. Weekly packages run from VND 600,000 to 1,000,000 depending on the bike, and monthly packages from VND 1,000,000 to 2,000,000, so VND 100,000 for both ways is a minor addition to the whole. This is the simple arithmetic that tends to make delivery sensible for longer rentals and awkward for short ones. It also shows why shops usually keep delivery fees low relative to long rentals but would struggle to deliver a short rental without raising the price a lot.

Please treat the figures as examples of the ranges on the price page, and check the current figures there, because bikes and packages differ.

## Fewer handovers, more continuity

A weekly or monthly rental means one handover and one return spread over many days. A series of one-day rentals means a handover and a return for each day. If delivery were free for short rentals, the shop's runner would spend much of the day travelling between hotels, and the same bike would spend little time with each renter. Offering delivery only for longer terms keeps the number of trips low compared with the rental days sold.

There is also a continuity point. Someone who commits to a week or a month is more likely to keep the bike for the full term, to arrange a fixed handover time and to be reachable, because they have planned the stay. A short rental booked the night before has more uncertainty about arrival time, which makes a scheduled delivery more likely to be wasted. This is a general observation about the trade, not a claim about any renter.

## Why 10 km, and why not the airport

A radius like 10 km is, in general, a way to bound the length of each trip. Hanoi traffic means distance on a map is not the same as time on the road: a short distance at rush hour can take far longer than a longer trip at midday. A shop that promises delivery to the whole city would be promising a variable amount of travel on a fixed fee. A radius centred on Hoan Kiem Lake covers the central districts where most visitors stay, and anything further out is agreed individually, so that the extra effort can be priced.

Airport delivery is excluded altogether, and the shop gives no reason. In general, airports bring uncertain arrival times, queues and strict rules about where vehicles may stop, and a renter who has just landed needs a place to sort out luggage and paperwork, not a bike handed over at an arrivals kerb. Vietnam's Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (Law 36/2024/QH15) restricts stopping and parking at several kinds of place, including pick-up and drop-off points, pedestrian crossings, bridges unless permitted and junctions and their immediate surroundings, and requires a stopped vehicle to keep close to the right-hand kerb. Not every handover location is therefore suitable, which is one more reason to have a fixed, tidy spot, and why delivery works best where the address is known and the stopping place is agreed in advance.

## What delivery never replaces

Delivery moves the place of the handover. It does not remove any step of the handover.

1. **The inspection.** You still check brakes, lights, horn, mirrors and tyres and photograph marks.
2. **The contract.** You still read and sign it. See our explanation of [what the clauses in a Hanoi rental contract mean](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-motorbike-rental-contract-explained/).
3. **The documents.** The licence and identity document are still checked, and the law expects a rider to carry a licence suited to the bike.
4. **The deposit.** It is still taken as cash at handover, between VND 2,000,000 and 5,000,000 depending on the bike and contract. Our guide to [how a rental deposit works in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbike-rental-deposit-hanoi/) explains how it comes back.
5. **The right to say no.** If the bike is not what was agreed, it is better to decline at the door than to ride away unhappy.

## If you are renting for a few days and want delivery

You may be renting for four or five days, or you may be unable to travel to Long Bien, for example with heavy luggage. The terms say weekly and monthly, so a shorter rental has no promised delivery. You can still ask. Frame the request so the shop can weigh it easily:

- State the exact dates, the hotel address and the time window you can accept.
- Ask whether a one-off arrangement is possible and at what fee, rather than expecting the standard fee to apply.
- Offer flexibility, such as a late-morning handover, which is easier to schedule than a fixed early slot.
- Accept that the answer may be no, and have a plan to collect the bike yourself.

Do not offer extra cash to a rider on the spot. Agree any fee with the shop in advance and confirm it in writing.

## When collecting the bike yourself is the better choice

Even where delivery is available, collecting from the shop has benefits. You see the shop, meet the staff, get directions for your first ride out of Long Bien and can raise problems in person. Our overview pages on the [Long Bien area](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/long-bien/) and the [Gia Lam area](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/gia-lam/) help you place the shop. Delivery suits a renter who has a fixed hotel address within the radius and a longer stay, and who values the convenience more than the hour spent travelling to the shop.

If you are weighing a long rental, our guide to [weekly and monthly rentals](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/weekly-monthly-motorbike-rental-hanoi/) lists the questions to ask about terms, repairs and returns.

## FAQ: delivery and rental length

### Why is delivery not offered for a one-day rental?

The shop has not published its reasons. In general, each delivery adds travel and handover time that is hard to recover from a single day of rent.

### Is delivery free for weekly and monthly rentals?

No. The published fee is VND 50,000 per trip or VND 100,000 both ways within 10 km of Hoan Kiem Lake.

### What if my hotel is more than 10 km from Hoan Kiem Lake?

Delivery beyond 10 km is agreed in advance with the shop.

### Can the bike be delivered to the airport?

No. Nguyen Ha does not offer an airport service.

### Does delivery change the deposit or contract?

No. The deposit, document checks, inspection and contract are the same wherever the handover takes place.
