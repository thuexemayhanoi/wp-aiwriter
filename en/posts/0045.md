The Honda Winner X and the Yamaha Exciter 155 are Vietnam's two best-known sport underbones: small motorcycles with a real clutch lever, a six-speed gearbox, a liquid-cooled engine of around 150 cc and the styling of a much bigger bike. Visitors who ride geared motorcycles at home often ask for one. This article explains what the two bikes are, how they differ from the semi-automatic Honda Wave that most rental fleets are built on, which riders they genuinely suit and the licence rule that changed on 1 January 2025 and rules many visitors out. Nguyen Ha's manual fleet is made up of Honda Wave, Yamaha Sirius and Honda Blade models, so this is a buying-guide for riders who may find a bike of this class elsewhere, not a description of Nguyen Ha's own bikes. The licence point applies wherever you rent.

## What makes these two different from a Wave

A Honda Wave or Yamaha Sirius is a semi-automatic. It has gears selected by a foot pedal but no clutch lever, because a centrifugal clutch engages and releases on its own. The Winner and the Exciter are fully manual motorcycles in an underbone frame. The left hand works a clutch lever, the left foot shifts through six gears and stalling, slipping the clutch and rev-matching are all part of riding them.

The engines are a class apart too. These figures come from the manufacturers' Vietnamese product pages in October 2026:

| | Honda Winner (current catalogue: Winner R) | Yamaha Exciter 155 VVA |
|---|---|---|
| Engine | 149.2 cc, liquid-cooled, single cylinder, DOHC four valves | 155.1 cc, liquid-cooled, single cylinder |
| Peak output | 11.5 kW at 9,000 rpm | 13.6 kW at 9,500 rpm |
| Peak torque | 13.5 N·m at 7,000 rpm | 14.4 N·m at 8,500 rpm |
| Gearbox | Six-speed with assist-and-slipper clutch | Six-speed, assist-and-slipper clutch listed as a feature |
| Brakes | Front ABS on the Sport and Special variants | Disc front and rear |
| Weight | 124 kg (kerb) | 120 kg (wet) |
| Seat height | 795 mm | 795 mm |
| Fuel tank | 4.5 litres | 5.4 litres |

Honda's catalogue now shows the model as the Winner R, a new-generation version of the bike that was sold as the Winner X from 2017. The engine class is the same, so the comparison holds, but a bike you find on the road or in a used fleet is likely to be an earlier Winner X, whose figures differ slightly by year and variant. Treat the table as the right ballpark and ask the shop for the exact model year.

Compared with a 110 cc Wave, that is roughly double the power in a bike that is only a little heavier, with discs at the front and a riding position that leans the rider forward. ## The licence rule since 1 January 2025

This is the point that decides the matter for most visitors. Under Article 57 of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (Law No. 36/2024/QH15), in force since 1 January 2025, class A1 covers two-wheeled motorbikes up to 125 cc or electric motors up to 11 kW. Class A covers motorbikes above 125 cc or above 11 kW, and the bikes in A1 as well. The Winner at 149 cc and the Exciter at 155 cc are above the A1 line, so they need class A or its foreign equivalent.

For a foreign rider that means two things. Your home licence must carry a motorcycle category that is not restricted to small engines, and you should hold an International Driving Permit under the 1968 Vienna Convention to accompany it. If your licence only covers smaller bikes, it will not cover a 150 cc machine in Vietnam. Our guide to [licence rules for foreigners in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbike-licence-rules-for-foreigners-in-vietnam/) goes through the categories.

The fines in Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, which we read on the Government portal, depend on engine size. As of October 2026, Article 18 sets a fine of VND 6,000,000–8,000,000 for riding a motorbike above 125 cc with no licence, or with a licence that does not match the bike. The same band applies to someone who holds a 1968-convention IDP but is not carrying the national licence that goes with it. For a bike of 125 cc or under the fine is VND 2,000,000–4,000,000. Decree 238/2026/ND-CP, which amends the decree from 15 August 2026, changes only the wording about young riders in that article. Travel insurers also routinely refuse claims where the rider was not licensed for the vehicle, which is a larger financial exposure than the fine. Our article on [travel insurance and motorbike rentals](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/travel-insurance-motorbike-rental-vietnam/) explains the usual wording.

If your licence covers only A1-sized bikes, the honest answer is that these two bikes are not for you in Vietnam, however well you ride at home.

## Who these bikes genuinely suit

The Winner and Exciter make sense for a specific rider: someone who already rides a geared motorcycle regularly, holds a full motorcycle licence and an IDP, and plans a route where the extra power and brakes are used. The obvious cases are the long mountain loops of the north, where a 110 cc Wave with two people and luggage is working hard on every climb, and multi-day trips on national roads where cruising at the legal limit with power in reserve is more relaxing than wringing out a small engine.

They also suit riders who simply prefer a clutch. Engine braking into a corner, choosing the gear rather than letting a centrifugal clutch decide, and the control a clutch gives on a steep, broken surface are real advantages for someone used to them. Our comparison of [Honda Wave and Yamaha Sirius manual rentals](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/honda-wave-vs-yamaha-sirius-rental/) is the better starting point for a rider who wants gears without the clutch.

For a visitor who is new to motorcycles, or who has only ridden automatic scooters, a 150 cc clutch bike is a poor place to learn, and Hanoi traffic is a poor place to learn it. Stalling at a green light with thirty riders behind you is unpleasant. Doing it in the middle of a junction is dangerous.

## How they behave in Hanoi traffic

In the city the two bikes are agile but not relaxing. The forward-leaning position puts weight on the wrists at walking pace, the clutch is in constant use in stop-start traffic and first gear on both is tall enough that very slow filtering needs clutch slip. The liquid-cooled engines cope with heat better than an air-cooled Wave, but the radiator fan running at every red light is a reminder that these engines are happiest moving.

Fuel economy is still good by any standard other than a Wave's. Yamaha lists the Exciter at 2.07 litres per 100 km and Honda lists the Winner R at 2.59 litres per 100 km, measured on a different test method from the previous generation, which Honda says was 1.98 litres per 100 km. Real-world city figures are higher than test figures for both, but a tank of 4.5 to 5.4 litres still covers a day of urban riding comfortably. Both have limited under-seat storage, so a bag or tail pack is needed for anything beyond documents and a rain jacket.

Brakes are the clear upgrade. A disc at the front on both, a disc at the rear on the Exciter and front ABS on the better-equipped Winner variants give a margin that a basic Wave's drum rear brake cannot. On wet Hanoi tarmac that margin is worth having. Our guide to [riding in Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) explains why.

## Winner or Exciter

The two bikes are closer than their fans admit. On paper the Exciter has a little more peak output and torque, and a variable valve system that gives it a stronger top end. The Winner has a DOHC engine that feels smooth in the mid-range and, on the ABS variants, an extra braking safeguard. Seat heights are the same at 795 mm, which is tall for a small rider. Styling is the usual tribal choice.

For a rental the choice is almost never between two models on paper. It is between the two specific bikes in front of you: their mileage, tyre condition, chain, brake pads and service history. A well-kept Winner beats a neglected Exciter and the other way round. Whichever you are offered, apply the same inspection as for any manual bike. Our [manual bike brake check before leaving the shop](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/manual-bike-brakes-what-to-test-before-leaving-the-rental-shop/) is written for exactly this moment, and the clutch, gearbox and chain deserve the same attention.

Ask the shop about the clutch lever's free play, whether the bike has been dropped and how recently the chain was adjusted. A clutch that slips under load or a gearbox that jumps out of second are deal-breakers.

## What a shop will want from you

A shop renting a 150 cc clutch bike to a foreigner will usually ask more than it does for a Vision. Expect to show the home licence with its motorcycle category and the IDP, to be asked about your riding experience and in some cases to ride a short loop while staff watch. A higher deposit than for a scooter is normal, reflecting the bike's value. Nguyen Ha's deposit for its own fleet runs from VND 2,000,000 to 5,000,000 depending on the bike and the contract, and other shops set their own figures for their own bikes.

The rest of the paperwork is the same as for any rental: the registration card and the insurance certificate travel with the bike and the contract records condition, fuel and the return time. ## When a smaller bike is the wiser choice

Three situations argue against a sport underbone even for a licensed, experienced rider. The first is a city-only stay: a scooter with flat-floor storage and no clutch is simply more useful for a week of cafes, markets and short hops across Hanoi. The second is riding with a passenger who is new to motorcycles. The sporty seat and the forward lean put the pillion in an awkward position and make the bike twitchier under braking than a Honda Lead or Vision. The third is cost and consequence: a dropped sport bike with a cracked fairing is a more expensive conversation at return than a scuffed Wave.

If your trip is a mountain loop with a confident pillion and a full motorcycle licence, the clutch bike earns its place. If it is Hanoi and a day trip to Ninh Binh, a Wave or a Vision will do the job with less to go wrong. ## FAQ: renting a Winner or Exciter in Vietnam

### Can I ride a Honda Winner X or Yamaha Exciter on an A1-equivalent licence?

No. Since 1 January 2025, class A1 covers motorbikes up to 125 cc. Both bikes are above that line and need class A or a foreign licence with a full motorcycle category plus a 1968-convention IDP.

### Does Nguyen Ha rent the Winner X or Exciter?

Nguyen Ha's manual fleet is the Honda Wave, Yamaha Sirius and Honda Blade. Clutch bikes of this class are offered by some other Hanoi shops. The licence rules apply wherever you rent.

### Are these bikes hard to ride in Hanoi traffic?

They are agile but demand constant clutch work and a forward-leaning position. Experienced manual riders manage, but beginners and scooter-only riders should not start here.

### Which is better, Winner X or Exciter?

On paper the Exciter has slightly more output and torque, and the Winner has the smoother engine and front ABS on some variants. In practice the condition of the specific rental bike matters far more than the badge.

### What is the fine for riding a 150 cc bike without the right licence?

As of October 2026, riding a motorbike above 125 cc without a licence valid for the class is fined VND 6,000,000–8,000,000 under Article 18 of Decree 168/2024/ND-CP.
