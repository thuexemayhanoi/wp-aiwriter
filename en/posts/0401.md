A motorbike handover checklist should record eight things on one sheet that both sides sign: who is renting, which exact bike, its starting condition, what goes with it, the fuel or battery level, the money paid and held, the return terms and the time and date. The sheet is the paper version of the moment you take the keys, and it is what both sides will turn to if there is a question at the end. This guide explains each block of a good checklist, why it is there and what a thin or missing version should make you ask. It is about the document itself, not the mechanical test of the bike or the photographs, which have their own guides.

## What the checklist is for

A checklist has three jobs. It identifies the bike so there is no doubt which machine was rented. It records the condition at the start so that damage can be separated from wear at the end. And it lists what was handed over so that nothing is later "missing" without a record.

The Civil Code 2015 explains why the first two matter. Article 476 says a lessor must deliver leased property in the agreed quantity, quality, type and condition, and must provide the information necessary for its use. Article 482 says the lessee must return it in the same condition in which it was received, normal wear and tear excepted. A handover sheet is the practical way of fixing "the condition in which it was received" on paper.

Our [10-minute rental scooter inspection checklist](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-scooter-inspection-checklist/) covers how to test the bike, and photographs of the marks are best taken with the form beside you. This page covers what the written checklist should contain.

## Block 1: the parties and the date

At the top, the sheet should name both sides and fix the moment of handover.

- Full name of the renter, nationality and a phone number you can be reached on in Vietnam.
- Name of the rental business and its address and phone number. Nguyen Ha Motorbike Rental is at Alley 5 Nguyen Van Cu Street, Bo De Ward, Long Bien District, Hanoi, and answers on 0334 699 969.
- The **date and exact time** of handover, because a rental day is counted in 24-hour blocks from that time at Nguyen Ha, and late-return charges run from the agreed return time.
- The **agreed return date and time**.

Many Vietnamese contracts call the lessor "Party A" and the lessee "Party B". That is a naming convention, not a rule. If your sheet uses it, check which of the two names is yours.

## Block 2: the identity of the bike

A good sheet identifies the vehicle so exactly that it could not be mistaken for another one.

| Field | What to write | Where to find it |
|---|---|---|
| Make and model | For example Honda Vision or Yamaha Sirius | Bodywork badge, registration paper |
| Colour | As it looks today | The bike itself |
| Plate number | Exactly as shown | Rear plate, and compare to the paper |
| Frame number | If the shop records it | A stamped number on the frame, shown in the owner's manual |
| Engine number | If the shop records it | Stamped on the engine, shown in the owner's manual |
| Odometer reading | The figure at handover | Dashboard |

Honda's owner's manual for the Vision explains that the frame number and engine number identify the bike and are needed for registration, and recommends recording them. The same manual describes the odometer as the total distance travelled, rolling back to zero after 99,999.9. Not every shop writes the frame and engine numbers on a short-term sheet, so do not insist if the plate and model are clear; but if the plate on the paper differs from the plate on the bike, stop and have it corrected before you ride off.

## Block 3: the papers that travel with the bike

Under Article 56 of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety, Law No. 36/2024/QH15, in force from 1 January 2025, a driver on the road must carry the vehicle registration certificate, a driving licence suitable for the vehicle and the compulsory civil liability insurance certificate, among other items, and the law also lists a technical inspection certificate for motor vehicles. The law sets out exceptions and detailed categories, so read it, or ask the shop, about the particular bike.

For a handover sheet, the sensible line items are:

- Registration certificate or copy: **given / not given**.
- Compulsory insurance certificate: **given / not given**, with the validity dates if shown.
- Whether the papers stay in the seat box or are carried by the rider.
- A note of what to do if the papers are lost, so that the clause is not a surprise later.

## Block 4: starting condition

This is the heart of the sheet and the part most often left vague.

A good form uses a simple outline of the bike, front, rear and both sides, on which marks are circled and numbered, with a short legend beside it: "1 scratch, left panel, about the length of a finger". If the form has no diagram, a written list can do the same job.

Record, at least:

- Each scratch, dent, crack or scuff, with its position.
- The mirrors, both of them, and whether they fold or adjust.
- The state of the seat, floor mat and footrests.
- The lights, horn and indicators, with "works" or "not working" for each.
- The tyres: front and rear, noting obvious wear or damage.
- The brakes: felt at the lever and pedal, and noted if soft or noisy.
- Anything odd noticed on the test ride.

Words such as "good condition" with nothing else are weak. If the bike is truly unmarked, write "no existing marks seen" and have the staff initial it. Photograph the marked diagram afterwards so that the written and photographic records match.

## Block 5: what goes with the bike

List every item handed over. Nguyen Ha's published terms say one basic helmet per rider is free, a raincoat is not included for short rentals, and no extra lock is provided, with the advice to lock the steering and park in guarded places. A sheet should therefore record:

- Number of **keys** handed over, including any spare or key fob.
- **Helmet**: how many, and any visible damage to the shell or strap. Our guide on [choosing a helmet that fits](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/choosing-a-helmet-that-fits-for-a-manual-motorbike-journey/) explains what a good fit means.
- **Lock, phone holder, rain cover, tool kit** and anything else, or "none".
- **Paperwork** as in Block 3.

If something is not given, write "none" rather than leaving a blank, because a blank can be filled in later.

## Block 6: fuel or battery

Petrol bikes at Nguyen Ha are handed over with about 0.5 to 2 litres and are expected back with about 0.5 to 1 litre; an empty tank at return costs VND 20,000. The sheet should record the fuel gauge position at handover, in a form both sides can compare later, such as "gauge shows about a quarter". For electric bikes, the shop confirms the battery level and charging at handover, so the sheet should show the percentage and any charger or cable handed over. ## Block 7: money and return terms

The sheet or the attached contract should say what was paid and what is held.

- **Rent** for the chosen package, with the amount in words and figures.
- **Deposit** held, as a separate line. At Nguyen Ha this is a cash deposit of VND 2–5 million depending on the bike and the contract, and the original identity documents are not kept; they are photographed for the contract.
- **Late return** terms: VND 30,000 per hour for one to five hours, and one extra rental day from six hours, at Nguyen Ha.
- **Early return**: no refund of unused rent, at Nguyen Ha.
- **When and how the deposit comes back**, and what it can be used for.

## Block 8: signatures

Both sides should sign and date, and each should keep a copy or a clear photograph of the signed sheet. Before signing, read the whole page once, check that the plate, odometer, fuel and money lines are right and ask for corrections to be written in, not promised. A statement such as "bike received in good condition" is only as reliable as the list of marks above it.

## A sample layout you can copy

If a shop has no printed form, you can propose this simple page and write it by hand together.

| Section | Entry |
|---|---|
| Renter, phone | Name, nationality, Vietnam number |
| Shop, phone | Business name, address, phone |
| Handover | Date and time |
| Return due | Date and time |
| Bike | Model, colour, plate |
| Odometer | Reading |
| Fuel or battery | Gauge or percentage |
| Marks | Numbered on a diagram |
| Lights, horn, mirrors, brakes | Works or not |
| Keys, helmet, other items | Count and condition |
| Papers | Given or not |
| Rent | Amount, paid or not |
| Deposit | Amount, paid or not, return terms |
| Signatures | Both sides |

## When the checklist looks thin

A very short handover note is not proof of bad faith, but it is a reason to supplement it. If the form has no marks section, add your own list and have the staff initial it. If the odometer or fuel is missing, write them in. If the deposit terms are not stated, ask. ## FAQ: motorbike handover checklists

### Is a handover checklist legally required?

The sources reviewed contain no specific rule requiring a handover sheet for motorbike rentals. The Civil Code leaves the form of a lease to the parties, so the checklist is good practice that supports the lessee's duty to return the bike in the condition received.

### Should the checklist be in English?

It helps if both sides can read it. If it is only in Vietnamese, ask the staff to go through it with you and write the key entries in English.

### Who keeps the signed copy?

Both. If the shop keeps the only copy, photograph it before you leave.

### What if I find a new mark after signing?

Tell the shop straight away, photograph it and ask for it to be added. Delay makes it harder to show that the mark was already there.

### Do I need a different checklist for an electric scooter?

The structure is the same, but replace the fuel entry with battery percentage and note the charger.
