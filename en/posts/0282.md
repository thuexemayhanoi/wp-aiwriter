A rental electric moped is quiet, so the three things that tell other road users where you are, mirrors for you and lights and horn for them, deserve a proper check before you leave the shop. The test takes about two minutes: sit on the bike with the stand up, adjust both mirrors, run through headlight, brake light, indicators and plate light, then tap the horn and listen. Vietnam's rules treat a missing left mirror, plate light, brake light or horn as an offence, and the manufacturer of one popular model adds that, because electric scooters are quiet, the horn should be used when needed. This guide gives the test in order, with what good looks like and what to do if something fails.

## Why a quiet vehicle needs a stricter check

A petrol scooter announces itself with engine noise. An electric one slides past pedestrians, cyclists and street vendors without warning. In Hanoi's dense streets that is a real difference, and it puts more weight on your lights, your signals and a well-judged tap of the horn.

The same applies to what you see. Mirrors that are loose or aimed wrongly are an easy fault to miss in a showroom and a hard one to cope with in traffic.

VinFast's owner's manual for the Evo 200, one model of electric moped-class scooter, makes the point in the controls section: because electric scooters run quietly, the rider should watch carefully and use the horn when needed. It also lists lights, indicators, horn, display, side stand and tail lights among the things to check before riding. The test below follows that idea, in an order that is easy to remember.

## Before you start

- **Park on level ground** with the bike on its stand.
- **Ask for a quick walk-through** of the key, the battery switch if the model has one, and any button that enables the throttle. On the Evo 200 manual, the vehicle has a button that activates or deactivates the throttle, and the battery switch sits under the seat.
- **Turn the key to the on position** and wait for the display to settle.
- **Ask someone to stand behind the bike** while you test, or use a shop window as a mirror.
- **Know which class you have.** The checks are similar, but the rules differ for an electric bicycle. Our guide to [electric bike classes in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/electric-bikes-in-hanoi-rules/) shows how to tell them apart.

## Step 1: mirrors

Sit on the bike as you would ride, with your helmet on.

1. **Check both mirrors are present and firm.** Hold each stem and try to wiggle it. A mirror that swings in the wind is a mirror that will not hold its aim.
2. **Look for cracks and damage.** A cracked glass can distort what you see.
3. **Adjust so you can see the lane behind you.** You should see a little of your own shoulder or elbow and the traffic behind, not the sky or the road surface.

4. **Check the left mirror first.** Under the national technical regulation for motorcycles and mopeds, QCVN 14:2024/BGTVT, a moped-class vehicle must have at least one rear-view mirror on the rider's left, and larger motorcycles need one on each side. On the road, the penalty for riding without a working left mirror is a fine of VND 400,000 to 600,000 under Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, as the Ministry of Public Security explains.
5. **Check the size and shape.** The mirror standard for new vehicles, QCVN 28:2024/BGTVT, calls for a reflecting surface of at least 69 square centimetres. You do not need to measure it, but a mirror that looks like a small decorative button probably will not do.

Our guide to [adjusting the mirrors on a Honda Wave](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-adjust-mirrors-on-a-honda-wave-rental/) covers the technique for a petrol bike, and the same eye-line logic applies here.

## Step 2: lights, front and rear

With the key on, check each light in turn.

| Light | What good looks like | If it fails |
|---|---|---|
| Headlight, low beam | Steady, bright, not flickering | Ask for another bike |
| Headlight, high beam | Switches on and shows on the display | Report it |
| Brake light, front lever | Comes on when you squeeze the front lever | Do not ride |
| Brake light, rear lever | Comes on with the rear lever | Do not ride |
| Rear light | Steady red when the lights are on | Report it |
| Plate light | Plate is lit at night | Report it |
| Indicators, left and right | Four blinkers flash, display icon flashes | Do not ride |
| Hazard lights, if fitted | All four flash together | Report it |

To see the brake lights, ask your helper to watch from behind or park so a window shows the rear of the bike. If you are alone, look for the reflection in a glass door.

### The rules behind the lights

Under Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, as set out on the Government's policy portal, riders of motorcycles and mopeds must use lights between 18:00 and 06:00, and in fog, smoke, dust, rain or other poor visibility. Riding without the required lighting is an offence. The same decree makes riding without a horn, plate light, brake light or left mirror an offence, and it also lists indicators that do not work.

The decree also restricts high beam when meeting pedestrians crossing, on well-lit residential roads, when meeting oncoming vehicles and when turning at junctions. So a high beam that works is not a licence to use it all the time. Our guide to [riding at night in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-at-night-in-hanoi/) covers the practical side.

## Step 3: horn

1. **Press the horn once, briefly.** It should sound at once, clearly and with a steady note.
2. **Press it again for a half-second.** A weak, croaking or intermittent sound is a fault.
3. **Listen for volume.** The national regulation for motorcycles and mopeds sets the horn range at 65 to 115 dB(A) measured at 2 m, with a continuous, unchanging note. You cannot measure that by ear, but a horn you cannot hear across the shop is too weak.
4. **Do not test it for long.** A short tap is enough, and the rules restrict horn use.

### When the horn may not be used

Decree 168/2024/ND-CP makes it an offence to sound the horn between 22:00 and 05:00 in populated areas and near medical facilities, except for priority vehicles on duty. It also covers continuous horn use and the use of an air horn. The sensible habit on a quiet electric scooter is a short, light tap when it matters, such as at a blind corner or when passing pedestrians, rather than leaning on the horn.

## Step 4: display, ready state and stand

The display is part of the test, because it tells you whether the vehicle is ready to ride.

- **Display lit and showing the battery level.** Ask the shop to confirm the figure and what range it means.
- **Ready indicator.** On many electric scooters nothing sounds when you turn the throttle, so the display's "ready" state matters. Ask how it looks on this model.
- **Side stand.** The Evo 200 manual lists the side stand among the electrical checks, which suggests a sensor. Fold the stand up and check the bike responds, then put it down and check it does not go.
- **Warning icons.** Any icon that stays on after the self-check is worth a question.

## If something fails

Do not ride away and hope. Take a photograph of the fault, tell the shop and ask for the bike to be repaired or swapped. ## Electric bicycles: a lighter test

Electric bicycles are treated as non-motorised vehicles, and the penalty rules are lighter. Decree 168/2024/ND-CP covers riding without a bell or horn, a front light or reflector, or a rear signal or reflector, at a fine of VND 100,000 to 200,000, and requires lights or signals front and rear between 18:00 and 06:00. The test is the same, with fewer parts: front light, rear light or reflector, bell or horn, and brakes.

## A one-minute summary to keep

1. Mirrors: firm, uncracked, aimed, left side present.
2. Lights: low beam, high beam, brake from both levers, rear, plate light.
3. Indicators: left, right, hazards if fitted.
4. Horn: one short tap, clear and steady.
5. Display: lit, battery level shown, ready state understood.
6. Stand: folds and the bike reacts.
7. Photograph anything odd and tell the shop.

## FAQ: electric moped mirrors, lights and horn

### Do I need a mirror on both sides of a rented electric moped?

The vehicle regulation requires at least one on the rider's left for mopeds, and both sides for larger motorcycles. Many scooters have two, and having both is better.

### Is the horn on an electric scooter different from a petrol one?

The regulation is the same. What changes is how much you rely on it, because the bike is quiet.

### Can I ride at night without the headlight on?

No. The road rules require lights between 18:00 and 06:00 and in poor visibility.

### When must I not use the horn?

Not between 22:00 and 05:00 in populated areas and near medical facilities, except for priority vehicles, and not continuously.

### What if the brake light fails after I leave the shop?

Stop in a safe place, tell the shop and ask for help. Do not ride in traffic without a working brake light.

### Does this apply to an electric bicycle?

The checks are similar but the vehicle is a bicycle in law, with lighter requirements.
