Near Hanoi, a toll road and an expressway are not the same thing, and mixing them up is the commonest navigation mistake on a rented motorbike. As of October 2026, motorcycles are listed among the vehicles exempt from the road tolls covered by Circular 32/2024/TT-BGTVT, so a toll booth is rarely your problem. The real problem is that motorbikes and mopeds are not allowed on expressways at all under Law 36/2024/QH15, and a car-mode route will happily send you onto one. This guide explains the difference, shows which fast roads out of the city are closed to you, and describes how to choose a detour that is slower but legal and sensible.

## Two words that get mixed up: toll road and expressway

A **toll road** is any road where a fee is collected at a plaza or gantry. A **national highway** can be a toll road, and the toll booth may sit on an ordinary road that carries bikes, trucks and buses side by side. A motorbike is generally allowed on that kind of road.

An **expressway** (cao toc) is a controlled-access road with ramps, no junctions at grade and a minimum speed. Vietnam's Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety states that motorcycles, mopeds and similar vehicles are not allowed to move on expressways, apart from people, vehicles and equipment serving expressway management and maintenance. Article 25 of the same law sets out how traffic must behave on expressways, which is a separate matter from who may enter.

So the two labels do not map onto each other. A road can be a toll road and open to motorbikes; a road can be an expressway and closed to them. A phone setting that says "avoid tolls" is not the same as "avoid expressways", and it is the second one that protects you.

## What the rules say, and what they do not

Two official documents matter here, and it helps to be exact about what each says.

- **Law 36/2024/QH15** (in force since 1 January 2025) bars motorcycles and mopeds from expressways. This is the rule behind the "no motorbikes" sign at a ramp.
- **Circular 32/2024/TT-BGTVT**, effective 1 January 2025, covers charges on roads built under commercial investment projects under central management. Thuvienphapluat's English summary lists twelve exempt vehicle types; the last one is "motorcycles, mopeds, and non-motorized vehicles".

The circular concerns roads under central management and excludes expressway service charges. It does not tell you what a province or a city may do on its own roads or bridges, and it does not make any expressway open to a bike. Treat it as a reason not to worry about most toll booths, not as a licence to ride through a gate. If you see a booth, follow the signs for two-wheelers or ask the staff, and do not follow a car into an automatic lane. ## Setting up a phone for toll-free, expressway-free riding

Open your map app before you start the engine and work through these steps while stationary:

1. **Choose the two-wheeler mode if the app offers it** in Vietnam. If it does not, use car mode only as a draft and inspect the result closely.
2. **Switch on both "avoid highways" and "avoid tolls".** They remove different things; neither is a guarantee.
3. **Zoom out and read the road numbers** along the line. QL1A, QL5 and similar numbered national highways are the ones motorbikes normally use. A road whose name starts with CT or contains "cao toc" is an expressway.
4. **Look for a long straight line with few exits.** That is the signature of a controlled-access road.
5. **Add a waypoint on the legal road** you want, such as a town on QL1A, so the app does not reroute you to the fast one.
6. **Download an offline map** and save the destination and the return address.

Our guide to [checking whether a navigation app suggests an expressway](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-check-if-a-navigation-app-suggests-an-expressway/) goes step by step through the check, and this page adds the toll angle.

## Reading the signs when the app and the road disagree

A navigation voice is not a traffic authority. At a ramp, look for the motorbike prohibition sign and for the word "cao toc" on the gantry. If either is present, do not enter, even if the voice insists. Ride on past, find a safe place to stop and recalculate. Never reverse on a ramp, and never cross lanes at the last second to catch a late instruction.

If you realise you are already on an access ramp, slow down, switch on your hazard lights if it is safe to do so and follow the signs to the nearest exit. Do not stop in a travel lane, do not reverse and do not attempt a U-turn. The aim is to leave the road in the direction of travel as calmly as you can, then recalculate on a permitted road.

## Detours: what a good one looks like

A detour is a different road that gets you to the same place. A good one is numbered, paved, lit near towns and used by other motorbikes. A bad one is a gap on the map that only looks shorter. Some rules of thumb:

- **Prefer the parallel national highway** to an unnamed shortcut, even if it adds time.
- **Avoid dyke tops and field roads after rain.** Surfaces become slick, and our guide to [riding in Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) explains why.
- **Do not add a ferry or pontoon you have not checked.** A crossing that is closed turns a short detour into a long one.
- **Expect trucks.** Heavy goods vehicles that avoid expressway tolls crowd the old roads. Hanoi's Decision 01/2026 restricts trucks inside Ring Road 3.5 at peak hours, which are 06:00 to 09:00 and 16:00 to 19:30, but the national highways beyond that boundary remain truck routes, as the Hai Phong report above shows for National Road 5.
- **Compare times honestly.** A car estimate on an expressway is not your time. Use a two-wheeler estimate and add margin.

## A pre-ride navigation checklist

- Destination and return address saved, with an offline map.
- Mode set to two-wheeler or, failing that, "avoid highways" and "avoid tolls".
- Road numbers read along the route; no expressway names.
- A named alternative route in mind if the first road is blocked.
- Fuel above half a tank and a charged phone with a mount.
- Documents with you. Our guide to [documents to carry when riding in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/documents-to-carry-riding-in-vietnam/) lists them.
- Time to be back before dark.

## FAQ: toll roads and detours near Hanoi

### Do motorbikes pay road tolls near Hanoi?

At toll plazas on roads covered by Circular 32/2024/TT-BGTVT, motorcycles are listed as exempt as of its effective date, 1 January 2025. Local rules and other roads may differ, so follow the signs at the booth.

### Can I use an expressway if I pay the toll?

No. Motorcycles and mopeds are not allowed on expressways under Law 36/2024/QH15, whether or not a toll is paid.

### Is "avoid tolls" enough to keep me off an expressway?

No. Tolls and expressways are different things. Use "avoid highways" as well, and read the road names on the route.

### Which road should I use from Hanoi to Ninh Binh?

National Road 1A, which runs alongside the expressway. Our [Ninh Binh guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-to-ninh-binh-by-motorbike/) describes it.

### Who do I ask if I am unsure about a road?

Ask the shop before you leave, and check official traffic information for current closures. The [area guides](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/guides/) offer wider planning ideas.
