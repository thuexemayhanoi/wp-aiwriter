To ride through a roundabout, slow down on the approach, read the signs and any signals, give way to traffic already circulating, enter when there is a real gap, keep your lane and your speed steady, signal before your exit and leave in the correct lane. In Vietnam, traffic circulates anticlockwise around a roundabout. The Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (Law 36/2024/QH15) sets the give-way rule in Article 22: at an intersection with "roundabout ahead" signs, a vehicle must give way to the vehicle approaching from the left, and at an intersection without such signs, a vehicle gives way to the vehicle approaching from the right. So the sign changes who has priority, which is why you should read it before you read the traffic.

This guide explains the give-way rule and the types of circular junction you may meet, describes the technique step by step, covers large vehicles, pedestrians, wet roads and missed exits, and lists the common mistakes. It is general information based on the law as of October 2026, not legal advice.

## The rule that matters most

A roundabout works because everyone follows the same give-way rule. Article 22 of the Law says that when approaching an intersection, the operator of a vehicle must observe the surroundings, slow down and give way under these provisions:

1. At an intersection between a non-priority road and a priority road, or between a feeder road and a main road, the vehicle on the non-priority or feeder road gives way to vehicles on the priority or main road from any direction.

2. At an intersection without "roundabout ahead" signs, a vehicle gives way to the vehicle approaching from the right.

3. At an intersection with "roundabout ahead" signs, a vehicle gives way to the vehicle approaching from the left.

On a roundabout where traffic circulates anticlockwise, the vehicles already in the circle come from your left as you enter. So, at a signed roundabout, you give way to the traffic in the circle. The risk comes at a circular junction that has no "roundabout ahead" sign. There the general rule applies, and you give way to the vehicle on your right. If you are not sure which rule applies, slow down, look at the traffic and let others go first. ## Know what kind of junction you are entering

Not every circular junction in Hanoi is a formal roundabout.

| Type | What to look for | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Signed roundabout | A "roundabout ahead" sign on the approach | Give way to traffic from the left, in the circle |
| Signalled circular junction | Traffic lights on the approaches | Obey the lights. Signals outrank signs under Article 11 |
| Junction with a ring marking | A ring-shaped line at the centre of a crossing | Go around the marked area anticlockwise, and do not cut across it |
| Junction with a small island | A raised island in the middle | Treat it as a normal junction, follow the signs and give way to the right unless a sign says otherwise |
| Large circular junction with no clear signs | Several roads meeting around a central space | Slow down, give way to anyone who may have priority and enter only when clear |

The standard on road signs, QCVN 41:2024/BGTVT, describes a ring-shaped channelising marking placed at the centre of a crossing to direct vehicles to go around it anticlockwise. Article 11 of the Law ranks traffic guards first, traffic lights second, road signs third and markings fourth, so if a guard or a light is in control, follow it. ## Step 1: approach slowly and read the junction

Article 12 of the Law requires operators to observe the surroundings and slow down at road intersections. For a roundabout, that means:

- **Slow down well before the entry.** Aim to reach the give-way line at a speed from which you can stop.

- **Look for the sign,** the lights and the markings.

- **Look at the exits,** and work out which one you need before you arrive. Use the signs, or your navigation app before you set off, but do not look at your phone while riding. Article 9 bans holding and using a phone while operating a vehicle.

- **Choose your lane early.** Our guide to [lane rules for motorbikes in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/lane-rules-motorbikes-vietnam/) explains lanes, and Article 13 requires that you change lanes only where it is allowed, one at a time, after signalling and checking all sides.

- **Keep right.** Move to the lane that suits your exit: generally the right-hand lane for the first exit, and a lane further left for later exits, if the approach has several lanes and the markings show it.

## Step 2: give way and find the gap

At a signed roundabout, you give way to traffic in the circle, coming from your left.

1. **Stop or slow at the give-way line.** Do not roll through.

2. **Look left,** at the vehicles in the circle, and then at the vehicles about to enter.

3. **Judge the gap.** A gap is a space in which you can enter and reach the same speed as the traffic without making anyone brake.

4. **Watch for scooters** between larger vehicles, and for vehicles leaving at the exit just before yours.

5. **Wait if there is no gap.** Waiting is part of the junction.

6. **Look right and ahead too,** at pedestrians crossing at the entry and at the vehicle in front of you.

Do not enter on the strength of a gap that closes as you arrive. If you hesitate halfway, you will be stopped in the path of a vehicle you did not see.

## Step 3: enter smoothly and hold your lane

Once you commit:

- **Accelerate gently** to match the speed of the circle.

- **Go anticlockwise,** as the signs and the markings direct.

- **Stay in your lane,** and do not drift between lanes. Article 13 requires you to keep within one lane.

- **Do not stop in the circle.** A stopped vehicle in a roundabout is a danger to everyone behind it.

- **Do not overtake in the circle.** Article 14 bans overtaking at road intersections, and a roundabout is a place where vehicles enter and leave from all sides.

- **Keep a gap from the vehicle in front,** because it may brake for an entering vehicle.

## Step 4: signal and leave

Article 15 requires a signal when changing direction, and leaving a roundabout is a change of direction.

1. **Decide your exit before you reach it.** Do not choose at the last moment.

2. **Signal right,** shortly before the exit before yours, so that vehicles waiting to enter know you are leaving.

3. **Check your right mirror,** and the vehicles beside you, before moving to the exit lane.

4. **Move across to the exit lane** gradually, one lane at a time.

5. **Watch for pedestrians** at the exit. Article 12 requires you to observe and slow down or stop where there are markings or pedestrians crossing.

6. **Cancel the signal** once you are on the exit road.

If you do not need the first exit, do not signal right at it. Signalling right and then not leaving misleads the traffic waiting to enter.

## Large vehicles

Buses and trucks cannot follow the same line as a scooter.

- **A long vehicle may use the full width of the circle** to get round, and may swing out to the left before entering or turning.

- **Do not ride beside a bus or truck in the circle.** If you cannot see the driver's mirror, the driver may not see you.

- **Stay behind a large vehicle** and give it the space it needs.

- **Do not enter right beside one** that is also entering. Wait for the next gap.

## Rain, night and poor visibility

- **In rain,** slow down more, avoid leaning hard on painted lines and metal covers and leave a bigger gap. Our guide to [riding in Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) explains how.

- **At night,** use your low beam. Article 20 requires you to turn off high beams and turn on low beams when changing direction at intersections. Our guide to [riding at night in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-at-night-in-hanoi/) covers lights.

- **If the view is blocked** by a vehicle at the entry, creep forward slowly until you can see, and do not enter blind.

## Priority vehicles and unusual situations

If you hear or see a priority vehicle such as an ambulance or fire engine, Article 27 says people and vehicles must slow down, move close to the right roadside or stop to give way. In a roundabout, do this where it is safe, usually by moving to the right and slowing, and not by stopping in the middle of the circle. If a traffic guard directs traffic, follow the guard, who outranks the signs and the lights.

## If you miss your exit

Go round again. A second lap costs a minute. Cutting across lanes to reach the exit puts you in the path of vehicles that did not expect you. If you realise that you are in the wrong lane, stay in your lane and take the next exit, then turn back at the next safe place.

## Common mistakes

1. **Entering without giving way** to the traffic in the circle.

2. **Entering too fast,** so that you cannot stop.

3. **Stopping in the circle.**

4. **Signalling late, or not at all.**

5. **Cutting across lanes** to reach an exit.

6. **Riding beside a bus or truck.**

7. **Going the wrong way,** clockwise, or across the central marking.

8. **Treating every circular junction as a signed roundabout.**

## A quick checklist

1. **Slow down on the approach, and read the sign, the lights and the markings.**

2. **Choose your lane and your exit early.**

3. **At a signed roundabout, give way to traffic in the circle, from your left.**

4. **Enter when the gap is real.**

5. **Hold your lane and a steady speed, and do not stop or overtake.**

6. **Signal right before your exit, and check your mirror.**

7. **Give way to pedestrians at the exit.**

8. **If you miss the exit, go round again.**

## FAQ: roundabouts for scooter riders in Hanoi

### Who has priority at a roundabout in Vietnam?

At an intersection with "roundabout ahead" signs, a vehicle gives way to the vehicle approaching from the left, so the traffic in the circle has priority (Article 22). Without such signs, a vehicle gives way to the vehicle approaching from the right.

### Which way does traffic go round?

Anticlockwise. The standard describes a ring marking that directs vehicles around it anticlockwise.

### Do I signal when leaving?

Yes. Leaving is a change of direction, and Article 15 requires a signal.

### What if the roundabout has traffic lights?

Obey the lights. Under Article 11, traffic lights rank above road signs.

### Can I stop inside the circle?

No. Do not stop or overtake in the circle, and go round again if you miss your exit.

### What is the safest way to cope with buses and trucks?

Stay behind them, give them room and do not ride alongside in the circle.
