The insurance attached to a rental motorbike and your own travel insurance do different jobs, and neither is a damage waiver for the bike. The compulsory civil-liability insurance on the motorbike compensates third parties, such as a pedestrian you hurt or another rider whose vehicle you damage, within limits. Your travel insurance is a private contract that may pay for your own medical treatment and evacuation, but only if its conditions are met, and many policies exclude or restrict motorbike riding. Damage to or loss of the rental bike itself is usually governed by your rental contract and the deposit, not by either kind of insurance.

This guide sets the three layers side by side, explains what each covers and excludes, gives a scenario table that shows who pays in common situations, lists questions to ask the shop and the insurer and explains what to carry. It is general information, not insurance or legal advice, and the policy wording and your contract are what count.

## The three layers in one view

Think of three separate boxes.

| Layer | Whose it is | What it is for | What it is not for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Compulsory civil-liability insurance | The owner of the bike (the shop) | Compensating third parties for injury or damage caused by the vehicle | Your own injuries, the rental bike, theft |
| Your travel insurance | You | Your medical costs, evacuation, sometimes your liability, if conditions are met | Usually not the rental bike; often restricted for motorbikes |
| The rental contract and deposit | You and the shop | Who pays for damage, loss and unpaid charges to the bike | Third-party injuries and your medical bills |

Keeping them apart in your head avoids most of the usual surprises. Our guide to [travel insurance and motorbike rentals](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/travel-insurance-motorbike-rental-vietnam/) gives a fuller account of the travel-insurance side.

## Layer 1: the compulsory insurance on the motorbike

Vietnamese law requires the owner of a motor vehicle, including a scooter or moped, to carry compulsory civil-liability insurance. The current rules come from Decree 67/2023/ND-CP, effective 6 September 2023. As of October 2026, it has been amended by Decree 220/2026/ND-CP, in force from 1 July 2026. The published summaries we found describe that amendment in general terms, so check the consolidated text if you need exact details.

Under Decree 67, the insurer pays for death or injury caused to third parties by the vehicle and for damage to third parties' property, with a fixed maximum per person for injury and a separate, lower maximum per accident for property damaged by a two-wheeler. Legal summaries also note that the cover extends to the health and life of passengers carried on the insured vehicle. The limits are modest compared with the cost of serious treatment.

What it does not do matters as much.

- **It does not pay for your injuries** as the rider.

- **It does not repair or replace the rental bike.**

- **It does not cover theft** of the bike or your belongings.

- **It does not replace evacuation or flights home.**

On a rental, the shop buys the certificate in the bike's name, and you must be able to show it. As of October 2026, Article 18 of Decree 168/2024/ND-CP provides a fine of VND 200,000–300,000 for a motorbike rider without a valid compulsory insurance certificate. Check at pickup that a current certificate is there. Our guide to [what documents to carry when riding in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/documents-to-carry-riding-in-vietnam/) explains the papers.

## Layer 2: your travel insurance

Travel insurance is a private contract between you and an insurer. What it pays depends on the policy wording, and wording about motorbikes varies. Common conditions include:

- **A valid licence for the bike.** Many policies pay only if you held a licence that is valid in the country for that class of motorbike. Our guide to [licence rules for foreigners in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbike-licence-rules-for-foreigners-in-vietnam/) explains what Vietnam requires.

- **An engine-size limit.** Some policies cover only small engines, others exclude all motorbikes.

- **A helmet.** Many policies require it, and a head injury without one is a strong reason to reduce or refuse a claim.

- **No alcohol.** Riding after drinking is a common exclusion.

- **Activity restrictions.** Racing, off-road riding or riding with extra passengers may be excluded.

- **Notification.** Many policies require you to call the insurer's assistance line at once.

Travel policies often exclude damage to hired property, so they rarely pay for the rental bike. Some include a "rental vehicle excess" cover for cars, but not for two-wheelers. Read the policy before you travel, and ask the insurer in writing whether it covers a rented motorbike of that engine size, and what it excludes.

## Layer 3: the rental contract and the deposit

Vietnam's Civil Code (Law No. 91/2015/QH13), in force since 1 January 2017, treats a motorbike rental as a lease of property. As of October 2026, the lease articles, which we read in the text published by the Tuyen Quang Department of Justice, say the lessee must preserve and maintain the property and compensate if it is lost or damaged, apart from natural wear, and must return it in the condition in which it was received. The contract sets the details.

At Nguyen Ha the cash deposit of VND 2,000,000–5,000,000 depending on the bike and contract is separate from the rent and is held against damage, loss and unpaid charges. The published terms do not describe any damage waiver or shop insurance for the rental bike, so ask whether any exists. Our guide to [understanding a rental contract's theft clause](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/understanding-a-rental-contracts-theft-clause/) and our guide to [what to ask about damage liability before renting](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-to-ask-about-damage-liability-before-renting/) explain how to read the wording.

## Shop-side cover and voluntary vehicle insurance

Some owners buy voluntary insurance on the bike, or offer a damage waiver, for an extra fee. We found nothing in Nguyen Ha's published terms about either, so do not assume it exists. If a shop offers one, ask:

- What does it cover: damage, theft, both?

- What is the excess, and is there a cap?

- What does it exclude: for example, riding without a valid licence, tyres, mirrors, water damage?

- Does it cost extra, and how much?

- What must you do after an incident to use it?

Have the answers in writing.

## Who pays what: a scenario table

The table shows who may pay in common situations. "May" matters: each outcome depends on the policy, the contract and the facts.

| Situation | Compulsory insurance | Your travel insurance | Rental contract and deposit |
|---|---|---|---|
| You hurt a pedestrian or another rider | May compensate them, within limits | May cover your legal liability, if the policy includes it | Not the main route |
| You damage another person's vehicle or property | May compensate, within a lower property limit | May cover liability, if included | Not the main route |
| You are injured | Does not pay | May pay medical costs, if conditions are met | Not applicable |
| Your passenger is injured | May cover passengers' health and life, within limits | The passenger's own policy may apply | Not the main route |
| You fall and damage the rental bike | Does not pay | Rarely pays | Usually the main route |
| The rental bike is stolen | Does not pay | Rarely pays | Usually the main route |
| Your belongings are stolen | Does not pay | May pay, subject to limits and conditions | Not applicable |
| Medical evacuation or flights home | Does not pay | May pay, if covered | Not applicable |
| Ordinary wear or a mechanical fault | Does not pay | Does not pay | Normally the shop's cost |
| A traffic fine | Does not pay | Does not pay | Depends on the contract |

## Questions to ask the shop

- Is there a valid compulsory insurance certificate for this bike, and may I see it?

- Does the shop have any insurance or damage waiver on the bike, and what does it cover?

- If the bike is damaged or stolen, what am I liable for, and is there a cap?

- What happens to the deposit?

- What do you need from me after an accident?

## Questions to ask your insurer before you travel

- Does my policy cover riding a rented motorbike of this engine size in Vietnam?

- Does it require a licence valid for that class, and what counts as valid?

- Does it require a helmet, and what other conditions apply?

- Does it cover my liability to others, and does it cover hired property?

- Is there a limit for medical costs and evacuation?

- Whom do I call after an accident, and within what time?

Ask for the answers in writing, and keep them. If the answer is no, consider a different type of cover, or choose a different way to get around.

## What to carry

- **A copy of your policy and the insurer's 24-hour number.**

- **Your licence and IDP,** matching the bike's class.

- **The bike's insurance certificate and registration copy,** which the shop should supply.

- **The rental contract,** with the shop's number, 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969), open 09:00 to 21:00 daily.

- **Your embassy's emergency number.**

## FAQ: rental motorbike insurance versus travel insurance

### Does the shop's insurance cover my injuries?

No. The compulsory insurance covers third parties, not the rider. Your own medical costs come from your travel insurance, if it applies.

### Does travel insurance cover damage to the rental bike?

Usually not. Damage to the bike is governed by the rental contract and the deposit.

### Is compulsory insurance the same as a damage waiver?

No. It compensates third parties and does not pay for repairs to the rental bike.

### Do I need a licence for my travel insurance to pay?

Many policies require a licence valid for the bike in the country. Read the wording and ask the insurer.

### What is the fine for no insurance certificate?

As of October 2026, VND 200,000–300,000 for a motorbike rider without a valid compulsory insurance certificate under Article 18 of Decree 168/2024/ND-CP.

### Who should I ask about all this?

The shop for the compulsory cover and the contract, and your insurer for your policy.
