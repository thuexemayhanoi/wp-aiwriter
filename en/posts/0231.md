For a short-term renter, plug-in charging is usually the system you will actually use, and battery swapping is a convenience you can use only if the bike, the account and the rental arrangement all allow it. Swapping is faster, because you exchange a depleted pack for a charged one in a minute or two instead of waiting hours. But the network is built around subscribers, and a visitor on a rented bike is rarely one. This article compares the two systems on the points that matter to a renter: time, access, equipment, where the charging risk sits, cost to plan for and what to ask the rental shop. It explains the choice, not the shop's own stock, which is confirmed when you book.

## What each system actually is

**Plug-in charging** means you connect a charger to a wall socket. Either you charge the whole bike through a port, or you lift out a removable battery and charge it indoors. The charger is normally supplied with the bike. A full charge takes hours.

**Battery swapping** means the bike has one or more removable battery packs, and you exchange a low pack for a charged one at a cabinet or station. Honda's description of its own swapping system for its Mobile Power Pack e: is typical of the idea: subscribed users find a station with an app, authenticate with a smart card, return the depleted battery and take a charged one. Honda says this frees riders from long charging times and from the stress of running flat. That is Honda's system in other markets, not a Hanoi rental, but the logic is the same everywhere: the charging happens at the station, not in your hotel.

In Vietnam the swap network most visitors will see is VinFast's. In January 2026, Tuoi Tre reported that V-Green, VinFast's energy subsidiary, had installed its first 4,500 swap stations and aimed for 45,000 nationwide, in residential areas, shopping centres and along main roads. Those are targets from the operator, not a promise that one is next to your hotel.

## The comparison at a glance

| Point | Plug-in charging | Battery swapping |
|---|---|---|
| Time to a full battery | Hours | A minute or two at a working station |
| Needs | A charger, a socket and a safe place | A swappable bike, an account, a station nearby |
| Who it suits | Most rentals | Subscribers and riders who use the bike daily |
| Risk sits | Where you charge, such as a hotel | At the station, which the operator manages |
| Weak point | Waiting, and finding a safe place | Access: you may not be allowed to use the cabinet |
| What the renter controls | Almost everything | Very little |
| Best question to ask the shop | "Where and when do I charge?" | "Who does the swap?" |

The pattern is clear. Plug-in is slow but within your control. Swapping is quick but depends on arrangements you may not control.

## Time: why it matters less than people think

A swap wins on speed. A minute at a cabinet beats an evening at a socket. But look at how the time actually falls in a renter's day.

For plug-in charging, a 2023 study by the International Council on Clean Transportation of VinFast scooters reported full-charge times of about 4.8 to 12 hours on a household supply, with a public charging station reaching a full charge in about four hours. Those are figures for VinFast's own models at the time, not for the rental bike you will be given, but they give the scale. If you ride a modest distance and return in the afternoon, a few attended hours may be enough, and the charge sits inside your day without costing you any time.

For swapping, the speed only matters if you would otherwise be stuck. If your route is short, a plug-in bike that starts the day full needs no swap at all. If your route is long, you may want one, but that is the case where a renter is least likely to be able to use it. ## Access: the real difference for a renter

Swap networks are designed around accounts. Tuoi Tre's reporting on VinFast describes a subscription model, with batteries rented monthly and a fee per swap. Honda's system likewise starts with a subscription and a registered user. That design makes sense for an owner who uses the bike every day. It makes much less sense for a visitor.

The practical consequence is that, in many cases, a swap cabinet will not work for a renter who is not the account holder. Our guide to [charging an electric scooter in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/charging-an-electric-scooter-in-hanoi/) sets out the conditions that V-Green publishes, including that the person swapping needs a valid battery-rental arrangement. The rule of thumb is simple: do not scan a cabinet's code and expect it to work. Ask the shop how swaps are handled for the bike you are given. Possible answers include:

- "Staff do the swap for you." This depends on the shop's arrangements. Do not assume it.
- "You return to the shop to exchange the battery." Practical only if you stay close to the shop.
- "This bike does not swap, it charges by plug." Common, and the simplest to plan.
- "You can swap using the shop's account." Ask what that costs you and how it is recorded.

Do not accept "it should be fine" as an answer.

## Equipment: what you carry and what you rely on

With plug-in charging you rely on the charger and a socket. Check the cable and plug before you leave the shop, and ask which charger belongs to the bike. London Fire Brigade advises using the charger supplied with the battery or one recommended by the manufacturer, and replacing a damaged one only with a reputable replacement. A swap setup reverses this: you carry no charger, but you rely on a cabinet, an app and a working network.

One detail about swappable packs is easy to miss: they are heavy and meant to be handled. Honda says one of its Mobile Power Pack units weighs about 10 kg, and its design includes a handle for carrying. That is Honda's pack, not the one on any rental bike, but it shows why swapping is built for a cabinet and not for a hotel room. If a rental bike has a removable battery, ask whether staff expect you to lift it out, because that is a heavy job to do in a hotel corridor.

## Where the fire risk sits

Fire services say the most dangerous moment for a lithium battery is while it charges. London Fire Brigade states that most fires involving e-bikes and e-scooters happen during charging. The Gia Lai provincial fire-prevention department's recommendations, published in March 2026, say to charge in a dry, airy place, to wait about 20 minutes after use before charging, not to charge in a closed room, not to charge continuously beyond eight hours and not to charge overnight or when no one is at home.

With plug-in charging, that risk is in your hands. You choose the place and the hours, which is a responsibility as much as a freedom. If the place is a hotel, ask the hotel in writing before you arrive, and tell the shop what it said.

With swapping, the charging happens at the station, which the operator designs and manages. Vietnam's Ministry of Construction was reported in December 2025 to be considering requiring electric-vehicle parking, charging and swap facilities to sit outside residential buildings, partly to reduce pressure on basement garages and improve safety. That is a proposal under discussion, not a current rule. It does show the direction of thinking: move the charging away from where people sleep.

This does not make swapping risk-free, and it does not make plug-in charging dangerous when done sensibly. It means the two systems put the responsibility in different places.

## Cost: what to plan for without quoting numbers

For a renter, the cost of charging is usually folded into the rental, but check. Ask the shop:

- whether electricity at your hotel is something you need to pay for;
- whether any swap fee, if swaps are used, is part of the rental or an extra;
- what happens if the bike comes back with a lower battery level than at handover.

## Which suits which renter

**Plug-in suits you if:**

- you rent for a day to a few weeks and ride moderate distances;
- your hotel allows charging in a safe place;
- you can charge in the afternoon or evening while someone is around;
- you want to know exactly what the bike is doing.

**Swapping is worth asking about if:**

- you will ride long distances daily;
- the shop's bike is a swappable model and the shop has a clear arrangement for swaps;
- your hotel does not allow charging at all;
- you would otherwise have no way to keep the battery topped up.

**Choose neither if:**

- you want no charging to think about. A petrol scooter has no charging question, and our [motorbikes page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbikes/) shows the range.

## Questions to put to the shop

Put these to the shop before you pay:

1. Does this bike charge by plug, by swap, or both?
2. Is the battery removable, and who may remove it?
3. Which charger belongs to the bike, and who keeps it?
4. If swaps are possible, who does them, and what does it cost me?
5. What battery level is recorded at handover, and what is expected at return?
6. What do I do if the battery runs low away from the shop?

## FAQ: swapping versus plug-in charging

### Is battery swapping better than plug-in charging?

For an owner who uses the bike daily, often yes. For a renter, it depends on access. A swap is faster but is built around subscribers, and a visitor may not be able to use a cabinet. Plug-in is slower but works with almost any rental.

### Can a tourist use a battery-swap cabinet?

Possibly, but do not assume it. The operators' systems are account-based, and our guide to charging an electric scooter in Hanoi describes the conditions V-Green publishes. Ask the shop how it handles swaps.

### How long does plug-in charging take?

It depends on the model. A 2023 study of VinFast scooters reported roughly 4.8 to 12 hours on household power and about four hours at a public station. Your rental may differ, so ask the shop.

### Which is safer?

Neither is automatically safer. Plug-in puts the choice of place and time in your hands, which is why fire services stress attended, dry, ventilated charging. Swapping moves the charging to a station. Follow the guidance for whichever system your bike uses.

### Do I need an app?

A swap system normally uses one. Honda's description of its system, for example, starts with finding a station in an app. For a plug-in rental you usually do not need one, but ask the shop.

### What if I cannot charge or swap at all?

Tell the shop. Do not try to improvise with another charger or a stranger's socket. The shop can say whether to return the bike, swap it for another or arrange another solution.
