The best way to deal with a pothole on a rental motorbike is to see it early, slow down before it and, if you cannot avoid it safely, cross it upright and straight with relaxed arms and the brakes released. Most damage and most falls come from late braking in the hole, a swerve into other traffic or hitting a hole you could not see because it was full of water. Law 36/2024/QH15 supports the approach. Article 12 requires operators to keep a speed suited to the condition of the road, traffic density and weather, and to observe the surroundings and slow down in rain, wind and on slippery or muddy surfaces. If you do hit one hard, stop somewhere safe, check the tyres, wheels and steering and tell the rental shop promptly.

This guide explains why potholes are a particular problem on small-wheeled scooters, how to scan for them, how to ride over one if you must, how to deal with water-filled holes and night riding, what to inspect afterwards and what to say to the shop. It is general information based on the law and published riding guidance as of October 2026, not legal advice.

## Why potholes matter on a scooter

A pothole is a hole or break in the road surface, and on a two-wheeler it affects more than comfort.

- **It can throw the bike off line.** A wheel dropping into a hole pulls the steering, and a front-wheel hit can twist the handlebar.

- **It can damage the bike.** Tyres, rims, suspension and steering parts take the impact, and a hard hit can cut or bulge a tyre or bend a rim.

- **It can cause a fall,** particularly if you brake hard, swerve or are leaning at the time.

- **Small wheels are more sensitive.** As a general principle, a smaller wheel drops into a hole more readily than a larger one, and many scooters have small wheels, so what a car passes over can be a real jolt on a scooter.

On a rental, an impact also raises a question about who pays for damage. The shop's published pages say that damage from a collision, an accident or misuse is priced by its cause, the actual condition of the bike and the contract, and that the renter may bear a cost in proportion to the real damage. That is a reason to ride carefully and to report any hit promptly.

## Step 1: scan the road ahead

Most pothole problems begin with late detection.

1. **Look well ahead,** not at the wheel of the vehicle in front. Scan as far ahead as traffic allows.

2. **Look for the signs of damage:** dark patches, broken edges, patched asphalt, shine on wet surfaces, a line of vehicles all swerving at the same point.

3. **Watch the riders in front.** If several swerve or brake at the same spot, there is probably a hole.

4. **Look at the road after rain,** when new holes open and old ones fill with water.

5. **Be careful near roadworks, trenches and manhole covers,** where the surface is often uneven or missing.

A US motorcycle-insurance safety guide groups potholes, broken pavement and railroad tracks with the other road hazards that riders should plan for in advance. ## Step 2: slow down before the hole

The most useful thing you can do is to reduce speed before you reach it.

- **Ease off the throttle early,** and let the scooter slow.

- **Brake before the hole, not in it.** Squeeze both brakes smoothly while the bike is upright and moving straight. Braking hard as the front wheel drops in can lock the wheel or dive the front.

- **Check your mirror** before slowing, so that the rider behind is not surprised.

- **Keep your speed low and steady.** A slow roll through a hole is less violent than a fast one. Our guide to [speed limits for motorbikes in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/speed-limits-motorbikes-vietnam/) explains the limits.

## Step 3: avoid it, if you can do so safely

If the hole is small and the lane is clear, you can ride around it.

- **Check behind and beside you** before you move. Article 13 of the Law requires you to keep within one lane and to check the vehicles in front, behind and on both sides before changing lanes.

- **Signal** if you are changing line, and move gradually.

- **Do not swerve into oncoming traffic or into the rider beside you.** A hole is better than a collision.

- **Do not swerve at the last moment,** because a sudden jerk on the bars can be worse than the hole.

If you cannot avoid it safely, go over it deliberately.

## Step 4: crossing a pothole you cannot avoid

When you must cross it:

1. **Slow right down,** and release the brakes just before the hole.

2. **Keep the bike upright and straight,** and hit the hole squarely if you can, not at a shallow angle across its edge.

3. **Keep your arms and shoulders relaxed,** with a firm but not rigid grip, so that the impact does not turn into a steering input.

4. **Keep a steady, light throttle,** and do not accelerate or brake in the hole.

5. **Lighten your seat slightly** if you are comfortable doing so, bending your knees and taking some weight off the seat, so that your legs act as springs. On a step-through scooter, that means rising a little while keeping your feet on the floor.

6. **Look past the hole,** to where you want to go, not at the hole itself.

7. **Straighten up and rebuild speed gently** once you are through.

If you have a passenger, tell them to hold on and expect a jolt, and slow down more.

## Holes hidden by water

Rain turns the road into a puzzle.

- **A puddle can hide a pothole,** broken pavement, a submerged kerb or debris. A guide from a rider-gear company says that a shallow puddle may be harmless, but a deep one may conceal any of these.

- **If you cannot see the depth,** slow down, avoid sudden inputs and leave space to change line if traffic allows.

- **Do not follow so closely** that spray hides the road ahead.

- **Do not ride through standing water** that looks deep, because it can also flood the engine or the electrics. ## Night riding

At night, potholes are much harder to see.

- **Slow down,** so that you can stop within the distance your light shows you.

- **Use your low beam** and make sure it is aimed properly. Article 20 of the Law requires front lights from 18:00 to 06:00. Our guide to [riding at night in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-at-night-in-hanoi/) covers lights.

- **Follow the line of the vehicle in front** only as a hint, and not as a guarantee, because it may avoid a hole you cannot see.

- **Avoid unfamiliar back streets after dark.** Our guide to [how to plan a scooter route with limited daylight](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-plan-a-scooter-route-with-limited-daylight/) explains how to plan.

## After you hit a pothole

If you take a hard hit, do not just carry on.

1. **Stop in a safe place,** off the road, as soon as you can.

2. **Check yourself,** and any passenger, for injuries.

3. **Look at the tyres.** Look for cuts, bulges, flat spots and low pressure. A tyre may leak slowly after a hit.

4. **Look at the wheels.** Look for a bent or dented rim, a wobble when you spin it, or a rub on the brake or the frame.

5. **Check the steering.** The handlebar should be straight, turn freely and not feel loose.

6. **Check the brakes and the suspension.** The bike should not pull to one side, and it should not bottom out.

7. **Check for leaks and loose parts.**

8. **Photograph any damage,** and the hole if you can do so safely.

9. **Test the bike at low speed** before you continue. If it pulls, wobbles or feels wrong, do not ride on.

## What to tell the rental shop

Nguyen Ha's published pages say to contact the shop if there is a mechanical issue, a puncture, an accident or unexpected damage, and not to assume the cost or the repair approach without checking the agreement. So:

1. **Call or message promptly** on 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969), open 09:00 to 21:00 daily.

2. **Describe what happened:** where, when, how fast and what you felt.

3. **Send photographs** of the bike, the tyre and the hole.

4. **Say whether the bike is safe to ride.**

5. **Do not have it repaired** unless the shop agrees, and keep receipts if it does. Our guide to [how to keep receipts for repairs on a rental motorbike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-keep-receipts-for-repairs-on-a-rental-motorbike/) explains how.

## Common mistakes

1. **Looking at the road too late.**

2. **Braking hard in the hole.**

3. **Swerving into other traffic.**

4. **Gripping the bars rigidly.**

5. **Riding through puddles without slowing.**

6. **Riding on after a hard hit without a check.**

7. **Not telling the shop.**

## A quick checklist

1. **Scan ahead, and watch the riders in front.**

2. **Slow down before the hole.**

3. **Avoid it only if you can do so safely.**

4. **Cross upright, straight, relaxed and with the brakes off.**

5. **Treat puddles as hidden holes.**

6. **Slow down at night.**

7. **After a hard hit, stop, check and report.**

## FAQ: potholes on a rental motorbike

### Should I brake when I see a pothole?

Slow down before it, while the bike is upright and straight. Do not brake hard in the hole.

### Should I swerve around a pothole?

Only if the lane is clear and you have checked behind and beside you. A hole is better than a collision.

### What if the pothole is full of water?

Treat it as a hidden hazard. Slow down, avoid sudden inputs and keep a steady throttle.

### What should I check after hitting one?

The tyres, the wheels and rims, the steering, the brakes and the suspension, and any leaks or loose parts.

### Who pays for damage from a pothole?

It depends on the facts and your contract. The shop's published pages say damage is priced by its cause, the actual condition of the bike and the contract, so report it promptly and keep photographs.

### Can I have a tyre repaired myself?

Only with the shop's agreement. Keep any receipt.
