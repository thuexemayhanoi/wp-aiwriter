After a minor fall on a 50cc scooter, deal with people first and the bike second. Stop, switch the engine off, get yourself and the bike out of the traffic, and check whether you or anyone else is hurt. Clean and cover grazes, watch for signs of a head injury even if you feel fine, and call 115 for an ambulance if anything looks serious. Only then look at the bike: with the ignition off, inspect it for fuel leaks, loose parts, a bent handlebar or lever and the state of the brakes and wheels, ride on only if it feels normal, and tell the shop promptly with photographs. A 50cc moped is light and slow, so most falls are low-speed tip-overs, but a low-speed fall can still hurt. This guide gives the sequence, the warning signs, the legal duties when other people are involved and the checks that matter on a small bike.

## The first minute: people before the bike

1. **Stop and switch the engine off.** Take the key out if you can reach it safely.
2. **Do not jump straight up.** Take a few seconds to notice whether anything hurts, especially your neck, back, wrists, ankles and head.
3. **Get out of the traffic.** Move yourself to the roadside or pavement, and then the bike if it is blocking the lane. If anyone else was involved, read the section on duties below before you move vehicles.
4. **Check anyone else who was involved,** including a passenger or a pedestrian.
5. **Take a slow breath and look at the bike from a distance.** Smell for petrol. If you smell fuel or see it leaking, keep away from flames and cigarettes.
6. **Take your time.** Shock can hide pain for several minutes.

## When to call for help

Vietnam has separate emergency numbers. According to the UK Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office, the ambulance number is 115, fire is 114, police is 113 and national search and rescue is 112, and the lines are operated in Vietnamese only. In practice, if you or someone nearby has to make a call, ask a passer-by, a shop worker or hotel staff to help, give the street name and a landmark first, and say clearly that someone is injured.

Call for an ambulance if there is heavy bleeding that does not stop, severe pain, a suspected broken bone, trouble breathing, confusion, loss of consciousness or anything you cannot judge. Do not move someone with a possible neck or back injury unless they are in danger.

## Treating grazes and cuts

A fall on tarmac typically gives grazes on the hands, elbows, knees and shins. The NHS guidance for cuts and grazes is simple and applies anywhere:

- **Wash your hands** if you can, and put on gloves if you have them.
- **Stop any bleeding** by pressing firmly with a clean cloth for about ten minutes.
- **Rinse the wound** with clean water, or wipe it with sterile wipes, and clean the skin around it, not inside it.
- **Cover it** with a dressing and keep it clean and dry.
- **Do not try to remove** anything stuck in a wound.

The NHS says to get medical help if a wound has dirt that will not come out after cleaning, if it is larger than about 5 cm, if it becomes red, swollen and more painful, or if you feel unwell with a high temperature. It also notes that a wound that could be infected may need a tetanus injection or antibiotics, so a dirty road graze is worth showing to a clinic. A chart review of road rash after motorcycle trauma, indexed on PubMed, found that local wound care is enough for most of these injuries, though that is not a reason to ignore one that looks deep or dirty.

## Head knocks: watch the next hours and days

If your head hit the ground, treat it seriously even if your helmet looks fine. NHS advice says to go to A&E, which in Vietnam means a hospital emergency department, if someone has been knocked out, has vomited, has a headache that does not go away, a memory problem, a change in behaviour, or an open wound on the head. It says to call an ambulance if there is bleeding from an ear, numbness or weakness in part of the body, problems with balance or speech, or the person hit their head at speed. NHS inform adds that a person with a minor head injury should avoid driving or riding until symptoms that affect sight or thinking have gone.

Practical points:

- Rest, and let someone stay with you for the first 24 hours.
- Symptoms can appear days later, so watch for a worsening headache, sickness or drowsiness.
- Do not ride again until you feel clear.
- Replace a helmet that took a hard knock, since it may be damaged inside where you cannot see it. Our guide on [helmet rules in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/helmet-rules-in-vietnam/) explains what riders and passengers must wear.

## When other people or vehicles are involved

If it was a low-speed tip-over with no other person, vehicle or property involved, the practical step is to tell the shop. If you are unsure whether the rules apply to your case, ask the shop or a local to help. If another person, vehicle or property was involved, specific duties apply. As summarised by a Da Nang rental guide, Article 80 of Vietnam's Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety, Law 36/2024/QH15, effective from 1 January 2025, requires those involved in an accident to stop, warn others of the danger, keep the scene unchanged, help injured people and report the accident to the police, a medical facility or the nearest People's Committee. As of October 2026 the law is available on thuvienphapluat.vn, and this is a summary, not legal advice.

In practice that means you should not leave, you should photograph the scene before moving anything if it is safe to do so, and you should call 113 if the other party is injured, if there is a dispute or if there is significant damage. If language is a barrier, ask for help from a local, a hotel or your embassy, and phone the shop.

## Inspecting the 50cc before you ride on

Honda's owner's manuals for other models give the clearest manufacturer guidance. They say that after a crash, if you decide to continue, you should turn the ignition off and evaluate the condition of the vehicle, inspect for fluid leaks, check the tightness of critical nuts and bolts, and check the handlebar, control levers, brakes and wheels, then ride slowly and cautiously and have the vehicle thoroughly checked at a qualified service facility as soon as possible, because damage may not be immediately apparent. Those lines come from the manuals for the CB500X and Super Cub C125, which are different models from a typical 50cc rental, but the steps apply to any small bike.

| Check | What to look for | If it is wrong |
|---|---|---|
| Fuel | Smell of petrol, drips under the tank or carburettor | Do not start the engine; call the shop |
| Handlebar | Straight when the front wheel is straight | Do not ride; do not force it back |
| Levers and pedals | Bent, cracked or loose brake lever, gear pedal or kick lever | Do not ride |
| Brakes | Both firm, not spongy, no dragging | Do not ride |
| Mirrors | Loose or cracked | Report; ride only if you can still see behind |
| Lights and horn | Headlight, tail light, indicators, horn work | Report; avoid riding after dark |
| Tyres | Cuts, bulges, low pressure | Do not ride on a damaged tyre |
| Body panels | Cracks, sharp edges | Photograph; avoid riding if a panel can touch the wheel |
| Rack and seat | Loose or bent | Report |
| Chain cover and stands | Loose or dented | Report |

Do not try to straighten a bent handlebar, lever or frame part by force. Bending metal back can weaken it. If any item in the table is marked "do not ride", stop and phone the shop. ## Lifting the bike safely

A light 50cc moped can be lifted by one person, but lift with your legs, not your back. Honda lists a curb weight of 96 kg for the Japan-market Super Cub 50, which is a guide to how light these bikes are, though an older rental may differ. Stand on the high side, grip the frame or rack rather than a lever or mirror, and keep clear of a hot exhaust, which can burn skin. If the bike is on top of someone, lift only if you can do it safely, and call for help.

## Tell the shop promptly

Tell the shop on the day, not at the end of the rental. You can ring 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969); the shop is open 09:00 to 21:00 daily. Be ready to say:

- Where you are and whether anyone is hurt.
- What happened, in plain words: a slow tip-over, a slip on a wet road, a gust, a collision.
- Whether the bike will start and ride, and what you can see broken.
- Whether any other person or vehicle was involved.

Photograph the bike from several angles, the scene, and any road feature that played a part, such as a wet patch, a pothole or gravel. Keep the pictures with the date. ## Common mistakes after a small fall

- **Riding away to "see if it is fine"** without checking the bike.
- **Ignoring the head** because there was no blood.
- **Straightening bent parts** with a roadside spanner or a foot.
- **Hiding the fall** until the return, when the damage is harder to explain.
- **Leaving without photographs.**
- **Skipping a clinic** for a dirty graze.

## Reducing the chance of a repeat

Most low-speed falls are caused by surfaces and habits, not speed: wet paint lines, loose gravel, a high kerb, a sudden brake in a corner, a bag swinging from the handlebar. ## FAQ: minor falls on a 50cc scooter

### Should I ride on after a minor fall?

Only after you have checked yourself and then the bike, and only if both feel normal. If a lever is bent, the brakes feel odd or the handlebar is crooked, stop and call the shop.

### Do I need to see a doctor for a graze?

Many grazes can be cleaned and covered at home, but see a clinic if it is dirty, deep, larger than about 5 cm or becomes red and painful. A tetanus injection may be needed.

### What if I hit my head but feel fine?

Rest, have someone stay with you, and watch for headache, vomiting, drowsiness or confusion. Get urgent help if any appear.

### Do I have to tell the shop about a small tip-over?

Yes, and do it promptly. Honest early reports are easier to deal with than damage discovered at return.

### What number do I call for an ambulance in Vietnam?

115 for an ambulance, 113 for police. The lines are operated in Vietnamese, so ask a local to help if you can.

### Who pays for damage?

That depends on the contract and the shop, so ask. Do not agree to a figure you do not understand.
