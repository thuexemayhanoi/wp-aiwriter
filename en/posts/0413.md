To estimate a late-return charge at Nguyen Ha, find your exact due time, measure how long after it you will bring the bike back, and apply the published scale: for a return 1 to 5 hours late the reference is VND 30,000 for each hour, and from 6 hours late one more rental day is charged at the daily rate of that bike. Five hours of the hourly charge adds up to VND 150,000, the same as the lowest published daily rate for manual and automatic bikes, so the step from hour five to hour six matters. This guide shows the calculation step by step with worked examples written as hours and units, lists what the published scale does not say, explains how it differs by bike type and rental length, and gives the questions that turn an estimate into a figure you can rely on.

## The two published rules

The shop publishes two rules for a late return, on its prices page, its FAQ and its terms.

1. **One to five hours late:** VND 30,000 per hour.
2. **Six hours late or more:** one more rental day, charged at the daily rate of that bike.

Two related rules shape the calculation. First, a rental day is 24 hours from the handover time written in the contract, so your due time is a specific hour. Second, telling the shop in advance is appreciated but does not waive the charge. These are reference terms; the contract you sign for your bike is what finally applies, and the exact price of that bike is confirmed before you sign.

## Step 1: find the exact due time

Everything is measured from the due time, so get it right first.

- **For a one-day rental,** the due time is the handover time plus 24 hours. A scooter collected at 10:00 is due at 10:00 the next day unless the contract says otherwise.
- **For longer periods,** use the due time written in the contract. Do not work it out from a rule of thumb, because the shop's weekly and monthly packages are priced on their own, and the contract is the record.
- **If you extended,** the due time is the new time that the shop confirmed, not the original one.

Look at your copy of the contract and write the due time on your phone. Our guide to [how to ask for an extension](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-ask-for-an-extension-on-a-motorbike-rental/) explains how a new due time gets agreed.

## Step 2: measure the delay

Delay is the time between the due time and the moment you hand the bike back at the shop, not the moment you set off for the shop. Measure it from the due time to the time the shop checks the bike in. Allow for the way back: queues at a petrol station, the traffic near the shop and parking all add minutes. If you are cutting it close, decide in advance which side of an hour boundary you are aiming for.

## Step 3: apply the scale

Use the delay to pick the rule. The table uses a due time of 14:00 as an example, and shows the charge as units of the published hourly reference.

| You return at | Delay | Published rule | Charge in terms of the rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| 14:00 or earlier | None | No late charge | Nothing for lateness |
| 15:00 | 1 hour | Hourly reference | 1 unit of VND 30,000 |
| 16:00 | 2 hours | Hourly reference | 2 units of VND 30,000 |
| 17:00 | 3 hours | Hourly reference | 3 units of VND 30,000 |
| 18:00 | 4 hours | Hourly reference | 4 units of VND 30,000 |
| 19:00 | 5 hours | Hourly reference | 5 units, which is VND 150,000 in total |
| 20:00 | 6 hours | Extra-day rule | One more rental day at your bike's daily rate |

The reason the table stops at six hours is that the published scale does. Remember that the shop closes at 21:00, so a due time late in the evening leaves little room for any delay at all.

The numbers in the table are an illustration of how the scale works, not a quote. They assume whole hours. The shop does not publish how a part-hour is counted, so a return twenty minutes after the hour might be a full unit or might not. Ask.

## Step 4: find the daily rate that applies

From the sixth hour the charge is a full extra day at the daily rate of that bike. The published reference ranges are:

- **Manual and semi-automatic bikes** such as the Honda Wave, Yamaha Sirius and Honda Blade: VND 150,000 to 200,000 per day.
- **Automatic scooters** such as the Honda Vision, Air Blade, Click and Lead: VND 150,000 to 200,000 per day.
- **50cc bikes:** VND 200,000 per day.
- **Electric models:** quoted by model, with no single daily figure published, so ask.

The exact rate is confirmed on the actual bike before you sign, so look at your contract for the daily rate of your bike. A weekly or monthly renter should note that the extra day is charged at the daily rate of that bike and that the published terms do not describe a proportion of the weekly or monthly package. Our [prices page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/prices/) shows the reference table.

### The step from hour five to hour six

Five hours of the hourly scale come to VND 150,000. The lowest daily rate for manual and automatic bikes is also VND 150,000, so for a bike at that rate the sixth hour changes the label of the charge but not the amount. For a bike at the top of the range, VND 200,000 per day, the extra-day rule takes the charge above five hours of the hourly scale. In other words, the cliff at six hours is steeper for the more expensive bikes. If you are on the borderline, ask the shop in advance which rate applies to your bike and think about arranging an extension instead.

## Step 5: add anything else that belongs at the return

A late charge is not the only item the shop checks at the return. The shop compares the bike with the handover record: condition, fuel and time, and settles any late or fuel charge before the deposit is returned under the contract. Petrol bikes leave with about 0.5 to 2 litres and should come back with about 0.5 to 1 litre, and an empty tank at return is charged VND 20,000. If you are racing the clock, do not arrive with an empty tank to save five minutes at a petrol station. Our guide on [returning your rental motorbike in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/returning-your-rental-motorbike-hanoi/) lists the checks, and our explanation of [how a deposit works](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbike-rental-deposit-hanoi/) explains the settlement.

## What the published scale does not say

A calculation is only as good as its inputs. The following points are not spelled out in the published terms, so confirm them before you rely on an estimate.

- **How a part-hour is counted.** Whether twenty minutes counts as an hour.
- **What happens beyond the first extra day.** The terms describe one more rental day from six hours, and do not explain a delay of a day or more.
- **How a return after closing time is handled.** The shop is open 09:00 to 21:00.
- **How the charge is taken.** From the deposit, in cash at the counter, or another way.
- **Whether the cause of the delay changes anything.** A technical fault that did not come from the way the bike was used is the shop's to deal with, according to the FAQ, so ask how a breakdown delay is treated.

Asking these questions at the counter when you sign, and not at the return counter, is the easiest way to avoid a dispute.

## Late charge versus extension

A late return and an extension can look similar on paper, but they are different arrangements. A late charge is what applies when you are past the due time without an agreement. An extension is agreed before the period ends, including price and return arrangements. If you expect to be later than the due time by more than a few hours, an agreed extension gives you a figure in advance, while a late return gives you a figure afterwards. Our guide to [weekly and monthly rental questions](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/weekly-monthly-motorbike-rental-hanoi/) helps if the real problem is that the period was too short.

## What the Civil Code adds

Under Vietnam's Civil Code, Article 481 says a lessee must pay rent in full and on time as agreed. Article 482 says that where a lessee delays returning the property, the lessor may demand its return, rent for the period of delay and compensation for damage, that a contractual penalty is payable if the parties agreed one, and that the lessee bears the risk to the property during the delay. The hourly scale and the extra-day rule are the shop's published reference terms, which is why the contract is the document to read. If you disagree with a charge, keep the contract copy, the handover photographs and any messages, and raise it with the shop calmly and in writing; the shop's terms suggest seeking a documented resolution.

## A quick calculation checklist

1. Write down the due time from the contract.
2. Note the time you expect to hand the bike back.
3. Subtract to get the delay in hours.
4. If it is 1 to 5 hours, multiply the hours by VND 30,000 and ask how part-hours count.
5. If it is 6 hours or more, look up the daily rate of your bike.
6. Add the empty-tank charge if it applies.
7. Ask the shop to confirm the figure before you pay.

## FAQ: calculating a late-return charge

### How much is the late charge per hour?

The published reference is VND 30,000 per hour for a return 1 to 5 hours late.

### What happens at six hours late?

One more rental day is charged at the daily rate of that bike.

### Is the late charge reduced if I call ahead?

No. Advance notice helps with planning but does not waive the charge.

### Is the extra day the same price for every bike?

No. It is the daily rate of that bike, so a 50cc bike and a manual bike can differ. Electric models are quoted individually.

### Do I get my deposit back if I am late?

The deposit is returned under the contract once the bike has been checked and any late or fuel charge has been settled.

### Can I avoid the charge by extending?

An extension agreed before the period ends has its own price and arrangements. Ask the shop for the figure.
