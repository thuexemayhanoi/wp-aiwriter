A high odometer reading is not a verdict on a rental bike, but it is a good reason to ask questions. Small manual bikes such as the Honda Wave are built to cover many kilometres, and a well-serviced one with a high reading can be a better ride than a lightly used bike nobody has looked at. What matters is how the bike has been looked after, which parts have been replaced and whether it behaves properly when you test it. This guide lists the questions worth asking the shop, grouped by topic, explains what Honda's maintenance pages say about the parts that age, sets out a ten-minute test that turns your impressions into evidence, names the red flags and explains how to ask for another bike politely. It is general information, not an inspection, and the shop's answers matter more than any rule of thumb here.

## What the odometer does and does not tell you

The number on the dial counts distance. It does not say how the distance was covered, whether the oil was changed on time, how the chain was kept or how the bike was ridden. Two bikes with the same reading can be in very different condition.

It also has limits as a record. An odometer can be replaced, can stop working or can be hard to read. If the reading looks too low for a bike that is clearly old, ask whether the odometer has been changed. You do not need to accuse anyone. It is an ordinary question, and a shop that tracks its bikes will have an answer.

So treat the figure as a prompt. A high reading means more of the wear items described below have had time to age, which is why the questions matter. A low reading is no guarantee either, because a bike that has sat unused can have a flat battery, old fuel and perished rubber.

## Questions about service history

Start with when the bike was last looked at, and by whom. Honda's maintenance pages describe the items a service covers, and the points below follow them.

- **"When was the engine oil last changed?"** Honda says oil lubricates, cools, seals and cleans, and that its protection weakens as it ages, which can shorten engine life if it is not replaced regularly.

- **"When was the chain last cleaned, lubricated and checked?"** Honda's Wave RSX FI manual gives an interval of every 500 km for checking and lubricating the chain, sooner on poor roads or with hard riding.

- **"When were the brake shoes last checked or replaced?"** Honda says shoes wear down and free play grows until the brake works poorly.

- **"When were the tyres last replaced?"** Honda's tyre guidance notes tyres in use for more than five years should be inspected periodically, and that worn tyres lengthen stopping distance and slip more in rain.

- **"When was the spark plug last changed?"** Honda says plug electrodes wear away and plugs should be replaced periodically.

- **"When was the air filter last cleaned or replaced?"** Honda says a clogged element can cause an unstable idle, weaker acceleration and worse fuel economy.

- **"How old is the battery?"** Honda says a weak starter can mean a weakening battery.

You do not need all the answers in detail. You are listening for whether the shop knows, can find the answer quickly and has a routine. "We do a full check after every rental" is reassuring. A shrug is information too.

## Questions about wear items now

Ask the shop to show you, rather than tell you, where it can.

- **Tyres.** "Can you show me the tread wear indicator on both tyres?" Honda describes it as a triangle marking where the grooves are shallow. If the tread has worn down to it, Honda's Wave RSX FI manual says to replace the tyre. ## Questions about faults and repairs

- **"Has this bike had any problems recently, and what was done?"** A shop that has fixed something and says so is better than a shop that says there has never been a fault.

- **"Has it been in a fall or accident?"** Marks on the bike are evidence of falls. A bent lever, scuffed footrest or replaced mirror suggests one, and is not necessarily a problem if it was repaired properly.

- **"What was replaced after that?"** New parts on one side of an otherwise old bike are a sign of an honest repair.

- **"Is there anything about this bike I should know?"** An open question often gets the most useful answer, such as "the gear lever is stiff in second until it warms up".

## A ten-minute test that turns impressions into evidence

Do this with the shop's staff, in the forecourt or on a quiet street, before you accept the bike.

1. **Cold start.** Ask to start the engine from cold, not after staff have warmed it up. Our guide to [what to check on a manual bike's electric starter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-to-check-on-a-manual-bikes-electric-starter/) explains what a healthy start sounds like.

2. **Battery check.** Honda's check is to switch the ignition on with the headlight, indicators and brake lamp lit, then try the starter. A strong starter is fine. A weak or slow one suggests the battery is ageing.

3. **Idle.** Listen to the idle once warm. A steady note is good. Honda says a clogged air filter can make idling unstable.

4. **Pull-away.** Roll off gently. The bike should move smoothly. A hesitant, rough or surging start is worth a note.

5. **Gear changes.** Go through the gears with a gentle throttle. Our guide to [how to avoid lurching when changing gears](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-avoid-lurching-when-changing-gears/) explains what is your technique and what might be the bike. Watch for a gear that will not engage or jumps out, and see our guides to [what a slipping gear feels like](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-a-slipping-gear-feels-like-on-a-rented-motorbike/) and [what to do if a rental motorbike will not shift gears](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-to-do-if-a-rental-motorbike-will-not-shift-gears/).

6. **Acceleration.** Honda says a worn plug or a clogged filter can weaken acceleration. A bike that revs but does not pick up speed may be slipping.

7. **Brakes.** Test both slowly, as in our guide to [manual bike brakes: what to test before leaving the rental shop](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/manual-bike-brakes-what-to-test-before-leaving-the-rental-shop/).

8. **Noises.** Listen for a rattle, grind or squeal from the chain, wheels or engine, using our guide to [recognising chain noise on a Honda Wave or Yamaha Sirius](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/recognising-chain-noise-on-a-honda-wave-or-yamaha-sirius/).

9. **Steering.** Ride a few metres on a quiet street and notice whether the bike tracks straight and steers smoothly. With the bike stationary, turn the handlebars gently from lock to lock and feel for stiff or notchy spots.

None of this requires mechanical knowledge. You are looking for things that feel wrong, and the shop can explain or fix them.

## What the answers tell you

| What you hear or see | What it suggests |
|---|---|
| Specific, quick answers with dates | A shop that tracks its bikes |
| "We check it after every rental" | A routine, though ask what the check covers |
| Vague or contradictory answers | The history may not be recorded |
| Fresh oil, a new chain, newer tyres | Recent attention, a good sign |
| Tread at the wear indicator | A tyre that needs replacing before you ride |
| Weak, slow starter | An ageing battery |
| A grinding or heavy rattle from the chain | Wear or a loose chain, to be fixed first |
| A pedal that goes a long way before biting | Worn shoes or loose adjustment |
| A gear that jumps out or will not engage | A fault that should not leave the shop |

## Red flags

Ask for another bike, or a fix before you leave, if you notice:

- tyres at the wear indicator, or with cuts, cracks or bulges;

- a brake that is soft, slow to return, grinding or dragging;

- a chain that is rusted, dry, very loose or noisy;

- a weak starter or an engine that struggles to start from cold;

- slipping, a gear that will not hold, or revs rising without speed;

- fuel, oil or other fluid leaking;

- lights, indicators or the brake lamp not working;

- a shop that cannot or will not answer basic questions.

## Contract questions that matter more on an older bike

A bike with many kilometres is more likely to need attention during your rental, so settle these before you sign.

## FAQ: high-mileage manual rental bikes

### Is a high-mileage bike unsafe?

Not by itself. Condition and care matter more than the number, so ask about service and test the bike.

### Can I trust the odometer?

Treat it as a rough guide. An odometer can be replaced or faulty, so ask the shop whether it has been changed.

### What should I ask first?

When the oil, chain, brake shoes, tyres, spark plug, air filter and battery were last attended to.

### How do I test the battery?

Honda suggests turning the ignition on with the lights lit, then trying the starter. A weak or slow starter suggests an ageing battery.

### What if the shop cannot answer?

Ask for a mechanic's check or for another bike. A shop that cannot answer basic questions is a reason for caution.

### Who pays if a worn part fails?

It depends on the contract. Ask before you sign and get the answer in writing.
