Xuan Mai is the western gateway of Hanoi, about 40 kilometres from the centre along National Highway 6 (QL6), where the capital's built-up edge gives way to the first foothills. It makes a useful short out-of-city ride: far enough to feel like leaving Hanoi, close enough for a half day, and a good rehearsal for longer trips toward Hoa Binh or Mai Chau. The catch is that the road itself is the main event. QL6 between Ba La and Xuan Mai is a long, busy construction zone, so this ride rewards patience and good timing more than scenery.

## Why Xuan Mai makes a good short ride

Many first-time visitors jump straight to a famous destination and discover that the hardest part is the traffic, not the sightseeing. Xuan Mai lets you practise the hard part on its own. You cross the whole western suburban belt, deal with lorries and coaches, and reach a place with cafes, fuel and a clear turning point, without committing to a mountain road.

It also suits riders who want a short, low-pressure test of a rental bike before a bigger journey. If something feels wrong with the brakes, the gearbox or your own comfort at highway speed, you find out within a couple of hours of the shop rather than on a pass. That is a better use of an afternoon than guessing.

## What and where Xuan Mai is

Xuan Mai is now a commune, formed on 1 July 2025 by merging the former Xuan Mai town with Nam Phuong Tien and Thuy Xuan Tien communes and most of Tan Tien. Hanoi's broadcaster describes it as covering about 50.72 square kilometres with a population of about 67,000, located at the junction of QL6 and the road formerly known as National Highway 21A, now called the Ho Chi Minh road.

It is the start of QL6 in the sense that matters to travellers: from here the highway continues toward Luong Son, Hoa Binh and the north-west. The commune borders Phu Tho province, which now includes the old Hoa Binh province. You may still see older signs and websites that refer to Xuan Mai town in Chuong My district, so expect a mix of names.

## Distance and the route out

The Ba La to Xuan Mai project begins at kilometre 14 of QL6 in Ha Dong and ends at kilometre 38 at Xuan Mai, adjacent to Luong Son. That puts Xuan Mai roughly 38 kilometres along the highway from its Hanoi starting point, so from the Old Quarter or Long Bien allow about 40 kilometres or a little more each way, as an estimate. Check the exact figure on your map.

A rider should plan to use QL6 or the local roads that run beside it, not the car-only Thang Long Avenue central lanes or the Hoa Lac - Hoa Binh expressway. As of October 2026, Vietnam's Road Traffic Order and Safety Law (Law 36/2024/QH15, effective 1 January 2025) states in Article 25 that motorcycles and mopeds may not use expressways. Our [ring roads and highways guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-ring-roads-highways-motorbikes-allowed/) explains the signs, and the [navigation-app check](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-check-if-a-navigation-app-suggests-an-expressway/) helps you set your phone correctly.

## The roadworks, in plain terms

The Ba La to Xuan Mai upgrade covers about 21.7 kilometres and is designed as a 50 to 60 metre wide road for 80 to 100 km/h. Construction began in December 2022. Before the project, parts of the road were only about 7 to 10 metres wide, with heavy traffic, according to Hanoi Moi. In July 2026, reports described construction speeding up and a target of completion in the first quarter of 2027.

On the road this means a patchwork: new sections beside old ones, temporary lane shifts, uneven edges, dust and construction vehicles entering and leaving. Lao Dong also reported prolonged congestion at the Mai Linh bridge in late September 2026. Do not assume that a surface you rode last month is unchanged. Slow down at transitions, avoid squeezing between lorries and barriers, and follow marshals and signs.

## Best time of day

Early weekday mornings are generally better than weekends. Heavy vehicles and commuter flows build through the morning, and weekend traffic toward Hoa Binh and the north-west can make the highway slow in both directions. If you can, leave between 07:00 and 08:00, reach Xuan Mai by mid-morning and be back before the late-afternoon rush.

Avoid riding after dark on this road. Construction zones, unlit vehicles and temporary diversions are much harder to read at night, and our [night riding guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-at-night-in-hanoi/) explains why. A short ride should end early rather than late.

## A pleasant stop on the way: Tram Pagoda

If you want a destination with a bit of history, the Tram Pagoda complex lies in the Chuong My area. Nhan Dan newspaper describes it as about 20 kilometres from central Hanoi, a scenic mix of limestone hills, greenery and old spiritual buildings. It names Chua Hang (Long Tien cave), where Ho Chi Minh read a New Year message in 1947. Another travel report puts the Tram mountain eco-tourism area about 25 kilometres from the centre.

Because the complex is a religious and historical site, dress modestly, keep noise low and follow posted rules. Check the opening hours and any access restrictions on the day. Use a map pin for the exact entrance rather than a place name, and do not assume a particular road is open to motorbikes without checking the signs.

## Xuan Mai itself

Xuan Mai is a working town more than a visitor attraction. It sits at a major junction, with fuel, food, small cafes and shops serving drivers and the local population. For a rider, the appeal is practical: a place to stop, rest, check the bike and decide whether to continue or turn back.

Do not expect polished tourist facilities. Choose a cafe or restaurant that looks busy with locals, keep your bike in sight and lock the steering whenever you park. The shop does not provide an extra lock, so park somewhere visible or guarded; our [parking guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-motorbike-in-hanoi/) has more detail.

Because the trip is short, it is a good chance to practise habits you will need on longer rides:

- **Handover:** allow time to return the bike calmly; see [returning your rental motorbike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/returning-your-rental-motorbike-hanoi/).

## Choosing and checking the bike

For a ride of about 80 kilometres return, an automatic scooter or a semi-automatic bike is fine if it is in good condition. Run through the [10-minute inspection checklist](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-scooter-inspection-checklist/) before you leave the shop, and ask staff to confirm tyre pressure and fuel. A rider who is new to gears may want to read how a [semi-automatic bike works](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-ride-a-semi-automatic-honda-wave/) before the trip.

## When Xuan Mai is not the right choice

If you want mountain scenery, Xuan Mai is a poor substitute. Look instead at [Tam Dao](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-to-tam-dao-by-motorbike/) or [Ba Vi National Park](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-to-ba-vi-by-motorbike/), which are destinations in their own right. If rain is heavy, roadworks look chaotic or you feel tired, postpone. A short ride is only worthwhile if it stays short and calm.

## FAQ: Hanoi to Xuan Mai

### How far is Xuan Mai from central Hanoi?

Roughly 40 kilometres along QL6, based on the project's kilometre marks (km 14 at Ba La to km 38 at Xuan Mai). Check your own start point on a map.

### Is Xuan Mai still in Hanoi?

Yes. It is a commune of Hanoi on the border with Phu Tho province, formed on 1 July 2025 from the former Xuan Mai town and neighbouring communes.

### Can I use the expressway to get there faster?

No. As of October 2026, motorcycles and mopeds are excluded from expressways under Law 36/2024/QH15. Use QL6 and ordinary roads.

### Are the roadworks finished?

No. Reports in 2026 target completion in the first quarter of 2027, so expect construction and congestion. Check conditions on the day.

### Is it a good first ride outside the city?

Yes for a confident rider who starts early and keeps the plan short, but it is not a relaxed scenic route.
