On a hot day, drink water before you feel thirsty, stop in shade at least once an hour, wear light, covering clothes under your helmet, ride outside the hottest hours where you can, and stop at once if you feel dizzy, weak, nauseous or confused. Public-health guidance from the United States Centers for Disease Control and Prevention advises people who work outdoors to drink plenty of water without waiting for thirst, avoid alcohol and sugary drinks, wear loose, lightweight, light-coloured clothing and a hat, and, if they feel faint or weak, to stop all activity and get to a cool place. Riding in the sun adds wind that hides how much you are sweating, hot engine parts and a helmet that traps heat.

This guide explains why heat catches riders out, how to recognise dehydration, heat exhaustion and heat stroke, how to prepare and what to carry, how to dress, how to plan a day trip around the heat, what to do if someone is unwell and when to call for help. It is general information based on published public-health guidance as of October 2026, not medical advice.

## Why heat is a rider's problem

Riding feels cool because of the wind, and that is the trap. Moving air evaporates sweat quickly, so you may not notice how much fluid you are losing until you stop. At the same time, you are exposed to direct sun, hot tarmac, a hot engine and a helmet that holds heat. Rider-fatigue guides note that dehydration can lead to dizziness, confusion and slower motor skills, which are exactly the things a rider cannot afford. Heat also adds to tiredness, as our guide to [what to do if you feel fatigued on a long ride](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-to-do-if-you-feel-fatigued-on-a-long-ride/) explains.

Hanoi's summers are hot and humid, and humid air makes it harder for sweat to cool the body. Check the forecast and the heat before a day trip, and be ready to change the plan.

## Know the signs

The US National Institute for Occupational Safety and Health, part of the CDC, lists the main heat-related illnesses. The signs below are adapted from its guidance and from university health-service guidance on dehydration.

| Condition | Typical signs | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **Dehydration** | Thirst, headache, tiredness, dry or sticky mouth, dark yellow urine, passing little urine, muscle cramps | You need fluids and rest now |
| **Heat cramps** | Painful muscle cramps or spasms, often after heavy sweating | Stop, rest in the shade, drink water or an electrolyte drink |
| **Heat exhaustion** | Headache, nausea, dizziness, weakness, irritability, thirst, heavy sweating, raised body temperature, less urine | Move to a cool place, remove extra clothing, drink liquids, cool the skin, and seek medical evaluation |
| **Heat stroke** | Confusion, slurred speech, loss of consciousness, hot dry skin or profuse sweating, seizures, very high body temperature | An emergency: call for help at once |

Heat stroke can cause permanent disability or death if emergency treatment is delayed, according to the CDC's guidance. A rider with confusion, slurred speech or collapse needs urgent help, not rest.

## Before you go

**Hydrate the day before and the morning of the trip.** Drink water through the day, not just once you are on the road. A health-service guide advises drinking water before you are thirsty, noting that thirst is often the first sign of dehydration.

**Carry more water than you think you need.** A small bottle is not enough for a long day. Many riders carry a bottle in the seat storage or in a bag. Do not hang a heavy bag on the handlebars. Our guide to [why loose scarves and bags are dangerous on motorbikes](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/why-loose-scarves-and-bags-are-dangerous-on-motorbikes/) explains how to carry things safely.

**Eat lightly.** Small snacks are better than a big meal before riding, which can make you sleepy.

**Skip alcohol.** It dehydrates and, for a rider, it is also illegal: Article 9 of Law 36/2024/QH15 bans riding with any alcohol in the blood or breath. Our guide to [avoiding alcohol and impairment before riding](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/avoiding-alcohol-and-impairment-before-riding/) explains.

**Check your medicines.** Some medicines affect how your body handles heat or make you drowsy. Our guide to [medicines and drowsiness: when not to ride a scooter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/medicines-and-drowsiness-when-not-to-ride-a-scooter/) explains what to ask a pharmacist.

**Apply sunscreen,** and wear sunglasses or use a visor.

## What to wear

Our guide to [basic protective clothing for short scooter trips](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/basic-protective-clothing-for-short-scooter-trips/) lists the basics. For heat:

- **Light colours and breathable fabrics** that cover your arms and legs, which protect against sunburn as well as scrapes.

- **A helmet with vents,** if you have a choice, and a thin, light, breathable cap or scarf-free neck cover. Do not wear a thick layer under the helmet.

- **Fitted layers,** not loose, flapping ones.

- **Closed, breathable shoes** and thin gloves.

CDC guidance recommends loose, lightweight, light-coloured clothing and a brimmed hat for outdoor work. On a bike the hat cannot be worn, so rely on the helmet and light fitted layers. Sunburn also makes it harder for the skin to release heat, so use sunscreen.

## Plan the day around the heat

- **Start early,** and aim to be off the road, or in the shade, during the hottest part of the day, usually around midday and the early afternoon.

- **Plan shade stops** at least every hour, and take longer ones in the heat. A health-service guide suggests at least ten minutes in the shade for every hour of activity outdoors.

- **Choose shorter routes** on hot days. Our guides to [hanoi to Ecopark by scooter: roads and parking](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-to-ecopark-by-scooter-roads-and-parking/) and [Hanoi to Soc Son by scooter: forest stops and traffic checks](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-to-soc-son-by-scooter-forest-stops-and-traffic-checks/) describe routes with shade and places to stop.

- **Know where water is.** Mark shops, cafes and petrol stations on your route. Our guide to [how to plan fuel stops on a scooter day trip](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-plan-fuel-stops-on-a-scooter-day-trip/) helps.

- **Park in shade,** and do not leave water in a hot seat box for hours.

- **Be ready to turn back.** Heat can build suddenly, and a short route is better than a collapse.

Heat also affects the bike. ## What to drink

- **Water is the main drink.** Public-health guidance advises drinking regularly, before you are thirsty. One university health service suggests 8 ounces (about 240 ml) every 20 minutes for people working in heat.

- **An electrolyte drink can help** on a long, sweaty day, since sweat also loses salt.

- **Avoid alcohol, sugary drinks and too much caffeine.** The CDC advises avoiding alcohol and drinks with large amounts of sugar, and caffeine can add to fluid loss.

- **Sip, do not gulp.** Small, regular drinks are easier to take on a ride.

- **Check your urine colour.** Dark yellow urine and passing little urine are signs of dehydration.

## If you or a companion feel unwell

**Mild signs (thirst, headache, tiredness, cramps):**

1. **Stop at the next safe place** and get into shade.

2. **Take off your helmet and any extra layers.**

3. **Sip water or an electrolyte drink,** and rest.

4. **Cool the skin** with a wet cloth on the neck, wrists and face.

5. **Do not restart** until you feel clear. If you are not better in about half an hour, or feel worse, seek medical advice.

**Heat exhaustion signs (dizziness, nausea, weakness, heavy sweating):**

1. **Move to a cool, shaded or air-conditioned place,** lie or sit down and loosen clothing.

2. **Sip cool water,** and cool the skin with cold compresses or water.

3. **Seek medical evaluation.** CDC guidance advises taking the person to a clinic or emergency room, or calling emergency services if medical care is not available.

4. **Do not ride on.** Arrange a taxi or a lift, and call the shop about the bike.

**Heat stroke signs (confusion, slurred speech, collapse, fits, very hot skin):**

1. **Call 115 for an ambulance, or 112.** Our guide to [emergency numbers and accident reporting in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/emergency-numbers-and-accident-reporting-in-vietnam/) explains how to describe your location.

2. **Move the person to shade, remove outer clothing and cool them quickly** with cold water on the skin or wet clothing and air movement, and stay with them until help arrives.

Do not give drinks to a person who is confused or unconscious. If in doubt, treat it as an emergency.

## A quick checklist

1. **Drink before you are thirsty, and carry plenty of water.**

2. **Light, fitted, covering clothes, sunscreen and a well-fitted helmet.**

3. **Start early, and avoid the hottest hours.**

4. **Stop in shade at least once an hour.**

5. **No alcohol.**

6. **Know the signs: headache, dizziness, nausea, weakness, dark urine.**

7. **Stop, cool down and drink at the first signs.**

8. **Call 115 or 112 for confusion, collapse or fits.**

## FAQ: heat and dehydration

### How much water should I carry on a day trip?

Enough to drink regularly through the day, with spare. One health service suggests about 240 ml every 20 minutes when working in heat, which adds up quickly. Carry more than you expect to need.

### Is it safe to ride in the midday heat?

It carries more risk. Plan to be in the shade, or off the road, in the hottest hours.

### What are the first signs of dehydration?

Thirst, headache, tiredness, a dry mouth, dark yellow urine and muscle cramps.

### Can I keep riding if I feel dizzy?

No. Stop in a safe place, cool down, drink and rest. If the dizziness continues, seek medical help.

### Does the wind keep me cool enough?

Not necessarily. Wind hides how much you are sweating, so drink even if you do not feel hot.

### Who do I call in an emergency?

Call 115 for an ambulance or 112 for the national emergency centre, and then the shop.
