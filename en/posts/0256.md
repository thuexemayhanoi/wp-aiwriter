Vietnam draws two power lines for electric two-wheelers: 4 kW, which separates the moped class from motorcycles, and 11 kW, which separates class A1 from class A. The difficulty for renters is that a spec sheet rarely gives one clean number. Brands print a nominal (rated) figure and a higher peak figure, shops round to "1,000 W" or "2 kW", and a battery rating in kWh sits close by and is easy to confuse with the motor. This guide explains how to read those figures, how the two thresholds work in the 2024 traffic law, why design speed still decides some cases whatever the motor says, and what to ask before you rent. Figures come from VinFast's own published specifications as examples of how to read a sheet; they are not a description of any specific bike at the shop. Rules are as of October 2026.

## Watts, kilowatts and the two lines that matter

A kilowatt is 1,000 watts, so 1,500 W is 1.5 kW and 2,450 W is 2.45 kW. Two thresholds appear in Law 36/2024/QH15, the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety, which took effect on 1 January 2025:

- **4 kW** is the upper limit for an electric moped. Article 34 defines a moped as a two- or three-wheeler with a design speed of no more than 50 km/h and, if electric, a motor power of no more than 4 kW. Motorcycles are all other motorised two- and three-wheelers.
- **11 kW** is the upper limit of class A1. Article 57 gives class A1 to two-wheel motorcycles up to 125 cm3 or with an electric motor of up to 11 kW, and class A to those above, with class A also covering everything class A1 covers.

Put together, there are three power bands for electric two-wheelers that have a motor and no pedal assist: up to 4 kW (and under the speed limit), over 4 kW and up to 11 kW, and over 11 kW. Most scooters in Hanoi rental fleets sit in the first two bands. Machines above 11 kW are performance motorcycles and rare among rental offers.

## Nominal, peak and rated: which figure is the motor power?

Electric scooter makers quote power in more than one way. VinFast's published sheet for the Evo Grand family shows the pattern clearly:

| Model (VinFast sheet, July 2025) | Nominal power | Maximum (peak) power | Top speed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Evo Grand Lite | 1,500 W | 1,900 W | 48 km/h |
| Evo Grand | 1,500 W | 2,250 W | 70 km/h |

The same company's comparison of Evo200 and Evo200 Lite also lists a 1,500 W nominal figure beside peak figures of 2,500 W and 2,450 W. The nominal number describes what the motor can deliver continuously. The peak number is a short-burst maximum, such as when you open the throttle hard from a standstill or climb a ramp.

Whether the law's "motor power" means nominal or peak is not spelled out in the passages quoted by the official and legal portals used for this article. This article therefore does not assume either reading. For the models above the question never arises, because both figures are well under 4 kW, but it matters for any bike where nominal is below 4 kW and peak is above. In that case, ask the shop which figure the registration certificate and the manufacturer's type approval use, and ask for the vehicle class printed on the registration.

## Power is only half the moped test

The 4 kW limit never stands alone. The same Article 34 definition says the vehicle must also have a design speed of no more than 50 km/h. A scooter with a 1.5 kW motor that is built to reach 70 km/h is a motorcycle. That is why VinFast's Evo Grand Lite, limited to 48 km/h, and the Evo Grand, built for 70 km/h, sit in different legal classes though their nominal motors are identical, and why VinFast's own page says the Evo200 Lite needs no licence while the Evo200 needs class A1.

For the licence question the sequence is therefore:

1. Is it a motor-assisted bicycle (pedals, assist cut at 25 km/h)? Then no licence.
2. Is the design speed no more than 50 km/h and the motor no more than 4 kW? Then it is a moped, with no licence but a minimum age of 16.
3. Otherwise it is a motorcycle: class A1 up to 11 kW, class A above, minimum age 18.

For the wider picture of how the classes work on Hanoi streets, see our guide to [electric bikes in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/electric-bikes-in-hanoi-rules/).

## Mapping kilowatts to licence categories

| Motor power | With design speed up to 50 km/h | With design speed above 50 km/h |
|---|---|---|
| Up to 4 kW | Moped: no licence, age 16 | Motorcycle: class A1, age 18 |
| Above 4 kW, up to 11 kW | Motorcycle: class A1, age 18 | Motorcycle: class A1, age 18 |
| Above 11 kW | Motorcycle: class A, age 18 | Motorcycle: class A, age 18 |

The table is a reading aid built from Articles 34, 57 and 59 of the law. It is not a substitute for the vehicle's registration paper, which carries the class the authorities have recorded.

## Licences issued before 2025

The 2025 reform changed the class boundaries. Under the transition rule in Article 89, a class A1 licence issued before 1 January 2025 may continue to be used for two-wheel motorcycles from 50 cm3 to under 175 cm3 or with an electric motor from 4 kW to under 14 kW, for the period shown on the card. An old class A2 licence continues for larger machines and for everything covered by old class A1. A resident with an older card should read the wording on it, and should not assume the 11 kW line applies. A visitor holding a foreign licence plus International Driving Permit is judged on the categories printed on the permit; our [motorbike licence guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbike-licence-rules-for-foreigners-in-vietnam/) explains the permit side.

## Numbers that look relevant but are not the motor rating

Several figures on a spec sheet are easily confused with motor power:

- **Battery capacity in kWh.** The Evo Grand sheet lists 2.4 kWh for its main battery and 1.2 kWh for the Lite. That is stored energy, similar to a fuel tank, not power. A larger battery gives range, not a different licence class.
- **Charger power.** The same sheet lists a 210 W charger for the Lite and a 400 W charger for the Evo Grand. It tells you charging speed, not how strong the motor is.
- **Voltage and ampere-hours.** These multiply to battery energy and say nothing about the class.
- **Range.** VinFast notes its range figures are measured at 30 km/h in its own test conditions, so they say nothing about the speed the bike can reach.
- **Marketing names.** "Lite", "Plus" or "Pro" do not fix the legal class.

## What to ask the shop

Use short, factual questions:

1. What is the bike's model and variant?
2. What are its design speed and its nominal and peak motor power?
3. What class is printed on the registration certificate?
4. Which licence does the shop require for this bike?
5. Can I see the manufacturer's specification?

## Speed limits on top of the class

Class also sets the maximum speed. Circular 38/2024/TT-BGTVT, in force since 1 January 2025, sets a maximum operating speed of 40 km/h for mopeds and similar vehicles on roads other than expressways. Motorcycles follow the general tables, which are lower in built-up areas. Our [speed limits guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/speed-limits-motorbikes-vietnam/) lists the figures, so there is no need to repeat them here. The practical point for renters is that a moped-class scooter capable of 48 km/h still has a 40 km/h legal ceiling.

## FAQ: power ratings and licences

### Is 4 kW a licence limit?

It is the moped limit for an electric motor, together with the 50 km/h design speed. Below both you need no licence for age 16 and over. Above either you are on a motorcycle.

### Is 11 kW the limit for class A1?

Yes. Article 57 of Law 36/2024/QH15 gives class A1 to two-wheel motorcycles with an electric motor of up to 11 kW, and class A above that.

### Which figure should I use, nominal or peak?

The passages quoted in the sources do not say. Ask the shop which figure the registration uses and ask to see the class printed there.

### Does a larger battery change the licence?

No. Battery capacity in kWh is stored energy. The class follows design speed and motor power.

### My old A1 licence says 175 cm3. Is that still valid?

Licences issued before 1 January 2025 continue for the period on the card under the transition rule, but the new class boundaries apply to licences issued or exchanged since. Read your own card.
