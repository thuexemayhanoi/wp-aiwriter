On Vietnamese roads, colour tells you who the line separates and the pattern tells you whether you may cross it. Yellow lines divide traffic travelling in opposite directions and white lines divide lanes travelling the same way. A dashed line may be crossed when it is safe and a solid line may not be crossed or ridden on. Where a double line has one solid and one dashed line, the side next to the dashed line may cross when necessary and the side next to the solid line may not. These are the meanings given in the national technical regulation on road signs, QCVN 41:2024/BGTVT, and Law 36/2024/QH15 defines a road marking as a line indicating the division of lanes, positions or directions, and stop positions.

This guide explains each common marking, how markings fit into the order of priority, what the law says about changing lanes, how kerb, crossing and hatched markings work, why a scooter rider should treat paint with respect in wet weather and what to do when lines are worn or missing. It is general information based on the texts as of October 2026, not legal advice.

## The two rules that cover most lines

Before you learn individual markings, learn two rules.

1. **Colour:** yellow separates opposing traffic, white separates traffic going the same way.

2. **Pattern:** a dashed line allows crossing when safe, a solid line does not.

Nearly every centre line and lane line on a Hanoi road can be understood with these two rules. The exceptions are the special markings for kerbs, crossings, stop lines and hatched areas, which we cover below. Our guide to [lane rules for motorbikes in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/lane-rules-motorbikes-vietnam/) explains where motorbikes belong on multi-lane roads, and this guide concentrates on reading the paint itself.

## Where markings sit in the order of priority

Article 11 of the Law lists road signs and signals in a fixed order: commands of traffic guards, traffic light signals, road signs, road markings and other signs on the road surface, and then delineator posts, barriers and similar devices, with audible signals last. Markings therefore rank below signs and lights. If a sign and a marking say different things at the same place, the sign governs. If a traffic guard directs you, the guard governs everything. Our guide to [understanding common motorbike road signs in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/understanding-common-motorbike-road-signs-in-vietnam/) explains the signs.

## Yellow lines: opposing traffic

Under the standard, the line that divides two directions of traffic, the centre line, is yellow. The common types are:

| Marking | What it looks like | What it means for you |
|---|---|---|
| Single dashed yellow | One broken yellow line | Divides opposing directions. May be crossed from both sides, for example to overtake when it is safe |
| Single solid yellow | One continuous yellow line | Do not cross or ride on it. Typically used where visibility for overtaking is poor and the risk of head-on collisions is high |
| Double solid yellow | Two continuous yellow lines | Do not cross or ride on it. Typically used on wider roads without a median |
| Solid and dashed yellow | One continuous and one broken line | Traffic on the side next to the dashed line may cross when necessary. Traffic on the side next to the solid line may not |

For the double line with one solid and one dashed line, the key is to look at which line is on your side. If the dashed line is next to you, you may cross when it is safe and necessary. If the solid line is next to you, you may not.

On a narrow road the standard also allows a single dashed line to divide traffic where there are not two full motor-vehicle lanes but many motorbikes. So a dashed yellow line on a narrow Hanoi street is still a centre line.

## White lines: lanes in the same direction

White lines divide lanes going the same way.

| Marking | What it means for you |
|---|---|
| Single dashed white | Divides lanes in the same direction. You may change lanes across it when it is safe |
| Single solid white | You may not change lanes or ride on the line, because lane changes are not permitted there |
| Solid and dashed white | The side next to the dashed line may cross when necessary, and the side next to the solid line may not |
| Solid white line around a reserved lane | The lane is reserved for a certain type of motor vehicle, and other vehicles may not enter except in emergencies |

The last item matters in a city with bus lanes. If a lane is marked and signed as reserved, a scooter is one of the "other vehicles". ## What the law says about changing lanes

Article 13 of Law 36/2024 sets the rules, and the markings are how you see them.

- **Keep to one lane.** On a road with several lanes in the same direction, separated by lane markings, you keep your vehicle in one lane and change only where it is allowed.

- **One lane at a time.** You may move only to the adjacent lane in a single movement.

- **Signal in advance.** Send out your signal before you move.

- **Check all sides.** Observe the surroundings to ensure safe distances with the vehicles in front, behind and on both sides before the change.

- **Reserved lanes.** If a lane is exclusively reserved for a type of vehicle, operators of other types may not enter it.

Article 15 adds that a change of direction should be signalled and made only when safety conditions are satisfied. Our guide to [crossing Hanoi intersections](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/crossing-hanoi-intersections/) shows how this looks in a busy junction.

A dashed line gives permission to cross. It does not give priority over a vehicle already in the other lane. Cross only when there is space, and not when someone is alongside you.

## Kerb markings: stopping and parking

Painted kerbs are common in Hanoi, and they matter to riders because they tell you where you may leave a bike. Under the standard:

- **A yellow dashed marking** on the kerb or at the edge of the road marks the side where parking is prohibited.

- **A yellow solid marking** on the kerb or at the edge of the road marks the side where stopping and parking are prohibited.

Parking and stopping are different things in the law. Article 18 defines stopping as a temporary halt long enough to pick up or drop off people or load and unload goods, and parking as stopping without time restriction. It also lists places where stopping and parking are banned altogether, including pedestrian crossings, intersections and within 5 metres of their edges, bridges and bends with obscured visibility, unless the place is designated for it. ## Crossings, stop lines and hatched areas

These markings cause the most confusion for visitors.

- **Pedestrian crossings.** Where there are road markings or other signs for pedestrians, or where pedestrians are crossing, the law requires you to observe, slow down or stop (Article 12). It also bans overtaking on pedestrian crossings (Article 14), and stopping or parking on them (Article 18). Never stop with your front wheel on the crossing.

- **Stop lines.** At a traffic light, the amber signal means stop behind the stop line, and the red signal means stop (Article 11). A rider already on or past the line when the amber appears may continue. Stop with the front wheel behind the line.

- **Hatched areas.** Areas of diagonal stripes, called channelising markings in the standard, guide traffic around an island or an obstacle. Treat them as space that is not a lane to ride in, unless a sign or the road layout clearly tells you otherwise.

- **Ring markings at junctions.** The standard describes a ring-shaped channelising marking at the centre of a crossing, which tells vehicles to go around it anticlockwise. Where you see one, do not cut across it.

## Overtaking and turning across solid lines

The meaning of a solid line limits two manoeuvres that riders are tempted to make.

- **Overtaking.** Article 14 allows overtaking only when there is no obstacle or oncoming traffic ahead, and bans it on bends with limited visibility, at crests of slopes, at intersections, on pedestrian crossings and in tunnels, among other places. A solid centre line often marks exactly those places, so it reinforces the ban.

- **Turning round.** Article 15 bans U-turns at pedestrian crossings, on bridges, on narrow roads, on bends with obscured visibility and in several other places, unless a traffic guard or temporary sign allows them. A solid centre line should be read as telling you not to cross it. Find a place where the turn is allowed, even if it costs a few minutes.

## Wet, worn and missing markings

Markings fade, get covered by dust or repainting and disappear in rain and after dark.

- **Wet paint can be slippery,** especially wide lines and crossing stripes. Brake early and in a straight line, and avoid leaning hard on painted surfaces.

- **At night and in rain,** lines can be hard to see, which is a reason to slow down. Our guides to [riding in Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) and [riding at night in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-at-night-in-hanoi/) explain the conditions.

- **Where lines are worn or missing,** fall back on the general rules in Article 10: move on the right side in your direction, follow the designated lanes and road sections and obey signs and signals.

- **Where markings and cones or temporary signs disagree,** follow the temporary signs. The law says a temporary sign beats a fixed one in the same place.

## What a missed marking can cost

Not obeying road markings is an offence. Under Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, as read on the Government portal, the general offence of not obeying signs or road markings carries a fine of VND 200,000 to 400,000 for motorbike riders, and changing lanes without a signal or where it is not allowed carries the same range. Other offences, such as turning where it is prohibited, are priced separately. Camera notices can reach the vehicle's owner, so ask the shop how it handles a notice. ## A routine for reading lines

1. **Look up the road,** and read the lines before you reach them.

2. **Identify the colour,** yellow for opposing traffic, white for the same direction.

3. **Identify the pattern,** dashed or solid, and which line is on your side if there are two.

4. **Check the signs,** because signs outrank lines.

5. **Signal and check all sides** before any lane change.

6. **Slow down on painted areas in rain.**

7. **When in doubt, stay in your lane** and keep right.

## A quick checklist

1. **Yellow means opposing traffic, white means the same direction.**

2. **Dashed may be crossed when safe, solid may not.**

3. **With a double line, check which line is next to you.**

4. **Reserved lanes are closed to other vehicles.**

5. **Never stop on a pedestrian crossing or beyond the stop line.**

6. **Treat wet paint with care.**

7. **Signs and traffic guards outrank lines.**

## FAQ: lane markings for motorbike riders in Hanoi

### What does a solid yellow line mean?

It divides opposing directions of traffic, and you may not cross it or ride on it.

### Can I cross a dashed white line?

Yes, to change lanes when it is safe, after signalling and checking all sides.

### What if a double line has one solid and one dashed line?

Look at which is next to you. If the dashed line is on your side, you may cross when necessary. If the solid line is on your side, you may not.

### What do yellow markings on the kerb mean?

Under the standard, a yellow dashed marking means parking is prohibited on that side, and a yellow solid marking means stopping and parking are prohibited.

### Do markings outrank signs?

No. Traffic guards, traffic lights and signs all rank above road markings.

### What if the markings are worn out?

Follow the general rules: keep right, stay in your lane, obey signs and signals and slow down.
