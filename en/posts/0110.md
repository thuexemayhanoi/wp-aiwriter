For a short Hanoi rental of one to three days, the Honda Vision is the easier choice if you want the lightest, simplest scooter for city streets, and the Honda Click is the better choice if you want a stronger liquid-cooled 125 cc engine, a slightly bigger frame and a little more fuel range for a day out of town. On Honda's published figures the Vision weighs about 93 to 97 kg against roughly 113 kg for the 125 cc scooter that Honda Vietnam sells under the Vario name, which is the same family as the Click. Both are automatics with a twist-and-go throttle, both have 14-inch wheels and both have a seat at about the same height. This guide compares them for the way short renters actually use a bike, and ends with questions to ask before you ride away.

## First, which bike is the "Click"?

The naming needs a moment, because it affects what you will actually be handed. Honda's scooter family known as the Click in Thailand and the Philippines is sold as the Vario in Indonesia, Malaysia and Vietnam. Honda Vietnam's current range lists the Vario 125 and Vario 160, not a Click. A rental shop that offers a "Honda Click" may therefore have a Vario, an imported Click or an older unit with that badge.

Nguyen Ha lists the Honda Click and the Honda Vision among its automatic scooters, and also says the bike you receive is confirmed at booking. So the sensible habit is to ask three things when you reserve: the exact model name on the body, the engine size (110, 125 or 160 cc) and the year. The specifications below use Honda Vietnam's Vario 125 page as the reference for the Click family and its Vision page for the Vision, and they will differ slightly from an older bike.

The fuel figures are Honda's laboratory-style numbers, so real use in Hanoi stop-and-go traffic will be higher. Still, simple arithmetic on them gives a feel for range: about 260 km on paper for the Vision's 4.8 litres and about 255 km for the Vario's 5.5 litres. The Vision's smaller consumption cancels the bigger tank, so neither bike has a decisive range advantage. For a short rental you will refuel once at most.

## Weight: the difference you feel in the first minute

The weight gap of roughly 16 to 20 kg is the first thing most people notice, and it shows up mainly when the bike is not moving. Pulling the scooter off its stand, backing it out of a row of parked bikes, turning it round in an alley or holding it upright at a red light with a passenger all feel easier on a Vision.

At speed the picture reverses a little. The heavier Vario feels steadier over rough patches and crosswinds on a bridge, while the Vision can feel lighter and more nervous. If you are a new rider, or a smaller one, that parking-lot ease usually matters more across a three-day stay than the steadiness at 50 km/h. If you are an experienced rider who will mostly be moving, the difference shrinks.

## Seat, legroom and posture

The two seat heights are close, 761 to 785 mm for the Vision versions and 769 mm for the Vario, so the choice will not hinge on reaching the ground. Both have the flat-ish floor typical of an automatic scooter. What differs is the feel of the frame: the Vario is longer in the wheelbase (1,280 mm against about 1,255 mm on the standard Vision) and a little roomier for a taller rider, while the Vision's compact frame suits shorter riders who want to plant both feet at a stop.

A quick test at the shop settles it better than any table. Sit on the bike, put both feet down, and check that you can hold it upright without stretching. Then check that your knees are not jammed against the front panel and that you can see both mirrors clearly. If either bike fails this test for you, ask for the other.

## Storage for a day out

Short renters usually carry the same things: a phone charger, a rain jacket, a small bag and sometimes a second helmet if they are not wearing the free one. Both bikes have a lockable under-seat compartment and both fit a half-head helmet plus a few small items, which is about what Honda says for them. Neither is a luggage scooter. If you need a full backpack out of sight, the larger Lead is covered in our [two-rider and luggage comparison](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/scooter-for-two-riders-and-luggage-hanoi/), and the [Vision and Air Blade comparison](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/honda-vision-vs-air-blade-which-scooter-to-rent-in-hanoi/) shows what the mid-size option adds.

Current Vision versions also add a covered front compartment with a USB-C port, which is handy for a phone. Whether the bike you are given has it depends on its age, so ask rather than assume.

## Engine and the roads you plan to ride

The Vision's air-cooled 110 cc engine is tuned for economy and smooth city running. In central Hanoi, where speeds are mostly 20 to 40 km/h, it has all the power you need. It works harder with two adults aboard, on long bridge ramps and on the faster roads in the outskirts.

The 125 cc liquid-cooled engine in the Vario has more in reserve. It pulls away from junctions more briskly, copes better with a passenger and is less strained on the climb to a flyover. If your short rental includes a day trip such as [Ninh Binh](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-to-ninh-binh-by-motorbike/) or [Tam Dao](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-to-tam-dao-by-motorbike/), that reserve is a real comfort. For a rental that stays inside the ring roads, it is a luxury rather than a need.

Both engines fall within the 125 cc class. If a 160 cc version of a scooter is offered to you, read our explanation of the [licence line between 125 and 160 cc](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/honda-air-blade-160-vs-125/) first.

## Fuel, handover and return on a short rental

The rules around fuel matter more on a short rental, because there is less time to sort out a mistake. Nguyen Ha hands petrol bikes over with about 0.5 to 2 litres and expects them back with about 0.5 to 1 litre. An empty tank at return carries a fee of VND 20,000. Neither scooter has a large tank, so on a day trip fill up early rather than waiting for the last bar. Our guide to [fuel in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/fuel-in-hanoi-petrol-stations-rental-scooter/) explains how stations work and which pump to use.

Remember, too, that a rental day is 24 hours and shorter use is charged as a full day, so a short rental is about fitting your plan into one or two full days. Returning early does not refund unused rent. The [returning your rental motorbike guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/returning-your-rental-motorbike-hanoi/) walks through the return routine so no surprises arrive on the last evening.

## Smart Key, steering lock and the small stuff

Honda Vietnam describes Smart Key on current Vision and Vario models: a fob that lets you unlock and start the bike without inserting a key, and that helps you find the bike. It is convenient, but on a short stay it creates two habits to learn:

- Keep the fob in a zipped pocket, not loose in a bag where it can fall out.
- Ask the staff to show you how to lock the steering and how to open the seat, because the sequence differs from a normal key.
- Ask what happens if the fob is lost. Our guide to [lost keys, flat tyres and breakdowns](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-bike-breakdown-hanoi-what-to-do/) covers the usual process.

Not every rental bike has Smart Key, and older Click or Vision bikes use a conventional key. Either is fine. The point is to know which you have before you leave.

## Three short-rental plans and the likely pick

**One day, central Hanoi only.** Old Quarter, Hoan Kiem, West Lake and a cafe stop. The Vision is the natural choice: lighter to park, ample power at these speeds and simple to learn. Read about [riding in the Old Quarter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-old-quarter/) before you go, because on some streets walking is faster.

**A weekend with a longer loop.** One city day and one out-of-town day. The Click family's 125 cc engine and heavier feel are worth having on the second day. If the loop is short, the Vision still works fine.

**Three days with a passenger.** Weight and engine reserve matter more. Choose the 125 cc bike, or consider the Lead for the extra luggage space, and ask the shop to check tyre pressure for two people.

## FAQ: Click or Vision for a short rental

### Is the Honda Click the same as the Honda Vario?

Honda sells the same scooter family under the Click name in Thailand and the Philippines and as the Vario in Vietnam, Indonesia and Malaysia. A bike called a Click on a Hanoi rental list may be a Vario or an older Click, so ask the shop for the exact model.

### Which is easier for a first-time rider?

Usually the Vision, because it is lighter to move, park and hold upright at a stop. The Click family is also an automatic and is not hard to ride, so the difference is smaller than the table suggests once you are moving.

### Which is better for two people?

The 125 cc Click family has more engine reserve and a slightly longer frame. The Vision carries two adults at city speeds but works harder on ramps.

### Do I need a different licence for the Click?

A 125 cc engine falls in the same class as the Vision's 110 cc. A 160 cc version would not, so check the engine size before you accept a bike.

### Can I choose the exact model online?

Not reliably. The bike actually available is confirmed at booking, so ask the shop on the [contact page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/contact/) or by phone.
