If the helmet you are given does not fit, say so at the counter before you leave and ask for a different size or a different helmet. Do not ride away with one that moves on your head, pinches or will not fasten properly. A helmet that does not fit is a problem for two reasons. It protects you less in a fall, because a loose helmet can shift or come off and a tight one can cause pain that distracts you. And in Vietnam it is also a legal matter, because the law requires a helmet that meets the national standard and is fastened correctly. Nguyen Ha includes one basic helmet per rider free of charge, but the rental price does not include a perfect fit, so it is up to you to check. This guide sets out a one-minute fit test, the signs that a helmet is wrong, what to say to the shop, the options if no size fits, special cases such as glasses and children and the rules and fines that apply as of October 2026.

## Why a poor fit matters

A helmet only works if it stays on your head and stays in the right place. Honda Vietnam's safe-riding training document gives a short list of checks for choosing a helmet: it should fit when turned back and forth on your head, its standard labels should be complete, the strap should be strong, two fingers should just fit between chin and strap, the shell should be smooth and hard, the foam liner should not sink when pressed and a helmet that has broken or taken a heavy blow should not be used.

Most of those checks can be done in the shop in a couple of minutes. The first and the fourth are about fit. If the helmet slides when you turn it, or if you cannot get the strap to the right tightness, the rest of the list does not help you.

Our guide to [choosing a helmet that fits for a manual motorbike journey](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/choosing-a-helmet-that-fits-for-a-manual-motorbike-journey/) goes through the full test, and our guide to [moped helmets and why fit matters at lower speeds too](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/moped-helmets-fit-matters-at-lower-speeds-too/) explains why a slower bike does not make the problem smaller.

## A one-minute fit test at the counter

Do this standing up, with the helmet in your hands and a staff member nearby.

1. **Put it on without fastening it.** It should go on with a little resistance, not drop on loosely. If you have to force it over your ears, it is probably too small.

2. **Check where it sits.** The front edge should be level and roughly a finger's width above your eyebrows. If it tilts back and exposes your forehead, or tilts down over your eyes, it is the wrong size or shape.

3. **Fasten the strap,** and use the two-finger check from Honda's guidance: two fingers should just fit between your chin and the strap.

4. **Nod and shake your head.** The helmet should move with you.

5. **Hold the helmet and try to turn it** left and right on your head. Your skin should move with it. If the helmet slides over your skin, it is too big.

6. **Push up from the back of the rim.** The helmet should not roll forward off your head.

7. **Wait a minute.** Wear it while you handle the paperwork. A helmet that feels fine for ten seconds can start to press on your forehead or temples after a minute.

None of these steps needs tools or any knowledge.

## Signs that the helmet is wrong

| What you notice | What it usually means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| It slides over your head when you shake it | Too big | Ask for a smaller size |
| You can twist it without your skin moving | Too big | Ask for a smaller size |
| It tips back and shows your forehead | Too big or the wrong shape | Ask for another size or model |
| It presses hard on the sides or forehead | Too small | Ask for a larger size |
| It will not go over your ears | Too small | Ask for a larger size |
| You get a pressure point within a minute | Wrong shape | Try a different model |
| The strap cannot be fastened or adjusted | Damaged or wrong strap | Ask for another helmet |
| The liner is flattened, torn or smells | Worn or unclean | Ask for another helmet |
| The shell is cracked, or labels are missing | Not fit to use | Ask for another helmet |

If a helmet passes the fit test but has a cracked shell, a damaged strap or missing labels, it is not a good helmet to use whatever the size.

## What to say to the shop

You do not need to complain. Say plainly what you notice and what you would like. For example:

- "This one is a little loose. Could I try a smaller size?"

- "This one presses on my forehead. Do you have a bigger one?"

- "The strap will not fasten properly. Could I have another?"

- "My passenger's helmet is too big. Could we try another?"

Most shops keep helmets in several sizes and would rather swap one than see you ride with a bad fit. If the first helmet you try is wrong, try two or three. ## If no helmet in the shop fits

Sometimes none of the shop's helmets suit your head. You have options, and none of them is to ride without one.

- **Wait and try again.** Ask whether other sizes are kept elsewhere, or whether another helmet can be brought.

- **Bring or buy your own.** A helmet you choose and try on yourself will fit better. Choose one that meets the national standard and carries the conformity mark, as described below. Do not assume the shop will refund or reimburse a helmet you buy.

- **Adjust what can be adjusted.** Strap length and, on some helmets, internal pads can be changed. Ask staff before altering anything, and do not cut or modify a helmet.

- **Choose a different time or a different shop** if the helmet issue cannot be solved. A rental that starts with an unsafe helmet is not worth it.

Whatever you decide, do not leave the shop wearing a helmet that you know does not fit.

## Special cases

**Glasses.** Wear them during the fit test. A helmet that squeezes the arms of your glasses against your head will become painful.

**Thick hair, a bun or a ponytail.** Try the helmet with your hair in the style you will ride with. A bun can push the helmet forward or make the fit looser.

**Headwear.** If you wear a religious or cultural head covering, bring it to the fitting and try the helmet over it. Tell the shop in advance if you need a larger size.

**Children.** The law applies to children too. A child carried on a motorbike must wear a helmet that meets the standard, fastened properly. Rental shops may not stock child sizes, so ask before booking. Our guide to [helmet rules in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/helmet-rules-in-vietnam/) and [child passengers on a motorbike in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/child-passengers-motorbike-vietnam/) explain the rules.

**Hygiene.** Ask for a clean helmet. If the liner is dirty, damp or smells, ask for another. Do not wear a cap or cloth under it unless it does not change the fit.

## What the law requires

As of October 2026, Article 33 of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (Law No. 36/2024/QH15), in force since 1 January 2025, requires the rider and every person carried on a two-wheeled motorbike or moped to wear a helmet that meets the national technical standard, with the chin strap fastened properly. The national standard is QCVN 2:2021/BKHCN, and a compliant helmet carries the CR conformity mark.

Under Article 7 of Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, which we read on the Government portal, a rider who wears no helmet, or wears one without fastening the strap properly, is fined VND 400,000–600,000. A rider who carries a passenger without a helmet, or with the strap unfastened, is fined the same amount. Decree 238/2026/ND-CP, which amends the decree from 15 August 2026, does not change Article 7.

A helmet that does not fit may not fasten properly, and a thin "fashion" helmet without the conformity mark is treated as no helmet. So the fit problem is also a legal one. This is general information, not legal advice.

## Record the helmet at pickup

Once you have a helmet that fits, take a minute to record it.

- **Photograph the helmet,** outside and inside, including any marks, cracks or scuffs.

- **Note the helmet on the contract** if the form lists equipment. Our guide to [how to document existing scratches on a rental bike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-document-existing-scratches-on-a-rental-bike/) explains the three-photo method, and it works for helmets too.

- **Check the label** for the conformity mark.

- **Bring it back** at the end. Our guide to [returning your rental motorbike in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/returning-your-rental-motorbike-hanoi/) lists the items that are handed back.

## A quick checklist

- **Try it on at the counter, not later.**

- **Run the one-minute test,** including the two-finger strap check.

- **Wear it for a minute** while you do the paperwork.

- **Ask for another size** if it is loose, tight or painful.

- **Check the shell, liner, strap and label.**

- **Try it with glasses, hair and any headwear.**

- **Do not leave without a helmet that fits.**

- **Photograph it** and note it on the contract.

## FAQ: a helmet that does not fit at pickup

### Is a helmet included in the rental?

Nguyen Ha's published terms include one basic helmet per rider, free. Ask in advance about sizes and about a passenger's helmet.

### What if the helmet is too loose?

Ask for a smaller size. A helmet that moves on your head, or that you can twist without your skin moving, is too big.

### Can I ride with a helmet that does not fit if the strap is fastened?

No. A poorly fitting helmet protects you less, and the rules require a helmet that meets the standard and is fastened properly.

### What is the fine for no helmet or an unfastened strap?

As of October 2026, VND 400,000–600,000 under Article 7 of Decree 168/2024/ND-CP.

### Can I bring my own helmet?

Yes. Choose one that meets the national standard and carries the CR conformity mark.

### What if the shop has no child helmet?

Ask before booking. A child must wear a helmet that meets the standard, and a shop may not stock one.
