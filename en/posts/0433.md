To plan a rental pickup from Gia Lam bus station, remember that Nguyen Ha's shop is near the bus station but not at it: it is at Alley 5, Nguyen Van Cu Street, Bo De Ward, Long Bien District, and there is no separate pickup counter in Gia Lam. Message the shop before you travel to confirm the bike, the price for your dates and the deposit, time your arrival inside its opening hours of 09:00 to 21:00, save the exact map pin, get from the station to the alley by taxi or ride-hailing app unless the shop tells you it is walkable and arrive with your documents, cash for the deposit and a plan for your luggage.

This guide sets out what the shop publishes about its location, how to fit a bus arrival to its hours, how to get from the station to the shop, what to do with bags, what to carry, how to handle the first ride out and what to do if something goes wrong on arrival. It is based on the shop's own pages, which give no walking distance from the station, so we do not invent one.

## What the shop publishes

These are the facts on Nguyen Ha's own contact and Gia Lam pages as of October 2026.

- **Address:** Ngo 5 Nguyen Van Cu, Bo De Ward, Long Bien District, Hanoi, which in English is Alley 5, Nguyen Van Cu Street.

- **Landmark:** close to the bus station named Gia Lam.

- **No separate counter:** the shop's Gia Lam page says daily renters should come to the shop for paperwork, inspection and collection, and that there is no separately advertised pickup counter in Gia Lam. It also says not to confuse Gia Lam bus station with a Nguyen Ha rental branch in Gia Lam.

- **Hours:** 09:00 to 21:00, every day.

- **From the Old Quarter:** across the Red River by the Chuong Duong bridge, about ten to twenty minutes by taxi or motorbike depending on traffic, according to the contact page.

- **Before you travel:** get in touch so the shop can confirm the bike you want, the price for your dates and the deposit.

- **Contact:** phone and Zalo 0334 699 969, WhatsApp +84 334 699 969.

- **Delivery:** discussed only for eligible weekly or monthly hires, and whether a Gia Lam address qualifies depends on its location and the shop's published policy.

The pages do not give a distance or walking time from the bus station, or a description of the alley entrance, so use the map pin and ask the shop for directions on the day. Our guide to [Gia Lam and the east bank](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/gia-lam-east-bank-bat-trang-by-scooter/) describes the area, and the shop's [Gia Lam page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/gia-lam/) gives its own account.

## Why "near the bus station" is not "at the bus station"

A visitor arriving by bus often assumes that a business named after a landmark is at it. Here that is not so. The bus station is a landmark that helps you find the area, and the shop is in a side alley off Nguyen Van Cu Street. If you walk into the station looking for a rental counter, you will not find a Nguyen Ha desk. Our guide to [how to check the rental shop's opening hours and pickup location](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-check-the-rental-shops-opening-hours-and-pickup-location/) explains how to read a Hanoi alley address, and why you should copy the full line, including Hanoi and Long Bien, into your map app.

## Step 1: confirm before you travel

Message or call the shop before you set off, ideally a day or more ahead.

1. **Confirm the bike** you want, or the class, and that it is available on your dates. The bike you actually get is confirmed at booking.

2. **Confirm the price** for your dates, and any extras. The published reference rates for a manual bike are VND 150,000–200,000 per day, and the final price is confirmed on the actual bike.

3. **Confirm the deposit,** which is cash, VND 2,000,000–5,000,000 depending on the bike and contract.

4. **Confirm the pickup time,** and that the shop will be open when you arrive.

5. **Send your arrival details,** such as the bus company and the expected arrival time, so staff can expect you.

## Step 2: fit the bus arrival to the shop's hours

Buses do not always arrive on time, and the shop is open only from 09:00 to 21:00. Plan with a margin.

| Planned bus arrival | What to do |
|---|---|
| Before 09:00 | Have breakfast and rest near the station, then go to the shop after 09:00 |
| Mid-morning to afternoon | Go straight to the shop, after a call or message |
| Late afternoon or evening | Allow for delays; aim to reach the shop well before 21:00 |
| Near or after 21:00 | The shop will be closed. Plan the night, and collect in the morning |

The published terms do not offer an out-of-hours handover, so do not assume one exists. If a late arrival is likely, tell the shop in advance and ask whether anything can be arranged, and get the answer in writing. Our guide to [what to do if the rental shop is closed when you arrive](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-to-do-if-the-rental-shop-is-closed-when-you-arrive/) explains the fallback. Our guide to [how a 24-hour rental day is counted in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-a-24-hour-rental-day-is-counted-in-hanoi/) explains how the clock starts at handover, so a late pickup shifts your return time.

If you arrive tired after a long journey, consider collecting the bike the next morning, after you have rested. A first ride in Hanoi traffic after a night on a bus is not a good idea.

## Step 3: get from the station to the shop

The shop's pages do not say how far it is from the station or whether it is walkable, so do not assume. Choose a method that suits your bags and the time of day.

- **Ride-hailing app or taxi.** The simplest option with luggage. Enter the full address, copy the pin and show the driver the alley name. Ask the driver to stop at the entrance to the alley.

- **Walking,** only if the shop confirms it is a short and safe walk for you, and you have little luggage. Check the route on your map, and avoid walking along a busy road with heavy bags.

- **A short ride on a motorbike taxi** is possible in Hanoi, but with luggage it is less comfortable, and you should agree the fare first.

Whichever you choose, send your location to the shop as you set off, and message again when you are close. Do not follow anyone who offers to "take you to the shop" unless you have confirmed it with the shop's own number.

## Step 4: your luggage

Think about your bags before you reach the shop, because a bus passenger often has more luggage than a small bike can take.

- **A small manual bike carries little.** Honda's manuals limit the storage compartment to 5 kg and the total load to 115 kg or 155 kg, depending on the model. Our guides to [how much luggage you can safely carry on a Honda Wave](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-much-luggage-can-you-safely-carry-on-a-honda-wave/) and [Honda Wave storage options when renting without a top box](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/honda-wave-storage-options-when-renting-without-a-top-box/) explain the limits.

- **Leave big bags at your hotel,** and take the bike with a light pack. Arrange the hotel first, then collect the bike.

- **Ask the shop** whether you can leave a bag while you ride, and for how long. The published terms do not say that the shop stores luggage, so do not assume it does.

- **Do not leave valuables** on a parked bike.

If you have a lot of luggage and a long onward trip, a larger scooter with more storage, or a different plan, may suit you better. Our guide to [two riders and luggage](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/scooter-for-two-riders-and-luggage-hanoi/) compares the options.

## Step 5: bring the right things

- **Your passport or identity card.** The shop checks and photographs it, and hands it back.

- **Your driving licence and IDP,** matching the class of bike. Our guide to [what identity documents a rental shop may ask to see](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-identity-documents-a-rental-shop-may-ask-to-see/) explains why.

- **Cash for the deposit and the rent,** as agreed.

- **A charged phone,** with the shop's number saved and offline map of the area.

- **A helmet if you prefer your own.** One basic helmet per rider is included free.

- **A copy of your hotel address,** for the return trip.

Do not leave your passport as a deposit. Our guide to [how to keep your passport safe during a scooter rental](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-keep-your-passport-safe-during-a-scooter-rental/) explains why.

## Step 6: the handover

When you reach the shop, allow time for the handover. Do the inspection in daylight if you can, and do not rush because you are tired. Walk round the bike with staff, check both brakes, the tyres, lights and mirrors, photograph existing marks and note the fuel level, as our [10-minute inspection checklist](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-scooter-inspection-checklist/), our guide to [how to document existing scratches on a rental bike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-document-existing-scratches-on-a-rental-bike/) and our guide to [how to record the fuel level at handover](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-record-the-fuel-level-at-handover/) explain. Check the helmet fits, as our guide to [what to do if a helmet does not fit at pickup](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-to-do-if-a-helmet-does-not-fit-at-pickup/) describes. Note the return deadline and keep the shop's number.

## Step 7: the first ride out

If you are new to Hanoi traffic or to a gear bike, start gently. Spend the first few minutes in the quiet lanes near the shop before you join a busier road. Our guide to [a first-day practice route for an experienced manual-bike rider](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/a-first-day-practice-route-for-an-experienced-manual-bike-rider/) gives a staged plan, and our guide to [Long Bien and the Red River as an easy first ride](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/long-bien-red-river-first-ride/) describes a gentle first outing. ## If something goes wrong

- **You cannot find the alley:** call the shop and send your pin and a photograph of where you are.

- **The shop looks closed:** follow the steps in our guide to what to do if the rental shop is closed when you arrive.

- **The bus is delayed:** message the shop with the new time, and be ready to collect the next morning if you will arrive after 21:00.

- **The bike is not what was agreed:** ask the shop to explain, and do not accept a bike you are unhappy with. Our guide to [how to confirm the exact model you will receive](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-confirm-the-exact-model-you-will-receive/) explains how to check.
