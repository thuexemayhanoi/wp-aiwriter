The safe way to downshift for a corner on a small motorbike is to do it before the bend, while the bike is upright and moving in a straight line, and to finish the change before you turn in. Slow down with the throttle and the brakes first, change to the gear you want for the exit, and then roll on the throttle gently as you come out. Changing gear in the middle of a bend is what causes the lurches, the rear-wheel chatter and the occasional fall. A Honda Wave or Yamaha Sirius is forgiving, but it has little power in reserve, so being in the right gear at the exit matters. This guide sets out Honda's cornering sequence, shows how to match the gear to the kind of corner you meet in Hanoi, explains what goes wrong when a change is made too late and covers the junction rules that sit alongside the technique. It is general riding advice, not a substitute for supervised training.

## Why the gear is chosen before the bend

A bend asks the bike to do three things in a short space: slow down, turn and then accelerate. If the gear is wrong, the third part fails. A bike in third gear at a walking-pace corner has no pull on the exit, and a rider who hits the throttle finds the engine labouring and the bike hesitating. A bike in first gear at a faster bend revs loudly and the engine braking is harsh.

Changing down in the bend itself adds a second job at the moment when the bike needs your full attention. The back wheel can momentarily slow with the change, the lever movement can jolt the foot position, and a rider who is leaned over has less margin. For that reason, the principle is simple: decide the gear before the turn.

## The sequence Honda teaches for bends

Honda Vietnam's safe-riding training document, dated August 2026, sets out a cornering technique. It says to reduce speed before the bend and to limit braking in the bend itself. Its steps are:

1. Reduce speed gradually by easing the throttle and braking before the bend begins.
2. Check ahead and behind.
3. Sound the horn if necessary to warn other road users.
4. Keep to the right side of your lane.
5. Keep your posture, with your head level with the road so you can see where you are going.
6. Increase the throttle gently once the bend ends.

The document also illustrates what goes wrong when a rider ignores the sequence: the bike becomes unbalanced, the wheel can slide because of heavy braking, and the bike can drift out of its line or across into the oncoming lane. The downshift belongs in step 1, as part of reducing speed.

## The gear change itself

Honda's Wave RSX FI owner's manual says to always reduce the throttle before changing gear and to press the shift lever gently with the toe until it moves. It also warns against needless gear changes and against resting a foot on the shift lever, which can damage the shifting and clutch mechanisms. For a corner, that gives a simple routine:

- **Ease the throttle** and begin braking early, while the bike is straight.
- **Tap down one gear** when the speed is low enough for the lower gear.
- **Let the engine settle** and finish any braking.
- **Look through the bend** and keep the throttle neutral or very light.
- **Roll on the throttle** as the bike straightens out.

If you need two gears, do the changes in the straight part of the approach, with a moment between them. Do not change down twice in the bend.

## Matching the gear to the corner

The right gear depends on the speed you will carry and the pull you need on the exit. These are general guides for a 110 cc bike, not rules.

**A sweeping bend at moderate speed.** Often second or third gear, chosen so that the engine is comfortable and you can roll on the throttle on the exit without changing again.

**A tight junction turn at walking pace.** First or second gear. Slow right down first, with both brakes, then change down, then turn with a light, steady throttle.

**A hairpin on a climb.** First or second gear, selected on the approach. On a climb the bike will lose speed in the bend, so being in a low gear before you turn in keeps the pull. Our guide to [shifting gears on hills without straining a rental motorbike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/shifting-gears-on-hills-without-straining-a-rental-motorbike/) explains the climbing side.

**A downhill bend.** Change down before the bend so engine braking helps and you do not have to use the brakes in the turn. Our guide to [engine braking on a semi-automatic motorcycle](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/engine-braking-on-a-semi-automatic-motorcycle/) explains how to use it.

**A bend you cannot see round.** Treat it as slower than you think, take a lower gear early and keep to the right.

## What goes wrong when the change is late

**The rear wheel chatters or locks.** A downshift made at too high a speed lets the engine slow the rear wheel suddenly. On a wet or dusty surface the wheel can skid.

**The bike lurches in the bend.** Releasing and re-applying the drive while leaned over unsettles the bike.

**The rider looks at the foot or the lever,** not at the road, at the moment when vision matters most.

**The engine over-revs.** A change down at too high a speed makes the engine scream. Slow down first.

**The rider brakes hard in the bend.** Honda's document specifically shows that heavy braking in a bend can make the wheel slide and the bike lose its line.

If you find yourself at the bend in the wrong gear, do not change. Hold the throttle steady, keep to the right, make the turn and change down on the next straight.

## Right-of-way rules at junction corners

In the city, most of your corners are junction turns, and the law sets right-of-way rules that sit alongside the technique. Under Article 7 of Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, which we read on the Government portal, a rider who fails to slow down and give way to vehicles coming from the right at a junction without roundabout signs, or to vehicles coming from the left at a roundabout, is fined VND 2,000,000–3,000,000, and two licence points are deducted from a Vietnamese licence. The same article fines a rider who turns without checking, without a signal or without keeping the signal on throughout the turn VND 600,000–800,000. Decree 238/2026/ND-CP, which amends the decree from 15 August 2026, does not change Article 7. For turn rules in general, see our guide to [lane rules for motorbikes in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/lane-rules-motorbikes-vietnam/), and for junction behaviour see our guide to [crossing Hanoi intersections](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/crossing-hanoi-intersections/).

The practical point is that slowing early and changing down before the junction gives you time to look, signal and give way, which is what the rules require.

## Wet and slippery corners

On a wet road, the grip available for turning and braking is much lower. Honda's Wave manual notes that wet surfaces are slippery and that wet brakes lose effect, and suggests applying the brakes lightly at low speed to dry them. In the rain, slow down earlier, change down gently and avoid braking in the bend. Painted lines, metal covers and oily patches at junctions are the worst places to lean the bike. ## With a passenger or luggage

A passenger and luggage make the bike heavier and slower to respond. Honda's Wave manuals list a maximum load of 115 kg or 155 kg depending on version, and warn that overloading affects handling and braking. Enter bends slower than you would alone, change down earlier and keep the throttle smooth so the passenger does not lurch. ## A practice drill

Find a quiet, wide, legal area with a gentle bend or a large empty loop. Ride towards the bend in third gear at a modest speed. Ease the throttle, brake lightly, change down to second, then ride through the bend with a steady throttle and roll on as you exit. Repeat, then try a tighter bend with first gear. Add speed only when the sequence feels automatic. ## FAQ: downshifting before corners

### When should I change down for a corner?

Before the bend, while the bike is upright and moving in a straight line, after you have slowed with the throttle and brakes.

### Can I change gear in the middle of a bend?

Avoid it. It unsettles the bike at the moment when you need all your attention on the road.

### How many gears should I drop at once?

One at a time, with a moment between changes. If you need to lose a lot of speed, use the brakes.

### What if I am in the wrong gear at the bend?

Hold the throttle steady, keep to the right, take the bend and change down on the next straight.

### Should I brake in the bend?

Honda's training advice is to reduce speed before the bend and limit braking in it.

### What are the right-of-way rules at junctions?

At a junction without roundabout signs give way to vehicles from the right, and at a roundabout give way to vehicles from the left. Failing to slow and give way is fined VND 2,000,000–3,000,000 as of October 2026.
