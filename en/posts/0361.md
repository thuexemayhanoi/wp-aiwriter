Van Long Nature Reserve is the quiet alternative to Ninh Binh's busier boat trips, and it suits a rider who would rather watch water birds and limestone cliffs than queue at a pier. It lies in Gia Vien District, roughly 80 km from Hanoi by the national tourism authority's figure, and a scooter or a semi-automatic bike can reach it on the same national road that serves Tam Coc and Trang An. What you do there is slow: a small boat drifts through a wetland, past caves and cliffs, with a chance to see Delacour's langur, one of the world's rarest primates. Sightings are never guaranteed. This guide explains what the reserve is, how to reach it, how to time the visit and how to behave around wildlife. Information is general planning guidance as of October 2026.

## What Van Long is and why people go

A national tourism article describes Van Long as the largest wetland nature reserve in the northern delta, about 3,500 hectares in the wider area, opened to tourism in 1998 and considered a land counterpart to Ha Long Bay. It describes 33 caves of which only some are open to visitors, and bamboo basket boats that carry you to see the langur at Ca Cave and the mountain reflections at Bong Cave.

A visitor guide adds a point of history: the wetland was not an ancient lake but formed after a long dyke was built in 1960, and it was established as a nature reserve in 2001. It is gazetted under Vietnam's biodiversity regulations, and it became Vietnam's ninth Ramsar site in 2018, per a document on UNESCO's tentative list. That document also says the IUCN Green List recognises it. The calm water, the reflected cliffs and the birdlife are the draw.

## The langurs, and what to realistically expect

UNESCO's tentative-list document says Van Long hosts the world's largest viable population of Delacour's langur, with a survey count of 176 to 184 individuals in 21 groups (a 2019 study cited there), following a reintroduction programme that began in 2011. The same document also cites a later drone-based estimate, so treat the exact number as approximate.

What this means for a visitor:

- The langurs live on limestone cliffs and are not in a zoo. Some visitors see them, some do not.
- Early morning and late afternoon are the times visitor guides suggest for wildlife and storks returning to their nests.
- Binoculars or a phone with a good zoom help far more than moving closer.

Do not promise yourself a sighting. A calm boat trip with water birds and cliffs is a good outcome by itself.

## The ride from Hanoi

The route is the same national road described in our guide to [riding from Hanoi to Ninh Binh](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-to-ninh-binh-by-motorbike/). Motorbikes are not permitted on expressways under Article 25 of Law 36/2024/QH15 (in force from 1 January 2025), so ride Highway 1A, not the expressway. A route preview in a map app will show if it tries to send you the wrong way; our note on [checking a navigation app for expressway suggestions](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-check-if-a-navigation-app-suggests-an-expressway/) explains how.

Published distances are about 80 km from Hanoi (Vietnam National Authority of Tourism) and roughly 17 km from Ninh Binh city centre (visitor guides). One guide names the Gian Khau crossroad as the turn-off, with about 5 km to Van Long market. We have not ridden it ourselves, so confirm the turn in a map app as you approach and follow the local signs.

Because Van Long is closer to Hanoi than Tam Coc, it can suit riders who want a shorter day. The official figure is for the distance, not the time, so plan your own time using a map app and leave a margin.

## On the water

Visitor guides describe small boats, usually with two passengers each plus a rower, and a trip of about one to one and a half hours depending on the water level. The national tourism article says a sightseeing trip takes around an hour and a half. You can expect:

1. A short walk or wait at the landing.
2. A boat that moves slowly, so you have time to look.
3. Passages beneath the cliffs and past caves.
4. Birds such as storks and herons, and sometimes langurs on the cliffs.

Tickets are bought on site and prices change, so rely on the provincial tourism office's published ticket list or ask at the landing. Keep voices low and avoid sudden movements, because noise makes shy wildlife retreat. Do not feed any animals and do not throw anything into the water.

- A hat and sunscreen; the boats have little cover.

## Seasons at the reserve

A Ninh Binh tour operator's guide to the reserve says May and June are especially striking when lotus fields bloom, and that the dry season from November to April suits wildlife watching because cooler weather makes the boat more comfortable and migratory birds return. These are operator observations, not official calendars, so treat them as a guide. Summer heat and sudden showers are the usual issues for a rider, and winter mornings can be cool on a scooter, so bring a light jacket for the early ride.

## A sample plan if you stay overnight

This is a planning outline, not a timetable we have tested, and the clock times depend on your own pace:

1. **Day one.** Collect the bike when the shop opens, ride south on the national road with a break every hour or so, and settle in near Ninh Binh. Use the afternoon for something gentle such as a short ride around the lanes.
2. **Day two, early.** Ride to the reserve for the first boats, when wildlife is most active and the water is calm. Allow time for a slow trip and for the walk back to the bike.
3. **Day two, later.** Either add a second stop, for example the park's neighbour Cuc Phuong if you are staying another night, or begin the ride home with plenty of daylight.

The point is to keep the boat at the best time of day and to keep the ride home away from darkness and from the heaviest afternoon traffic near Hanoi.

## Behaving around wildlife

Wild langurs are shy, and the reserve's conservation record depends partly on visitors not adding pressure. Basic courtesy goes a long way: stay seated in the boat, speak quietly, switch the phone's flash and sound off, and follow the rower's directions. If you see langurs, enjoy them from a distance and do not call out or try to attract them. Leave no rubbish, and carry out anything you bring.

## Pairing Van Long with another stop

The Cuc Phuong park site itself recommends Van Long, where Delacour's langurs can be seen in the wild, as a trip from the park, so the two make a nature-themed pair for an overnight trip. Pairing it with Trang An or Tam Coc also works: the national tourism guide calls Van Long, Tam Coc and Trang An the region's main boat areas, with Van Long as the lesser-known and less busy option. Do not try to do two boat trips and a long ride home in one day. ## FAQ: Van Long by motorbike

### How far is Van Long from Hanoi?

The Vietnam National Authority of Tourism gives about 80 km. Visitor guides put it around 17 km from the centre of Ninh Binh city.

### Will I see the langurs?

Not necessarily. UNESCO's tentative-list document reports a large population, but they live on cliffs and sightings vary. Early morning and late afternoon give the best chance.

### Is Van Long busier than Tam Coc?

Guides describe it as quieter and lesser known than Tam Coc and Trang An, but holidays and weekends are busier everywhere.

### How long should I allow?

The boat trip is about an hour to an hour and a half. Add the ride and a margin, and consider staying overnight.

### Can I ride the expressway to save time?

No. Motorbikes are not allowed on expressways. Use the national road.
