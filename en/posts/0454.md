To pass a parked car on a scooter without entering oncoming traffic, decide early, signal, slow down and take the gap only if it is clearly yours. Law 36/2024/QH15 gives a direct rule for this in Article 17: where a vehicle faces an obstruction ahead, it must give way to the oncoming vehicle that does not face an obstruction. A parked car is an obstruction on your side, so oncoming traffic has priority and you wait behind the car until the way is clear. If you are on the free side and the other rider is facing the obstruction, they should wait for you. In practice, that means looking ahead, slowing before the car, not speeding up to beat an oncoming vehicle and leaving yourself room for a door, a pedestrian or a car pulling out.

This guide explains the legal rule, describes the technique step by step, covers narrow streets with cars parked on both sides, explains what to check for before you pass, and offers alternatives when the gap is not safe. It is general information based on the law as of October 2026, not legal advice.

## Why this situation is so common in Hanoi

Many streets in central Hanoi are narrow, and cars and motorbikes park along them. Parked vehicles take space from the lane on their side and leave a gap that may be only just wide enough for a scooter. Oncoming riders face the same gap from the other direction, and in a busy street the two streams meet in the middle. Passing a parked car without entering the oncoming lane is a daily skill, and it is easy to get wrong because everyone is in a hurry. ## What the law says

Four provisions of Law 36/2024/QH15 apply.

**Article 17, passing oncoming traffic.** On a road not divided into two separate directions, when passing oncoming traffic you must slow down and keep your vehicle to the right in your direction of travel. In giving way, the vehicle moving downhill gives way to the vehicle moving uphill, and the vehicle facing an obstruction ahead gives way to the vehicle not facing an obstruction. On a narrow road that is only wide enough for one vehicle, where a lay-by is available, the vehicle closer to the lay-by pulls in and gives way.

**Article 12, speed and attention.** Operators must observe the surroundings and slow down or stop when they pass oncoming traffic or allow overtaking, and when they change direction. They must also keep a speed suited to the road, traffic density and conditions.

**Article 13, lanes.** Keep to your lane, and change lanes only where it is allowed, after signalling and checking all sides.

**Article 18, stopping and parking.** Vehicles may stop and park only close to the right edge or roadside, and on streets the nearest wheel must not be more than 0.25 metre from the roadside or sidewalk, and must not cause obstruction or danger. So a correctly parked car sits at the edge of your lane, and a car that is parked wider than that is obstructing the road.

## What counts as an obstruction

An obstruction is anything that blocks part of your lane: a parked car, a delivery vehicle, a stall, a pile of materials, a stopped bus. When you meet one, the rule in Article 17 asks which side faces it. If the obstruction is on your side, you are the one who has to give way, unless the oncoming vehicle is not there. If it is on the other side, you have priority and should be ready to proceed, but watch for the oncoming rider who may not stop.

Treat priority as a rule for working out who waits, not as a licence to force the gap. If the other rider is not slowing, slow and let them through, and keep the peace.

## Step 1: look ahead early

Most trouble starts when a rider sees the parked car at the last moment.

- **Look well ahead down the street,** and read the road: parked vehicles, stalls, a bus at a stop, a delivery truck.

- **Look through the gap,** to the traffic coming towards you. Is there a stream of oncoming scooters, or a gap in it?

- **Decide before you reach the car,** whether you will pass or wait. A late decision leads to a late swerve.

- **Choose your speed accordingly.** Our guide to [speed limits for motorbikes in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/speed-limits-motorbikes-vietnam/) explains the limits, and on a narrow street a low speed is what makes the manoeuvre safe.

## Step 2: check behind, and signal

Article 15 requires a signal and a check of the vehicle behind before a change of direction, and moving out around a car is a change of line.

1. **Check your mirror and glance behind,** especially on the side you will move to.

2. **Signal early,** with the indicator, so that the rider behind knows you will move out.

3. **Slow smoothly,** and do not brake hard.

4. **Keep the signal on** until you have passed and moved back.

Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, as read on the Government portal, sets a fine of VND 200,000 to 400,000 for changing lanes without a signal or where it is not allowed. Check the current text if a figure matters to you.

## Step 3: give way, or take the gap

This is the decision.

- **If oncoming traffic is clear,** or the nearest rider is far enough away that they do not need to change speed, you may move out, pass and return.

- **If an oncoming rider is already close,** stop behind the parked car and let them pass. Article 17 says the vehicle facing the obstruction gives way.

- **If a stream of oncoming traffic is continuous,** wait. A gap will come, and you are no worse off than the riders behind you.

- **Do not accelerate to beat an oncoming vehicle.** A gap you need speed to take is not a gap.

- **If both sides have an obstruction,** the narrower gap decides: the one nearer a lay-by or wider spot pulls in.

Eye contact and a small hand signal help, but do not rely on them. Stop if there is any doubt.

## Step 4: leave room as you pass

When you do pass, leave a margin between you and the parked car.

- **Leave at least the width of a car door** where the street allows, because a door can open without warning. Our guide to [opening car doors as a hazard for scooter riders](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/opening-car-doors-a-hazard-for-scooter-riders/) explains why, and Article 19 puts the duty on the person opening the door.

- **Leave room on the oncoming side too,** so that you are not pushing into the other lane. If you cannot do both, the gap is too narrow, and you should wait.

- **Keep your speed low,** and a hand near the brakes.

- **Do not weave.** Choose one line and hold it.

- **Watch the car as you pass:** its doors, wheels, lights and mirrors, and whether anyone is inside.

## Step 5: watch for what comes out from behind the car

A parked car hides things.

- **Pedestrians** often step out from between parked cars. Article 12 requires you to slow down where pedestrians may cross.

- **Children** may run out. Article 12 lists areas with schools and crowded places.

- **Other riders** may pull out of a gap beside the car.

- **The car itself** may pull out. Look for brake lights, reversing lights, a turned front wheel, an engine running, a driver in the seat, exhaust smoke.

- **A door** may open.

If you see any of these signs, slow right down, and be ready to stop.

## Narrow streets with cars on both sides

Where cars are parked along both sides, the street may be wide enough for only one stream at a time.

1. **Stop at the entrance** and look. If a rider or vehicle is already in, wait for them to come through.

2. **Use the nearest wider spot,** a driveway or a gap between cars, to let oncoming traffic pass.

3. **Follow the vehicle in front** at a safe distance, and let it clear the gap before you enter.

4. **Do not enter a narrow gap behind a large vehicle** that fills it, because you cannot see what is ahead.

5. **If you meet a vehicle head-on,** stop and reverse slowly if needed. Do not push forward.

Article 17 also notes that where a road is narrow and a lay-by is available, the vehicle closer to the lay-by pulls in to give way.

## When the gap is not safe

If the gap is too narrow, or the view is blocked, or traffic is dense, do not force it.

- **Wait behind the car** with your signal on and your foot down, until the way is clear.

- **Turn back or take another street,** if the street is blocked.

- **Dismount and walk,** in a very tight spot.

- **Plan your route** to avoid streets that are lined with parked vehicles at peak times. Our guide to [motorbike route planning around Hanoi's restricted roads](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbike-route-planning-around-hanois-restricted-roads/) explains how to review a route.

## Night, rain and poor visibility

- **At night,** parked cars are harder to see, and a dark car with no lights can appear suddenly. Use your low beam and slow down. Our guide to [riding at night in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-at-night-in-hanoi/) covers lights.

- **In rain,** the road beside parked cars may be wet and slippery, and visibility is lower. Slow down and leave a bigger gap. Our guide to [riding in Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) explains how.

- **After heavy rain,** puddles can hide potholes beside parked cars.

## Common mistakes

1. **Seeing the car too late.**

2. **Swinging out without a signal or a mirror check.**

3. **Passing too close to the car.**

4. **Speeding up to beat an oncoming rider.**

5. **Entering a gap that will not take two.**

6. **Not looking for people between cars.**

7. **Treating priority as a right to force the gap.**

## FAQ: passing parked cars on a scooter

### Who gives way when a parked car blocks my side?

Under Article 17 of Law 36/2024/QH15, the vehicle facing an obstruction ahead gives way to the oncoming vehicle that does not face an obstruction.

### How close can I pass a parked car?

The law does not set a distance for this. Leave at least the width of a door where possible, and wait if you cannot do that without entering the oncoming lane.

### Do I need to signal?

Yes. Check your mirror and signal before you move out, as Article 15 requires for a change of direction.

### What if oncoming traffic is continuous?

Wait behind the car. A gap will come, and a forced gap is how collisions happen.

### Can I use the oncoming lane to get round the car?

Only if no oncoming vehicle is affected. Article 17 tells you to keep right in your direction of travel and to give way to oncoming traffic that does not face an obstruction, so if anyone is coming, wait.

### What should I check before I pass?

The car's doors, lights, wheels and driver, people between cars and the oncoming traffic.
