Store a rented electric scooter somewhere dry, shaded, locked and away from exits, and do not leave its battery either nearly full or nearly empty if it will sit unused. For a few idle days, VinFast's guidance for its lithium scooters is to leave the battery above about half charge and to disconnect it so it does not drain. After a fortnight, the same maker suggests keeping about half charge and, where the battery is removable, storing it separately in a cool dry place. On a weekly or monthly rental the scooter spends most of its life parked, so these habits protect both the battery and your deposit. This guide covers overnight parking, idle days, longer breaks, building rules, security and what to tell the shop.

## Why storage matters more on a long rental

A day-rental scooter is parked for a night at most. A rental of a week or a month is different: it sits outside a guesthouse, in a courtyard, in a stairwell or in a car park for most of the hours you hold it, and some of those hours may stretch to days when you travel or fall ill.

Two things happen while it sits. The battery slowly loses charge on its own, and the surroundings act on it: heat, damp, rain, passers-by and sometimes a building manager with views about electric vehicles. Storage is therefore a mix of battery care, weather protection and security, and the shop's terms decide who carries the cost when something goes wrong. ## Match the storage to the length of the gap

The right routine depends on how long the scooter will be untouched. The figures come from VinFast guidance for its lithium-battery scooters and may differ for other makes, so treat them as a guide and follow the shop's instructions for your bike.

| Gap | What VinFast's guidance suggests |
|---|---|
| Overnight | Charge when needed, but let a hot battery cool for about half an hour after riding before you plug it in |
| Two to three days or more | Leave the battery above 50 per cent; unplug the battery from the bike or disconnect its cable to stop drain |
| Battery under 5 per cent | Charge it within three days |
| Over 14 days | Keep at least 50 per cent; remove a detachable battery and store it dry and cool |
| Weeks to months (a dealer's advice) | About 50 to 60 per cent, checked and topped up about once a month |

The pattern is consistent across the sources: neither full nor empty, and cool rather than hot. The exact percentages vary a little by page, so aim for about half and avoid extremes.

## Where to keep it overnight

The ideal spot is indoors, dry and shaded, on level ground, with the steering locked and the bike clear of walkways and exits. In practice you will often have to choose among imperfect places.

- **Indoors, in the lobby, courtyard or a designated parking room.** Best for weather and theft, but ask first. Some buildings do not allow electric vehicles inside because of charging risks.
- **Guarded outdoor parking.** Acceptable if it is shaded and covered. Shop guidance for rentals in general is to lock the steering and park in guarded places, since the shop does not provide an extra lock.
- **Open street parking.** The weakest option for a scooter you will be responsible for over weeks. Rain, sun and curiosity all act on it.

News reports in 2026 note that some Hanoi apartment buildings have banned e-bikes, and that many residential buildings lack safe charging points or restrict electric-vehicle parking. If you are renting a flat, ask the building manager before you bring the scooter in, not after. Our guide to [parking a motorbike in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-motorbike-in-hanoi/) explains guarded lots and tickets.

## Keep it out of the heat and the wet

VinFast says lithium batteries are particularly sensitive to high temperatures and advises avoiding charging a battery that is hot. A scooter standing all afternoon in direct sun, then plugged in immediately, combines both problems. Choose shade, and let the scooter rest for about 30 minutes after a ride before charging.

The battery on some VinFast models is rated IP67, meaning it can survive being submerged to half a metre for 30 minutes. That is a design rating, not an invitation. VinFast's own advice is to limit riding through water and to dry the battery and motor area with a cloth after rain. Storage in a place that floods, or that stays damp for weeks, is still a poor idea. Hanoi's wet season is hard on any vehicle left outdoors, as our guide to [riding in Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) explains.

## Set the battery level before you leave it

Before an idle spell of two or three days or more:

1. **Charge or discharge toward about half,** and avoid leaving it at 100 per cent for long. VinFast's page on its scooters notes that a battery lasts longer when used and stored between about 20 and 80 per cent.
2. **Never leave it nearly empty.** A deeply discharged lithium battery can fall into a protective state that is hard to recover from.
3. **Disconnect the battery or its cable** if the model allows it and the shop agrees, to stop slow drain.
4. **Note the percentage** and photograph the display, so you can show the level you left it at.

Do not experiment with the electrics yourself. If the scooter has a removable battery, ask the shop whether you may take it out, and how.

## If the battery comes out

Only some models have a removable battery, and renting one does not mean you should carry it indoors without asking. When it is allowed, the dealer and maker guidance is consistent:

- Store it in a dry, cool place away from direct sun, at roughly 15 to 25 degrees Celsius.
- Put it on a hard, non-flammable surface, away from fabric, paper and chemicals.
- Do not stack heavy objects on it or turn it upside down.
- Keep the connection points clean and dry.
- Check it about once a month, and charge back toward half if it has dropped.

Do not charge it inside a closed box, under bedding or in a cupboard. A dealer blog also mentions fire-resistant lithium battery bags as an extra precaution, which is an option for a long stay rather than a requirement.

## Security while it sits

A scooter left for weeks is a target if it looks unattended. Practical steps:

- Lock the steering every time, and take the key or smart key with you.
- Park where other people pass and a guard or host can see it.
- Do not leave the charger, documents or valuables in the boot.
- Photograph the bike at each parking spot if you will be away for several days.
- Tell your host or guard that it belongs to a rental and how long it will stay.

The shop does not supply an extra lock. If you buy your own, tell the shop what you have added.

## If you are away from the scooter

Plans change on a long rental: a trip to the mountains, a stay in hospital, a week at a conference. Raise the options with the shop in advance.

- **Keep paying rent and store it safely.** The simplest option, but it costs rent for days you do not ride.
- **Ask whether the shop can hold the scooter.** The shop decides if it will, and on what terms.
- **Ask about returning early.** Nguyen Ha's published rule is that early return does not refund unused rent, so check what a pause or a swap would cost before you act. ## Restarting after a stored period

When you come back to a scooter that has been idle:

1. Walk round it for damage, damp or anything out of place.
2. Check tyre pressure, brakes and the steering lock.
3. Switch on and read the display for warnings; do not ride with a warning showing.
4. Charge it to a comfortable level before a long ride, because the storage level is meant for resting, not for travelling.
5. Ride a short loop first to feel the brakes and the acceleration.

If the scooter will not turn on or shows a fault, stop and ring the shop. ## FAQ: storing a rented electric scooter

### What battery level should I leave it at?

For a few idle days, above about half, and not at 100 or near 0. VinFast's guidance for long idle spells is to keep at least 50 per cent.

### Can I leave it outside overnight?

Only in a guarded, shaded spot, with the steering locked. Indoors is better if the building allows it.

### Should I leave the scooter plugged in while I am away?

Not for long periods. Ask the shop, and avoid leaving a battery on charge unattended or at full charge for days.

### Can I take the battery inside?

Only if the model has a removable battery and the shop agrees. Keep it cool, dry and on a hard surface.

### Will the shop refund rent for days I do not ride?

Nguyen Ha's rule is that early return does not refund unused rent. Ask about holding the bike before you travel.
