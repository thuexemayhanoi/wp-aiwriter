A Hanoi motorbike rental contract is a short lease agreement: the shop lets you use a specific bike for a fixed period, and you promise to pay, look after it and bring it back on time and in the condition you received it. Most of the paper is about what happens when something goes wrong, such as a scratch, a stolen bike, a late or early return or an empty tank. This guide explains what each common clause type means in plain English, shows how Nguyen Ha Motorbike Rental in Long Bien applies the clauses it publishes, and lists the questions worth asking before you sign. It is general information, not legal advice.

## The legal frame: a lease of property under Vietnam's Civil Code

In Vietnamese law, renting a motorbike is treated as a lease of property. The Civil Code 2015 (Law No. 91/2015/QH13), which has been in effect since 1 January 2017 and is still the governing code as of October 2026, sets out a dedicated group of articles on asset lease contracts. In broad terms, the lessor hands over the asset and must let you use it; the lessee pays rent, uses the asset for its normal purpose, preserves it and returns it when the term ends.

Two points from that framework matter most to a traveller. First, the Code expects a leased asset to come back in the condition in which it was received, allowing for natural wear and tear, and lets the owner claim compensation if the value has dropped beyond that. Second, the Code leaves a great deal to the parties' own agreement. Charges for late return, how damage is assessed and how a deposit is settled are therefore mostly set by the written contract rather than by a fixed national tariff. That is why reading the clauses is worth ten minutes of your time.

## Parties, the bike and the identity documents

The opening block of a rental contract identifies who is renting and exactly what is being rented. Expect your full name, nationality, a contact number in Vietnam, where you are staying, and the details of the bike: model, colour, plate number and often the odometer reading. If the plate on the paper does not match the plate on the bike, stop and correct it before you ride off, because every later clause depends on that identification.

At Nguyen Ha, staff check your identity documents and driving licence and photograph them for the contract. The originals are not kept. The shop handles security through a separate cash deposit, so you leave with your passport in your pocket. This is not a no-deposit service, and the documents step is about recording who is responsible for the bike, not about holding something hostage.

Some contracts also contain a declaration that you hold a licence valid for the bike's category. That sentence shifts responsibility onto you, so read it honestly. ## The rental period clause and how a day is counted

Every contract states a start time and a return time. The detail that catches people out is the definition of a "day". Nguyen Ha counts one rental day as 24 hours from handover, and shorter use is still charged as a full day. Collecting a scooter at 15:00 on Monday for two days means returning it by 15:00 on Wednesday, not "some time on Wednesday".

Weekly and monthly packages are priced as their own products rather than as the daily rate multiplied by seven or thirty. If you are weighing those packages, our article on [weekly and monthly motorbike rental in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/weekly-monthly-motorbike-rental-hanoi/) covers the questions long-stay renters should ask. For the contract itself, the rule is simple: write the exact date and clock time on both copies and make sure they match.

The shop is open from 09:00 to 21:00 daily. If your planned return falls outside those hours, agree a workable time in advance rather than assuming someone will be waiting.

## Rent, payment and the deposit clause

The payment clause records the agreed rent for the specific bike and the package you chose. Nguyen Ha confirms the final price on the actual bike before the contract is signed, so the figure on paper should be the one you discussed at the counter, not a range from a website. Current reference rates are on the [prices page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/prices/).

The deposit clause sits next to it but is a different thing. Rent pays for using the bike; the deposit is security that is returned at the end once the bike, the fuel and any extra charges have been checked. Nguyen Ha takes a cash deposit, generally VND 2–5 million depending on the bike and the contract. A well-written clause states the amount, says what it can be used for and explains when it comes back. ## The condition clause: what "received in good condition" commits you to

Most rental papers contain a line saying you received the bike in good working order, sometimes followed by a short list of existing marks. Once you sign, that line becomes the baseline for every damage discussion at return. If a scratch, a cracked mirror or a worn tyre is not recorded, the contract assumes it was not there.

The fix is practical rather than legal. Walk round the bike with the staff member, photograph or film each side, and ask for existing damage to be noted on the paper. Our [10-minute rental scooter inspection checklist](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-scooter-inspection-checklist/) gives a step-by-step routine. Keep the photos until your deposit is back in your hand.

Natural wear is a fair exception. Tyres lose tread, brake pads wear and paint fades in the sun. A reasonable contract, and the Civil Code's general approach, separates that ordinary wear from damage caused by a fall, a collision or misuse.

## Damage clauses: who pays and how the amount is decided

The damage clause usually says that the renter is responsible for loss or damage to the bike during the rental period, and that repairs are charged at the cost of parts and labour. Some contracts name a preferred repair workshop; others let the shop repair the bike itself and present the bill. A few set fixed amounts for common parts.

Three questions help you understand any damage clause. Who decides what the repair costs? Can you see the damaged part and the receipt? Is the charge taken from the deposit, or could it exceed the deposit? If the clause is silent, ask before you sign and note the answer.

It is also worth understanding what insurance does and does not do. Vietnamese law requires motorbike owners to carry compulsory civil liability insurance, and that cover is aimed at injury or damage to third parties. It is not a damage waiver for the rental bike, and it does not protect you from a repair bill. If you want cover for your own injuries, that usually comes from personal travel insurance, which you should check before riding.

## Theft and loss clauses

Theft is the clause renters most often skim and most regret. A typical wording makes the renter responsible if the bike is stolen while in their care, especially if it was left unlocked or parked somewhere unguarded. Many contracts also make you responsible for the key and the registration paper, because losing either can stop the bike being used or ridden legally.

Nguyen Ha does not provide an extra lock. The practical guidance is to lock the steering and park in guarded places, where you are given a ticket. Our article on [parking a motorbike in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-motorbike-in-hanoi/) explains how guarded lots work. If a bike does go missing, contact the shop immediately on 0334 699 969 and report the theft to the local police so there is an official record.

Ask the shop, before signing, how a theft would be handled and what evidence it would need. Do not assume a theft clause is generous because it is short.

## The late return clause

A late return clause tells you what you pay if the bike comes back after the agreed time. Under the Civil Code's general approach, a lessee who keeps an asset beyond the term remains liable for the use of it, and the parties can agree a specific charge in the contract. In practice, that agreed charge is what you will meet.

Nguyen Ha's published terms are clear. If you return the bike between one and five hours late, the charge is VND 30,000 per hour. From six hours late, one extra rental day is charged at the bike's rate. There is no automatic grace period, so a long lunch on the way back can cost money.

If you can see you will be late, message or call the shop before the deadline. An agreed extension is a contract change, so ask for the new return time and the additional rent to be confirmed in writing, even in a chat message. That record protects both sides if there is a disagreement later.

## The early return clause

Early return is the mirror image of late return, and renters are often surprised by it. Returning the bike before the end of the agreed period does not, by itself, create a right to a refund for the unused time. Nguyen Ha's published terms state plainly that early return does not refund unused rent.

That rule makes sense once you think of the contract as a booking: the bike was reserved for you and could not be offered to anyone else. The practical response is to choose a realistic period at the start. If your plans are uncertain, a shorter rental with the option to extend is usually safer than a long package you might abandon halfway through. The deposit is a separate matter and is settled at return under its own clause.

## Fuel, battery and equipment clauses

Fuel clauses look minor but generate many small disputes. Nguyen Ha hands over petrol bikes with roughly 0.5 to 2 litres in the tank and expects them back with about 0.5 to 1 litre. If the tank is empty at return, a fee of VND 20,000 applies. For electric bikes, the battery level and the charging arrangement are confirmed at handover rather than measured in litres.

Equipment clauses list what comes with the bike. At Nguyen Ha, one basic helmet per rider is included free. A raincoat is not included for short rentals, though it may be considered for monthly contracts, and no extra lock is supplied. If the contract lists items you must return, such as helmets or a document holder, check that they are all present before you leave. ## Use restrictions: riders, routes and the law

Many contracts restrict who may ride and where. A common clause says only the named renter may ride, which matters if you plan to share the bike with a friend. Another may limit trips outside the city or require you to tell the shop about long journeys. Our article on [taking a Hanoi rental motorbike out of the city](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/take-rental-motorbike-out-of-hanoi/) looks at that question in more depth.

The contract also sits on top of traffic law, which it cannot override. Vietnam's Road Traffic Order and Safety Law (No. 36/2024/QH15), effective from 1 January 2025, bars ordinary motorbikes from expressways, and fines are set by government decree rather than by the rental shop. A clause that makes the renter responsible for traffic fines during the rental is common and generally consistent with that system: the person riding is the person who committed the violation. ## Questions to ask before you sign

- Does the plate number on the paper match the bike in front of me?
- What exact date and clock time must I return the bike?
- Is existing damage written down, and do I have my own photos?
- How is a repair cost decided, and can I see the receipt?
- What happens if the bike or the key is stolen or lost?
- What is the late return charge, and how do I request an extension?
- Is there any refund if I return early?
- How much fuel is in the tank now, and how much should be in it at return?
- Who else, if anyone, may ride the bike?

Keep your copy of the contract with your passport while you have the bike. ## FAQ: Hanoi motorbike rental contracts

### Is a handwritten or one-page rental contract legally valid in Vietnam?

A lease of a motorbike does not need a long or notarised document to be binding. What matters is that the parties, the bike, the period, the rent and the key obligations are clear. Make sure both sides keep a copy and that any changes are confirmed in writing.

### What does Nguyen Ha charge for returning a bike late?

Under the published terms, VND 30,000 per hour for one to five hours late. From six hours late, one extra rental day is charged. Contacting the shop before the deadline lets you agree an extension instead.

### Will I get money back if I return the bike early?

No refund of unused rent is given for an early return under Nguyen Ha's terms. The deposit is settled separately when the bike is checked in.

### Does the shop keep my passport under the contract?

No. Your identity documents and licence are checked and photographed for the contract, and the originals stay with you. Security is provided by a cash deposit of VND 2–5 million depending on the bike and contract.

### Am I responsible if the rental bike is stolen?

Theft clauses usually make the renter responsible while the bike is in their care, particularly if it was left unlocked or in an unguarded place. Ask how a theft would be handled before signing, park in guarded lots and report any theft to the shop and the police straight away.

### Does compulsory motorbike insurance cover damage to the rental bike?

Generally not. Compulsory civil liability insurance is aimed at third-party injury and damage. Repair of the rental bike itself is governed by the contract's damage clause.
