On a wide Hanoi boulevard the traffic can look like a free-for-all, but the law is specific about where a motorbike belongs. Slower vehicles keep to the right. On a road with marked lanes you stay in one lane, change lanes only where it is allowed and only one lane at a time, and you never enter a lane reserved for another type of vehicle. Most of the confusion visitors feel comes from three things: signs that are small and placed high, markings that change from one block to the next, and local riders who bend the rules in ways a visitor cannot safely copy. This article explains the rules as they stand in October 2026, how to read the signs and markings, how to handle a left turn across several lanes and what the fines are when you get it wrong. It is general information, not legal advice.

## The rule in the law: keep right, one lane at a time

Article 13 of the Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (Law No. 36/2024/QH15), in force since 1 January 2025, sets out the basics in four short clauses:

1. A vehicle moving at a lower speed must keep to the right in its direction of travel.
2. On a road with several lanes in the same direction separated by lane lines, the driver must keep to one lane and change lanes only where this is permitted. Each change may only move into the adjacent lane, must be signalled in advance and must follow a check that the distance to the vehicles in front, behind and beside is safe.
3. On a carriageway with lane lines, non-motorised vehicles use the right-most lane and motor vehicles and special-purpose machinery use the lane to its left.
4. On a lane reserved for one type of vehicle or a group of types, drivers of other vehicle types must not enter it.

Everything else in this article is a consequence of those four rules plus the signs and markings on the road. When no sign or marking separates vehicle types, the lanes are mixed and the keep-right rule decides where a motorbike rides.

## Reading signs and markings

Three kinds of sign do most of the work on Hanoi's roads. A blue rectangular sign showing lanes with vehicle symbols tells you which lane is for which vehicle. A round red-bordered sign with a motorbike symbol, or a sign banning entry for the vehicle type you are riding, means you may not enter. A sign for a reserved lane with a bus symbol marks a lane that only buses may use.

Painted symbols on the surface repeat the message. A motorbike symbol on the right-hand lane at the start of a block tells you where you belong. A solid line forbids changing lanes, a broken line allows it where it is safe. The practical habit is to look at the sign at the start of each block and the painted symbols just ahead of you, rather than following the rider in front.

Some roads and structures are closed to motorbikes altogether: expressways, the elevated sections of the ring roads and some bridges and tunnels. These are not lane offences but "entering a road closed to motorbikes" offences with larger fines, covered below. Our guide to [which Hanoi ring roads and highways allow motorbikes](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-ring-roads-highways-motorbikes-allowed/) lists them.

## Mixed lanes, motorbike lanes and car-only lanes

Hanoi's roads use three arrangements, sometimes within a single kilometre.

**Mixed lanes** are the most common in the city centre. Cars, buses and motorbikes share the carriageway and the only rule is to stay right and keep to a lane.

**Separated motorbike lanes** appear on some of the wider roads, where a kerb, a barrier or a painted buffer puts motorbikes and bicycles on the outer lanes and cars on the inner ones. Where signs or markings allocate lanes this way, a motorbike in a car lane is in the wrong lane, even if the motorbike lane is slow.

**Car-only roads and decks** are closed to motorbikes entirely. Riding onto one is a different and more expensive offence than riding in the wrong lane.

The skill is reading the arrangement as it changes. A road that is mixed at one junction can have a separated motorbike lane after the next, and the sign at the start of the new section is the only reliable guide.

## Changing lanes and overtaking

A lane change needs a signal, a shoulder check and a gap, and under Article 13 it may only move you one lane across. The indicator must be on before the manoeuvre and stay on until it is complete. Changing lanes without a signal, or in a place where it is not permitted, is the cheapest lane offence in the penalty table, but it is also the cause of most of the small collisions you will see in the city.

Overtaking is done on the left in general. Passing on the right is allowed only in limited cases defined in the law, for example when the vehicle ahead is turning left, and overtaking on the right where it is not allowed is a separate offence. On a separated motorbike lane you pass other motorbikes within that lane and do not move into the car lane to get by.

Filtering between stationary cars at a red light is widespread and tolerated in practice, but it is not a lane right. Do it slowly, watch for opening doors and for pedestrians crossing between vehicles, and stop behind the line. Our article on [crossing Hanoi intersections](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/crossing-hanoi-intersections/) covers the junction itself.

## The left turn across several lanes

The left turn is where lane rules and self-preservation meet. On a multi-lane road a motorbike turning left should move into the lane that allows left turns before the junction, signal early and turn on the signal or in a safe gap. Turning left from the outer right-hand lane across moving traffic is the manoeuvre that hurts the most riders, and where lanes are allocated by markings it can also break the lane rules.

Where the road has a separated motorbike lane on the right, the layout often provides a marked opening before the junction for motorbikes to move inward, or a separate signal phase. If you cannot see one, the safe alternative is the box turn: ride straight through the junction, pull over on the far right, wait for the signal in the cross street and proceed. It costs a minute and avoids crossing three lanes of cars.

Many junctions post a sign banning left turns for the vehicle type you ride. A left turn where a sign forbids it is fined in the same band as turning without a signal. The sign is a red-bordered circle with a crossed-out left arrow, placed on the right before the junction.

## U-turns, right turns and the keep-right trap

A U-turn is allowed only where a sign or marking permits it. On the large radial roads the permitted turning points are set every few hundred metres, and motorbikes queue in the inner lane to use them. Use those points rather than improvising a turn across the central reservation.

A right turn on a red light is not allowed by default. Under Article 11 of the law, a red signal means stop, and a right turn is permitted only where a green right-turn arrow, a supplementary sign or a channelising island allows it. Vietnamese riders often creep round on red anyway. A visitor who copies them and meets a camera pays the red-light fine described below.

The keep-right rule has a trap of its own. The right-hand lane is also where buses pull in, cars park half on the kerb and food carts push out. Keeping right does not mean hugging the kerb. Ride a metre or so from the edge so you have room when a door opens, and use the inner part of the right-hand lane as your working position.

## Bus lanes and reserved lanes

Clause 4 of Article 13 is the rule for reserved lanes: if a lane is reserved for one type of vehicle, other vehicles may not enter it. In Hanoi that mostly means lanes marked for buses. The bus rapid transit line that runs from Kim Ma along Le Van Luong and To Huu towards Yen Nghia has had lanes reserved for it, but reports from earlier years describe plans to replace the line with an urban railway, so its layout can change. Reports in 2025 put the city's dedicated bus lanes at only about 13 kilometres and argued for more. Treat any lane with a reserved sign and a bus symbol as closed to you, whatever the local riders beside you do.

Two cautions. First, the extent of a reserved section and the hours at which it applies can change, so read the sign at the start of the section instead of relying on memory of the route. Second, where a reserved lane ends and mixed traffic resumes, the signs say so, and that is the only place to cross into it.

Bicycle lanes, taxi ranks and the pedestrian zones around Hoan Kiem Lake at weekends work the same way. Our article on [riding in the Old Quarter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-old-quarter/) explains the weekend closures.

## Fines for lane and junction offences in 2026

The amounts below come from Article 7 of Decree 168/2024/ND-CP for riders of motorbikes and mopeds. We read the text on the Government portal, and Decree 238/2026/ND-CP, which amends the decree from 15 August 2026, does not change Article 7. As of October 2026:

- **Changing lanes where not permitted or without a signal, or more than one lane at a time:** a fine of VND 200,000–400,000.
- **U-turn where not permitted:** a fine of VND 400,000–600,000.
- **Not keeping to the right, riding in the wrong part of the road or the wrong lane, or crossing a fixed central divider:** a fine of VND 600,000–800,000.
- **Turning without a signal, or turning left where a sign bans it:** a fine of VND 600,000–800,000.
- **Overtaking on the right where this is not allowed:** a fine of VND 600,000–800,000.
- **Entering an area or road signed as closed to your vehicle type:** a fine of VND 2,000,000–3,000,000 and two licence points.
- **Riding the wrong way on a one-way road or on the pavement:** a fine of VND 4,000,000–6,000,000 and two licence points.
- **Entering an expressway:** a fine of VND 4,000,000–6,000,000 and six licence points.
- **Ignoring a red light:** a fine of VND 4,000,000–6,000,000 and four licence points.
- **Any of the lane offences above that causes an accident:** a fine of VND 10,000,000–14,000,000.

Licence points are deducted from the record of a Vietnamese licence. A foreign licence has no Vietnamese point record, so for a visitor the deduction has no practical effect, but the fine and any record of the offence remain. Our guide to [traffic fines that catch foreign riders out](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/traffic-fines-vietnam-foreign-riders/) explains how a fine is issued and paid.

## Reading the road like a local, riding it like a visitor

Local riders know which rules are enforced on which road, and they take positions a visitor should not: squeezing between a bus and a kerb, turning left from the right-hand lane because a junction has no camera, or using the car lane because the motorbike lane is clogged. A rental rider on a foreign licence has no local tolerance to fall back on, so the conservative reading of the rules is the right one.

In practice: pick the lane meant for motorbikes and stay in it, move inward only to turn left or overtake and only where the markings allow it, never follow a car onto a deck or road closed to bikes, treat every bus-marked lane as a wall and plan left turns a block early. None of this slows you much in Hanoi traffic, where average speed is set by congestion and not by lane position.

## FAQ: motorbike lane rules in Vietnam

### Which lane should a motorbike use on a multi-lane road in Vietnam?

The right-hand lane, or the lane marked for motorbikes where lanes are allocated by sign or marking. Slower vehicles keep right under Article 13 of the 2024 law. Move left only to turn left or overtake and only where the markings allow it.

### Can a motorbike ride in a car lane if the motorbike lane is congested?

Where lanes are allocated by sign or marking, no. Riding in the wrong lane is fined VND 600,000–800,000 as of October 2026.

### How do I turn left safely across several lanes?

Move into the lane that allows left turns before the junction, signal early and turn on the signal. Where that is not possible, ride straight through, pull over on the far right and wait for the cross street's green.

### Are motorbikes allowed in Hanoi's bus lanes?

No. A lane reserved for buses is closed to other vehicle types under Article 13 of the law, and the sign at the start of the section is the guide.

### Is turning right on a red light allowed in Vietnam?

Only where a green right-turn arrow, a supplementary sign or a channelising island allows it. Otherwise it is running the red light, fined VND 4,000,000–6,000,000 for a motorbike.

### Do licence-point deductions affect a foreign licence?

The point system runs on Vietnamese licence records, so a visitor's foreign licence does not accumulate deductions. The fine and the record of the offence still apply.
