The safest way to ride a manual motorbike through standing water is usually not to: stop, look and take another route unless the water is clearly shallow, still and on a road you can see. A Honda Wave or Yamaha Sirius is a small, low, air-cooled machine with its exhaust outlet and air intake close to the ground, so deep water can stall it, damage it and unbalance it. Honda's manuals for these bikes give no wading depth, so there is no official number to lean on. If you do cross a shallow puddle, do it slowly, in a low gear, with a steady throttle, no braking and your feet on the footrests, then dry the brakes before you rely on them. This guide explains how to judge the water, the technique, what to do if the engine stops and how Hanoi's flooding fits in. It is general advice, not a guarantee of safety.

## Why standing water is a problem for a small bike

Several things go wrong at once.

The first is **depth you cannot see.** Water hides potholes, open drains, missing manhole covers and debris, and a road that is smooth when dry can have a hole a hand's breadth under the surface.

The second is **the engine.** Water that reaches the exhaust outlet or the air intake can stop the engine, and water drawn into the engine can cause serious damage.

The third is **the brakes.** Honda's Wave RSX FI manual says that water in the braking system reduces its effectiveness.

The fourth is **balance.** A front wheel pushing through water can be turned by the flow or by a hidden edge, and a small bike has little weight to resist it.

The fifth is **the electrics.** Water in the wrong place can cause misfires, a flat battery or a short.

None of this makes a shallow puddle dangerous. It means that depth and certainty matter more than the look of the surface.

## Decide before you enter

Stop at the edge and ask four questions.

1. **Can I see the road surface under the water?** Clear, shallow water over a visible surface is one thing. Muddy or oily water that hides everything is another.
2. **Is it still or moving?** Flowing water pushes on the wheels and can wash a bike sideways. Do not enter flowing water.
3. **How deep is it?** Look at other vehicles. If a bike's wheel hub is covered, or water reaches a car's door sills, it is too deep for you. Look at kerbs, parked vehicles and fence posts for a waterline. When in doubt, wait.
4. **Is there a safe alternative?** Another street, a pavement ramp where legal or a short wait often costs less than a stalled bike.

Honda's training document lists flooded roads among the situations where careful slow-speed balance is needed, but it does not give a depth. Our conservative rule is to avoid water that would reach the bottom of the wheel hub, and to avoid any water you cannot see through. That is our own judgement, not a manufacturer's figure.

## If you decide to cross a shallow puddle

Honda's balance technique for slow, difficult conditions applies directly:

- **Use a low gear,** first or second, so the engine has torque and you can keep a steady speed at walking pace.
- **Keep your head level and look straight ahead** with a wide field of view, not down at the water.
- **Use your hips and abdomen** to keep your body and the bike in balance, with relaxed arms and a light grip.
- **Add throttle if the bike is going too slowly,** to keep it upright, as Honda's document says.

Add these points for water:

- **Enter slowly and smoothly.** A fast entry throws up a bow wave and can push water into the exhaust or over the engine.
- **Keep a steady throttle through the water.** Closing it can let water back up the exhaust, and opening it hard can spin the rear wheel.
- **Do not brake or change gear in the water.** Cross, then do both.
- **Keep your feet on the footrests** unless you are about to put one down.
- **Ride a straight line,** and avoid following another vehicle closely, because its wake can push water into your path.
- **Keep to the shallowest part,** usually the crown of the road.

## After the crossing

Once you are through:

1. **Dry the brakes.** Honda's manual says water in the brakes reduces their effect and that you should apply them lightly while riding at low speed to dry them. Do this on a clear stretch with nobody close behind, a few gentle applications, before you need them hard.
2. **Check the lights and indicators** still work.
3. **Listen to the engine.** A cough, a misfire or a rough idle may mean water got in.
4. **Wipe your shoes and the footrests** if they are wet and slippery.
5. **Test the gear change and the brakes** gently.

If anything seems wrong, stop and call the shop.

## If the engine dies in the water

If the engine stops in standing water:


1. **Do not try to restart it in the water.** Repeated cranking can pull water into the engine and cause damage, and it drains a small battery. Honda's manual says that if the engine does not start within five seconds with the electric starter, you should switch off, wait ten seconds and try again, which also tells you not to keep cranking.

2. **Put your feet down and hold the brakes.**

3. **Push the bike out to dry ground.** Honda's training document describes moving a bike with the engine off: stand it upright, straighten the handlebars, retract the side stand and push forward with a hand on the front brake lever to control the speed.

4. **Switch off and wait.** Give the bike a few minutes to drain.

5. **Check for water.** Look at the exhaust outlet and the underside. If water pours out, leave it.

6. **Try the starter once or twice** only if the bike seems dry and nothing is wrong. Use the kick starter if the bike has one and the battery is weak. Our guide to [how to use the kick starter on an older rental motorbike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-use-the-kick-starter-on-an-older-rental-motorbike/) and our guide to [what to check on a manual bike's electric starter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-to-check-on-a-manual-bikes-electric-starter/) explain the procedures.

7. **Call the shop** on 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969), open 09:00 to 21:00 daily, if it will not start or runs badly.

Do not tilt or lay the bike down to drain it, and do not open the engine.

## Common mistakes in standing water

These are our own observations, not manufacturer rules, but each follows from the points above:

- **Following a car or bus closely** and riding in its wake, where the water is deeper and disturbed.
- **Treating a smooth surface as a safe one.** Calm water can sit over a hole, a drain or a step in the road.
- **Stopping halfway** because the water looks deeper than expected. Stopping in the middle is worse than turning back before you enter.
- **Revving hard** to keep the engine alive, which can spin the rear wheel and change the bike's behaviour at the worst moment. A steady throttle in a low gear is calmer.
- **Carrying a passenger who shifts or panics.** Tell a passenger beforehand to sit still, keep feet on the footrests and hold on.
- **Skipping the brake check afterwards** because the bike seems fine.

## Hanoi's flooding

Hanoi's heavy rain can flood underpasses, low streets and some roads around the lakes quickly. Our guide to [riding in Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) lists the season and the streets that flood and describes the city's flood-point planning. Treat an underpass full of water as a no-go. Flooded roads hide open drains, and water can rise fast. If you are caught in a heavy shower, find shelter and wait, as in our guide to [riding a semi-automatic bike in heavy Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-a-semi-automatic-bike-in-heavy-hanoi-rain/).

Remember the lights. Under Article 7 of Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, which we read on the Government portal, riding without lights in rain or poor visibility is fined VND 200,000–400,000 as of October 2026. Decree 238/2026/ND-CP, which amends the decree from 15 August 2026, does not change Article 7.

## What to tell the shop

If the bike went through water, tell the shop, even if it seems fine. Water can cause problems later, such as rust, a failing battery or a rough engine, and staff can check the bike. Describe where you crossed, how deep it was, whether the engine stalled and what you did. ## FAQ: standing water on a small bike

### How deep can I ride through?

Honda's manuals give no wading depth. A cautious rule is to avoid water that would reach the bottom of the wheel hub and to avoid any water you cannot see through.

### Should I speed up through a puddle?

No. A fast entry throws up a bow wave and can push water into the exhaust. Go slowly with a steady throttle in a low gear.

### What should I do after crossing water?

Dry the brakes by applying them lightly at low speed, as Honda's manual advises, and check the lights and engine.

### What if the engine stalls in water?

Do not keep cranking. Push the bike to dry ground, wait, and try the starter once or twice. Call the shop if it will not start.

### Is it safe to ride in a flooded underpass?

No. Water can be deep, hide hazards and stall the bike.

### Should I tell the shop if the bike got wet?

Yes. Water can cause delayed problems, and the shop can check the bike.
