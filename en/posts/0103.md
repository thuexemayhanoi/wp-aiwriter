A rented manual bike such as a Honda Wave, Yamaha Sirius or Honda Blade is a good choice for a tour outside Hanoi when four things line up: you are a confident rider who has already spent a day on that kind of bike, the route is on ordinary roads with gentle gradients, your luggage is light and the shop has agreed to the trip. It is a poor choice when any of those is missing, above all for steep mountain roads, heavy loads or a first long ride. These bikes are small, simple and economical, but they are also light on power and carry very little. This guide gives a five-question scorecard, shows what Honda's own figures say about the bike, compares three kinds of trip and lists the alternatives when the answer is "not this bike".

## The short answer

Think of the decision as a scorecard rather than a yes or no. Score each of the five questions below as green, amber or red. All green or mostly green with one amber means go, with preparation. Two ambers means shorten the trip or change the plan. Any red on questions 1 to 3 means choose a different bike, a different route or a different way to travel.

The questions are about you, the route, the load, the time and the support behind you. The rest of this guide explains how to score each one.

## What the bike actually is

Honda Vietnam lists the Wave RSX FI with a 110 cc, four-stroke, single-cylinder engine, a maximum power of 6.46 kW at 7,500 rpm, a four-speed gearbox and a 4.0 litre tank. Its quoted fuel use is 1.56 litres per 100 km, a manufacturer's test figure that real riding with a load and hills will not match. The bike weighs about 98 kg, sits 760 mm off the ground and has 135 mm of ground clearance.

Those numbers describe a light, frugal commuter. On paper the tank would take it about 250 km, but nobody should plan a day around that, and the bike's real strength is that it is cheap to run, easy to ride and simple for any roadside mechanic to understand.

The weaknesses follow from the same numbers. Six kilowatts is modest for a loaded bike on a long climb. The Wave manuals give a maximum load of 115 kg or 155 kg depending on version, covering rider, passenger, luggage and accessories together, and a limit of 5 kg for the storage box. The brakes, tyres and suspension are sized for a small bike at modest speed.

Nguyen Ha's manual bikes are Honda Wave, Yamaha Sirius and Honda Blade, and the shop confirms which model you actually get when you book. Our comparison of [Honda Wave and Yamaha Sirius](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/honda-wave-vs-yamaha-sirius-rental/) goes into the differences.

## Question 1: how much have you ridden this kind of bike?

This is the most important question and the one people answer most generously.

- **Green:** you have ridden a semi-automatic or manual bike regularly and spent at least a full day on this kind of bike in Hanoi traffic.
- **Amber:** you ride manual bikes at home but have not yet ridden a semi-automatic, or you have only done an hour of city riding.
- **Red:** you have never ridden a manual bike, or you are still learning to read Hanoi junctions.

A tour is a poor place to learn. A practice day on the semi-automatic, with our [10-minute inspection checklist](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-scooter-inspection-checklist/) and a quiet loop near the shop, is the way to turn an amber into a green, and our piece on [whether a new rider can learn semi-automatic shifting in Hanoi traffic](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/can-a-new-rider-learn-semi-automatic-shifting-in-hanoi-traffic/) explains why a red should wait.

## Question 2: what is the route like?

Score the road, not the destination name.

- **Green:** mostly flat or gently rolling roads on a main route that motorbikes may use, in daylight.
- **Amber:** a long climb, a mix of rough road or an exposed highway with heavy lorries.
- **Red:** steep, winding mountain roads, unsealed tracks or a route that depends on an expressway.

Motorbikes may not use expressways. Under Law 36/2024/QH15, in the consolidated text of 23 March 2026, motorcycles and mopeds are not allowed to enter an expressway, apart from maintenance staff and vehicles. That matters because many car routes out of Hanoi use one. Our guide to [Hanoi's ring roads and highways](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-ring-roads-highways-motorbikes-allowed/) shows which roads are open and our note on [navigation apps that suggest an expressway](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-check-if-a-navigation-app-suggests-an-expressway/) explains how to avoid the trap.

Speed limits also shape a day. Circular 38/2024/TT-BGTVT, effective 1 January 2025, sets general limits for motor vehicles of 60 km/h on divided roads in built-up areas and 50 km/h on two-way roads without a median. Outside built-up areas the table gives motorcycles 70 km/h on divided roads and 60 km/h on two-way roads. A 110 cc bike with a load will often run below those numbers on a climb, so allow for lorries passing you.

For a steep road, read our guide to [manual bikes and steep mountain roads](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/manual-bikes-and-steep-mountain-roads-when-to-choose-another-vehicle/) before you decide.

## Question 3: how much are you carrying?

- **Green:** one rider, a small bag and a rain jacket, comfortably under Honda's 5 kg luggage figure plus a light day pack.
- **Amber:** one rider with a medium bag strapped on, or two light riders with a small bag, within the maximum load.
- **Red:** two adults with luggage, or a rider and a big pack approaching the 115 kg or 155 kg limit.

Work out the sum rather than guessing. ## Question 4: how much time do you have?

Time decides how many kilometres you ask of the bike and of yourself.

- **Green:** a short day with long margins and a return well before dark.
- **Amber:** a full day that leaves little slack, or a two-day trip with a fixed deadline.
- **Red:** a schedule that needs you to arrive at a set time regardless of weather.

Small bikes are slow, and breaks, fuel stops and traffic add more time than the map suggests. Our guide to [planning breaks on a long semi-automatic bike ride](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-plan-breaks-on-a-long-semi-automatic-bike-ride/) and the note on [when to refuel during a day trip](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/when-to-refuel-a-manual-motorbike-during-a-day-trip/) turn this into a plan.

Remember that a rental day is 24 hours from handover, that shorter use is charged as a full day and that late returns are charged by the hour for the first five hours and as an extra day after that. For several days compare the weekly package, which is priced as its own product rather than as seven single days, on the [prices page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/prices/).

## Question 5: who is behind you if it goes wrong?

- **Green:** the shop has agreed the destination in writing, you have the shop's number saved and a plan for a puncture or a breakdown.
- **Amber:** agreement given by message only, or a route with long gaps between towns.
- **Red:** no agreement, no plan and no data signal on parts of the route.

Whether a Hanoi rental may leave the city is a contract question, not a road question, and the answer comes from the shop. ## Three kinds of trip, compared

| Trip profile | Typical shape | Manual bike fit | What to watch |
|---|---|---|---|
| Day trip on flat roads | Out and back before dark, such as the routes to Ninh Binh or Ba Vi | Good for confident riders | Daylight cutoff, fuel, lorries |
| Two or three days with one overnight | A loop with a hill section, such as Mai Chau or Tam Dao | Workable with light luggage and a steady rider | Brakes on descents, weather, the shop's agreement |
| A week or more, or mountains | Long loops through the north | Usually the wrong tool | Power, load, servicing and time |

## What makes a manual bike a good touring tool

The reasons riders still choose a Wave-type bike are real:

- **Fuel economy.** The tank is small but fuel use is low.
- **Simplicity.** Fewer parts, easy repairs and widespread familiarity at roadside workshops.
- **Light weight.** It is easy to push, turn and pick up.
- **Modest running cost.** Manual rentals have their own rates on the [prices page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/prices/), and the shop confirms the final price on the actual bike.
- **Predictable gearing.** A four-speed gearbox gives engine braking on descents, which our guide to [engine braking on a semi-automatic motorcycle](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/engine-braking-on-a-semi-automatic-motorcycle/) explains.

If the scorecard gives you a red or two ambers, you have options:

## Before you commit

1. Score the five questions honestly.
2. Spend a day on the bike in Hanoi first.
3. Ask the shop in writing whether the trip is allowed.
4. Check tyres, brakes, lights and chain, using our [inspection checklist](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-scooter-inspection-checklist/).
5. Plan fuel and break stops, and save the shop's number.
6. Set a turn-back time and keep to it.

## FAQ: touring outside Hanoi on a manual bike

### Is a Honda Wave powerful enough for a long trip?

On flat or rolling roads with light luggage, yes. Its 110 cc engine and 6.46 kW output, per Honda Vietnam, are modest, so steep climbs with a load are where it struggles.

### Can I ride a rental motorbike to another province?

Only if the shop agrees. That is a contract question, and the shop is the only one who can answer it.

### Can I take a manual bike on an expressway?

No. Motorcycles and mopeds are not allowed on expressways under Law 36/2024/QH15.

### How far can a Wave go on a tank?

Honda's test figure suggests roughly 250 km on 4.0 litres, but real figures are lower. Plan to refuel well before that.

### Is a manual bike better than an automatic for touring?

Neither is universally better. The manual bike is simple and frugal, while an automatic scooter has more storage.
