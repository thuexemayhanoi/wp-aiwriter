If a rented 50cc bike stops working, the safe order is: get yourself and the bike off the traffic lane, switch the key off, look for anything obviously wrong such as a flat tyre, leaking fluid or a dead dashboard, and then phone the shop before you try anything else. A 50cc moped is light enough to wheel to a pavement or a quiet side street, and most stoppages on small bikes come from a short list of causes: no fuel, a flooded engine, a weak battery or a loose connection, a blown fuse or a punctured tyre. Some of these you can check in two minutes, and some should never be attempted by a renter. This guide explains how to tell the difference, which checks Honda itself puts in its owner's manuals, what to avoid, and what information to give the shop. It is general information and not a repair manual.

## The first minute: get out of the traffic

What you do in the first minute matters more than the cause of the problem. Hanoi traffic flows around a stopped scooter, but a rider crouching over a bike in the middle of a lane is easy to miss at dusk or in rain.

1. **Slow down gradually and signal.** If the engine cuts out while you are moving, keep your hands on the bars, use both brakes smoothly and coast towards the kerb. Do not brake hard across the lane.
2. **Stop where you are visible.** A pavement edge, a side street or a guarded parking area is better than a bend, a bridge approach or the front of a junction.
3. **Switch the key off.** Honda's owner's manuals say to turn the ignition to Off before assessing a problem.
4. **Put the bike on its stand on firm, level ground** and step away from the traffic side.
5. **Check yourself and any passenger first.** If anyone is hurt, you are no longer dealing with a breakdown. Get help for the person before the bike.

The kerb is also a good place to stand while you make the phone call, so keep the bike between you and the road, not the other way round.

## Look before you touch: a one-minute visual check

Honda's Wave RSX FI manual says that, if you want to keep riding after something has gone wrong, you should first turn the key off and assess the machine: look for oil or fluid leaks, check that important nuts and bolts are tight, and test the steering, controls, brakes and wheels. It adds that damage can exist where you cannot see it, so any further riding should be very slow and careful. That manual is for a 110cc model, not a 50cc, but the sequence is sensible for any small bike.

Look at the following:

- **Tyres:** a flat or soft tyre, a nail or screw in the tread, a bulge on the sidewall.
- **Chain or belt area:** on a Cub-style bike, a chain hanging loose or off its sprocket; on a twist-and-go scooter, a new grinding noise or no drive when you twist the throttle.
- **Fluid on the ground:** a dark patch below the engine or a petrol smell near the tank.
- **Controls:** a brake lever that pulls to the grip, a throttle that does not spring back, handlebars that feel loose.
- **Dashboard and lights:** whether the lights come on when you turn the key on, which tells you whether the battery is alive.

If anything on that list is wrong, the bike should not be ridden further. Take photographs and call the shop.

## Which problem do you have?

| What happened | Common causes | What a renter can do |
|---|---|---|
| Engine cranks but will not start | No fuel, flooded engine, fouled spark plug, blocked fuel supply | Check the fuel level, try the standard starting steps, then call the shop |
| Starter button does nothing, lights dim or dead | Weak battery, loose battery connection, blown fuse | Check the key, the stand switch and kill switch, then call; do not take the seat or panels apart |
| Engine dies at idle or when you stop | Idle setting, fuel supply, dirty plug | Restart once; if it repeats, stop riding and call |
| Loss of power, bike will not climb | Slipping drive belt or clutch, fuel supply problem | Stop riding; it will not recover on its own |
| Warning light or flashing fault light | Electronic fuel injection fault, on bikes that have it | Ride slowly to a safe place and call the shop |
| Flat tyre | Puncture, valve leak | Do not ride on it; wheel the bike to a tyre repair stall or call |
| Chain off or broken | Wear, slack, lack of lubricant | Stop, keep clear of the rear wheel and do not refit it yourself |
| Brake feels wrong | Cable stretch, worn pads or shoes | Stop riding; ask for another bike |

## If it will not start: the checks Honda puts in writing

Honda Vietnam's owner's manual for the Wave RSX FI has a troubleshooting page for an engine that does not start. For a starter motor that turns the engine but does not fire it, Honda lists three checks: whether the standard starting procedure was followed, whether there is petrol in the tank, and, on fuel-injected bikes, whether the fuel-injection fault light is on. If that light is on, the manual says to contact the dealer as soon as possible.

For a starter that does nothing at all, the manual lists the standard procedure again, then whether the fuse has blown, whether the battery connections are loose or the terminals corroded, and the condition of the battery. If the fault continues, it says to take the bike to the dealer.

Translated into renter's language, the checks are:

1. **Is there fuel?** Open the seat or filler cap only as the shop showed you, and look. Do not use a lit match or lighter to see inside.
2. **Did you follow the starting steps?** The ignition is on, the bike is in neutral or the brake lever is held if the bike requires it, and the throttle is fully closed.
3. **Is the stand or kill switch in the wrong position?** Many small bikes will not start with the stand down or the engine cut-off switch off.
4. **Do the lights and horn work?** If they are dead, suspect the battery or its connection, and stop there.

### The five-second rule and the flooded engine

Honda's manual says that if the engine does not start within five seconds, you should turn the key off, wait ten seconds and try again. This lets the battery voltage recover. Holding the button down for long periods drains a weak battery further and can make things worse.

The same manual describes a procedure for an engine that will not start after several tries, which suggests it may be flooded with petrol: key off, throttle fully open, use the kick starter a few times, then repeat the normal start. If it starts, ease the throttle if the idle is rough. That procedure is Honda's for that model, and a carburettor 50cc may behave differently, so treat it as an idea to mention to the shop, not a script to follow forever. If three tries do not work, stop. Repeated cranking is a common way to turn a minor problem into a flat battery.

## Fuel: the roadside bottle problem

In Vietnam, bottled petrol is sold at roadside stalls, and a rider who runs dry may be tempted to buy a litre from the nearest one. Honda's manual warns against old, dirty petrol, against petrol mixed with engine oil and against letting dirt or water into the tank. It asks for unleaded petrol of the recommended octane rating. If you must refuel from a bottle, use a stall that looks busy and clean, and say so to the shop afterwards. Better still, refuel at a station and keep to the bike's usual range; our guide to [petrol stations and filling up a rental scooter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/fuel-in-hanoi-petrol-stations-rental-scooter/) explains the routine. Nguyen Ha hands petrol bikes over with a small amount of fuel, so check the gauge early in the day.

## Battery and lights

A weak battery announces itself in familiar ways: a slow, tired crank, dim lights that brighten when the engine revs, or an engine that starts only on the kick starter. Honda's guidance says to charge a flat battery with a charger made for motorcycles, to remove it before charging, and not to use a car battery charger because it can overheat and damage it. It also recommends against jump-starting a motorcycle from a car battery, since that can damage the bike's electrical system. A renter should therefore not accept a "jump" from a passing car. If a bike has a kick starter, try it while you wait for the shop to decide what to do. Honda's manual notes that the battery on its model is maintenance-free and should not be opened, and that terminals with white deposits can be cleaned by a mechanic.

If a bulb has blown, Honda's manual says to switch the key off, let the bulb cool, use only the specified type and test the replacement before riding. On a rental you should not be removing headlight units. Tell the shop, and avoid riding after dark with a headlight or brake light out.

## What not to do

- **Do not keep cranking.** Repeated starter use flattens the battery, and a flooded engine gets wetter.
- **Do not ride on a flat tyre.** Honda says that tube or tyre repair needs special tools and skill and recommends leaving it to the dealer. If a temporary repair is made, the manual advises riding slowly and not faster than 50 km/h until the tyre or tube is replaced. A 50cc moped's own speed cap is lower than that in any case.
- **Do not let a roadside mechanic strip the bike.** Small repairs may be fine if the shop agrees. Taking panels, the carburettor or the electrical system apart is not something a renter should authorise.
- **Do not fit a higher-rated fuse.** Honda warns that a higher rating raises the risk of electrical damage.
- **Do not add electrical accessories.** Honda notes that non-genuine electrical accessories can overload the system and drain the battery.
- **Do not tow with a rope from another scooter.** It is dangerous for both riders and for the bike.
- **Do not abandon the bike without telling the shop.** An unattended rental can be stolen, towed or damaged.

## Pushing, wheeling and waiting

One advantage of a 50cc bike is its weight. For a sense of scale, Honda Global lists the Japanese-market Super Cub 50 Pro at 108 kg kerb weight, which is far less than a large scooter. A 50cc rental is likely to be a light machine as well, though the actual bike you are given may differ. With the engine off, the steering unlocked and the stand up, most adults can wheel such a bike a short distance on level ground. Keep the bike on the pavement edge, walk it on the traffic-facing side only if you must, and ask a passer-by for help before pushing uphill or over a kerb. If the bike is heavy, or if the problem could be a seized wheel or drive, do not force it.

If you have to wait, stand away from the traffic, keep the key and helmet with you, and keep the phone charged. In heat or rain, wait under cover and keep the bike visible. If you have a passenger, ask them to stand at a safe distance from the road.

## What to tell the shop when you call

Call Nguyen Ha on 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969). The shop is open 09:00 to 21:00 daily, so a breakdown outside those hours may have to wait for the next morning; for that reason, avoid ending a ride far away late in the evening. The shop will tell you what happens next. Do not assume a replacement bike or a mechanic will be sent, since delivery is arranged only for weekly and monthly rentals and anything else is agreed with the shop. Have these facts ready:

- The bike's number plate and, if you know it, the model.
- Where you are: a map pin, a street name or a landmark.
- What happened, in plain words: "the engine stopped and will not restart", "the lights are dead", "the rear tyre is flat".
- Whether you fell, whether anyone is hurt and whether the bike is in a safe place.
- What you have already tried.
- How long you have had the bike and the fuel you last put in it.

Send photographs of the dashboard, the tyre or the damage. Keep any receipt if a repair is approved, and do not pay for work the shop has not agreed to. ## FAQ: 50cc rental breakdowns

### Who should I call first if my 50cc rental breaks down?

The shop. Call Nguyen Ha on 0334 699 969 before you accept help from a roadside mechanic, so that the shop can tell you what is acceptable and what is not.

### Can I try to fix a small fault myself?

Only the simple checks: fuel, key, stand and kill switch, and the starting steps. Leave the battery, fuses, bulbs, tyres and the chain to a mechanic. Honda's manuals say that tyre repair needs special tools.

### What if the engine will not start after several tries?

Stop. Honda says to wait ten seconds after five seconds of cranking, and repeated attempts drain the battery. Call the shop and describe what you have tried.

### Can I jump-start a 50cc bike from a car?

Honda recommends against it, because it can damage the electrical system. Use a charger made for motorcycles, ideally through the shop.

### Is it safe to ride on a temporary tyre repair?

Honda advises riding slowly and carefully, not above 50 km/h, until the tyre or tube is replaced. Ask the shop before you decide to ride at all.

### What if it happens at night or after the shop has closed?

Move the bike to a lit, guarded place, take photographs, lock the steering and call the shop as soon as it reopens at 09:00. Message the shop details in the meantime.
