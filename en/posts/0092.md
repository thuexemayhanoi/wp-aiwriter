If you drop a rented semi-automatic bike, check yourself first, make the scene safe, then lift the bike using your legs and hips, inspect it before you start it and call the shop straight away, even if the damage looks small. Most drops at low speed cause scuffs and bent levers rather than serious harm, and shops see them often. What makes a small drop expensive is not telling the shop, riding on with a bent part or arguing about when it happened. This guide sets out what to do in order: injuries, safety, lifting with Honda's method, a damage check, photographs, the call to the shop, what the contract may say and how to avoid the next drop. It is general information, not medical or legal advice.

## Step 1: check yourself and anyone with you

Before the bike, the people. Stay where you are for a moment and check whether you are hurt: head, neck, back, wrists, ankles. Adrenaline can hide pain. If you or a passenger are injured, do not move unnecessarily and get help. In Vietnam the emergency numbers are 113 for the police, 114 for the fire service and 115 for an ambulance. Our guide to [traffic fines that catch foreign riders out](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/traffic-fines-vietnam-foreign-riders/) explains what happens if a collision involves other people.

If another vehicle or a pedestrian was involved, or someone is hurt, the situation is a traffic accident, not just a drop. Under Article 7 of Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, which we read on the Government portal, a rider involved in an accident who does not stop at once, keep the scene and help the injured is fined VND 1,000,000–2,000,000, and a rider who causes an accident and fails to stop, stay at the scene or report to the police or local authorities is fined VND 8,000,000–10,000,000, as of October 2026. Decree 238/2026/ND-CP, which amends the decree from 15 August 2026, does not change Article 7. Stop, help and report.

## Step 2: make the scene safe

If you are in the road, get yourself and the bike out of the traffic as soon as it is safe. Switch off the engine and the key at once. A fallen bike can leak fuel, and a running engine or a spark is a risk. Do not smoke. If there is a fuel smell or a puddle of petrol, keep people and ignition sources away and be careful when you lift the bike.

Put on hazard lights if the bike has them, or ask someone to warn traffic. Do not stand in the lane. If you are on a slope or soft ground, take extra care when you lift. Our guide to [safely parking a semi-automatic motorbike on a slope](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/safely-parking-a-semi-automatic-motorbike-on-a-slope/) explains how stands and slopes interact.

## Step 3: lift the bike safely

A small bike weighs about 98 kilograms, and lifting it badly is a common way to hurt your back. Honda Vietnam's safe-riding training document has a method. In both cases the key is switched off first, and you use your hips and legs.

If the bike has fallen to the left, hold the handlebars with both hands and turn the front wheel to the left, squeeze the front brake, lift the bike in one movement using your hip to help and, once it is upright, deal with the side stand and turn the bars fully to the left.

If it has fallen to the right, switch off the key, deal with the side stand, turn the handlebars to the left, lift the bike in one movement using your hip to help and squeeze the front brake once it is upright, then turn the bars fully to the left.

Practical points:

- **Bend your knees, keep your back straight** and lift with your legs.
- **Squeeze the front brake** so the bike does not roll as it comes up.
- **Ask for help** if the bike is on a slope, if it is on top of someone or if you cannot lift it.
- **Do not lift by the mirror, lever or plastic panel.** Use the frame, the grab rail and the handlebars.

## Step 4: inspect before you start

Do not start the engine until you have looked at the bike. Honda's training document has a pre-ride checklist, and after a drop it is worth repeating in full. Look at:

- **Levers.** Are the brake lever and clutch-free gear lever bent, cracked or loose? Does the brake lever return?
- **Handlebars and mirrors.** Are the bars straight, and are the mirrors in place and adjustable? Our guide to [adjusting mirrors on a Honda Wave rental](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-adjust-mirrors-on-a-honda-wave-rental/) explains the set-up.
- **Footrests and gear lever.** Is the gear lever bent or pushed in? Are the footrests intact?
- **Indicators, headlight and brake light.** Are the lenses cracked, and do they work?
- **Fuel.** Is there a leak from the tank, cap or line? Is the cap on tight?
- **Oil and other fluids.** Any drips under the engine?
- **Tyres and wheels.** Any cuts, bulges or a bent rim? Honda advises inspecting tyres after any impact. Spin the front wheel if you can and watch for a wobble.
- **Chain and chain guard.** Is the guard bent into the chain?
- **Frame and forks.** Any visible bend, or a fork that looks twisted?
- **Exhaust.** Is it dented or loose?

If you see a bent lever, a cracked light, a fuel leak, a damaged wheel or anything you are unsure about, do not ride on.

## Step 5: take photographs

Photograph the bike where it fell, then from all sides, and take close-ups of any damage: scuffs, cracks, bent parts. Photograph the surface and the surroundings if they explain what happened, for example gravel, oil or a pothole. Keep the photos with their time stamps. They protect both you and the shop, because they record the damage and when it happened. Compare them with the photographs you took at handover; our [10-minute inspection checklist](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-scooter-inspection-checklist/) explains how to record the bike's condition at pickup.

## Step 6: call the shop

Call the shop on 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969), open 09:00 to 21:00 daily, as soon as you are safe. Give clear facts:

- your name, the bike and its plate number, and where you are;
- whether anyone is hurt;
- what happened, simply and honestly;
- what the damage is and whether the bike will start and move;
- whether anyone else or another vehicle was involved.

Send photos if you can. The staff will say whether you can ride slowly to the shop, whether they will send someone or whether the bike should not be moved. Follow their advice, and do not ride a bike with a fuel leak, a bad brake or a bent wheel. Do not try to hide the drop. Shops see these bikes daily, and damage is obvious to staff.

## Step 7: starting the bike, if the shop agrees

If the shop says it is fine to try, check that it is in neutral and that the key and kill switch are on. Honda's start procedure is key on, neutral with the N indicator lit, throttle fully released, then the start switch. If the engine does not start within five seconds, switch off and wait ten seconds. A bike that has been on its side may need a moment, because petrol can flood. Honda's flooding procedure is key off, throttle fully open, several kicks, then start normally. Our guides to [what to check on a manual bike's electric starter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-to-check-on-a-manual-bikes-electric-starter/) and [how to use the kick starter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-use-the-kick-starter-on-an-older-rental-motorbike/) explain the steps. Ride off slowly and test the brakes gently. If anything feels wrong, stop.

## What the contract may say

Rental contracts usually make the renter responsible for damage during the rental, charged at the cost of parts and labour, apart from ordinary wear. Our explanation of [what the clauses in a Hanoi rental contract mean](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-motorbike-rental-contract-explained/) covers the damage and theft clauses, and our guide to [returning your rental motorbike in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/returning-your-rental-motorbike-hanoi/) explains how damage is assessed at return. Typical points to ask:

- Who decides the cost of the repair, and can you see the part and receipt?
- Is the charge taken from the deposit, or could it exceed it?
- Does the shop have a fixed price for common parts such as a mirror or a lever?
- What happens if the bike cannot be ridden back?

Do not agree on a figure at the roadside without understanding it. Ask for the amount in writing. ## Reducing the chance of a drop

Most drops happen at low speed, at a stop, on a slope or when parking. A few habits help:

- **Put both feet down when stopping** on uneven or slippery ground.
- **Use the side stand on firm, level ground,** turn the bars fully left and lock them, as Honda's manual advises.
- **Do not park on a slope** if you can avoid it.
- **Watch for oil, gravel and wet paint** at junctions.
- **Practise slow turns** in a quiet place. Honda's balance advice is to use a low gear, keep your head up and use your hips.
- **Do not overload the bike.** Honda warns it affects handling and stability.

## FAQ: dropping a rented bike

### Should I tell the shop even if the damage is small?

Yes, straight away. Small damage is obvious to staff, and honesty avoids disputes.

### How do I lift a fallen bike?

Switch off the key, turn the front wheel to the left, squeeze the front brake and lift using your hip and legs, as Honda's training document describes. Ask for help if you need it.

### Can I ride the bike after a drop?

Only if you have inspected it and the shop agrees. Do not ride with a bent lever, a fuel leak, a damaged wheel or a bad brake.

### What if someone else was involved?

Stop, help anyone hurt, keep the scene and call 113 or 115 as needed. Failing to stop after an accident carries large fines.

### Who pays for the damage?

It depends on the contract. Ask the shop how damage is charged and keep records.

### Should I take photographs?

Yes. Photograph the bike, the damage and the place, and compare them with your handover photos.
