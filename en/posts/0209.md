Legally, almost nothing changes between a 50cc petrol bike and an electric moped, because Vietnam treats both as mopeds. What changes is everything about how you power and live with the bike: fuel against a battery, a rattle against near silence, a quick refill against a charging plan and a gear shift on some Cubs against none at all. Both have a 40 km/h road limit, need no motorcycle licence, require a helmet and carry one rider. The risks are different too, so a petrol bike asks you to watch the fuel, while an electric moped asks you to watch the range and the speed rating. This guide explains the differences point by point so you can choose, and shows how to check that an electric scooter really is a moped.

## What stays the same

Law 36/2024/QH15, in force since 1 January 2025, defines a moped in Article 34 as a two- or three-wheeled motor vehicle with a design speed of no more than 50 km/h. If its engine is a heat engine, the capacity may not exceed 50 cm3, and if it is electric, the motor may not exceed 4 kW. The same rules then apply to both:

- **Licence and age.** No motorcycle licence is needed for a moped, and Article 59 sets the minimum age at 16.
- **Helmet.** The rider and any passenger must wear a helmet that meets the national standard, with the strap fastened (Article 33).
- **People on board.** One person, with a second allowed only in the cases listed, such as a child under 12.
- **Speed.** A moped may not exceed 40 km/h on the road under Circular 38/2024/TT-BGTVT, even if the design speed is higher.
- **Everything else.** Lights, lanes, one-way streets, red lights and the ban on riding on pavements.

So the choice is not about the law. It is about the ride.

The table is a guide, not a promise about any one bike, because models vary. The important point is that both fall in the same legal class but need different habits.

## Riding feel and noise

A petrol 50cc makes a sound that tells pedestrians and other riders you are there. An electric moped does not, and in a Hanoi alley full of people and scooters, silence changes how others react. Use the horn early and lightly, slow down in alleys and markets and assume people do not hear you. The motor also delivers its power smoothly from a standstill, so a careless twist at a junction launches the bike faster than you expect. Practise pulling away gently in a quiet place first.

A Cub-style petrol bike adds a foot gear shift, which a new rider must learn, while a 50cc petrol scooter and an electric moped are both twist-and-go. If you want the simplest bike to learn, those two are the easiest. Our guide to [renting a 50cc scooter in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/renting-a-50cc-scooter-in-hanoi/) describes the petrol kinds in more depth.

## Is your electric scooter really a moped?

This is the most important check. A scooter sold as an electric bike may be legally a motorcycle. The moped limits are a design speed of up to 50 km/h and a motor of up to 4 kW. A faster or more powerful electric scooter is a motorcycle, and Article 57 of the law says a class A1 licence covers two-wheeled motorcycles up to 125 cc or with an electric motor of up to 11 kW.

Before you ride, ask:

1. What are the maximum speed and the motor power in the vehicle papers?
2. Is the bike registered, and does the plate match the papers?
3. Is the bike in the moped class, and do I need a licence to ride it?

If the answers are unclear, ask the shop to show the registration papers or choose a petrol 50cc. The shop's fleet includes 50cc-equivalent electric scooters and some VinFast electric motorbikes, and the bike that is actually available is confirmed when you book, so tell the shop which licences you hold. ## Hanoi's low-emission zone and what it means for you

Hanoi launched a pilot low-emission zone on 1 July 2026. In its first zone, which covers the Hoan Kiem Lake pedestrian area, the Old Quarter and the Hoan Kiem night market, VOV reports that all motorbikes, motorcycles and cars are banned from 7 p.m. to midnight every Friday, Saturday and Sunday. That ban applies to petrol and electric alike. In the wider Old Quarter zone, restrictions on fossil-fuel vehicles are to be introduced gradually.

Vietnamnet reported in May 2026 that the city dropped plans for an outright petrol ban and that private petrol motorcycles would remain permitted in the pilot phase. The plan expands in later phases, starting with a larger area in 2027. For a visitor this suggests two practical points. First, plan weekend evenings around the closure whichever bike you ride. Second, electric bikes are likely to face fewer restrictions as the zone grows, but the details are still developing, so check current notices. Our guide to [riding in the Old Quarter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-old-quarter/) explains the streets.

## Rain, hills and loads

Both types can be ridden in the rain with care, and wet roads lengthen braking distances on either. Avoid standing water on any bike, since it can stall an engine or damage electrics, and our guide to [riding in Hanoi in the rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) has the details. Hills and loads drain a battery faster, so if you plan to carry luggage or climb, expect less range than on a flat, empty ride. A petrol bike simply slows down.

## Handover checklist for either bike

1. Confirm the bike's class, speed and power, and ask for the papers.
2. Walk round and photograph scratches with the staff.
3. Check brakes, lights, indicators, horn and mirrors.
4. For petrol: confirm the fuel level and how the bike is started.
5. For electric: confirm the battery level, the display, the charger and the plan for charging.
6. Ask the shop's number and opening hours (09:00 to 21:00), and save 0334 699 969.
7. Take the helmet and ask how the bike is locked.

## FAQ: petrol 50cc or electric moped

### Do I need a licence for an electric moped?

Not if it meets the moped limits of a design speed up to 50 km/h and a motor up to 4 kW. A more powerful or faster electric scooter is a motorcycle and needs the right licence.

### Is an electric moped faster than a 50cc petrol bike?

The legal road limit is the same, 40 km/h for both. Electric motors can feel quicker away from a standstill, but the limit does not change.

### Which is cheaper to rent at Nguyen Ha?

They are listed differently: a 50cc bike by the day, week and month, and electric by the week and month. Check the prices page and ask for a quote for your dates.

### Can I charge the battery in my hotel room?

Ask your hotel first, and read our charging guide for the safety advice. Never leave a charging battery unattended in a hallway.

### Do both need a helmet?

Yes. A helmet is compulsory on a moped, whether petrol or electric, for the rider and any passenger.
