When you return a rental scooter, photograph it in the same order and from the same angles as you did at pickup: eight overall views of the bike, close-ups of any marks, both tyres, the dashboard with the odometer and fuel or battery level, the key or fob and helmets you are handing back and any paper the shop signs. Do it before you hand over the key, in good light, and keep the photographs until the deposit is back in your hand. The reason is the same as at pickup. A rental is judged by comparing the bike at the end with the bike at the start, and a set of photographs taken at the moment of return is your evidence of what the bike looked like when it left your hands.

This guide gives a shot list in order, explains scooter-specific points such as the smart key and the under-seat compartment, covers electric scooters, shows how to take clear photographs, how to time them, how to store them and how to use them at the return counter. It is general information, not legal advice.

## Why photograph at return

At pickup you recorded the starting condition. At return, the bike is compared with that record. If a mark appears on the shop's inspection that you do not think was yours, or the shop disputes the fuel level or the odometer, your return photographs show the bike as you left it, with a time stamp.

Nguyen Ha's pages say that before leaving the shop both sides should inspect brakes, tyres, lights, mirrors, bodywork, accessories and the fuel or charge level and record the handover time, and that the deposit is reconciled on return according to the recorded condition. They also say that if a disagreement arises, you should keep copies of the signed agreement and handover photos and seek a documented resolution. Photographs at both ends make that possible. Our guides to [how to document existing scratches on a rental bike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-document-existing-scratches-on-a-rental-bike/) and [how to record the fuel level at handover](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-record-the-fuel-level-at-handover/) explain the pickup side.

## Before you start

- **Arrive with time to spare.** Photographs taken in a rush are poor. Our guide to [returning your rental motorbike in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/returning-your-rental-motorbike-hanoi/) explains how to time the return.

- **Choose good light.** Daylight, away from deep shade and strong glare, shows marks best.

- **Park on level ground** and put the bike on its stand.

- **Clear the bike:** remove bags, rain gear and anything from the compartment.

- **Have your pickup photographs open** on your phone, so you can match the angles.

- **Charge your phone** and check there is storage.

## The shot list

Take the photographs in this order. The first eight are the overall views.

| No. | Shot | What it shows |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Front, straight on | Headlight, front mudguard, apron, plate |
| 2 | Front left, at an angle | Left front side, mirror, panel |
| 3 | Left side, full length | Whole left side |
| 4 | Rear left, at an angle | Left rear, tail light, rear panel |
| 5 | Rear, straight on | Tail light, plate, rear mudguard |
| 6 | Rear right, at an angle | Right rear, indicators, exhaust |
| 7 | Right side, full length | Whole right side, footrest, chain or panel |
| 8 | Front right, at an angle | Right front side, mirror, panel |

Then add detail shots.

| No. | Shot | What it shows |
|---|---|---|
| 9 | Top of the bike | Seat, handlebar, instrument panel |
| 10 | Seat, close up | Cuts, tears or stains |
| 11 | Both mirrors | Cracks, scratches, looseness |
| 12 | Both handlebar grips and levers | Scuffs, bends |
| 13 | Footrests or floor | Scuffs and wear |
| 14 | Exhaust and guard | Dents, scrapes |
| 15 | Front tyre, tread and sidewall | Wear and damage |
| 16 | Rear tyre, tread and sidewall | Wear and damage |
| 17 | Dashboard, ignition on | Odometer and fuel gauge or battery level, warning lights |
| 18 | Key or fob, and helmets | What you are handing back |
| 19 | The number plate | The plate is intact and legible |
| 20 | Any marks, close up | Each mark with a finger or coin for scale |

Eight overall views and a dozen details take about five minutes. Our guide to [returning an automatic scooter: fuel and bodywork checklist](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/returning-an-automatic-scooter-fuel-and-bodywork-checklist/) lists what staff look at, and our [10-minute inspection checklist](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-scooter-inspection-checklist/) shows the pickup routine you are mirroring.

## Scooter-specific points

Scooters have parts that a manual bike does not, so look at these as well.

- **The front apron and leg shield.** These large plastic panels catch scrapes and cracks.

- **The floor or running board,** where shoes and bags leave marks.

- **The side panels,** which crack at their edges.

- **The under-seat compartment.** Open it, photograph it empty and check that you have taken your own things out. Honda's Vision manual describes a storage box under the seat, and what is left in it can easily be forgotten.

- **The front basket or hook,** if fitted.

- **The smart key or fob,** if the scooter has one. Photograph it with the bike's ignition so that it is clear which fob goes with which scooter. Our guide to [smart key versus traditional key on rental scooters](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/smart-key-versus-traditional-key-on-rental-scooters/) explains the differences. If you have more than one key or fob, photograph them all.

- **The mirrors,** which can be loose or turned.

## Electric scooters

For an electric bike or scooter, the charge level takes the place of fuel. Photograph the display showing the battery percentage or bars, and photograph the charger and any cable you are returning. The shop's published terms say that for electric bikes the battery level and charging arrangement are confirmed at handover. Our guide to [how pickup battery level is recorded on an electric scooter rental](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/electric-scooter-rental-how-pickup-battery-level-is-recorded/) explains the pickup side, so match it at return.

## Fuel and the odometer

These two numbers settle many questions.

- **The odometer.** Photograph the dashboard with the ignition on, close enough to read the digits. The kilometres you covered are the difference from the pickup reading.

- **The fuel gauge.** Photograph the needle or bars with the bike upright. The published terms say that petrol bikes come back with about 0.5 to 1 litre, and that an empty tank costs VND 20,000. Our guide to [how to record the fuel level at handover](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-record-the-fuel-level-at-handover/) explains why the bike should be upright and the ignition on.

## Taking clear photographs

- **Hold the phone steady,** and tap to focus on the area you want.

- **Fill the frame** with the bike or the part, and avoid cutting off the ends.

- **Avoid strong glare** on shiny plastic; change the angle a little.

- **Use a finger or coin** beside a mark for scale.

- **Take a second shot** if one looks blurred.

- **Keep the plate number visible** in at least some of the overall views, so it is clear which bike it is.

- **Do not edit or filter** the photographs. Keep the originals.

- **Do not use a flash** in bright light, because it can wash out scratches.

A short video walk-round, with you saying the date and the plate number, is a useful extra. It does not replace the photographs.

## When to photograph

Timing matters.

1. **Before you hand over the key,** while the bike is still in your care.

2. **At the place of return,** in or near the shop's yard, so that the location is clear.

3. **Before the walk-round with staff,** so that your record exists first.

4. **Again after the walk-round,** if any new mark is pointed out or any change is made, to record it.

5. **Before you leave,** photograph any paper the shop signs.

If staff are ready before you are, ask for a minute. A shop that sees these bikes daily will not mind.

## Photograph the paper

At the end of the return, record the paperwork as well.

- **The signed return note,** or the line on the contract showing the return and the time.

- **The deposit record:** the amount returned, any deduction and the reason, if written.

- **Any list of damage** the shop writes, with the signature visible.

Our guide to [what should be written on a rental payment receipt](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-should-be-written-on-a-rental-payment-receipt/) explains what the paper should show, and our guide to [how to check deposit return conditions before signing](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-check-deposit-return-conditions-before-signing/) explains what the deposit return should look like. If the shop does not write anything, write a short note yourself, ask staff to initial it and photograph it.

## Use the photographs at the walk-round

When you do the walk-round with staff:

- **Stand with them,** and compare the bike with the pickup record and photographs.

- **Point out anything you know about,** before they find it.

- **If a mark is queried,** show the pickup photograph and your return photographs.

- **If it is disputed,** ask for the shop's reasons in writing, and agree a documented resolution.

- **Do not hand over the key until you have your photographs and the paper.**

Vietnam's Civil Code (Law No. 91/2015/QH13), in force since 1 January 2017, says the lessee must return the leased property in the condition in which it was received, apart from natural wear, and that the lessor may claim compensation if its value has dropped (Article 482). We read the lease articles in the text published by the Tuyen Quang Department of Justice. As of October 2026 that is the general rule, and a record made at return, compared with the record at pickup, is the best evidence of the condition at both ends. This is general information, not legal advice.

## Storing and keeping them

- **Back up the same day** to a cloud account.

- **Keep them in one folder,** named by date.

- **Do not delete** them to free space.

- **Keep them until the deposit has been returned** and for a few weeks afterwards, because a traffic notice or a query can arrive late. Our guide to [who is responsible for parking and traffic fines](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/who-is-responsible-for-parking-and-traffic-fines/) explains why.

## A quick checklist

1. **Good light, level ground, bike cleared.**

2. **Eight overall views, in the same order as at pickup.**

3. **Mirrors, levers, seat, footrests, exhaust and plate.**

4. **Both tyres.**

5. **Dashboard with the odometer and fuel or battery level.**

6. **The under-seat compartment, empty.**

7. **The key or fob, and the helmets.**

8. **Any marks, close up with scale.**

9. **The signed paper.**

10. **Back up the same day.**

## FAQ: photographing a rental scooter at return

### How many photographs should I take?

About twenty: eight overall views, then details of the seat, mirrors, levers, footrests, exhaust, tyres, dashboard, key, helmets and any marks.

### Should I use the same angles as at pickup?

Yes. Matching angles make comparison simple.

### Do I need to photograph the dashboard?

Yes. The odometer and the fuel or battery level settle many questions.

### When should I take them?

Before you hand over the key and before the walk-round with staff.

### Should I photograph the paper the shop signs?

Yes. A signed return note or deposit record is part of the evidence.

### How long should I keep the photographs?

Until the deposit has been returned, and for a few weeks afterwards.
