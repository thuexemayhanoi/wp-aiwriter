Truc Bach is a small lake just north of the Old Quarter, and it is best treated as a place to ride slowly in, park once and then walk. The streets around the water are short, the lanes on and around Ngu Xa island are narrow, and the main road beside the lake, Thanh Nien, is a scenic causeway rather than a place to stop. A rented automatic scooter is a good way to arrive from another part of Hanoi, but a poor way to tour the cafes one by one. This guide explains where Truc Bach sits after the 2025 ward changes, how to approach it, how to read the small streets, where cafe stops work, when sunset crowds build up and what to do about parking. It is general information, not a live traffic report.

## Where Truc Bach is, and what the 2025 reorganisation changed

Truc Bach Lake lies in the Ba Dinh area, immediately south-east of West Lake. A single embankment road, Thanh Nien, separates the two lakes. Hanoi's tourism and press sources describe the two as once being one body of water, divided in the seventeenth century when local residents built a dyke that later became the road. Quan Thanh Temple and Tran Quoc Pagoda stand close together along that road, which is why many visitors treat the whole stretch as one outing.

Place names on maps and in older guides can confuse you. Under Resolution 1656/NQ-UBTVQH15, the old district level was dropped from 1 July 2025 and Hanoi now runs on 126 wards and communes. A Vietnamnet report on the plan says the new Ba Dinh ward absorbs the former Quan Thanh and Truc Bach wards. So "Truc Bach, Ba Dinh District" on an older page and "Ba Dinh ward" on a newer one point to the same neighbourhood. Street names did not change, and street names are what you navigate by.

## Reaching the lake without improvising

Truc Bach is a short ride from the Old Quarter, so approach roads are busy rather than long. One local guide describes a motorbike route through Hoang Hoa Tham, Phan Dinh Phung, Nguyen Bieu and Tran Vu. Treat that as a starting point and check one-way signs on the day. If you are coming from the east bank, where Nguyen Ha Motorbike Rental is based in Long Bien, cross the Red River by a bridge that allows motorbikes, then head through the Old Quarter edge and north. Our [Long Bien route guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/long-bien-red-river-first-ride/) describes an easy first crossing, and the [ring roads guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-ring-roads-highways-motorbikes-allowed/) explains which fast roads are closed to bikes.

Two practical points:

- **Set the destination to a street, not a cafe.** Search for "Thanh Nien" or "Truc Bach street" and choose your parking spot from there. A pin on a tiny lakeside cafe can route you into a lane that is a walkway at that hour.
- **Arrive in daylight the first time.** Lakeside lighting is pleasant but patchy, and our [night riding guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-at-night-in-hanoi/) explains why unfamiliar small streets are harder after dark.

## Reading the streets around the water

According to a Hanoi travel guide that describes the layout, Truc Bach Street and Tran Vu Street run around the lake and join Thanh Nien Street. That makes a rough loop, but it is not a racetrack. The lakeside is lined with cafes, small restaurants and strolling residents, and the west side is shaded by tall trees. Expect people walking or exercising in the road edge, stools spilling out from cafes and scooters parked at an angle.

A few habits make the area easy:

1. **Ride at walking speed beside the water.** Pedestrians step out between parked bikes without looking.
2. **Keep right and leave a gap.** Cafe doors open straight onto the carriageway in places.
3. **Do not try to circle the lake repeatedly.** If you miss a turn, carry on to the next one rather than reversing.
4. **Watch for bridges.** Several small bridges lead from the shore to the island, and they are narrow.

## Ngu Xa island and its lanes

Ngu Xa, the island inside the Truc Bach area, is connected to the shore by small bridges. Two travel guides describe it as a quiet mix of narrow lanes, old houses, small temples, street art and cafes. It was historically a bronze-casting craft village and is known today for pho cuon, rolled noodles with beef, and for puffy fried pho. One guide reports that the main street through the island is closed to motor traffic in the evening so that restaurants can set tables on the road.

That report is useful but not a guarantee. Barriers, hours and enforcement can change, so follow whatever the people on the street are doing. If chairs are across the road, park outside and walk in. If you are unsure whether a lane is open to bikes, it usually is not worth the risk, because the lanes are only as wide as a bike and a pedestrian.

Practical rules for the island:

- **Park at the edge and walk.** The island is small enough that walking takes minutes.
- **Never push through a table line.** Residents and restaurant staff will not expect an engine.
- **Take one bridge in and the same bridge out** if you are not sure where the others lead.

## Cafe stops that work from a scooter

The shore and island have lots of cafes, from small canal-side counters to terraces with lake views. Cafes open and close often, so this guide does not name specific ones. Instead, judge each stop with a short checklist:

| Check | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Is there a clear spot for bikes that is not on the pedestrian path? | Blocking the walkway is the main nuisance in narrow streets |
| Is the place on a one-way or tiny lane? | You may have to leave by a different route from the one you arrived on |
| Does a staff member direct parking? | Informal attendants often know where bikes are tolerated |
| Can you see the bike from your seat? | Useful for a short stop, though not a substitute for a lock |
| Will you be there past sunset? | Evening crowds make it harder to get out |

## Sunset on Thanh Nien Road

Thanh Nien is the road between the two lakes. Vietnamnet describes it as the only road between West Lake and Truc Bach Lake and as a favourite for sunset walks, and a Hanoi tourism writer calls it a classic spot for lake views on both sides. Local guides suggest arriving about half an hour before sunset.

For a scooter rider that has an obvious catch: everyone else has the same idea. The pavements fill with walkers and photographers, and cars and bikes slow down. Do not stop on the road itself for a picture. Park off the carriageway in a proper space and walk. Stopping tight to the kerb is the standard Hanoi rule, and Law 36/2024/QH15 requires that on city streets you stop or park close to the kerb, with the nearest wheel no more than 0.25 metres away, and without blocking pedestrians or other vehicles.

## Temples and dress

Quan Thanh Temple and Tran Quoc Pagoda are active places of worship. Tran Quoc stands on a small islet beside West Lake, at the end of a causeway off Thanh Nien Road. Travel sources give typical visiting hours of about 08:00 to 16:00 for Tran Quoc, with variations on festival days, and report that Quan Thanh is busiest on the first and fifteenth days of the lunar month. Treat hours as indicative and look at the posted notice.

Dress modestly with shoulders and knees covered. Park outside the temple gates rather than inside the grounds unless an attendant waves you in.

## Parking and the Ring Road 1 plan

Parking near the lake is usually informal, with attendants, pavement rows and guarded spaces in houses or lots. Our [parking guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-motorbike-in-hanoi/) explains tickets and what to keep. A few additions for Truc Bach:

- Do not park on the pedestrian side of the lakeside, which Law 36 reserves for people on foot.
- Avoid a bike across a lane mouth or bridge approach.
- Photograph the plate, spot and attendant if there is one.

There is also a policy point worth knowing. Hanoi has begun a low-emission zone inside Ring Road 1. As of October 2026 the pilot covers only parts of Hoan Kiem ward, with weekend evening limits on motor vehicles in its first zone, according to reports from VOV on 1 July 2026 and Lao Dong on 30 June 2026. Those reports say the zone is to widen to the whole of Ring Road 1 from 2028. The Truc Bach area is within the Ring Road 1 corridor on current plans, so the situation may change. Check a recent notice before a longer stay, and ask the shop about your bike.

## A relaxed half-day loop

This sequence avoids repeated stopping:

1. Ride in during mid-afternoon and park once near Thanh Nien Road, away from the temple entrances.
2. Walk to Tran Quoc Pagoda and Quan Thanh Temple.
3. Walk the lake edge toward Ngu Xa and have a cafe stop.
4. Stay for sunset on Thanh Nien, then walk back to the bike.
5. Leave before the busiest hour, or after it, rather than in the middle.

## FAQ: Truc Bach by scooter

### Can I ride around Truc Bach Lake on a scooter?

Yes, on the roads that circle it, at slow speed. Ngu Xa island and some lanes are narrow or closed to engines at times, so park and walk there.

### Is Truc Bach good for cafes?

It is known for lakeside cafes and small canal-side counters. They change often, so judge each by parking, access and noise rather than relying on a list.

### Where should I park near Thanh Nien Road?

Use a signed or attendant-run space off the carriageway. Do not use the pedestrian side of the lake, and keep clear of temple gates.

### Is Truc Bach affected by the Ring Road 1 low-emission plan?

As of October 2026 the pilot covers only parts of Hoan Kiem ward, but the plan extends to the whole Ring Road 1 area later. Check a recent notice.

### Is it better to visit at sunset?

It is beautiful but busy. Arrive earlier, park first and walk, and leave either before or well after the crowd.
