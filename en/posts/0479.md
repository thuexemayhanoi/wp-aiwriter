Stop and check the map whenever you need more than a glance: when you are lost, when the app changes the route, when you need to read a street name or a message, or when the road ahead looks wrong for a motorbike. A short look at a mounted screen is like checking a mirror. Anything longer, or anything that needs your thumb, belongs at the kerb. Article 18 of Law 36/2024/QH15 tells you where you may and may not stop, and Article 9 bans holding and using a phone while operating a vehicle on the road.

This guide explains the signs that tell you it is time to stop, where to stop in Hanoi and where not to, how to pull over and rejoin traffic, what to do while you are stopped, what the law says about stopping and the fines reported for stopping in the wrong place and what to do when the map and the road disagree. It is general information based on the law and published guidance as of October 2026, not legal advice.

## Signs that it is time to stop

Look for these signals in yourself and on the road.

- **You have missed a turn or are not sure which way to go.** Do not slow suddenly or U-turn in the lane. Keep going to a safe place and stop there.

- **The route has changed.** The app has recalculated, and you cannot take in the new route at a glance.

- **You cannot read the screen.** Glare, rain or a cracked glass makes you stare.

- **You need to read text,** such as a street name, an address or a message.

- **Two instructions conflict.** The map says turn left, but a sign says no entry or no motorbikes. Our guides to [understanding prohibited vehicle symbols on Vietnamese signs](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/understanding-prohibited-vehicle-symbols-on-vietnamese-signs/) and [how to check which roads restrict motorbikes](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-check-which-roads-restrict-motorbikes/) explain how to resolve it.

- **You are on an unfamiliar, busy road.** Roundabouts, multi-lane roads and junctions need your full attention. Our guides to [riding through a Hanoi roundabout](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-ride-through-a-hanoi-roundabout/) and [safe right turns at busy Hanoi intersections](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/safe-right-turns-at-busy-hanoi-intersections/) explain why.

- **You feel tired, stressed or hurried.** A glance at a map takes longer when you are tired, and you are more likely to make a mistake.

- **The battery is low or the signal drops,** and you need to change the setting.

- **A passenger is asking you something,** or someone is calling.

A simple rule: if your eyes leave the road for longer than a quick check of a mirror, or your hand has to touch the phone, stop first.

## What the law says about holding and using a phone

Article 9 bans holding in hands and using phones or other electronic devices while operating vehicles on the road. Vietnamese legal commentary says that a phone fixed in a holder that you only look at is not the banned act, but the commentary also stresses that you must still ride safely. If your phone is in your hand, in your pocket or in your lap, stop before you use it. Our guide to [scooter phone mounts: safer navigation without distraction](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/scooter-phone-mounts-safer-navigation-without-distraction/) explains the mount, the fine and the rules for earphones.

## Where you may stop

Article 18 sets general rules for stopping and parking on the road.

- **Stop close to the right edge** of the road in your direction of travel. On a street, the nearest wheel should be no more than 0.25 metre from the roadside or sidewalk.

- **Do not obstruct people or vehicles.** Your stop must not cause danger or block others.

- **Signal when you leave or enter the stopping place** so that others know your intention.

- **While stopping on the road,** the Law describes the operator as neither turning off the engine nor leaving the driver's seat, except for limited reasons such as opening and closing a door, loading or unloading, or carrying out technical checks, in which case the operator uses the parking brake or takes other safety measures. For a quick check you may stay on the bike. If you need a longer stop, park lawfully, put the stand down and switch off.

Good places for a quick map check include:

- **A wide street with a clear kerb** and no junction nearby.

- **A guarded parking place or a car-park entrance** where you are not blocking the flow, if the attendant allows it.

- **A quiet side street,** where you can stop at the right edge and still be seen.

- **A lay-by or a wide shoulder** outside the city.

- **A cafe or shop frontage with parking** where you are customer or a short-term visitor, if the staff do not mind.

## Where you may not stop

Article 18(4) bans stopping and parking in many places. The ones most relevant to a rider checking a map are:

| Where | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| On pedestrian crossings | People cannot pass, and drivers cannot see them |
| At intersections and within 5 metres of their edges | You block the view and the turn |
| On bridges, except where stopping is allowed | Narrow and often busy; strong wind adds risk. See our guide to [riding on bridges during strong winds](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-on-bridges-during-strong-winds/) |
| On bends and near the crest of a slope with obscured visibility | Others cannot see you in time |
| At pick-up and drop-off points | Buses and passengers need the space |
| In front of gates and within 5 metres of both sides of gates of offices with driveways | Vehicles need access |
| On road sections wide enough only for one motor vehicle lane | You block everyone |
| Under overpasses, and on the roadway or pavement where the law or a sign prohibits it | Poor visibility and limited space |
| Within the railway safety scope | Danger from trains. See our guide to [motorbike safety at railway crossings in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbike-safety-at-railway-crossings-in-hanoi/) |
| Where you obscure signs and signal lights | Others cannot see them |
| On the left side of one-way roads | Against the flow of traffic |

Stopping at a bus stop or where a "no stopping" sign stands is also an offence. If in doubt, move on a little.

## The fines reported

Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, read on the Government portal, penalises stopping and parking offences in Article 7. As summarised from the text, stopping or parking on a pedestrian crossing, at a junction, at a bus pick-up point or under a no-stopping sign carries a fine of VND 400,000 to 600,000, stopping or parking on a bridge a fine of VND 600,000 to 800,000, and stopping or parking in a road tunnel at a place not designated a fine of VND 800,000 to 1,000,000. Check the current text of the decree before relying on these figures, and see our guide to [how to verify the current fine for a traffic offence](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-verify-the-current-fine-for-a-traffic-offence/). Parking offences can be recorded by camera, and a notice can reach the registered owner of a rental. Our guide to [who is responsible for parking and traffic fines](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/who-is-responsible-for-parking-and-traffic-fines/) explains the process.

## How to pull over safely

1. **Choose the place early.** Look ahead for a legal gap, and do not stop at the last moment.

2. **Check your mirrors and blind spot.** Our guide to [checking mirrors and blind spots before changing lanes](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/checking-mirrors-and-blind-spots-before-changing-lanes/) shows how.

3. **Signal right,** and keep it on until you are in position. Our guide to [signalling turns while keeping control of a scooter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/signalling-turns-while-keeping-control-of-a-scooter/) explains how to do it without losing the bars.

4. **Slow down smoothly** and move to the right edge.

5. **Brake in a straight line** with both brakes, and put a foot down.

6. **Check that you are not blocking a crossing, a gate or a path,** and that you are visible.

7. **Switch off the indicator** when you have stopped.

## What to do at the kerb

1. **Look at the whole map,** not only the blue line. Check which way the bike is facing.

2. **Read the road names and landmarks** around you, and confirm where you are.

3. **Check the road type** along the route, including one-way streets, toll roads and any road with a motorbike ban. Our guide to [how to check if a navigation app suggests an expressway](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-check-if-a-navigation-app-suggests-an-expressway/) explains how.

4. **Fix the next three turns in your mind,** with a landmark for each.

5. **Set a different route** if the first is blocked or unsuitable.

6. **Answer or send any message,** and then put the phone away or back in its mount.

7. **Check the fuel or the battery.** Our guide to [how to plan fuel stops on a scooter day trip](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-plan-fuel-stops-on-a-scooter-day-trip/) helps you plan.

8. **Take a breath and drink some water if you are hot or tired.**

## Rejoining the traffic

1. **Put the phone in the mount, or away,** and check that it is secure.

2. **Look in the mirror and over your shoulder.**

3. **Signal left,** and wait for a gap. Do not pull out in front of a vehicle that has to brake.

4. **Move off smoothly,** and cancel the signal.

5. **Do not look back at the screen** until you are settled.

## When the map and the road disagree

Maps can be wrong in Hanoi. Streets may be one-way, closed for works or too narrow for a bike, and a barrier may have gone up this morning. Follow the signs and the officers on site, not the app. Our guide to [how to ride near construction sites and temporary barriers](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-ride-near-construction-sites-and-temporary-barriers/) explains how to treat a temporary closure.

If you are still lost, ask a shop, a parking attendant or a police officer, or call the rental shop on 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969), open 09:00 to 21:00 daily, and tell them where you are. A landmark and a street name are enough. Prices and terms are on the [prices page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/prices/).

## A quick checklist

1. **Stop for anything longer than a glance, and for anything that needs a touch.**

2. **Choose a lawful place: right edge, clear of crossings, junctions, gates and bridges.**

3. **Check mirrors, signal, slow down and stop in a straight line.**

4. **Look at the whole map, the road type and your next three turns.**

5. **Secure the phone before you move off.**

6. **Signal and check the gap before rejoining traffic.**

7. **Ask someone or call the shop if you are still lost.**

## FAQ: stopping to check a map

### Is it legal to stop on the road to look at my phone?

Yes, if you stop in a lawful place and do not obstruct others. Article 18 of Law 36/2024/QH15 bans stopping on pedestrian crossings, at intersections and many other places.

### Can I check the map at a red light?

Even at a red light, your attention is needed. Article 9 bans holding and using a phone while operating a vehicle on the road, and a traffic light can change quickly. Stopping at the kerb is safer.

### Where should I stop in the Old Quarter?

On a wide street, at the right edge, away from junctions, gates and pedestrian areas. Our guide to [riding in Hanoi's Old Quarter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-old-quarter/) explains why a narrow street is a poor place to stop.

### Is a quick look at a mounted phone allowed?

Vietnamese legal commentary says a phone fixed in a holder that you only look at is not the banned act, but you must still ride safely.

### Do I switch off the engine when I stop?

For a quick check, you may stay on the bike. If you stop for longer, park lawfully, use the stand and switch off.

### What if there is nowhere legal to stop?

Keep going to the next safe place. Do not stop on a crossing, junction or bridge because the map demands a decision.
