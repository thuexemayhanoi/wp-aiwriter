Before you ride a Hanoi rental beyond the city, get the shop's permission for that specific trip and have the area, dates and return time written down. A rental is a lease for a fixed term, and under Vietnam's Civil Code the lessee must use the property for its agreed purpose, so a trip outside the agreed limits can give the owner grounds to end the contract and claim compensation. Nguyen Ha's published terms cover the rental day, late return, early return, the deposit and fuel, but they say nothing about a geographic limit, so ask rather than assume. This guide explains what "beyond Hanoi" can mean on paper, which contract terms matter once you leave, what the traffic law says about papers and accidents away from home and how to keep a simple record of what was agreed. It is general information as of October 2026, not legal advice.

## What counts as "beyond Hanoi"

Hanoi is not a small city. Vietnam Law Magazine describes it as 30 administrative units covering about 3,359 square kilometres, including 12 urban districts, 17 rural districts and a town. That means many places visitors think of as "out of town", such as Bat Trang in Gia Lam or the hills at Ba Vi, are still inside the municipality, while other popular destinations such as Ninh Binh or Tam Dao are in other provinces.

So a clause that says "within Hanoi" and a clause that says "within the city" may not mean the same thing to you and to the shop. If you hear either phrase, ask what it covers. The safest wording names the places or the direction of travel, for example "Hanoi and the road to Ninh Binh, return the same day". Our separate guide to [taking a rental out of the city](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/take-rental-motorbike-out-of-hanoi/) looks at the practical side; this article focuses on the contract and the paper trail.

## Why the contract matters

A motorbike rental is a lease of property. Article 472 of the Civil Code (91/2015/QH13) defines a lease as an agreement under which a lessor delivers property to a lessee for use during a fixed term and the lessee pays rent. Article 473 says rent is as the parties agree, and Article 474 says the term is as agreed. The same Code requires the lessee to take care of the property and to return it in the condition received, apart from normal wear (Articles 479 and 482).

Most relevant here is the duty to use the property in accordance with its utility and the agreed purpose. Where a lessee does not, the lessor has the right to terminate the contract unilaterally and to demand compensation for damage. Whether a particular trip would be a breach depends on what you and the shop agreed, which is exactly why a written answer is more useful than a casual yes. Our guide to [what the clauses mean in a Hanoi rental contract](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-motorbike-rental-contract-explained/) goes through the usual terms line by line.

## Six contract terms that change when you leave town

| Term | Question to ask | Why it matters beyond Hanoi |
|---|---|---|
| Area | Which places or provinces are allowed? | Defines whether the trip is within the agreement |
| Period | What exact date and time must the bike be back? | A day is 24 hours, and a longer trip may need another package |
| Rider and passenger | Who may ride and carry a passenger? | A contract may allow only the named renter to ride |
| Roads | Are there roads I must avoid? | Expressways are closed to motorbikes |
| Support | Whom do I call and what happens if the bike breaks down? | The shop is in Long Bien, far from some routes |
| Costs | What are the late return, damage and recovery terms? | Distance makes delays and repairs more expensive |

Nguyen Ha's published terms are specific on the points they cover: one rental day is 24 hours, a return one to five hours late is charged at VND 30,000 per hour, a return from six hours late counts as one extra rental day, early return does not refund unused rent, and the cash deposit is generally VND 2–5 million depending on the bike and contract. Use those figures when you plan the return time. For anything beyond them, such as breakdown recovery, ask and get the answer in writing.

## The papers that travel with the bike

Article 56 of the Road Traffic Order and Safety Law (36/2024/QH15, effective from 1 January 2025) says a rider must hold a licence suited to the vehicle and, when riding, carry among other papers the vehicle registration certificate, the licence and the compulsory civil liability insurance certificate. Where a vehicle is mortgaged to a lender, the registration paper can take the form of a certified copy with the lender's original confirmation. The law also allows these papers to be shown through an electronic identification account where they are integrated into it.

You are the one riding, so these papers matter when you are stopped far from the shop. Before you leave, ask what the shop provides for the bike, check that you can see the plate number on any paper against the plate on the bike, and photograph what you are given. The shop checks and photographs your identity documents and licence for the contract and does not keep the originals. Our guide to [documents to carry when riding in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/documents-to-carry-riding-in-vietnam/) lists what to have with you, and the [licence rules for foreigners](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbike-licence-rules-for-foreigners-in-vietnam/) explain which licences are accepted.

## Delivery limits are not riding limits

One easy mistake is to read the shop's delivery terms as riding limits. Nguyen Ha offers delivery only for weekly or monthly agreements, within 10 kilometres of Hoan Kiem Lake at VND 50,000 per trip or VND 100,000 both ways, and beyond 10 kilometres by agreement. There is no airport service, and the shop itself is in Alley 5 Nguyen Van Cu Street, Bo De Ward, Long Bien. Those facts describe where staff will bring the bike, not where you may ride it, and they say nothing about collection or support far from Hanoi. Treat the two questions separately and ask each one.

## If something goes wrong away from home

Distance makes problems harder to handle. If there is a traffic accident, Article 80 of the Road Traffic Order and Safety Law sets out duties for the rider involved: stop at once, warn of danger, keep the scene as it is, help any injured person and report to the nearest police, medical facility or People's Committee. It also says the person must stay at the scene until the police arrive, unless they need to get emergency care or are threatened, and must give identifying information to the competent authority. Beyond this, call the shop on 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969), open 09:00 to 21:00 daily, so the owner knows where the bike is.

For a breakdown, the first duty is your own safety. ## When the plan changes mid-trip

Plans change: a friend suggests a detour, the weather improves, or you want a second night. If the new plan goes beyond what was agreed, stop and contact the shop before you ride on, not after you arrive. Ask for the change to be confirmed in the same chat or message thread as the original agreement, and ask what the extra time will cost. Because late return is charged by the hour for one to five hours and by a full extra day from six hours, an unplanned night away can be costly, and you should not assume the shop will simply overlook it.

If you decide the trip is too much for the bike you have, stopping is a sensible outcome. Our guide to [Hanoi to Ha Giang by bike, or renting there](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-to-ha-giang-ride-or-rent-there/) shows how some visitors split a long trip between modes.

## FAQ: riding beyond Hanoi on a rental

### Does a Hanoi rental let me ride anywhere in Vietnam?

Do not assume so. The Civil Code requires use in line with the agreed purpose, and the published terms do not state an area, so ask the shop and get the answer in writing.

### Is Bat Trang beyond Hanoi?

No, Bat Trang is in Gia Lam District, inside the municipality, though a clause worded around the city centre could still raise a question. Ask what the wording covers.

### What if the shop says yes by phone?

Ask for the same words in a message or on the contract, with the places, dates and return time.

### Who pays for a breakdown in another province?

The published terms do not say. Ask before you go who authorises repairs, who pays and how recovery would work.

### Can I extend the trip once I am away?

Contact the shop before the agreed return time and get the new time and cost confirmed in writing. Late return is charged, so do not leave it until you are late.
