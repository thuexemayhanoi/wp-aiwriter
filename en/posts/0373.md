Before you cross one of Hanoi's big Red River bridges on a rental motorbike, check five things: whether the bridge is open to motorbikes today, which lane is yours, what the time of day and the river level are doing, whether your bike and your fuel are ready for a stretch where you cannot easily stop, and what your backup crossing is. The answers differ from bridge to bridge. Long Bien Bridge has side lanes shared with bicycles, Nhat Tan and Thanh Tri have been reported with a separated motorbike lane, Thang Long has an upper deck for cars and a lower deck that motorbikes use, and Chuong Duong has been restricted for some vehicles in floods. This guide, general information as of October 2026, turns that into a routine you can run in a few minutes.

## Why bridges deserve their own check

A bridge looks like any other road, but it removes most of your options. You cannot turn round, you cannot pull over to the kerb and you cannot easily go around a stopped vehicle. Vietnam's Law 36/2024/QH15, effective 1 January 2025, reflects this: it bars U-turns on bridges and at bridge approaches, bars stopping or parking on a bridge except where traffic organisation permits, and bars overtaking on a narrow bridge with a single lane. A mistake that would be minor on a street can become awkward or dangerous halfway across a river.

Bridges are also where Hanoi changes its traffic arrangements most often. Barriers appear, lanes are redrawn and whole bridges close for repairs or high water. That is why the check below is about today's situation, not about what an old article said.

If you ride from the shop in Bo De Ward on the east bank, the crossings you meet first are Long Bien and Chuong Duong, with Vinh Tuy and Nhat Tan further along the river. The shop's own area guides on [Long Bien](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/long-bien/) and the [Old Quarter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/old-quarter/) describe the neighbourhoods at each end.

## Check 1: is the bridge open to motorbikes today?

Start with status. Recent history shows why.

- **Long Bien Bridge** was closed to road traffic for repairs in late 2025, reopened to motorbikes, bicycles and pedestrians from 1 January 2026, closed again from 28 March to 27 May 2026 for repairs to the motorbike and pedestrian lanes, and reopened on 28 May 2026. During the second closure, motorbikes were diverted to Chuong Duong Bridge.
- **Floods can shut a bridge quickly.** In September 2024, after Typhoon Yagi raised the Red River, the city closed Long Bien Bridge from 3 p.m. on 10 September until further notice, and barred coaches, large tourist cars and trucks from Chuong Duong Bridge.
- **Diversions cause congestion nearby.** Reports after the 2026 repairs noted heavy traffic on Chuong Duong and Vinh Tuy during the closure, and on roads such as Nguyen Van Cu and Tran Nhat Duat.

A practical way to check: look at current news from established outlets, look at the Hanoi city portal, and ask the shop before you leave. If a crossing is shut, you will rarely find it out by sight until you are at the approach, where the diversion is harder to follow.

## Check 2: which lane is yours?

Lane arrangements differ, so look at the signs, barriers and markings at the approach. The table summarises what published reports have described, with the date of each report so you can judge how fresh it is. Treat it as a starting point, not a current notice.

| Bridge | What was reported | Date of report | What to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| Long Bien | Cars banned; railway in the middle; motorbikes and bicycles on narrow side lanes, about 2.6 metres wide, with a pedestrian path | Tourism authority, 2021 | Keep to the lane, ride slowly and expect pedestrians |
| Chuong Duong | A mixed-traffic lane, which bicycles were allowed to use in both directions during the 2026 diversion; coaches, large tourist cars and trucks banned during the 2024 flood | Tuoi Tre, March 2026; government news portal, September 2024 | Expect dense traffic; check for temporary bans |
| Vinh Tuy | Two parallel bridges with eight lanes between them: four for cars, two for buses and two for smaller vehicles | Opening report, August 2023 | Find the lane for smaller vehicles early |
| Nhat Tan | Reorganised from four mixed lanes to three car lanes and one motorbike lane, divided by steel barriers; enforcement planned from 1 October | Tuoi Tre, September 2025 | Use the motorbike lane; do not cut across the barrier |
| Thanh Tri | Three car lanes and one motorbike lane, about 2.9 metres wide, separated by a divider | Nhan Dan, October 2022 | Check the markings, as this report is older |
| Thang Long | Two levels: upper deck for cars and trucks, lower deck for motorbikes, bicycles and trains | Government news portal, January 2021 | Follow signs to the lower deck |

Where a lane is reserved for a type of vehicle, Article 13 of the law says other vehicles may not enter it, so a motorbike lane is yours and a car lane is not. If a sign or an officer directs you differently, follow it; the law ranks the instructions of a traffic officer above lights, signs and markings.

## Check 3: time of day and traffic

Bridges are bottlenecks. Reports from the 2026 repairs noted that closing Long Bien pushed traffic onto Chuong Duong and Vinh Tuy and the roads leading to them, and that the congestion lasted for weeks. The lesson is general: at rush hour a bridge approach moves slowly, motorbikes bunch up and lane discipline matters most.

Ways to reduce the stress:

- **Avoid the commuting peaks** if you can, especially on the approach roads.
- **Allow extra time** if your trip depends on a particular crossing.
- **Cross in daylight** when you are new, because lane markings and barriers are easier to read. Our guide to [riding at night in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-at-night-in-hanoi/) explains the visibility problems.
- **Check the day of the week,** because evening restrictions in the centre can change which road you take after the bridge. In the Old Quarter core, the pilot low-emission zone that began on 1 July 2026 reportedly bars motorbikes and cars from 7 p.m. to midnight on Fridays, Saturdays and Sundays, so check current notices before you plan the road after the bridge.

## Check 4: river level, wind and rain

The Red River rises in the wet season and in typhoons, and bridges are the first places where the effects show. A tourism guide to Long Bien Bridge advises avoiding the bridge entirely in typhoon conditions, and the 2024 closure was announced because of rising water. Even without a closure, bad weather changes how a bridge feels:

- **Rain** makes steel decks, painted markings and expansion joints slippery, and lengthens braking distances. See our guide to [riding in Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/).
- **Wind** is stronger above open water. A light scooter, a loose raincoat or a bag on the seat can catch it.
- **Spray and glare** reduce visibility, so keep your lights on and your visor clean.
- **Water on approach roads** can hide holes. If the road ahead is flooded, do not go through it.

If the forecast is poor, choose another day or another crossing, and tell the shop if your plan changes.

## Check 5: your bike and your readiness

You cannot stop easily on a bridge, so take care of problems before the foot of the ramp.

1. **Fuel:** petrol bikes are handed over with only about 0.5 to 2 litres, so refuel before a longer ride. Our guide to [fuel in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/fuel-in-hanoi-petrol-stations-rental-scooter/) lists what to expect.
2. **Brakes and tyres:** run through the [ten-minute inspection](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-scooter-inspection-checklist/), especially in wet weather.
3. **Lights, horn and mirrors:** you will need to see and be seen.
4. **Helmet:** fasten the strap before you start. The shop provides one basic helmet per rider.
5. **Phone:** mount it safely or put it away. A bridge approach is the wrong place to read a map.
6. **Route:** know your exit before you are on the bridge. The right lane is usually the motorbike lane, so decide which side road you need after the far end.

If you are carrying a passenger or luggage, balance matters more on a narrow lane. Our guide to [carrying a passenger and luggage on a scooter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/scooter-for-two-riders-and-luggage-hanoi/) explains how.

## On the bridge: habits that keep you safe

- **Hold your lane and your speed.** Sudden changes cause the collisions that barriers are meant to prevent.
- **Do not overtake in a narrow lane.** The law bars overtaking on a narrow single-lane bridge.
- **Do not stop to take photographs.** A tourism guide to Long Bien Bridge says not to stop in the middle of the motorbike lane. Park somewhere permitted and walk if you want the view.
- **Expect pedestrians, bicycles and handcarts** on the older bridges. A tourism guide describes the Hanoi approach to Long Bien as a shared space where motorbikes, bicycles and pedestrians funnel onto the same narrow lane.
- **Leave space.** On a bridge the vehicle ahead can stop suddenly, so keep a longer gap than you would in the street.
- **Do not weave through barriers or use the car lanes.** Lane violations are enforced on reorganised bridges.

## If you miss your lane or your exit

Missing a lane or exit on a bridge is common, and the right reaction is not to correct it. Keep going to the far end, at the same steady speed, and use the first lawful place to stop or turn. Do not reverse, do not make a U-turn on the bridge or approach and do not cross barriers. Then check your map and choose your backup crossing.

If the bike has a problem in the middle of a crossing, switch on the hazard lights if the bike has them, move as far to the side of your lane as you safely can and call the shop on 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969), open 09:00 to 21:00 daily. ## FAQ: crossing Hanoi's bridges by motorbike

### Which bridge is easiest for a first crossing?

Many visitors start with Long Bien Bridge because cars are banned, but its lane is narrow and shared. Check that it is open that day.

### Can I stop on a bridge for photographs?

No. Law 36/2024/QH15 bars stopping or parking on a bridge except where traffic organisation allows it, and tourism guides advise against stopping in the motorbike lane.

### What if my bridge is closed?

Use your backup crossing. During the 2026 repairs, motorbikes were diverted to Chuong Duong Bridge, so expect extra traffic there.

### Do all bridges have a separate motorbike lane?

No. Some have a separated lane, some a mixed lane and some a lower deck. Read the signs at the approach.

### Is it safe to cross in rain?

Take extra care or wait. Wet steel and paint are slippery, and visibility drops.

### Where can I check the current situation?

Established news outlets, the Hanoi city portal and the shop. The signs at the approach are the final authority.
