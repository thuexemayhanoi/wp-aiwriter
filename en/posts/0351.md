The Kim Boi hot springs sit in hill country roughly 70 to 80 kilometres south-west of Hanoi, depending on which source and starting point you trust. On a motorcycle the trip is a two-part decision. The ride is moderately demanding: dense, roadwork-affected traffic out of the city, then a quieter but narrower and more winding road through the hills. The soak at the end is relaxing, and that is exactly why it needs planning, because a warm, tired rider has to get home on the same road. This guide covers the roads, the road conditions and how to sequence a bath and a ride safely.

## The short answer

Kim Boi is a realistic day trip for an experienced rider who starts early, takes the ordinary highway rather than any expressway, and leaves the hot water in time to ride back in daylight. It is a poor idea as a last-minute add-on to a tight day, or for a rider who is already tired. If you are unsure, make it an overnight stay and ride back the next morning.

As of October 2026, Vietnam's Road Traffic Order and Safety Law (Law 36/2024/QH15, effective 1 January 2025) excludes motorcycles and mopeds from expressways in Article 25. That matters here because the quickest car routes toward Hoa Binh use expressway sections that a motorbike may not enter.

## Where the springs are, and what has changed

Kim Boi hot spring is in the Ha Bi area, in what Phu Tho's official tourism website now lists as Kim Boi commune, Phu Tho province. Before 1 July 2025 it was in Kim Boi district, Hoa Binh province, and many guides still use those names. The same official page says the mineral water emerges from limestone about 250 million years old, with an average temperature of 34 to 46 degrees Celsius.

Several resorts and a public bathing area have grown around the springs, and prices and rules differ between them. This article does not quote entry fees, because they change and vary by facility; check the operator's own information on the day. Choose your destination by name and map pin before you set off, because the sign and the name on a map may not match.

## Two ways to ride there

ORIGIN Vietnam describes two options for private vehicles. The first, and the one most riders use, follows QL6 to Luong Son town. At the Bai Lang junction you turn left and follow the road toward Bai Chao to reach Kim Boi. A second option, described by Mai Chau Hideaway, heads out toward Chuong My, passes through Hoa Son and Yen Son, then joins the Ho Chi Minh road and follows signs to the resort area.

The Luong Son route is easier to remember, and it keeps you on a single main road for most of the journey. The Ho Chi Minh road route is quieter in places but has more turns and more chances to misread a junction. For a first visit, pick the Luong Son route and save the alternative for a different day or a loop.

## Road conditions between Hanoi and Luong Son

The first half of the journey is the same corridor used by riders heading to Hoa Binh: dense urban traffic, lorries and coaches, and an extended roadworks zone on QL6 between Ba La and Xuan Mai. That section is about 21.7 kilometres, is being widened, and has been targeted for completion in the first quarter of 2027, according to Lao Dong reports from July 2026. Expect uneven surfaces, temporary lane shifts and heavy vehicles.

You will notice the road improve and the traffic thin as you pass Xuan Mai and enter Luong Son. Keep your concentration anyway. This is where speeds creep up and where lorries overtake each other. For the first half, the useful companion is our [ring roads and highways guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-ring-roads-highways-motorbikes-allowed/), which explains how to recognise roads closed to motorbikes.

## The last stretch into the hills

After the Bai Lang turn, the road becomes more local. As general guidance, hill roads in this part of the north are often winding, with narrow sections, steep slopes and occasional fog, so treat them carefully. Kim Boi is not Mai Chau's high pass, but you should still expect curves, short climbs and sudden changes in surface.

Practical habits for this section:

- Slow before the bend, not in it.
- Keep your lane position and do not cut across the centre line.
- Watch for lorries, motorbikes and pedestrians at village entrances.
- Be ready for a damp, slick road after a shower, especially in shade.
- If the road is signed for local traffic, give way to buses and trucks that need the space.

If your bike has gears, our guides to [shifting gears on hills](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/shifting-gears-on-hills-without-straining-a-rental-motorbike/) and [engine braking](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/engine-braking-on-a-semi-automatic-motorcycle/) help with climbs and descents.

## Planning the soak around the ride

The hot-spring part of the day should be fitted around daylight, not the other way round. A sensible sequence is to arrive late morning, eat a light lunch, soak for a limited period, rest and then ride home. Do not rush from hot water straight onto the road: heat and a full stomach can leave you drowsy, and dehydration slows reactions.

Bring a small dry bag with a towel, change of clothes and plastic bag for wet items. Wear shoes you can walk in safely on wet floors, and keep your phone and documents in a waterproof pouch. Drink water before and after bathing. If you feel dizzy, stop, sit down and take longer than you planned. Anyone with a medical condition should follow the operator's and their doctor's advice, rather than relying on claims about the water's effects.

## Where the day can go wrong, and the simple fixes

Most problems on a trip like this are small ones that grow when ignored. A thirsty rider makes slower decisions, so carry a litre of water and top it up when you stop. A wet rider is cold on the ride home, so keep a dry layer in the dry bag rather than putting wet clothes back on. A phone on 10 percent battery is a poor navigation tool in unfamiliar hills, so start with a full charge and carry a cable.

Valuables deserve a plan as well. Leave anything you do not need at your accommodation in Hanoi, keep documents and phone in a sealed pouch and use lockers or staff-supervised storage if the facility offers them. Do not leave the key in the ignition or the bike unlocked while you bathe, and lock the steering when you park. The rental shop does not provide an extra lock, so choose guarded parking.

## Timing, weather and daylight

Aim to be back in Hanoi before dark. Our [night riding guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-at-night-in-hanoi/) explains why visibility, lighting and unmarked hazards make evening riding harder. If your plan runs late, it is better to stay the night than to push on.

Weather in the hills changes quickly. A dry start can turn into a wet afternoon, and heavy rain has led the Hoa Binh dam to open its spillways in recent years. Check the forecast, carry a rain layer, and read our [rain-riding guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) before you go. In a downpour, find shelter instead of pressing on.

## An example day

- **Before 07:30:** leave Hanoi, avoiding the heaviest QL6 traffic.
- **Mid-morning:** break near Xuan Mai or Luong Son; check fuel.
- **Late morning:** turn at Bai Lang and ride the final hill stretch.
- **Midday to early afternoon:** lunch and a short soak.
- **By about 15:00:** begin the ride back, keeping a margin for traffic.
- **Evening:** return and hand back the bike during opening hours.

This is a planning pattern, not a schedule. Roadworks, weather or fatigue can change it at any time, and shortening the day is always the right response.

## FAQ: Hanoi to Kim Boi hot springs

### How far is Kim Boi from Hanoi?

Sources range from about 66 to 80 kilometres, depending on the starting point and route. Plan for around two to two-and-a-half hours by car and longer on a motorbike, because of traffic and roadworks.

### Can I use the expressway?

No. As of October 2026, motorcycles and mopeds are excluded from expressways under Law 36/2024/QH15. Use QL6 and ordinary roads.

### Is it safe to ride after soaking in a hot spring?

Wait until you feel alert, drink water and rest. If you are drowsy, dizzy or dehydrated, delay the ride or stay overnight.

### What does it cost to enter?

Entry details vary by operator and change over time, so check the facility's own information on the day.

### Which name should I use on maps?

Search for the specific resort or spring by name. Administrative names changed in 2025, so older and newer labels both appear.
