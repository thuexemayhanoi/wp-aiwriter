When you bring a rented manual bike back after a long journey, the return goes smoothly if you do four things: sort the fuel and the time the day before, take your belongings and rubbish off the bike, tell the shop what the trip did to it before staff find it and do the walk-round together using your pickup photos and your trip notes. A long ride leaves more to report than a short loop, from a tyre that has worn faster to a chain that has gone dry, a lever that was knocked or a scuff from a stop on a rough verge. This guide is a checklist for that return. It covers preparation, a sensible clean-up, the places a long journey leaves marks, what to say at the counter and the paperwork to keep. For the general return process, our guide to [returning your rental motorbike in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/returning-your-rental-motorbike-hanoi/) is the companion piece.

## Why a long journey changes the return

On a short rental the bike comes back almost as it left. After several days and hundreds of kilometres, three things differ.

First, the bike has worked harder. Honda's Wave RSX FI manual gives an interval of every 500 km for checking and lubricating the chain, sooner on poor roads or with hard riding, and a journey of a few days can pass that mark. Tyres, brake shoes and the chain all wear with distance.

Second, the bike has met more surfaces. Dust, mud and rain reach places that a city loop never touches. Honda's care instructions say mud and dirt increase wear on the front suspension and can cause oil leaks, so Honda advises washing the bike after riding in mud.

Third, you know things the shop does not. Only you can say that the bike pulled left on a long descent, that a noise began on day two or that you put it down gently on a gravel bend. Telling the shop first turns a possible dispute into a service note. Our guide to [what counts as normal wear on a rented Honda Wave](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-counts-as-normal-wear-on-a-rented-honda-wave/) explains the difference between wear and damage.

## The day before: three decisions

**Fuel.** Nguyen Ha hands petrol bikes over with roughly 0.5 to 2 litres and expects them back with roughly 0.5 to 1 litre. The shop does not ask for a full tank. An empty tank at return costs VND 20,000. Read the gauge on the evening before, not the morning of the return, and add a litre at a station near the shop if it reads low. Our guide to [fuel in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/fuel-in-hanoi-petrol-stations-rental-scooter/) explains where to fill up.

**Time.** A rental day is 24 hours from handover. Late returns are charged at VND 30,000 per hour for hours one to five, and from six hours late the shop charges one extra rental day. The shop is open from 09:00 to 21:00 daily. After a long journey, add a margin for traffic on the last stretch, a stop for petrol and a slow approach to the shop, and call ahead if you will be late.

**Plan the last stretch.** Do not schedule the longest day of the trip for the day you return. Tired riders on a deadline make poor decisions. If the final leg is long, split it and return the next morning, and compare the cost with the late-return rules above.

## Build a trip log as you go

If you can, keep a short note on your phone from day one. It costs a minute each evening and saves the arguments that start with "I do not remember".

- **Start and end odometer readings.** Photograph the dial at pickup and at the end of each day.
- **Fuel stops.** Date, place and rough litres.
- **Incidents.** A drop, a puncture, a touch with another vehicle, a repair.
- **New noises or behaviours.** What happened, where and at what speed.
- **Receipts.** Any repair, tyre patch or towing you paid for.

Our guide to [what to do after dropping a rented semi-automatic bike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-to-do-after-dropping-a-rented-semi-automatic-bike/) explains what to record if the bike has been down, and our article on [the 10-minute inspection checklist](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-scooter-inspection-checklist/) shows what the pickup photos should include.

## Take everything off the bike

On the evening before, clear the bike of belongings:

1. **The under-seat box.** Honda's manuals describe a storage box rated for 5 kg, plus a pocket for the bike's papers and a tool kit. Remove your own items only. Leave the papers and tools where you found them.
2. **The rack or any straps.** Remove bags, cords and nets. If you borrowed any item from the shop, hand it back.
3. **Chargers and phone mounts.** Check that no cable or bracket stays on the bike.
4. **Parking tickets and receipts.** Keep them. Do not leave them in the box.
5. **The helmet.** Return it to the staff. One basic helmet per rider is included with the rental.

Look under the seat once more with a torch or your phone light, because a small item can slip behind the battery cover or the tool kit.

## A sensible clean-up

You do not have to hand the bike back spotless, and a bike that has travelled is expected to look used. But a quick tidy helps staff see the real condition. Dried mud can hide a scratch, and a dusty lens can hide a crack.

What Honda's manual says about washing, and what it means for a renter:

- **Let the engine, exhaust and brakes cool first.** Washing hot parts is poor practice.
- **Use low water pressure.** Honda says not to use a high-pressure jet, because it can damage moving and electrical parts and force water into places it should not reach.
- **Keep water away from the exhaust, the air filter and the area under the seat.** Water in the exhaust can make starting difficult and cause internal rust. Water under the seat can damage papers and other items.
- **Do not spray directly at the headlight.** Honda notes that mist can form inside the lens after washing or rain, and clears after a few minutes.
- **Dry the brakes.** After washing, Honda says to squeeze the brakes lightly at low speed to dry them.

If you want the bike cleaned, ask the shop first, because some prefer to wash their bikes themselves. A wipe with a damp cloth and a quick brush on the seat and footboards is enough. Do not oil the chain unless the shop has told you to. A wrong application can reach the tyre or the brake, and Honda warns that oil on the brakes cuts braking power a great deal. Tell the shop how dusty or wet the ride was and leave the lubrication to them.

## Where a long journey leaves marks

Walk round the bike before you return it and look in the places where distance and rough surfaces show first. The table gives you a quick tour.

| Where | What a long trip may have done | What to tell the shop |
|---|---|---|
| Tyres | Faster wear, nails, a slow leak | Any puncture or repair, and anything you noticed in handling |
| Chain | Dry, noisy, more slack | Dust or rain exposure, any noise or a thumping sound |
| Brakes | Longer lever or pedal travel, squeal | When it began and which brake |
| Lights and indicators | A bulb that stopped working | Which one and on what day |
| Mirrors and levers | Knocked out of position, loosened | Whether it was adjusted or dropped |
| Footrests and stand | Scrapes from a lean on rough ground | Any low-speed drop |
| Seat and side panels | Scuffs from parking, a tear from rubbing | How and where it happened |
| Exhaust cover | A dent from a stone or a lean | The incident |
| Key and key ring | Bent or lost items | A missing spare, if any |

For the chain, our guides to [checking chain tension before a longer ride](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/checking-chain-tension-before-a-longer-ride/) and to [how to notice a worn chain and sprocket set](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-notice-a-worn-chain-and-sprocket-set/) explain what to look for. For tyres, Honda Global advises inspecting pressure when tyres have cooled, so look at them in the morning rather than straight after a ride.

## Report first, in plain words

Before staff start their check, offer the information you hold. A short, factual list is enough:

- "I rode about X kilometres over Y days, with no passenger."
- "The rear tyre was repaired at a roadside shop on day two. Here is the receipt."
- "The left mirror was loose after a bump. I tightened it by hand."
- "I noticed a squeak in the rear brake on the last two days."
- "I dropped it gently at a stop on day three. The mark is on the left footrest."

This approach has two benefits. It shows that you looked after the bike, and it lets the shop decide what needs servicing before the next rider. Staff see the same models every day and will notice marks that are not on the pickup record. Do not try to hide a mark.

If the shop claims a mark you think was there at pickup, show the photo. ## Paperwork and money

At the counter, expect to do these in order:

1. Compare the bike with the pickup record and your photos, standing beside staff.
2. Agree any deduction, such as an empty tank or a late return, and ask for it in writing.
3. Take back your deposit once the bike is accepted. The cash deposit is generally between VND 2,000,000 and 5,000,000 depending on the bike and the contract, and it is separate from the rent.
4. Keep your copy of the contract and your photos for a few weeks.

Rent for unused days is not refunded on an early return. ## If the return does not go to plan

- **You will be late.** Call or message before the deadline and ask for the new time to be confirmed in a message.
- **You arrive after 21:00.** Do not leave the bike outside the shop. Park it in a guarded lot, keep the ticket and return in the morning. Our guide to [parking a motorbike in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-motorbike-in-hanoi/) explains guarded parking.
- **The bike broke down on the way.** Call the shop first, and read our guide to [what to do with a rental bike breakdown in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-bike-breakdown-hanoi-what-to-do/).
- **A key or the bike's papers are missing.** Say so at once. It is handled as a loss.

## The return checklist

**The day before**
1. Check the fuel gauge and add a litre if it is low.
2. Confirm the return time within 09:00 to 21:00.
3. Empty the box and remove straps, chargers and mounts.
4. Gather the key, the contract, your receipts and your photos.
5. Write your list of things to report.

**At the shop**
1. Park, switch off and hand over the key in person.
2. Walk round the bike with staff, comparing it with your pickup photos.
3. Read out your list before they ask.
4. Agree any deduction and get it in writing.
5. Collect the deposit and count it.
6. Keep the contract copy and photos.

## FAQ: returning after a long journey

### Do I need to wash the bike before returning it?

No. A tidy bike helps the check, but ask the shop first, and avoid high-pressure water.

### Should I refill the tank?

Roughly 0.5 to 1 litre at return is what the shop expects. An empty tank costs VND 20,000.

### What if I had a puncture on the trip?

Keep the receipt, tell the shop and mention which wheel was repaired.

### Can I lubricate the chain before returning it?

Leave it to the shop. Wrongly applied lubricant can reach the brake or tyre.

### How long should I keep my photos?

A few weeks after the deposit is settled.
