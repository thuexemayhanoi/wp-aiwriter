The risky parts of a petrol stop on a scooter are the two seconds at each end of it: turning in across a pavement from a busy road, and pulling out again into traffic. Slow down early, signal, give way to pedestrians and to traffic behind you, use the marked entrance, switch the engine off at the pump, keep your phone and cigarettes away from it, mind the slippery spills around the island, and look both ways before you leave. Article 15 of Law 36/2024/QH15 requires you to observe the surroundings, ensure a safe distance with the vehicle behind, slow down, signal and give way to pedestrians when changing direction. At the pump, Vietnamese legal commentary says leaving the engine running can be treated as using a source of fire or heat in a prohibited area, with a fine of VND 20,000,000 to 25,000,000 under Decree 106/2025/ND-CP.

This guide explains the legal rules for turning in and out, how to approach and queue, what to do at the pump, how to avoid spills and other hazards, how to leave safely, how fuel type and payment fit in and what to ask the shop. For the buying side, see our guide to [fuel in Hanoi: petrol stations, prices and filling up a rental scooter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/fuel-in-hanoi-petrol-stations-rental-scooter/). This guide is general information based on the law and published guidance as of October 2026, not legal advice.

## Why entrances and exits matter

A station entrance is a junction in disguise. Vehicles enter and leave across the pavement, pedestrians walk past, queues spill back onto the road and riders in a hurry cut across lanes. Several of Law 36/2024/QH15's rules apply at once.

- **Changing direction (Article 15).** Before turning, the operator must observe the surroundings to ensure a safe distance with the vehicle behind, slow down, send out a signal and gradually move to the lane closest to the direction of the turn. The signal must be used continuously during the turn. The Law says direction change may be made only when safety conditions are satisfied and it is not likely to obstruct people and other vehicles, and it requires giving way to pedestrians, rudimentary vehicles and oncoming traffic.

- **Slow down (Article 12).** Operators must slow down or stop at places crowded with people, populous areas and areas where pedestrians cross, and when visibility is limited.

- **Stopping (Article 18).** Operators may not stop or park within 5 metres of the edge of an intersection or on a pedestrian crossing, and must signal when leaving or entering a stopping place.

- **Pavements (Articles 33 and 77).** Pavements are for pedestrians, and motorcycles may not drive on sections reserved for pedestrians. A station's driveway is where vehicles are meant to cross the pavement. Cross it straight and slowly, and do not ride along the pavement to bypass a queue. Our guide to [how to avoid riding on footpaths and pedestrian streets](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-avoid-riding-on-footpaths-and-pedestrian-streets/) explains the rule and the reported fine.

## Approaching and turning in

1. **Spot the entrance early.** Stations are usually visible from some distance, with a canopy and price boards. Do not decide at the last second.

2. **Check behind.** Look in your mirror and over your shoulder. Our guide to [checking mirrors and blind spots before changing lanes](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/checking-mirrors-and-blind-spots-before-changing-lanes/) shows how.

3. **Signal early and keep it on.** Our guide to [signalling turns while keeping control of a scooter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/signalling-turns-while-keeping-control-of-a-scooter/) explains how to do it without losing the bars.

4. **Slow down before the turn,** not in it. The slope of the driveway, a kerb or a gutter can unsettle a scooter at speed.

5. **Move to the lane nearest the station,** as Article 15 describes. At a station on the right, stay on the right. If the station is on the other side of the road, think hard before you cut across, since our guide to [left turns across multi-lane roads on a motorbike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/left-turns-across-multi-lane-roads-on-a-motorbike/) shows how risky a left turn is. A safer plan is to continue to the next lawful place and use a station on your own side.

6. **Give way to pedestrians** on the pavement, and to vehicles leaving the station.

7. **Do not stop in the road waiting to enter.** If the queue reaches the street, do not block the lane. Continue to another station or wait at a point where you are clear of the carriageway and the junction.

8. **Cross the pavement straight, at walking pace,** with your feet ready.

## Queuing and positioning

- **Keep clear of the exit.** Do not park or stop where you block vehicles leaving the station. A Vietnamese law publication reports that stopping or parking improperly at a station so that you block the exit or the route of fire engines carries a fine of VND 5,000,000 to 10,000,000 under Decree 106/2025/ND-CP, a much higher figure than before.

- **Follow the attendants.** They direct bikes to the pump islands. Do not push into a queue.

- **Keep your distance.** A few bikes may squeeze together at the same pump, but give each other room to put feet down and to step off.

- **Watch the trucks and cars.** Large vehicles swing wide and reverse.

- **Switch off while you wait,** if the queue is long, and walk or push the bike forward rather than idling beside a pump.

- **Mind your mirrors and bags.** In a tight crowd of bikes, a handlebar bag or a loose strap can catch on another bike. Our guide to [why loose scarves and bags are dangerous on motorbikes](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/why-loose-scarves-and-bags-are-dangerous-on-motorbikes/) explains the risk.

## At the pump

Fuel vapour and ignition sources are the hazard. Stations in Vietnam are classified as facilities with a risk of fire and explosion, and the rules for them apply to everyone who enters.

1. **Switch the engine off,** and put the bike on its stand or hold it upright with both feet down, as the attendant prefers.

2. **No smoking, no lighters or matches,** and no open flames.

3. **Follow the station's signs about phones.** Vietnamese news reports say that Decree 106/2025/ND-CP, in force from 1 July 2025, dropped electronic devices from the list of items banned at stations and that many stations take phone payment by QR code, but some stations still display older signs. Follow the signs and the staff.

4. **Let the attendant fill the tank,** as most stations do. Tell them the fuel your bike needs. Our guide to fuel in Hanoi explains the E5 RON 92 and E10 grades and what the shop says.

5. **Do not overfill.** A VnExpress report on station safety lists, among its recommendations, not filling the tank to the brim and closing the cap tightly after refuelling, and it notes that the operator Petrolimex advises switching the engine off to remove the risk of sparks and fuel vapour igniting.

6. **Do not use the horn or revs** near the pump, and do not start the engine until you have moved clear if the attendant asks you to.

7. **Check the cap and the tank area** for spills before you go.

On the fine: Vietnamese legal commentary notes that there is no separate traffic offence for failing to switch off the engine when refuelling. A lawyer quoted by Tuoi Tre says that it can be treated as using a heat or ignition source in an area where it is banned, which Article 11 of Decree 106/2025/ND-CP penalises with a fine of VND 20,000,000 to 25,000,000, and with double that if a fire results. Whether a given case would be treated that way is for the authorities to decide. The safe course, and the one the station's staff expect, is to switch off.

## Spills, surfaces and other hazards

- **Fuel on the ground is slippery.** Look for rainbow-coloured patches and wet concrete, and avoid braking or turning on them.

- **Rain adds to it.** Wet tiles under a canopy and oil film can be very slippery. Our guide to [braking safely on wet road markings](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/braking-safely-on-wet-road-markings/) explains the same principle.

- **Pump islands have kerbs and hoses.** Watch your feet and your mirrors.

- **Hot engines and exhausts** are near your legs. Take care when you step off.

- **Children and pets** may be on the forecourt.

- **Cash and phone use** distract you. Do your payment before you start to move, and keep your wallet or phone away from your hand on the bars. Our guide to [paying for fuel, parking and tolls in Hanoi as a visitor](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/paying-fuel-parking-tolls-hanoi-visitor/) explains how it works.

- **Night.** Lighting varies, so slow down at unlit entrances. Our guide to [how to ride after dark with limited street lighting](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-ride-after-dark-with-limited-street-lighting/) explains how.

## Leaving the station

The exit is where many collisions happen, because the rider's eyes are on the road ahead and the traffic comes fast from the side.

1. **Stop before the pavement,** behind the line or at the edge of the forecourt, and put your foot down.

2. **Look both ways,** and look again. Include the pavement for pedestrians and cyclists.

3. **Give way** to pedestrians and to traffic already on the road, as Article 15 and Article 22 describe for turns and junctions.

4. **Prefer a right turn out.** Turn right into the nearest lane, and do not cut across several lanes to turn left. If you need to go the other way, ride on to a lawful place to turn. Our guide to [why sudden U-turns are risky in city traffic](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/why-sudden-u-turns-are-risky-in-city-traffic/) explains why not to turn on the spot.

5. **Signal, accelerate smoothly and join the traffic** at a speed that does not force others to brake.

6. **Do not ride along the pavement** to reach a gap.

7. **Do not enter the road in front of a bus or lorry** that is closer than you can judge.

## A quick checklist

1. **Spot the entrance early, check behind, signal, slow down.**

2. **Give way to pedestrians and cross the pavement straight, at walking pace.**

3. **Do not stop in the road, and do not block the exit.**

4. **Engine off at the pump; no smoking, no flames.**

5. **Follow the station's signs and the staff.**

6. **Mind the spills and wet surfaces.**

7. **At the exit, stop, look both ways, give way and prefer a right turn.**

8. **Do not ride along the pavement.**

## FAQ: petrol station entrances

### Do I have to switch off my engine at a petrol station in Vietnam?

Yes, in practice. Signs and staff ask for it, and Vietnamese legal commentary says leaving the engine on can be treated as using an ignition source in a prohibited area, with a fine of VND 20,000,000 to 25,000,000 under Decree 106/2025/ND-CP.

### Can I use my phone at the pump?

Vietnamese news reports say Decree 106/2025/ND-CP no longer lists electronic devices as banned at stations, but follow the signs and the staff.

### Can I ride across the pavement to enter the station?

The driveway is for crossing. Cross it straight and slowly, give way to pedestrians and do not ride along the pavement.

### Can I turn left into a station across traffic?

It is risky. Article 15 of Law 36/2024/QH15 requires a safe, observed turn that does not obstruct others. A safer plan is to use a station on your own side.

### What if the queue reaches the road?

Do not block the lane. Go to another station, or wait where you are clear of the carriageway and junction.

### Who fills the tank?

Usually an attendant. Tell them the fuel your bike needs, and check the cap afterwards.
