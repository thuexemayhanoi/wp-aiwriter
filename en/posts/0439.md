Before you sign a motorbike rental contract, check six things about the deposit: how much it is and in what form, exactly what it is held against, when and how it will be returned, what can be deducted and on what evidence, what record the bike's condition will be judged against and where all of that is written. Nguyen Ha's published terms say that the cash deposit is paid separately from the rent, is reconciled on return according to the recorded condition and obligations and is returned at the end of the rental once the bike has been checked against the handover record and any late or fuel charge has been settled.

Those are good principles. What they do not state is a timeline, so the useful questions are the ones that turn principles into specifics. This guide walks through the clauses and questions in order, explains how deductions should work, what evidence matters, what a fair return looks like and how to protect yourself, with a script you can use at the counter. It is general information, not legal advice.

## Why check before signing

After you sign, your bargaining power is much smaller. The deposit is the largest sum that changes hands in a rental, it is held for the whole period and the way it is returned decides whether the rental ends cleanly or with a dispute. Most deposit disputes come from a handful of unclear points: what the deposit covers, when it is returned, what was deducted and why, and what the bike looked like at the start. All of those can be settled before you sign, at no cost, in a few minutes.

Our guide to [how a motorbike rental deposit works in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbike-rental-deposit-hanoi/) explains the deposit in general, and our guide to [understanding the difference between rent and deposit](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/understanding-the-difference-between-rent-and-deposit/) separates it from the price. This guide is about the return conditions.

## What Nguyen Ha publishes

As of October 2026, these are the published points on the deposit.

- **Amount:** commonly VND 2,000,000–5,000,000, depending on the vehicle and the agreement.

- **Form:** cash, paid separately from the rental charge.

- **Not a passport deposit:** the shop checks and photographs identity documents and does not keep an original passport or identity card instead of the cash deposit.

- **Held against:** damage, loss and unpaid charges.

- **Reconciled on return** according to the recorded condition and obligations.

- **Returned** at the end of the rental, under the terms of the contract, once the bike has been checked against the handover record and any late or fuel charge has been settled.

- **Damage** from a collision, accident or misuse is priced by its cause, the actual condition of the bike and the contract, and the renter may bear a cost in proportion to the real damage.

- **Disputes:** keep copies of the signed agreement and handover photos, and contact the shop to seek a documented resolution.

The published terms do not say how quickly the deposit is returned, whether in cash or another form, or what happens if a dispute arises. Those are things to ask.

## The six checks

### 1. Amount and form

- **What is the exact deposit for my bike?** The published range is VND 2,000,000–5,000,000, so the answer should be a figure.

- **In what form is it paid and returned?** Cash is published for the payment.

- **Is the amount written in the contract?**

- **Is the amount the same however long I rent?**

Have the exact figure on the contract. A vague "deposit paid" is not enough.

### 2. What it is held against

Ask for the list. Typical items are:

- damage to the bike beyond ordinary wear;

- loss of the bike, a key or an accessory;

- unpaid charges, such as late return or an empty tank;

- fines that arrive after the rental, if the contract says so.

The published terms say the deposit is held against damage, loss and unpaid charges. Ask whether it is also held against fines, and against any cleaning or administrative fee. Our guide to [who is responsible for parking and traffic fines](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/who-is-responsible-for-parking-and-traffic-fines/) explains why fines sometimes arrive late.

### 3. When and how it is returned

This is the point the published terms leave open.

- **When is the deposit returned:** at the return counter, the same day, within a number of days?

- **In what form:** the same cash, a transfer, another way?

- **Who returns it,** and where?

- **What if I return the bike outside normal hours?** The shop is open 09:00 to 21:00 daily.

- **What if I leave the country the same day?**

The published practice is that the deposit is returned once the bike has been checked against the handover record and any charge has been settled, which suggests it can be done at the end of the check. Ask whether that is what happens, and have the answer written down.

### 4. What can be deducted, and on what evidence

Deductions are where disputes live. Ask:

- **Which deductions are allowed,** and for what?

- **How is the amount decided:** by a fixed price list, by the actual cost of repair or by an estimate?

- **Will I see the evidence,** such as the damaged part, a photograph or a receipt?

- **Will deductions be itemised in writing,** with the reason?

- **Is there any cap,** for example, can the deductions exceed the deposit?

- **What is treated as ordinary wear,** which should not be deducted?

The Civil Code (Law No. 91/2015/QH13), in force since 1 January 2017, says the lessee must return the property in the condition in which it was received, apart from natural wear, and that the lessor may claim compensation if its value has dropped, again apart from natural wear (Article 482). It also says the lessee is not responsible for natural wear (Article 479). We read the lease articles in the text published by the Tuyen Quang Department of Justice. As of October 2026 that is the general rule, and the contract fills in the details. Our guide to [what counts as normal wear on a rented Honda Wave](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-counts-as-normal-wear-on-a-rented-honda-wave/) explains how wear and damage are separated. This is general information, not legal advice.

### 5. The condition record

A deposit is reconciled against the recorded condition. So the record matters as much as the clause.

- **Will a written handover record be made,** and will I get a copy?

- **Will the bike be photographed at handover,** by me, by the shop or both?

- **Will existing marks be written on the contract?**

- **What is recorded about fuel and the odometer?**

Make your own record as well. Our guides to [how to document existing scratches on a rental bike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-document-existing-scratches-on-a-rental-bike/) and [how to record the fuel level at handover](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-record-the-fuel-level-at-handover/) explain how, and our [10-minute inspection checklist](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-scooter-inspection-checklist/) places the step within the pickup routine.

### 6. Where it is written

Verbal assurances do not survive a disagreement. Check that the answers appear on the contract, in a message from the shop or both. If the contract says one thing and the staff say another, ask for the contract to be changed or the answer added in writing. Our guide to [reading a motorbike rental agreement in English](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/reading-a-motorbike-rental-agreement-in-english/) explains how to add notes before you sign.

## A script for the counter

You can read these out one at a time and note the answers.

1. "What is the deposit for this bike, and is the amount written on the contract?"

2. "What is the deposit held against: damage, loss, unpaid charges, anything else?"

3. "When and how will I get it back, and will it be in cash?"

4. "What can be deducted, and how will the amount be decided?"

5. "Will I see the evidence for any deduction, and will it be itemised in writing?"

6. "Will we record the bike's condition together now, with photographs?"

7. "Can we write these answers on the contract?"

Most staff will answer these calmly. If a shop becomes irritated by reasonable questions about your own money, that tells you something.

## What a fair return looks like

At the end of a rental, you can expect:

1. **A joint walk-round** with staff, comparing the bike with the handover record and photographs.

2. **A check of fuel and the odometer,** and any late or fuel charge settled.

3. **Any deduction explained,** with the reason and the amount, and written down.

4. **The remaining deposit returned,** in the agreed form and time.

5. **A record that the deposit was returned,** such as a line on the contract or a signed note.

## Red flags

- **A passport or other original document** held instead of cash.

- **No amount written** on the contract.

- **A refusal to say** when the deposit comes back.

- **"Deductions as decided by the shop"** with no criteria.

- **No handover record,** or a refusal to note existing marks.

- **A deposit well above the published range** with no explanation.

- **Pressure to sign** before you have read the terms.

- **No receipt** for the deposit. Our guide to [what should be written on a rental payment receipt](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-should-be-written-on-a-rental-payment-receipt/) explains what it should show.

## If a dispute arises

Stay calm and keep it documented.

- **Show your photographs** and the handover record.

- **Ask for the shop's reasons in writing.**

- **Compare the amount with what the repair would normally cost.**

- **Agree a reasonable figure** rather than a standoff, and get it written on the contract.

- **Keep every message and receipt.**

The shop's own pages advise keeping copies of the signed agreement and the handover photos and seeking a documented resolution. ## A quick checklist

1. **Know the exact deposit amount and form, and get it on the contract.**

2. **Know what it is held against.**

3. **Know when and how it is returned.**

4. **Know what can be deducted, and on what evidence.**

5. **Make a joint handover record, with photographs.**

6. **Get the answers in writing.**

7. **Get a receipt for the deposit, and a record when it is returned.**

## FAQ: deposit return conditions

### How much is the deposit at Nguyen Ha?

Commonly VND 2,000,000–5,000,000, depending on the vehicle and the agreement. Ask the exact amount for your bike.

### When is the deposit returned?

The published terms say at the end of the rental, once the bike has been checked against the handover record and any late or fuel charge has been settled. They do not give a timeline, so ask.

### What can be deducted?

Damage beyond ordinary wear, loss and unpaid charges, according to the contract. Ask for any deduction to be itemised.

### Can the shop keep my passport instead?

Not at Nguyen Ha. The shop does not keep an original passport or identity card instead of the cash deposit.

### What if we disagree about damage?

Show your handover photographs, ask for the reasons in writing and agree a documented resolution.

### Should I get a receipt for the deposit?

Yes, with the amount on its own line, and a record when it is returned.
