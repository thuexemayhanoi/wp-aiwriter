On comfort and handling the Honda Vision is the more predictable rental: it is a current Honda model with a documented seat height of 761 mm, a weight of 93 to 97 kg and a well-known light, neutral feel. The Yamaha Mio is a lighter and lower scooter on paper, with a seat height around 750 mm and a wet weight near 90 kg, and it can feel just as friendly, but the Mio you meet in a Hanoi rental fleet is very likely an older machine, so its real comfort depends on age and condition far more than on the badge. In short, choose the Vision when you want a known quantity, and choose a Mio when the bike in front of you passes a proper test ride and you like the way it fits you.

## First, which Mio are we talking about?

"Mio" is not one scooter. Yamaha has used the name since 2003 for a long family of small automatic scooters across Southeast Asia, with names such as Mio Soul, Mio Fino, Mio Ultimo, Mio M3 and others, with air-cooled engines from about 113 to 125 cc. In Vietnam several of these were sold over the years, and they became popular for customising.

Here is the part renters should know. Yamaha Motor Vietnam's current scooter range lists the Grande, Janus, LEXi, Freego, NVX and Latte, not the Mio. So a Yamaha Mio or Mio Ultimo in a rental shop is usually a used bike from an earlier period, not a model you can look up on the current Yamaha Vietnam site. Nguyen Ha lists the Yamaha Mio and the Mio Ultimo among its possible automatic scooters, and confirms the actual bike at booking.

That changes how you compare. A new Vision and a ten-year-old Mio are not equals, however similar the sales brochures once looked. The sections below separate what the model is designed to do from what age does to it.

## Comfort: seat, footwell and ride

Comfort on a small scooter comes from four things you can judge in a few minutes: the seat, the space for your legs, the way the suspension absorbs bumps, and how much the engine buzzes through the grips and the footboard.

**Seat and legroom.** The Vision's seat is a little higher, and Honda's frame gives a tidy but not cramped footwell. The Mio's lower seat suits a shorter rider who wants to put both feet flat on the ground, while a rider over about 1.80 m may feel the Mio is small. Sit on both and see where your knees land.

**Suspension.** Both have a telescopic fork at the front and a single rear shock. A new Vision's suspension is tuned for city roads, and its damping is fresh. On an older Mio the rear shock and fork seals may have aged, which can make the bike feel bouncy or harsh over Hanoi's patched asphalt, speed bumps and tram-line edges. That is a condition issue, not a design flaw.

**Vibration.** Both engines are single-cylinder air-cooled units and will hum at idle. A heavy buzz in the handlebars or a loose-feeling footboard is a sign of wear and worth a note.

## Handling: weight, steering and balance

Low weight is a benefit in Hanoi. Both scooters are easy to flick through gaps, turn in narrow lanes and move by hand in a parking row. The Mio's lower seat and lower weight give it a slight edge in slow manoeuvres, which is why many riders find it friendly.

The Vision has a stable, neutral manner at 30 to 50 km/h and a long record as a first scooter. It steers lightly, tracks straight on a bridge and has predictable brakes. On an older Mio, steering feel depends on the condition of the steering bearings and the tyres, and a tired front end can wander or feel vague at speed.

Wheel size matters too. The Vision uses 14-inch wheels front and rear on the standard version, which cope reasonably with potholes. Mio wheel sizes differ by version, so check what the bike you are given has, and look at the tyres themselves: tread depth, cracks and age matter more than the size on paper. ## Age matters more than the badge

An older rental scooter can be perfectly good, and well-kept ones often are. What you need is a quick way to spot the tired ones. These are the things that most change comfort and handling:

- **Tyres:** cracked sidewalls, flat spots, uneven wear or very low tread.
- **Rear shock and fork:** oil stains, a spring that bounces more than once after you press on it, or a clunk over a bump.
- **Steering:** play in the handlebars when you rock the front wheel, or a notchy feel as you turn.
- **Brakes:** a lever that pulls nearly to the grip, a squeal, or a rear drum that grabs suddenly.
- **Belt and clutch:** a shudder when pulling away or a grinding noise, which suggests wear in the drive.
- **Seat:** a seat that has collapsed on one side or splits at the seams, which affects comfort over a whole day.

## Everyday use: storage, fuel and parking

Comfort is not only about the ride. The Vision's under-seat compartment is described by Honda as large enough for a helmet and personal items, and its newer versions add a covered front pocket with a USB-C port. Under-seat space on a Mio varies by version and by how the bike has been used, so open the seat at the shop and see whether your daypack, rain jacket or second helmet fits.

On fuel, the Vision's 4.8-litre tank and Honda's claimed 1.82 litres per 100 km give a long range on paper, and the Mio's slightly smaller tank is no handicap in the city. The rental terms are the same for both: petrol bikes are handed over with about 0.5 to 2 litres and returned with about 0.5 to 1 litre, as our guide to [returning a rental motorbike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/returning-your-rental-motorbike-hanoi/) explains. For parking, either scooter is light enough to push in and out of a guarded row, which our [parking guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/parking-a-motorbike-in-hanoi/) recommends over leaving the bike on the pavement.

## Fit for different riders

**A shorter or lighter rider.** The Mio's lower seat and lower weight are real advantages. If the bike passes the test ride, it may feel more confidence-inspiring than the Vision.

**A taller rider.** The Vision's slightly higher seat and longer frame give more room. A rider over 1.80 m often finds a Mio cramped on a longer ride.

**A first-time scooter rider.** Either is fine, but the Vision's well-known behaviour and newer condition remove several unknowns. A first-timer does not need the extra task of judging an older bike's worn suspension.

**A rider with a passenger.** The two are similar in size, but the Vision's newer suspension and brakes tend to carry two people more comfortably. See the [Honda Lead and Vision for two riders](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/scooter-for-two-riders-and-luggage-hanoi/) comparison for how load changes things.

## A five-minute test ride for either bike

1. **Slow-speed circle.** At walking pace, ride a tight circle each way. The steering should be smooth, with no catching and no wobble.
2. **Straight-line brake test.** From about 20 km/h on a quiet stretch, use both brakes and check the bike stops straight without a grab or a long lever pull.
3. **Bump test.** Roll over a speed bump or a rough patch at low speed. One bounce is normal; repeated bouncing means a tired shock.
4. **Stop and balance.** Stop, put both feet down, and see if the bike feels natural to hold.
5. **Listen.** With the engine idling and then rolling, listen for rattles, grinding and belt noise.

If any of these is off, ask for another bike. Do this before you sign, with the shop beside you. Remember that the final price and the bike are confirmed together, so there is no cost to asking.

## FAQ: Mio versus Vision

### Is the Yamaha Mio still sold new in Vietnam?

Yamaha Motor Vietnam's scooter range today lists models such as Grande, Janus, LEXi, Freego, NVX and Latte, and the Mio is not among them. A rental Mio is generally an older bike, so judge it on condition.

### Which is more comfortable for a long day?

On paper they are similar. In practice the better-conditioned bike is the more comfortable one, and a new-condition Vision usually has fresher suspension and a better seat than an older Mio.

### Which is easier for a beginner?

Both are automatics. The Vision's predictable behaviour and newer condition make it the safer default. A Mio that passes the test ride is also fine.

### Is the Mio faster than the Vision?

They are in the same small-scooter class and, at city speeds, the difference is not something you will notice. Condition matters more than the badge.

### What should I check on an older rental scooter?

Tyres, brakes, steering play, suspension rebound and unusual noises. The 10-minute checklist linked above shows the order.
