You can confirm the class of a rental electric vehicle in five checks, in order: look for working pedals, look for a plate, ask to see the registration certificate or a copy, compare the plate, chassis and engine numbers with it, and read the maximum speed and motor power from the vehicle's specification. Vietnam's Law 36/2024/QH15 sorts electric two-wheelers by those numbers, not by the name on a shop sign: a pedal-assisted machine that stops helping at 25 km/h is an electric bicycle, a scooter up to 50 km/h and 4 kW is a moped, and anything faster or stronger is a motorcycle. This guide shows how to check each step and why it matters for the licence you need.

## Why the class matters before you ride

The class decides three things: whether the vehicle is registered and carries a plate, who may ride it and what the rider must hold. A rental shop may call everything "electric bike", but in law the word covers three different vehicles. Our guide to [electric bike rules in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/electric-bikes-in-hanoi-rules/) describes what each class may do on the road, and this guide concentrates on how to confirm which one is in front of you.

Nguyen Ha Motorbike Rental lists electric bicycles, 50cc-equivalent electric scooters and some VinFast electric motorbikes as possibilities, and says the bike actually available is confirmed when you book. That is the right moment to ask about the class, before you pay for anything.

## The three classes in the law

Article 34 of Law 36/2024/QH15, which took effect on 1 January 2025, defines the vehicles. As summarised by the legal portal thuvienphapluat.vn and by VnExpress:

- **Electric bicycle.** A bicycle with motor assistance that cuts out when the rider stops pedalling or the vehicle reaches 25 km/h. It counts as a non-motorised vehicle. The national standards add that it has pedals, a motor of up to 250 W and a weight under 40 kg.
- **Electric moped (xe gan may).** A two- or three-wheeler with a design speed of no more than 50 km/h and, for an electric motor, a power of no more than 4 kW. It is a motor vehicle.
- **Electric motorcycle.** Anything above those limits. In practice, a design speed over 50 km/h, or a motor over 4 kW.

Note the word "or". A scooter that is limited in power but fast enough to exceed 50 km/h is not a moped. The two limits work together, and passing either puts the vehicle in the motorcycle class.

## Check 1: pedals

The quickest test needs no papers. If the machine has pedals and cuts assistance at 25 km/h, it is an electric bicycle. If it has a flat floor, a seat box and no pedals, it is either a moped or a motorcycle, and you need to look further. A pedal-less machine that is sold as an "e-bike" is not an electric bicycle in law, whatever the label says.

## Check 2: the plate

Electric mopeds and electric motorcycles are motor vehicles and must be registered and carry a plate, like petrol scooters. Electric bicycles are not registered and have no plate. Under Circular 79/2024/TT-BCA, which governs registration from 1 January 2025, plates are issued for motor vehicles, and electric bicycles are not among them.

So a fast, pedal-less scooter with no plate is a warning sign. Ask the shop why, and do not ride until you understand the answer. A plate alone does not tell you the class, though, because mopeds and motorcycles both carry one. Circular 24/2023/TT-BCA also notes that older five-digit plates with series such as MĐ stay valid, so do not guess a class from the letters.

## Check 3: ask for the registration certificate

The registration certificate, or a copy the shop provides, is the document that ties the plate to the vehicle. Under Circular 79/2024/TT-BCA the certificate form for motorcycles and mopeds records the brand, model code, vehicle type, chassis number, engine number and colour, and registration forms include fields for engine capacity and power. Ask to see it, and take a photograph.

Do not be shy about this. A shop that keeps its papers in order will show them without fuss. At Nguyen Ha the identity documents and licence are checked and photographed for the contract, and originals are not kept, so asking to see the vehicle's papers in return is fair. ## Check 4: compare the numbers

Match three things on the certificate with the bike itself:

1. **The plate** on the back against the plate number on the certificate.
2. **The chassis (frame) number** stamped on the frame, usually near the steering head or under a cover, against the certificate. Ask the shop to point it out.
3. **The model and brand** against what is printed on the bike and the display.

If any number differs, ask why. It may be a clerical difference, but it can also mean the bike is not the one on the paper. Do not argue about it on the street; call the shop.

## Check 5: read the speed and power figures

The specification is where the class is settled. Ask for the maximum speed and the motor power, then compare them with the thresholds.

VinFast's own guide to whether a licence is needed shows how this works with real models. It lists the Evo200 Lite at 49 km/h and 2,450 W, with no licence needed, and the Evo200 at 70 km/h and 2,500 W, with an A1 licence needed. Look closely: the two have almost the same motor power, but the second is faster than 50 km/h, so it is a motorcycle. The same page lists the Feliz S, Klara S and Vento S with top speeds of 78 km/h and above as needing A1. That page was published in 2023 and refers to the older traffic law, so use it as an illustration of the thresholds rather than as the current legal text. The speed limit of 50 km/h and power limit of 4 kW appear again in Law 36/2024/QH15.

These are VinFast models used as examples. The bike you are offered may be a different model, and a "Lite" or "Neo" name is not proof of anything, so read the figures.

## What each class needs from the rider

Under Law 36/2024/QH15, as of October 2026:

| Class | Typical figures | Rider age | Licence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Electric bicycle | Pedals, up to 25 km/h | No minimum set in the law | None |
| Electric moped | Up to 50 km/h and 4 kW | 16 or older | None |
| Electric motorcycle up to 11 kW | Over 50 km/h or over 4 kW | 18 or older | Class A1 |
| Electric motorcycle over 11 kW | Over 11 kW | 18 or older | Class A |

Class A1 covers two-wheelers up to 125 cc or with an electric motor up to 11 kW, and class A covers larger ones. For a foreign visitor the question is whether your home licence and an International Driving Permit cover motorcycles. Our guide to [motorbike licence rules for foreigners in Vietnam](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbike-licence-rules-for-foreigners-in-vietnam/) explains it. If you have no motorcycle entitlement, the legal choices are an electric moped or an electric bicycle, and the checks above tell you which one is in front of you.

## Red flags that call for a pause

- A pedal-less scooter with no plate.
- A shop saying "no licence needed" for a bike that tops 50 km/h.
- A name such as "Lite" used as the only evidence of class.
- A registration certificate that is missing, blurred or does not match the plate.
- A plate or chassis number that does not match the paper.
- A shop that cannot give the speed and power figures.

None of these proves anything improper, but each is a reason to ask, and if the answers do not satisfy you, choose another bike.

## What to do when the answers do not add up

Ask the shop to confirm the class in writing or on the contract, and to tell you which licence it expects you to hold. Photograph the certificate, the plate and the display. If the class is a motorcycle and you lack the right licence, ask for a moped-class bike or an electric bicycle instead. ## FAQ: confirming an electric vehicle's class

### How do I tell an electric bicycle from an electric moped?

Look for pedals and a 25 km/h cut-off. A pedal-less scooter is a moped or a motorcycle.

### Does an electric moped need a plate?

Yes. It is a motor vehicle and must be registered and carry a plate. Electric bicycles are not registered.

### What decides whether I need a licence?

The class. No licence is needed for an electric bicycle or an electric moped. A motorcycle-class machine needs class A1, or class A above 11 kW.

### Is power or speed more important?

Both. A moped is limited to 50 km/h and 4 kW, so a vehicle that exceeds either is a motorcycle.

### Can I rely on the shop's description?

Use it as a starting point, then check the figures and papers yourself.

### What if the registration certificate does not match the plate?

Ask the shop to explain, and do not ride until it is resolved.
