Walk, or take the metro or a bus, when your day stays inside the compact heart of Hanoi: the Old Quarter, Hoan Kiem Lake and the French Quarter are all within a short stroll of each other, the streets around the lake are closed to vehicles on weekend evenings, and a scooter in that area mostly means hunting for parking. Choose a 50cc rental when you want to string together several stops across the river or in the suburbs, start before public transport runs or finish after it stops, or base yourself in Long Bien for a week or more. This guide sets out the dividing line with real distances and operating hours, explains what the public network looks like in October 2026, and gives a simple table to decide day by day.

## The short rule: match the vehicle to the shape of the day

A 50cc scooter is a small, slow machine with a legal ceiling of 40 km/h and a rider minimum age of 16. Its strength is flexibility, not speed. That flexibility is worth paying for when your stops are scattered, and wasted when they are clustered.

Think of the day in terms of its shape:

- **A cluster** (several sights within one or two kilometres of each other): walk.
- **A line** (one long corridor, such as east to west across the city): a bus or the metro may do it, if the line happens to match.
- **A scatter** (stops in different directions with gaps between): a scooter or a ride-hailing car.
- **A base outside the centre** (you sleep in Long Bien, Gia Lam or further out): a scooter gives you a daily commute you control.

Our guide to [what a 50cc rental suits and what the rules say](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/renting-a-50cc-scooter-in-hanoi/) covers who can ride one. This article is about the opposite question: when you should not bother.

## Where walking clearly wins

The old centre is dense, and most of the things visitors come for are very close together. Published guides describe the Old Quarter as roughly one square kilometre between Hoan Kiem Lake and Dong Xuan Market, and report that you can reach the lake from its southern edge in under five minutes on foot, and the French Quarter from the lake in about ten more. The lake's own perimeter is about 1.8 km, which takes roughly 20 to 25 minutes at a leisurely pace.

| Trip | Approximate distance or time on foot | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Old Quarter to Hoan Kiem Lake | Under 5 minutes from the southern edge | Walk |
| Full loop of Hoan Kiem Lake | About 1.8 km, 20 to 25 minutes | Walk |
| Lake to the French Quarter | About 10 minutes | Walk |
| Old Quarter to Hoa Lo Prison | About 1.5 km | Walk, or a short taxi in heat |
| Old Quarter to the Temple of Literature | About 3 km | Taxi, ride-hailing or bus |

Distances come from visitor guides and are approximate, so check your own route on a map. The point is the pattern: inside the centre, a scooter saves little time. Our guide to [riding in the Old Quarter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-old-quarter/) explains the one-way streets and the hunt for a place to leave the bike. If you only want a single street-food or lake evening, walking is cheaper, simpler and often faster.

## The weekend pedestrian streets

On weekends the case for walking becomes a rule. Hanoi closes streets around Hoan Kiem Lake, and some connecting Old Quarter streets, to vehicles for a walking street. Visitor sources describe the usual window as starting at about 19:00 on Friday and running until midnight on Sunday, and say that the hours have changed in past years, so confirm them locally.

In that zone a rented scooter is not just unhelpful, it is excluded. Ride-hailing cars cannot drop you at the lake edge during those hours, so cars and taxis stop at the perimeter too. Visitors who arrive by motorbike are directed to designated parking areas on surrounding streets such as Hai Ba Trung, Ngo Quyen and Quang Trung, and finding a space on a Saturday night can be a challenge. Our guide to [where to park a scooter near Hoan Kiem Lake](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/where-to-park-a-scooter-near-hoan-kiem-lake/) explains the options.

The sensible plan for a weekend evening around the lake is to leave the bike at your accommodation, or park it once in a guarded lot, and walk from there.

## Public transport in October 2026

Hanoi's public network has changed quickly in the past two years, and several details in this section are recent, so treat them as a snapshot.

**Metro.** The official operator's site lists two operating lines, Line 2A (Cat Linh to Ha Dong) and Line 3 (Nhon towards Hanoi Station), both with a timetable of 05:30 to 22:00. Line 2A is fully elevated. Line 3's elevated section runs from Nhon to Cau Giay, and a travel site notes that the two lines do not currently connect. The underground stretch of Line 3 toward Hanoi Railway Station was still being finished in mid-2026, so check the operator's site for the current extent. The metro serves commuter corridors in the west and south-west, not the Old Quarter itself, so it helps if your accommodation or destination is along those lines. Last trains leave well before the evening life of the centre ends.

**Buses.** The city bus network is run mainly by Transerco and other operators. In October 2026 Transerco was still replacing diesel buses with electric ones, including routes starting at Gia Lam Bus Station and Long Bien, which are close to the area around the Nguyen Ha shop. Buses are the cheapest way to cross town, but slower in traffic and less obvious to read for a first-time visitor, so use a map app and the route numbers shown at the stop.

**Tickets.** From 25 June 2026 the city announced an interconnected electronic ticket system across public transport, accepting traffic cards, bank cards, QR codes and e-wallets in place of cash and paper tickets. Hanoi's Department of Construction also said that passengers travelling within Ring Road 1 would travel free from 1 July 2026 to 30 June 2027, under a City People's Council resolution. Whether that applies to every route, and to foreign visitors in the same way, is something to check at the stop or station before you rely on it.

**Metro and bus fares** are low and change, so look up the current figure rather than trusting an old article.

## Taxis and ride-hailing for the in-between trips

For distances of a few kilometres where walking is too far and a bus is awkward, a ride-hailing car or electric taxi is the usual answer. It suits the Temple of Literature, a museum across town, the airport or a late return after the metro has closed. The price depends on the app, the time and the traffic, and it does not need any riding confidence, helmet or parking. Pair this with walking for a centre-only stay and you may never need a bike.

## Where a 50cc rental does win

A small moped earns its place in these situations:

1. **You are based in Long Bien or Gia Lam.** Nguyen Ha Motorbike Rental is at Alley 5 Nguyen Van Cu Street, Bo De Ward, Long Bien District, near Gia Lam bus station. From there the river crossings and the quiet roads of the east bank are the natural territory of a scooter. Our guide to the [easy first ride out of central Hanoi via Long Bien](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/long-bien-red-river-first-ride/) shows a gentle option.

2. **You have several stops in different directions.** A craft-village trip, a market, a cafe and the riverbank in one day is a scatter pattern.

3. **You need to start early or finish late.** Public transport has fixed hours, and the metro stops at 22:00.

4. **You are staying a week or a month.** Weekly and monthly rentals change the arithmetic, since a flat package replaces a string of fares and rides.

5. **You cannot or do not want to ride a bigger bike.** A 50cc moped needs no motorcycle licence in Vietnam, subject to the minimum age and all the other rules of the road.

A fair warning on the other side: a 50cc bike is slow, and it is the wrong tool for the expressways, the main national roads or a long day trip.

## A simple decision table

| Your day looks like | Choose |
|---|---|
| Old Quarter, lake and street food within a few kilometres | Walk |
| Friday to Sunday evening around Hoan Kiem Lake | Walk, leaving the bike behind |
| A commuter route that follows the metro line | Metro |
| Cheap cross-town trip with no hurry | Bus |
| Single trip of 3 to 5 km, hot or rainy | Ride-hailing or taxi |
| Many scattered stops east of the river | 50cc rental |
| Before 05:30 or after 22:00 | 50cc rental or taxi |
| A week or more based in Long Bien | 50cc rental |
| Trip to Ninh Binh, Ha Long or the hills | Neither; see our guides on longer rides |

If you choose to rent, the 50cc group is VND 200,000 per day at Nguyen Ha, as set out on the [prices page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/prices/). A good compromise is to walk and use transport for the city centre days and rent for just one or two scattered days, rather than keeping a bike you rarely ride.

## Weather, heat and safety

Heat and rain argue for the metro or a taxi. A walk in summer midday sun is tiring, and riding in a downpour is harder on a small scooter. Our guide to [riding in Hanoi's rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) explains what changes. At night, a rental raises other questions, covered in our guide to [riding at night in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-at-night-in-hanoi/). If you will be out late, you may prefer to ride in daylight and use a taxi after dark.

## FAQ: walking and transport versus a 50cc rental

### Is Hanoi's Old Quarter walkable?

Yes. It covers roughly one square kilometre, the lake is minutes away, and the pavements are busy but walkable. Be alert to motorbikes crossing the pavement.

### Can I take a 50cc scooter into the weekend walking street?

No. The zone around Hoan Kiem Lake is closed to vehicles from about Friday 19:00 to Sunday midnight, with hours that can change. Park outside it and walk.

### Does the Hanoi metro go to the Old Quarter?

Not directly. The two operating lines, 2A and 3, serve routes to the west and south-west, and neither is a quick way into the centre. Check a map for the closest station to your stop.

### Are buses free in central Hanoi?

A report from June 2026 said travel within Ring Road 1 would be free from 1 July 2026 to 30 June 2027. Confirm the current arrangement and how to pay before relying on it.

### When is a 50cc scooter better than a taxi?

When you have several stops, want to start or finish outside public transport hours, or stay several days and want your own transport.

### Do I need a licence for a 50cc scooter?

Not for a moped under Vietnamese law, but you must be at least 16 and obey every other rule. Ask the shop about its own terms.
