A rental scooter feels comfortable on paved roads because small bumps are spread across a large contact area and a steady surface lets the suspension work within its range. On rough lanes the same machine feels harsh for four reasons: small wheels drop into holes, the short suspension runs out of travel, tyre pressure that was fine on asphalt feels wrong on broken ground, and ground clearance matters more when the surface is uneven. You cannot change the hardware on a rental, but you can choose a model that suits your route, check tyre condition and adapt your speed and posture. This guide explains each factor, compares the Vision, Air Blade and Lead, and gives a technique list for patched streets, speed bumps and unpaved lanes.

## What actually makes a scooter comfortable

Comfort on a scooter comes from a short chain of parts, and the weakest link sets the experience:

- **Tyres and wheels.** Tyres are the first suspension. A larger wheel rolls over a given edge or hole more easily than a smaller one. This is general engineering knowledge, not a Honda statement.
- **Fork and rear shock.** Honda Vietnam lists a telescopic front fork with hydraulic damping on the Vision and the Air Blade, and a single coil-spring rear unit with hydraulic damping. The Lead has a telescopic fork and a coil-spring rear unit.
- **Seat.** Foam depth and shape decide how much of the remaining shock reaches your spine.
- **Weight and load.** A heavier load compresses the suspension and leaves less travel for bumps.
- **You.** Relaxed arms and knees absorb more than a stiff body.

For a sense of scale, Honda's UK specification page for the Vision 110 lists 90 mm of front and 85 mm of rear suspension travel. Vietnamese models differ in detail, but the figure shows how short a scooter's travel is compared with a trail bike. Short travel is why speed matters so much on bad surfaces.

## Honda's figures that bear on comfort

These numbers come from Honda Vietnam's specification pages for the current models. Rental bikes may be older versions.

| Front wheel/tyre | 16 inch, 80/90-16 | 14 inch, 90/80-14 | 12 inch, 90/90-12 |
| Rear wheel/tyre | 14 inch, 90/90-14 | 14 inch, 100/80-14 | 10 inch, 100/90-10 |
| Ground clearance | 175 mm | 142 mm | 140 mm |
| Kerb weight | 97 kg | 113 kg | 114 kg |
| Seat height | 785 mm | 775 mm | 760 mm |

Read the table as a set of trade-offs rather than a ranking. The Vision has the largest wheels at the front and the most clearance, which helps on lumpy lanes. The Air Blade has matching 14-inch wheels and more weight, which tends to feel planted on smooth, fast roads. The Lead's small wheels make it agile and its low seat makes it easy to put a foot down, but a sharp edge or pothole will be felt more.

## Paved Hanoi: where a scooter is at its best

On good asphalt, any of these scooters is comfortable. The trouble spots are small and repeated:

- **Patched trenches** where utilities have been repaired, often slightly raised or sunken.
- **Manhole covers and drain grates**, which are slick when wet.
- **Speed bumps and rumble strips**, which come at awkward moments.
- **Painted lines and metal plates**, which lose grip in rain.
- **Gutter edges** at the kerb, where the road surface drops away.

Slow down for each of them and pass over with the wheel straight. Honda's owner manuals warn that tyres slip more easily on low-grip surfaces and that stopping distance lengthens, so the advice is to brake before the obstacle, not on it. Our guide to [riding in Hanoi rain](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) lists the streets that flood and become rougher than they look.

## Rough lanes: what changes

By rough lanes we mean broken concrete, gravel, packed earth, flooded dips and narrow village alleys with ridges. They are common on the east bank, in craft villages and on the outskirts. Our guide to [Gia Lam, the east bank and Bat Trang](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/gia-lam-east-bank-bat-trang-by-scooter/) describes the quiet roads out there, and the [Gia Lam area page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/gia-lam/) gives the local context.

On these surfaces:

- **Steering gets lighter and twitchier** when the front tyre loses grip on loose material.
- **Braking distances grow.** Honda's Air Blade manual says its front-wheel ABS does not shorten stopping distance and in some cases may lengthen it.
- **The underside can strike** a ridge, particularly with a passenger or luggage aboard.
- **Dust and water hide holes.** A puddle may be a few centimetres deep or a broken road.
- **Your own comfort drops fast** because each bump arrives through a short suspension at speed.

The simplest cure is speed. Half the speed gives each bump far less energy to deliver. If the lane looks worse than the bike can handle, stop and walk it.

## Tyre pressure and condition

Honda's tyre guidance explains that pressure drops gradually over time, that under-inflation is hard to see, and that a gauge should be used at least once a month on cold tyres. The correct figures vary by motorcycle and are usually printed on a label on the frame or in the owner's manual. A tyre that is too soft feels vague and may damage the rim on a sharp edge. One that is too hard feels harsh and gives less grip. On a rental, do not guess. Ask the shop whether the pressures were checked and what they should be for one rider or two.

Check these on the bike before you leave:

1. **Look at the sidewalls** for cuts, cracks or bulges.
2. **Find the tread-wear indicator.** Honda says to replace a tyre immediately once it shows.
3. **Press the tyre with your thumb** to feel if it is clearly soft, and ask the shop if in doubt.
4. **Remember the rims.** Honda's manuals say these scooters take tubeless tyres only, because the rims are designed for them.

## Riding technique for a rougher surface

A scooter has no foot controls to manage, which frees you to concentrate on posture and speed:

| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| Approaching a bump or hole | Slow before it, not on it. Keep the handlebar straight |
| Crossing a speed bump | Roll over at walking pace, then accelerate gently |
| Loose gravel or sand | Keep a steady throttle, do not brake hard, keep your feet up and ready |
| Wet or muddy lane | Add distance, avoid lines and plates, dab both brakes lightly to test grip |
| Ridged concrete | Cross ridges at a shallow angle only if you must, otherwise stop and walk |
| Narrow alley with pedestrians | Walking pace, horn ready, feet close to the ground |

Keep your elbows soft, grip loose, and your knees tucked against the leg shield. A tense grip passes every shock to your shoulders, and it makes the steering twitch.

Avoid closing the throttle suddenly on a slippery lane. Honda's PCX manual warns that closing the throttle abruptly on slippery surfaces can cause the rear wheel to skid because of engine braking, and that traction control may not compensate. The better habit is to roll off the throttle smoothly.

## Load, passengers and comfort

Weight changes comfort sharply. A passenger compresses the rear suspension, which reduces the travel left for bumps and also lowers the rear of the bike, which lowers ground clearance. A heavy bag at the back does the same. Honda's manuals warn that carrying more than the stated load degrades handling, braking and stability. If you plan a rough lane with a passenger, consider walking the worst parts, and see [two riders and luggage on a Honda Lead or Vision](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/scooter-for-two-riders-and-luggage-hanoi/) for guidance on loading.

## A short test ride that shows comfort differences

If the shop lets you try the bike before paying, spend five minutes on a stretch you already know. Ride it once at ordinary speed over a patched section and once at walking pace over a speed bump. Notice three things: whether the front wheel feels light or jittery over the patch, whether the rear end bounces or settles after the bump, and whether your wrists and lower back feel any strain. Then sit on the bike at a standstill and check that both feet reach the ground and that the handlebar turns fully without catching your knees.

Comfort is personal. A rider of 1.60 m and a rider of 1.85 m can prefer opposite bikes, and a model that suits one commute may disappoint on another. If the first bike feels harsh and the shop has a second option, ask to swap before the rental starts, not halfway through.

## FAQ: comfort on different surfaces

### Why does my scooter feel so harsh on a small bump?

Short suspension travel and small wheels leave little room to absorb a sharp edge. Slowing down before the bump reduces the shock more than anything else.

### Should I let air out of the tyres for a rough road?

No. On a rental, leave pressures to the shop. Changing them can affect grip and rim protection.

### Is a bigger wheel always better on rough ground?

Generally it rolls over holes and edges more easily, but weight, tyre width and your speed also matter.

### Can I ride a rental scooter on an unpaved lane?

Ask the shop about its terms and the state of the bike. Choose lanes you can walk if needed, and avoid them in the rain.

### What should I do if I hit a hole hard?

Stop somewhere safe and check both tyres for damage and the rims for dents. Look for fluid leaks and check that the front wheel turns straight. If anything looks wrong, call the shop on 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969).
