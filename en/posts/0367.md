A good two-day scooter trip from Hanoi is built from three decisions made before you collect the bike: how far you will ride each day, where you will sleep, and when the scooter must be back. Get those right and the destination almost chooses itself. A sensible rule is to keep each day's riding to a few hours with long stops, to book a night stop in advance, and to treat the second day as the day you must be back in Hanoi before the shop closes, not the day you explore. This guide is a planning framework rather than a single itinerary: it shows how to choose a trip shape, how to turn published distances into a realistic riding budget, how the rental day fits a two-day plan, what to carry and what to do if weather or the bike lets you down.

## Start with the rental clock, not the map

At Nguyen Ha, one rental day is 24 hours counted from handover, and shorter use is charged as a full day. The shop is open from 09:00 to 21:00 daily, so unless you agree a different arrangement, a 09:00 handover means the bike is due back around 09:00 on the next day for a one-day rental, or around 09:00 on day three for a two-day one. Late returns of one to five hours are VND 30,000 per hour, and six hours or more counts as another day. Weekly and monthly packages are priced separately and are not simply the day rate multiplied.

So the first question is practical: when do you want to collect and return? Ask the shop about an earlier or later arrangement rather than assuming. Then work backwards. If the return deadline is mid-morning, a long ride on the final morning is a bad idea. If you plan to come back in the evening, remember that the shop closes at 21:00 and that you should not be riding rural roads at that hour anyway.

Tell the shop your route and overnight plan when you book, because the contract and the vehicle should match the trip. Our guide on [taking a rental motorbike out of Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/take-rental-motorbike-out-of-hanoi/) lists the questions to ask, and the [prices page](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/prices/) and [deposit guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbike-rental-deposit-hanoi/) explain the rest.

## Pick a trip shape

Most two-day trips follow one of three shapes. Each has a different risk profile.

| Shape | How it works | Good for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Out and back with one night | Ride out on day one, sleep, return by the same or a different road on day two | Destinations 60 to 120 km away | The return day can be rushed |
| Base and loop | Ride to one base, spend day two exploring nearby without luggage, return on day two evening | Areas with several stops, like Ninh Binh | The return ride is longer than the morning ride |
| Two-ride triangle | Ride to point A, sleep, ride to point B, return | Experienced riders | Longer total distance and more booking risk |

If it is your first long scooter ride, choose the first shape. It has the simplest route and the fewest transfers.

## Turn published distances into a riding budget

Do not trust one number from a map app. Compare published figures and add your own margin. These are the figures we have seen in guides on this site and in travel sources, all one way from central Hanoi:

| Destination | Published distance | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Ba Vi area | about 60 km | Tourism authority figure for Ba Vi district; see [Hanoi to Ba Vi National Park](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-to-ba-vi-by-motorbike/) |
| Perfume Pagoda | about 60 km | Travel guides quote 1.5 to 2 hours by road |
| Tam Dao | about 70 to 85 km | Our [Tam Dao guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-to-tam-dao-by-motorbike/) covers the mountain section |
| Ninh Binh | about 93 to 110 km | Sources differ; see the [Ninh Binh route guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-to-ninh-binh-by-motorbike/) |
| Mai Chau | about 135 to 150 km | See the [Mai Chau guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-to-mai-chau-by-motorbike/) |
| Ha Long | about 155 to 170 km | See the [Ha Long guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-to-ha-long-bay-by-motorbike/) |

For speed, use the evidence we have. Our Ninh Binh guide budgets two and a half to three and a half hours for about 95 km on a 110 to 125 cc scooter, which is only around 30 to 40 km/h including traffic lights and small stops. Use that as a rule of thumb, not a measurement: it is the pace of a mixed route, not an expressway. A hill road or heavy rain will be slower.

A simple planning rule: keep riding to about three to four hours a day at that pace, which is roughly 90 to 150 km, and treat anything above that as a stretch. Most first-timers will be happier in the 60 to 120 km range each way, with Mai Chau and Ha Long at or beyond the top of a comfortable two-day budget.

## Rules and documents, briefly

Check the rules before you commit to a long ride. As of October 2026, the Road Traffic Order and Safety Law (No. 36/2024/QH15, in force since 1 January 2025) divides driving licences into categories. According to the English text on thuvienphapluat.vn, category A1 covers two-wheeled motorbikes up to 125 cm³ and category A covers larger engines. The same text says a holder of an international driving permit from a Vienna Convention state must carry both that permit and the national licence appropriate to the vehicle. Helmets must be worn by both rider and passenger. These points are a summary of an official source; for your own situation read our [licence guide for visitors](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbike-licence-rules-for-foreigners-in-vietnam/) and the list of [documents to carry](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/documents-to-carry-riding-in-vietnam/).

Motorbikes are not allowed on expressways. Many car routes to Ninh Binh, Ha Long and the western hills use them, so check your route with the [navigation-app guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-check-if-a-navigation-app-suggests-an-expressway/) and the [ring roads guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-ring-roads-highways-motorbikes-allowed/). If anything about your riding entitlement is unclear, stay in town and ask a qualified source instead of testing it on a long trip.

## Choose a bike for two days

An automatic scooter such as a Honda Air Blade is the comfortable default for stop-and-go exits from the city; see [Honda Vision vs Air Blade](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/honda-vision-vs-air-blade-which-scooter-to-rent-in-hanoi/) and the [motorbike range](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/motorbikes/). A semi-automatic is fine for riders who know gears. Electric scooters and small 50cc bikes are rarely a good fit for a two-day trip because of range and power; see [renting an electric scooter](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/renting-an-electric-scooter-in-hanoi/) before you decide. Whichever bike you take, the actual machine is confirmed at booking, and you should do the [10-minute inspection](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-scooter-inspection-checklist/) before leaving, paying attention to tyres, brakes, lights and chain.

If you will carry a passenger, read [scooter for two riders and luggage](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/scooter-for-two-riders-and-luggage-hanoi/).

## Book the night stop first

Because the ride depends on the night stop, book it before fixing the route. Choose accommodation with safe motorbike parking, or ask where to park nearby. The shop does not provide an extra lock, so lock the steering and keep helmets with you, not on the mirrors. Check the check-in time too: arriving after dark on an unfamiliar rural road is the situation to avoid. Our guide to [riding at night in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-at-night-in-hanoi/) explains the visibility problem.

If you can, choose a stop where you can walk to dinner and leave the scooter parked. A short walk to a restaurant makes the evening safer and means no second ride.

## Sample day structure

Use this as a template and adjust to your distance:

**Day one**
- Collect the bike at opening time, check it, fill the tank. See [fuel in Hanoi](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/fuel-in-hanoi-petrol-stations-rental-scooter/).
- Clear the city before the worst traffic if you can; the first 20 km are the slowest.
- Take a break roughly every two hours. Our guide on [how to plan breaks on a long ride](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-plan-breaks-on-a-long-semi-automatic-bike-ride/) helps.
- Arrive at the night stop with daylight left.

**Day two**
- Do the sightseeing in the morning, with no luggage if your accommodation allows storage.
- Start back at a time that puts you in Hanoi in daylight and before the shop closes.
- Refuel before the last leg, then return with plenty of margin. A late return carries charges, so plan to arrive early, not exactly on time.

## What to carry

Keep it small. A large backpack hanging off the rider strains the shoulders and affects balance; a small pack and a bag secured low on the bike is better.

- Driving documents, passport copy and the contract copy
- Phone with an offline map and a power bank; note the shop number, 0334 699 969
- Rain poncho, since a raincoat is not included in short rentals
- Water, sunscreen, a spare layer for evenings in the hills
- Cash in small notes, as many fuel stops and parking attendants prefer it; see [paying for fuel, parking and tolls](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/paying-fuel-parking-tolls-hanoi-visitor/)

## Plan for rain and breakdowns

Decide in advance what you will do if the forecast turns bad: shorten the route, delay a day or leave the scooter and take a bus. Our [rain guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/riding-in-hanoi-rain/) covers gear and flooding. For mechanical trouble, stop somewhere safe, take photographs of the location and call the shop; our [breakdown guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-bike-breakdown-hanoi-what-to-do/) explains what to do first, and [travel insurance and rentals](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/travel-insurance-motorbike-rental-vietnam/) shows what policies often exclude. Do not agree to expensive repairs by phone without the shop's approval.

## FAQ

### How far should I ride each day?

For most first-timers, around 60 to 120 km each way is comfortable if you leave early and stop often. Longer distances like Mai Chau or Ha Long are a stretch for a two-day trip.

### Can I take the scooter overnight outside Hanoi?

Ask the shop before booking. We explain the questions in [our out-of-city guide](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/take-rental-motorbike-out-of-hanoi/), and the answer depends on the contract, the route and the bike.

### What if I return after the shop closes?

Plan not to. The shop is open 09:00 to 21:00 daily. Contact the shop in advance if you think you will be late; charges apply for late returns.

### Is it better to ride out and back on the same road?

It is simpler, and you already know what to expect. A different return road can be rewarding but adds navigation risk. Check it against the expressway rules first.
