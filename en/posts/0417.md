If a rental motorbike turns out to have a safety defect, stop riding it, record the problem with photographs or video, contact the shop straight away with clear facts and ask for a repair or a replacement bike in writing. Do not carry on in the hope that it will improve, and do not repair a brake or steering part yourself.

A safety defect means something that could make the bike unsafe to ride: a brake that does not work properly, a damaged tyre, a loose or broken steering part, a chain that is about to fail, lights that do not work, a fuel leak. In Vietnam the Civil Code says that a lessor must keep the leased property in the agreed condition and fit for its purpose, and that a lessee who finds the property defective may ask for repair, a lower rent or a replacement. How that works in practice depends on the contract and on what the shop has available. This guide explains what counts as a safety defect, what to do first, how to ask, what the law says and what to expect.

## What counts as a safety defect

Not every fault is a safety defect. A scratched panel or a squeaky mirror is not a reason to demand another bike. A safety defect is one that affects your ability to control or stop the bike, to be seen or to avoid an accident. Examples include:

- **Brakes.** A pedal or lever that goes almost to the end of its travel, a brake that grabs or drags, a brake that does not hold, or one that feels spongy. Honda's guidance says brake shoes wear and free play grows until the brake no longer works well. Our guide to [checking the rear brake on a Honda Wave](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/checking-the-rear-brake-on-a-honda-wave/) explains how to judge one.

- **Tyres.** Cuts, cracks, bulges, tread worn to the indicator or a tyre that keeps losing air. Honda's tyre page says to stop using the motorcycle if its tyres show cracks or scratches.

- **Steering and suspension.** A handlebar that is loose, bent or hard to turn, a front fork that leaks, or a wheel that wobbles.

- **Chain and drive.** A chain that is very loose, kinked, rusted solid or rattling badly, or a gear that will not engage or keeps jumping out.

- **Lights and signals.** A headlight, brake light or indicator that does not work, which matters most after dark. Our guide to [checking a manual bike's lights before riding at night](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/checking-a-manual-bikes-lights-before-riding-at-night/) explains the checks.

- **Fuel or oil leaks.**

- **Starting and electrical faults** that could leave you stranded in traffic.

If you are unsure, treat it as serious and ask. A shop would rather hear about a brake that feels odd than about a crash.

## Step 1: stop and make it safe

As soon as you suspect a safety defect, stop in a safe place. Pull off the road, away from junctions and bends, and switch off the engine. Do not ride on to "see if it improves". If the problem is the brakes or the steering, do not ride at all, even slowly. Honda says brake inspection and replacement needs skill and should be done by a dealer.

If you are still at the shop, simply do not accept the bike. If you are already out, call the shop before you do anything else.

## Step 2: record what you found

Evidence helps you and the shop. Spend a couple of minutes on it.

- **Photograph or film the problem.** For a brake, film the pedal or lever being pressed and show how far it travels. For a tyre, photograph the cut or bulge with a finger for scale. For a light, show that it does not work.

- **Note the time, the place and the odometer reading.**

- **Photograph the whole bike and the plate number,** so that it is clear which bike it is.

- **Write down what happened,** in two or three plain sentences: what you noticed, when and what you did.

If the defect appeared during the ride, say so. If you noticed it at pickup but did not mention it, say that too. Honesty makes the discussion simpler. Our guide to [how to document existing scratches on a rental bike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/how-to-document-existing-scratches-on-a-rental-bike/) describes a useful photograph method.

## Step 3: contact the shop with clear facts

Call or message as soon as you can. Nguyen Ha can be reached on 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969), open 09:00 to 21:00 daily. If you message, include:

- your name and the plate number of the bike;

- where you are, with a map pin if possible;

- what the defect is, in one or two sentences;

- what you have done, such as stopping and switching off;

- a photograph or short video;

- what you would like: a mechanic's check, a repair or a replacement bike.

A sample message: "Hello, I rented the Honda Wave, plate [number], this morning. The rear brake pedal now goes almost to the footrest and the bike does not slow properly. I have stopped and switched off. I am at [place]. I have attached a video. Could you check it or arrange another bike?"

Keep the tone calm and factual. You are reporting a safety problem, not making an accusation.

## Step 4: ask for a repair or a replacement

You may not need a replacement. Many defects can be fixed quickly by a mechanic. But if the shop cannot fix it at once, or if you do not trust the bike after a safety fault, ask for another bike. Words you could use:

- "Could a mechanic look at it now?"

- "If it cannot be fixed today, could I have a different bike?"

- "Could you confirm in writing what you will do?"

Ask for the answer in writing, even if it is a short message. If the shop sends someone to collect the bike or to bring another, ask for a time. If you are far from Hanoi, ask how the swap will work. ## What the Civil Code says

Vietnam's Civil Code (Law No. 91/2015/QH13), in force since 1 January 2017, treats a motorbike rental as a lease of property. As of October 2026, the relevant general rules, which we read in the text published by the Tuyen Quang Department of Justice, are these:

- **Article 476** says the lessor must hand over the property in the agreed quantity, quality, type and condition, at the agreed time and place, and give the necessary information on its use. If the property is not of the agreed quality, the lessee may ask the lessor to repair it, reduce the rent or cancel the contract and claim compensation.

- **Article 477** says the lessor must keep the property in the agreed condition and fit for the purpose of the lease throughout the lease period, and must repair damage and defects, except minor damage that custom requires the lessee to repair. If the property's value in use falls without the lessee's fault, the lessee may ask the lessor to repair it, reduce the rent, or exchange it for other property or terminate the contract and claim compensation if the defect was unknown to the lessee or cannot be repaired so that the purpose of the lease is not achieved.

- **Article 477** also says that if the lessor has been notified but does not repair, or repairs late, the lessee may repair the property at reasonable cost, must tell the lessor and may ask the lessor to pay the cost.

These are general rules. A rental contract fills in the details, and the shop's own terms may set how swaps, refunds and repairs are handled. Nguyen Ha's published terms do not describe a standard replacement procedure, so ask. Our explanation of [what the clauses in a Hanoi rental contract mean](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/hanoi-motorbike-rental-contract-explained/) explains how to read the damage and breakdown wording. This is general information, not legal advice.

## Do not repair safety parts yourself

Article 477 allows a lessee to arrange a repair at reasonable cost if the lessor does not act after being told. That does not mean you should repair a brake or steering part yourself or have it done by whoever is nearby. Safety repairs need skill, and a poor repair can make matters worse. If you think a repair is needed and the shop is slow to respond, tell the shop what you plan to do and ask for its agreement first, and keep every receipt. Do not remove or modify parts, and do not authorise major work without telling the shop. ## What the shop may offer

A shop usually has a few options, depending on the fault and the stock:

- **Fix it on the spot** if the problem is small.

- **Send a mechanic** to you.

- **Swap the bike** for another of the same class, if one is available.

- **Offer a different class** of bike, which may change the price.

- **Agree to end the rental** in some cases, with the details settled in writing.

Which of these applies depends on the contract and the situation. If you are offered a different class of bike, ask whether the price changes and have it written down. If the rental is ended early, ask how unused time and the deposit are handled, because the published terms say that early return does not refund unused rent. That is the standard term for a rider's own choice to return early, and a defect may be a different matter, so raise it and ask for the answer in writing. ## If the defect was there at pickup

A defect that existed when the bike was handed over is the lessor's responsibility to put right, subject to the contract. That is why the pickup inspection matters. Our [10-minute inspection checklist](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/rental-scooter-inspection-checklist/) and our guide to [manual bike brakes: what to test before leaving the rental shop](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/manual-bike-brakes-what-to-test-before-leaving-the-rental-shop/) help you spot problems before you ride away. If you find a defect at pickup, do not accept the bike, and ask for another before you sign.

## If the defect appeared during the rental

A defect that appears during the rental is harder to classify. Normal wear is not the renter's fault, but damage from a fall or misuse may be. Do not hide anything, and describe what happened. Our guide to [what to do after dropping a rented semi-automatic bike](https://blog.rentbikehanoi.com/en/what-to-do-after-dropping-a-rented-semi-automatic-bike/) explains what to say. If you are unsure whether a fault is wear or damage, report it anyway and let the shop assess it.

## FAQ: requesting a replacement bike

### Can I ask for another bike if the brakes are faulty?

Yes, you can ask. The Civil Code says the lessor must keep the property fit for use, and a lessee may request repair or an exchange in some cases. What the shop offers depends on the contract and the stock.

### Should I keep riding to the shop?

Not if the brakes, steering or tyres are unsafe. Stop and call the shop.

### Can I repair the bike myself and claim the cost?

The Civil Code allows a lessee to repair at reasonable cost after notifying the lessor who fails to act, but safety parts need skilled work. Tell the shop first and keep receipts.

### Will I get a refund if the bike is replaced?

It depends on the contract. Ask the shop to confirm the arrangement in writing.

### What if the shop has no other bike?

Ask what alternatives it can offer, such as a different class of bike, a mechanic's visit or ending the rental, and have the answer written down.

### Is a scratched panel a safety defect?

No. A safety defect affects control, stopping, visibility or reliability.
