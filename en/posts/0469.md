In Vietnam, 112 is the national emergency call number, and it connects with the older services: 113 for the police, 114 for fire and rescue and 115 for ambulances. Decree 200/2025/ND-CP, in force since 23 August 2025, set up the 112 call centre for incidents, disasters and emergencies; calls are free from fixed and mobile phones, answered 24 hours a day, and forwarded to the 113, 114 or 115 services where they fall within their scope. For a road accident, Article 81 of Law 36/2024/QH15 requires anyone who detects one to report it immediately to the nearest police, medical facility or People's Committee, and Article 80 requires people involved in an accident to stop, keep the scene and help the injured. If you are on a rental scooter, call the shop as well as the emergency services.

This guide lists the numbers, explains what to say when you call, summarises the legal duties after an accident, describes what happens next, explains how to report a theft or other crime, and tells you what to keep and who to inform afterwards. It is general information based on Vietnamese law and official guidance as of October 2026, not legal advice or medical advice.

## The numbers

| Number | Service | Use it for |
|---|---|---|
| 112 | National emergency call centre | Any incident or emergency; calls are forwarded to the right service |
| 113 | Police | Crime, danger, traffic accident needing police |
| 114 | Fire and rescue | Fire, rescue, trapped people |
| 115 | Ambulance and emergency medical | Injury, illness, emergency medical help |

The 112 centre, created under Decree 200/2025/ND-CP, receives information on incidents, disasters and emergencies. It is free, available 24 hours a day and linked to 113, 114 and 115. All calls are recorded, and the decree provides that deliberately false reports are handled under the law. So call when you need to, and do not call as a joke or a test. Save the numbers on your phone before you need them.

## What to say when you call

You may have to speak in simple English to an operator who may not be fluent. Keep it short and clear.

1. **Say what has happened:** "Motorbike accident," "fire," "person injured."

2. **Say where you are.** Give a street name, a junction, a landmark, or a map pin if you can send one. In Hanoi, say the street, the ward and the nearest landmark.

3. **Say how many people are hurt,** and whether anyone is not breathing or not responding.

4. **Say whether there is danger,** such as fuel on the road, fire or traffic.

5. **Give your name and phone number,** and stay on the line until the operator ends the call.

6. **Do not hang up** until told to.

If you cannot be understood, hand the phone to a local person, a shop worker or a hotel employee, who can speak for you. Ask the rental shop on 0334 699 969 (+84 334 699 969), open 09:00 to 21:00 daily, to help translate if it is open.

## What the law requires after a road accident

Law 36/2024/QH15 contains several provisions.

**Article 80, responsibilities.** The driver who causes an accident, people involved and people present must stop immediately, warn of danger, keep the scene intact, help victims and report to the nearest police, medical facility or People's Committee. They must stay at the scene until police arrive, unless they are taking a victim for emergency care or face a threat to their safety, in which case they must report immediately to the nearest police or People's Committee. Bystanders must help protect victims' property and assist rescue.

**Article 81, reporting.** Agencies, organisations and individuals who detect a road traffic accident must report it immediately to the nearest Public Security agency, medical facility or People's Committee. Police who receive a report must send forces to the scene. A medical facility that gives first aid must report to the nearest police and conduct a blood test for alcohol, drugs or stimulants for vehicle operators, or take and preserve samples. A People's Committee that receives a report must pass it to the competent police. Insurers who receive a report send staff or representatives to the scene.

**Article 82, rescue.** Police lead rescue. Medical facilities must send staff and means to give first aid, take victims to hospital and support and treat victims of road traffic accidents in all cases.

**Article 83, investigation.** Accidents must be investigated and settled promptly, accurately and objectively. For accidents with no signs of a crime, the investigation includes alcohol and drug tests for operators, examination of the scene and vehicles, temporary seizure of vehicles, objects, documents and licences, recording statements and checking vehicle and operator conditions.

**Article 9, prohibited acts.** The law bans fleeing after causing an accident to avoid responsibility, and deliberately refusing to help victims when you are able to.

Decree 168/2024/ND-CP, as read on the Government portal, sets heavy fines for not stopping and not helping after an accident, including VND 8,000,000 to 10,000,000 for a driver who causes an accident and fails to stop, to keep the scene, to help the injured or to report to police. ## At the scene: step by step

1. **Stop, and make the area safe.** Switch off the engine, switch on hazard lights and, if safe, move vehicles that block traffic after taking photographs. Article 80 requires you to keep the scene intact, so do not tidy up more than safety demands.

2. **Check people.** Look for injuries. Do not move a seriously injured person unless there is immediate danger, such as fire.

3. **Call for help.** Dial 112, or 115 for an ambulance and 113 for the police.

4. **Give first aid** if you are trained.

5. **Photograph the scene:** vehicles, plates, positions, damage, road markings, signs and the surroundings.

6. **Exchange details,** names, phone numbers, vehicle plates and, if the other party is a driver, their licence and insurance details, and note any witnesses and cameras nearby.

7. **Wait for the police,** unless you are taking a victim for emergency care.

8. **Do not argue or admit fault.** Describe facts only.

9. **Call the rental shop** and send photographs.

10. **Keep your documents ready,** including the contract, your licence and your passport copy.

## What happens at the hospital

Article 82 requires medical facilities to support and treat road accident victims in all cases. In practice:

- **Bring ID and the rental contract,** if you can, and your travel insurance details.

- **Ask for medical records and receipts,** in English if available, for your insurer.

- **Expect an alcohol or drug test** for operators involved in an accident, as Article 81 provides.

- **Keep every receipt.**

## What the police may do

Under Article 83 of the Law, police investigating an accident may take statements, examine the scene and vehicles, test operators for alcohol and drugs and temporarily seize vehicles, documents and licences. This is why you should tell the shop quickly: the bike and its papers belong to the shop, and the shop may need to deal with the police about them. Ask for a record of what is taken, and keep a copy of any paper you sign. If you do not understand a document, ask for a translator and tell your embassy or consulate, and do not sign anything you do not understand.

## Reporting a crime, such as theft

For a crime, including theft of a rental bike, the Ministry of Public Security's guidance on reporting crimes says a report may be made orally or in writing, in person at the commune or ward police or a police station, and that the reporter has the right to be told the result. ## Who else to tell

1. **The rental shop,** immediately.

2. **Your travel insurer,** within the time its policy requires.

3. **Your embassy or consulate,** if you are seriously injured, detained or lose your passport.

4. **Your accommodation,** so that someone knows where you are.

5. **Family or friends,** so that they do not worry.

## Keep a record

Keep the following for several weeks, and until any deposit, claim or dispute is settled.

- **Photographs and videos** of the scene.

- **Names, numbers and plates.**

- **Any police paper,** with a note of the officer's name and station.

- **Medical records and receipts.**

- **Messages with the shop.**

- **Your own notes,** written the same day, of what happened, with times and places.

## A quick checklist

1. **Know 112, 113, 114 and 115.**

2. **Say what, where, how many hurt, any danger.**

3. **Stop, make safe, keep the scene, help victims.**

4. **Photograph and exchange details.**

5. **Report to the police, and stay until they arrive unless taking someone for emergency care.**

6. **Call the shop and your insurer.**

7. **Keep every paper.**

## FAQ: emergency numbers and accident reporting

### What is the emergency number in Vietnam?

112 is the national emergency call centre, free and open 24 hours a day. It connects with 113 for police, 114 for fire and rescue and 115 for ambulances.

### Must I report a road accident?

Yes. Article 81 of Law 36/2024/QH15 requires anyone who detects one to report it immediately to the nearest police, medical facility or People's Committee.

### Can I leave the scene?

Not if you are involved, unless you are taking a victim for emergency care or face a threat to your safety, in which case you must report immediately. Article 80 requires you to stay until the police arrive.

### Will the hospital treat me without insurance?

Article 82 requires medical facilities to support and treat road accident victims in all cases. Bring your ID and insurance details, and keep receipts.

### What if the police take my documents or the bike?

Article 83 allows temporary seizure during an investigation. Ask for a record, tell the shop at once and keep copies.

### Should I call the rental shop?

Yes, as soon as it is safe, with photographs.
